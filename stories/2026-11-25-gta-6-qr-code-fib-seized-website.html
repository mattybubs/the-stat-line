GTA 6 hid working QR codes in the world — scan one with your real phone and the FIB 'seizes' the website

By Matt

Rockstar hid real QR codes on posters and cars all over Leonida. Scan one with your actual phone and you land on a spoof federal seizure notice — a joke Rubius walked straight into live, in front of millions.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

Rockstar didn't just fill Leonida with detail — it wired the world to the real internet. Hidden QR codes are plastered on posters, bathroom walls, and cars all over GTA 6, and they're not decoration. Point your real phone at one and it opens a page on Rockstar's own website: a parody seizure notice from the Federal Investigation Bureau, the game's fictional FBI, declaring the site seized under warrant.

Spanish streamer Rubius found that out the hard way, live. Standing in a bathroom in front of a poster with a QR code, he tried scanning it with his in-game phone first. Nothing happened. So he pulled out his real phone and tried again — and the spoof seizure notice popped up on screen. The clip, shared by Movistar eSports, has passed 21.8 million views on X. He wasn't the only one caught out: streamer ohnePixel and other players have reported falling for the same gag.

What the codes actually do

The codes route through Rockstar's rsg.ms/qr1 short link to a page on rockstargames.com that pretends the FIB seized the site, citing a warrant under "47 U.S.C. § 11.25(A)." The joke keeps going once you're there: the page claims that, due to a lack of federal funding, the FIB is selling ad space on seized IP addresses. The rotating joke ads change between visits.

The pages tie into Peekhole, GTA 6's parody of creator subscription sites — QR stickers for it show up on cars around Leonida, and fan tracking site Peekhole.de has been cataloguing the codes' destinations. Players have also confirmed the same seized-page gag from a code at Dynamix Fitness, the in-game gym, and from a QR code in a shower during HasanAbi's charity stream on November 22.

QR codes are a real mechanic, not a one-off gag

This isn't a single hidden joke. Scanning QR codes is built into how the game works: the side mission "The Final Flush" starts by scanning a QR code on a handwritten poster by a toilet — with the in-game WaiNK IT app — which kicks off a conspiracy-theorist questline about Big Sewage. Rockstar just made some of the codes in the world point somewhere real, too, and let players discover the difference for themselves.

The in-game codes are safe to scan. The usual caution applies to codes anywhere else, but inside Leonida, the worst a QR code can do is get you fake-arrested by a fake federal agency.

Leonida keeps rewarding the curious

A week after launch, the discoveries haven't slowed down. The QR gag sits alongside the Mario and Luigi hats hidden in the Lummox County woods and the time-travel trip back to PS2-era Vice City — a game that keeps hiding jokes in places only the players who stop and look will ever find. The FIB seizure page is the funniest kind of Rockstar punchline: the game reaches out of the screen, grabs your real phone, and arrests you for it.
