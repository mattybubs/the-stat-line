Cubs land Japanese Triple Crown winner Teruaki Sato on six years, $120 million — $240 million in 12 hours

By Matt

Hours after landing Trevor Rogers, the Cubs struck again: Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato is headed to Chicago on the second nine-figure deal of a wild 12 hours.

The move

The Cubs are spending like a team that believes its window is now.

Hours after landing Trevor Rogers on Monday night, Chicago agreed to terms with Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato on Tuesday morning, ESPN's Jeff Passan first reported. The deal is for six years, with Fox Sports, the New York Post, theScore and MLB Trade Rumors all confirming the agreement. MLB Trade Rumors put the scale of the spree plainly: the Cubs have now committed $240 million in the span of about 12 hours.

Sato was posted by the Hanshin Tigers on Nov. 1, which opened a 45-day window for major league teams to negotiate with his representatives. Chicago won the race before that window closed — and before the collective bargaining agreement expires on Dec. 1, with a lockout expected to follow.

The player

Sato is 27 — he turns 28 at the start of the 2027 season — and he arrives with the kind of resume that does not need translating.

He is a two-time Central League MVP who won the 2025 NPB MVP Award, and this year he won the Triple Crown for Hanshin, hitting .306 with 39 home runs and 106 RBIs across 143 games. Over the last two seasons he has averaged 39.5 home runs, 104 RBIs and a .594 slugging percentage per year. He played six seasons for the Tigers, from 2021 through 2026, and suited up for Japan in the 2026 World Baseball Classic.

He was primarily a third baseman in Japan, but that is not where Chicago plans to play him. Passan reports Sato is expected to play a corner outfield spot for the Cubs, and the New York Post lists him as a corner outfielder and designated hitter.

The fit

The outfield needed the help. Seiya Suzuki and Ian Happ are both free agents, and the Cubs' corner spots were the softest part of a roster that won 89 games last season, made the playoffs for a second straight year, and was swept by the San Diego Padres in the National League Wild Card Series.

Sato gives them a middle-of-the-order bat who has already produced at an elite level against professional pitching — just in a different league. The Cubs will also owe a posting fee to Hanshin on top of the contract, which does not count against the competitive balance tax threshold.

What it means

Two signings, two nine-figure commitments, one night. The Rogers deal fixed the rotation. This one fixes the lineup. Together they say the Cubs are not waiting for the lockout to end to get better — they are doing it now, while the market is moving.

The risk is the one every Japanese star carries on arrival: the numbers are real, but they were put up in NPB, and the adjustment to major league pitching is the question no scouting report can fully answer. The Cubs just bet nine figures that the Triple Crown translates.
