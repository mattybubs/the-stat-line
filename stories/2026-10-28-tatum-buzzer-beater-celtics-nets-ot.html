Tatum's Buzzer-Beater Lifts Celtics Over Nets 125-124 in OT

By Matt

Jayson Tatum buried an elbow jumper as time expired in overtime — capping a 40-point night and a Celtics comeback from three down with 2.3 seconds left.

Jayson Tatum ended it at the buzzer. With Boston trailing 124-123 and 2.3 seconds left in overtime, Tatum took the inbounds, rose up from the elbow, and sank a turnaround jumper as time expired to give the Celtics a 125-124 win over the Brooklyn Nets at TD Garden.

The shot

The final sequence was pure end-of-game theater. Brooklyn had the lead and the ball late in OT, but Boston got the stop it needed and the ball back with 2.3 seconds to play, down one. Derrick White found Tatum, who turned and fired over the defense. The shot fell as the clock hit zero, and the review upheld it — Celtics 125, Nets 124.

It was Tatum's 40th point of the night, and the loudest two of them.

The game

Nothing about this one came easy for Boston. The Nets trailed 117-114 late in the fourth before rookie Mikel Brown Jr. — who scored 13 in the fourth quarter — buried a game-tying three with about a second left in regulation to force overtime.

Brooklyn struck first in the extra period. Julius Randle scored the first two buckets of OT to put the Nets up 121-117, but he fouled out with 24 points and nine rebounds, and the Nets never scored again. Paul George's free throws pushed Brooklyn ahead 124-121, then the Celtics closed with four straight — capped by Tatum's buzzer-beater.

The line

Tatum finished with a game-high 40 points on 10-of-20 shooting, plus five rebounds and eight assists in 42 minutes. The headline inside the line: a career-high 18 free throws on 22 attempts — he lived at the stripe all night and delivered when it mattered most.

Paul George added 20 points, seven rebounds and seven assists for Boston. For Brooklyn, Michael Porter Jr. led the way with 27 points and 12 rebounds, and Randle added his 24 before fouling out.

What it means

The win is Boston's third straight — the Celtics move to 3-1. The Nets fall to 0-4, and this one will sting: they had the game tied in regulation, led by four in OT, and led with 2.3 seconds left, and still walked out with a loss.

For Tatum, it is a statement night. The 40 points, the career-high free-throw mark, and a buzzer-beater to win it — that is the kind of game that defines an early season.
