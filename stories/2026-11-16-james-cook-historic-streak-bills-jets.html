James Cook runs into history — 157 yards, a 74-yard dagger, and a streak only four players have ever touched

By Matt

James Cook rushed 18 times for 157 yards and a touchdown against the Jets, becoming just the fifth player in NFL history with at least 75 rushing yards and a rushing touchdown in eight consecutive games.

The night

The Jets lost a fumble on a failed hook-and-lateral in the fourth quarter. Ten seconds later, James Cook made them pay for it — and made history in the process.

Cook took a handoff, broke free down the right side, and ripped off a 74-yard touchdown run that put Buffalo's 31-12 win over the Jets out of reach with 4:42 remaining. He finished with 18 carries for 157 yards and the score, and the run itself was a small piece of physics: 21.56 mph on the play, according to Next Gen Stats, the second-fastest touchdown run of his NFL career. It was Buffalo's longest run of the season and the second-longest in the NFL this year.

But the 74-yarder was only the exclamation point. The sentence it finished is the story: Cook became just the fifth player in NFL history with at least 75 rushing yards and a rushing touchdown in eight consecutive games.

The company

Here are the longest such streaks in NFL history:

• Terry Allen, Washington: 10 games (1995-96)

• John Riggins, Washington: 8 games (1983)

• George Rogers, Washington: 8 games (1985-86)

• LaDainian Tomlinson, San Diego Chargers: 8 games (2006)

• James Cook, Buffalo Bills: 8 games (2026)

Cook is the first to do it since Tomlinson in 2006 — the year the Hall of Fame running back was named NFL MVP. Three of the other four names on the list are in the Hall of Fame or on the short list of the greatest running backs ever to play. Cook now shares a line with them.

He also has at least 100 yards from scrimmage and a touchdown in five consecutive games, and a rushing touchdown in eight straight — a streak that has quietly become the engine of the best team in the AFC.

The team around him

Buffalo improved to 8-1 with its fifth consecutive win, and the Bills did it the way they've done it all season: a defense that gave up yards (376) but no touchdowns, four sacks, 12 quarterback hits and two takeaways, and an offense that needed just seven plays to score three second-half touchdowns covering 202 yards in 2 minutes, 49 seconds. Cook's 74-yarder was the last of them — one play, 74 yards, ten seconds.

The Bills are 8-1 for the first time since 1991 and have won eight straight road games. Cook has now rushed for 814 yards on the season, and the Jets — who came in having lost to Buffalo five straight times — fell to 2-8, with head coach Aaron Glenn now 0-9 against AFC East opponents.

Cook's own reaction to the speed number was the same as his coach's: both Josh Allen and head coach Joe Brady wondered aloud after the game how fast he was running. The answer — 21.56 mph — is now in the books next to a streak that only four players in the history of the league have ever touched.
