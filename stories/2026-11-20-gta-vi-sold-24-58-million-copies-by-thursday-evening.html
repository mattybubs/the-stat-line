GTA VI sold 24.58 million copies by Thursday evening

By Matt

Grand Theft Auto VI moved an estimated 24.58 million copies worth about $2.36 billion by the end of launch day, nearly triple GTA V's first-day haul — and the world watched along with it.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

Grand Theft Auto VI arrived on November 19, 2026, after 13 years of waiting — and by Thursday evening it had sold an estimated 24.58 million copies, worth about $2.36 billion. The launch didn't just break the old record. It nearly tripled it.

Launch day by the numbers

Copies sold by Thursday evening: 24.58 million (est.)

Estimated revenue: $2.36 billion

Peak simultaneous viewers: 7.91 million

Hours watched in first 24 hours: 125.47 million

PlayStation 5 share: ~75% (18.44 million copies, $1.77 billion)

Xbox Series X\: S share; ~25% (6.15 million copies, $588 million)

The numbers come from market intelligence firm Sensor Tower, as reported by Reuters, GameSpot, and Deadline. One caveat matters: none of this is official yet. Take-Two Interactive has not released its own sales figures, and chief executive Strauss Zelnick has said official data will be shared soon. Until then, every figure here is a third-party estimate — but it is an estimate built on store data, and it moved in one direction all week.

How the total climbed

The tracker told the story in real time. Pre-orders alone crossed $1 billion from an estimated 10.31 million units eight days before release, and by midday Wednesday — before the game had even unlocked — the running estimate stood at 17.1 million copies and $1.65 billion. Thursday morning it was 19.1 million and $1.84 billion. Then launch day did the rest, adding roughly $520 million between the morning and evening snapshots.

Pre-order revenue passed $2 billion through launch day, which made the game 2026's best-selling title on pre-orders alone before a single copy was played. Sensor Tower's Karl Kontus put it plainly: the game "has captivated consumers, becoming 2026's best-selling game based on pre-orders alone."

Against the old record

The comparison that frames the scale is GTA V. The previous game earned roughly $800 million in its first 24 hours in September 2013, on 11.21 million copies — a mark Guinness World Records recognizes as the best single-day haul for any video game. It needed three days to pass $1 billion.

The new game cleared that bar before most players had finished downloading it. The estimated day-one revenue is nearly three times the old figure, and the unit count is more than double. The old record stood for 13 years. The new one arrived in a single evening.

Leonida, finally open

The game itself is set in Leonida, a fictional Florida, with Vice City at its center. Lucia and Jason — the dual protagonists introduced across the trailers — anchor a story 13 years in the making, and the launch finally puts the state players have been dissecting frame by frame since the first trailer in December 2023 into their hands.

It is out now on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. There is no PC version yet.

The world watched, too

The launch was a viewing event as much as a sales event. The game peaked at 7.91 million simultaneous viewers, with 125.47 million hours watched in the first 24 hours — launch-day streams, first playthroughs, and reaction content running around the clock. The sales record and the viewership record arrived together, which is fitting: for a game this anticipated, watching it was half the launch.
