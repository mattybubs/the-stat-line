There's a glitch that runs GTA 6 at 60 FPS on Xbox — but the whole game runs at double speed

By Matt

A Quick Resume trick on Xbox Series X uncaps GTA 6's 30 FPS limit — Digital Foundry confirmed it works. The catch: the game's speed is tied to its frame rate, so at 60 FPS everything moves at twice the speed.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

GTA 6's 30 frames-per-second cap is the one thing about the launch that players will not stop arguing about, and over the weekend someone found a way around it. Reddit user Meowwie64 discovered that a quirk in the Xbox's Quick Resume feature lifts the cap entirely on Xbox Series X — and Digital Foundry has confirmed the trick works. The game can run at 60 frames per second and beyond.

There is a catch, and it is a big one. The game's speed is tied to its frame rate. Push past 30 and the whole world speeds up with it.

How it works

The trick, posted to the r/GTA6unmoderated subreddit on November 21 and demonstrated on game version 01.002.001, needs an Xbox Series X and a 120Hz display:

1. Set the Xbox display output to 60Hz

2. Launch GTA 6 and wait until the six stars appear in the intro

3. Switch the display settings back to 120Hz

4. Cold-boot another game from the Xbox Guide without quitting GTA 6

5. Switch back to GTA 6 through Quick Resume — the 30 FPS cap is gone

It also works on Xbox Series S, according to testing by the YouTube channel Fuzion Xbox Testing, covered by Pure Xbox. There is no equivalent on PlayStation 5, because Quick Resume is an Xbox feature.

What you actually get

It is not a locked 60. It is uncapped, which means the frame rate goes wherever the hardware can take it in a given scene — and the hardware usually cannot take it far. Digital Foundry measured the Series X at an average of 38.8 frames per second on a test road, peaking at 48, while the Series S averaged 32 on the same stretch. Highway driving in the glitched state lands around 35 to 40 FPS. Readings of 60 and above show up only in light scenes — an empty sky while flying a plane, or a quiet interior, where Meowwie64 reported a stable 90 FPS and cutscenes clearing 60.

That is still a real improvement over 30 in the moments where it holds. It is just not the smooth 60 FPS mode players have been asking for, and the reason it is not tells you why Rockstar never shipped one.

The catch: the world runs at double speed

GTA 6 ties its simulation to its frame rate, the way games did a generation ago. Above 30 FPS, character animations, movement, and physics all accelerate. At 60 FPS the game effectively runs at twice its intended speed. Meowwie64 put it plainly: "The game speed seems to be tied to your framerate. I have a stable 90fps and everything works fine but Jason's animations run at 2x speed or more."

Cutscenes and dialogue are the exception — those play at normal speed even in the glitched state. Everything else, from driving to shootouts, runs fast-forward. The same trick can also lock the game at 15 FPS if it goes wrong, and running in an unsupported state risks crashes. Quitting GTA 6 completely and relaunching it restores the normal 30 FPS cap.

Why Rockstar didn't ship this

Rockstar has said the current consoles' processors cannot run GTA 6's simulation any faster — even the PS5 Pro is not powerful enough for 60 FPS, in the studio's telling. Digital Foundry's own testing backs up the constraint: its console benchmarks found the Series X dipping to 21 or 22 FPS in the heaviest shootouts at the standard 30 FPS target. An unlocked mode on that hardware would swing wildly between fast-forward and slideshow.

The footage from the glitch also briefly fueled claims that Rockstar artificially capped the game, but the numbers cut the other way. If the hardware could hold 60 in real gameplay, the glitch would show it. Instead it shows 35 to 40 on an open highway and a world that sprints whenever the frame rate climbs. Digital Foundry's takeaway is that a proper 40 FPS mode is the realistic ask — not an unlocked one.

Whether the glitch survives the game's first patch is an open question. Rockstar began rolling out update 1.002.002 on Monday, and its published patch notes do not mention the frame-rate trick. Rockstar has not commented on it.
