Steelers Throttle Browns 27-6: Eight Sacks, 143 Warren Yards, and Sole Possession of the AFC North

By Matt

Pittsburgh sacked Deshaun Watson eight times and ran for three touchdowns in a 27-6 rout of Cleveland, taking sole possession of first place in the AFC North.

The Pittsburgh Steelers did not just beat the Cleveland Browns on Sunday. They throttled them, 27-6 at Acrisure Stadium, behind eight sacks, three rushing touchdowns, and a running game Cleveland never slowed down.

The win moves Pittsburgh to 6-2 and into sole possession of first place in the AFC North. It was the Steelers' 23rd consecutive regular-season home victory over the Browns, and revenge for a 27-24 loss in Cleveland four weeks ago.

The pass rush set the tone

It took three plays for the game's first significant moment. Pittsburgh's Jaquan Brisker was called for roughing the passer on Cleveland's opening possession, sending Deshaun Watson to the medical tent for a concussion evaluation. Watson returned, but nothing got better from there.

Watson finished 27-of-39 for a season-high 324 yards, but with no touchdowns, no interceptions, and eight sacks for 41 yards. He was hit 13 times, lost a fumble on a strip-sack that set up Pittsburgh's first touchdown, and the Browns failed to reach the end zone for the first time this season, scoring on just two of 12 possessions. T.J. Watt led the rush with two sacks, while Cam Heyward and Patrick Queen added 1.5 apiece.

Shedeur Sanders mopped up late, going 2-of-2 for two yards.

The ground game did the rest

Jaylen Warren ran 17 times for 143 yards, including a twisting 51-yard run that set up Pittsburgh's second score. Rico Dowdle added 56 yards and a 9-yard touchdown at 7:57 of the first quarter after Keeanu Benton recovered Watson's fumble. Pat Freiermuth rushed for a 1-yard score to cap a 69-yard drive in 44 seconds for a 20-6 halftime lead. In the third quarter, Aaron Rodgers hit Darnell Washington on a 58-yard catch-and-run down to the 1, and Warren scored from a yard out at 9:39 to make it 27-6.

Washington finished with three catches for 94 yards. Rodgers went 13-of-25 for 172 yards with an interception, and did not need to be more than that.

The one bright spot for Cleveland

Harold Fannin Jr. caught 10 passes for 154 yards, the third-most yardage ever by a Browns tight end, but it was almost all of Cleveland's damage. Quinshon Judkins was held to 25 rushing yards, and the Browns went 0-for-3 in the red zone. Early in the fourth, Cleveland had first-and-goal at the Pittsburgh 2, but Watson threw three straight incompletions for a turnover on downs with 12:53 to play.

Cleveland falls to 5-3, a game behind Pittsburgh in the North, without another head-to-head chance to close the gap. The rivals split their two meetings. Sunday showed how wide that gap can look.
