Air Jordan 6 'White Infrared' drops today — MJ's first championship shoe returns with Nike Air on the heel

By Elijah

The OG 'White Infrared' Jordan 6 returns today, November 7, for $215 — the first retro since 2014, and the first since 1991 to bring back the Nike Air branding on the heel.

The Air Jordan 6 Retro "White Infrared" drops today, Saturday, November 7, 2026 — and for Jordan collectors, this is the one circled on the calendar all month. The OG colorway from 1991 returns in full-family sizing for $215 for men ($155 grade school, $95 preschool, $80 toddler), through Nike and select Jordan retailers.

This is the shoe Michael Jordan wore to his first championship. Tinker Hatfield designed the 6 with a streamlined shape inspired by German sports cars, and the "White Infrared" — crisp white leather, sharp red accents — is one of the model's original colorways. It has been 35 years since the debut, and 12 years since the last retro in 2014 (before that, 2010). For a lot of collectors, this is the first real shot at the pair in over a decade.

What makes this retro different

The detail that matters most: Nike Air is back on the heel. The 2010 and 2014 retros swapped the original Nike Air heel branding for a Jumpman logo. This release returns to the original construction, with the Nike Air graphic embroidered in white on the heel — the way the 1991 pair looked.

The rest of the shoe is faithful to the OG too. A crisp white leather upper with the 6's signature zig-zag structure and perforated panels across the sides, Infrared details on the midsole, the lace toggle, and the Jumpman logo on the tongue. Style code CT8529-105.

Where to get them

The "White Infrared" 6 is a wide release — Nike SNKRS and select Jordan Brand retailers, in-store and online, in full-family sizing. At $215 for men, it is also one of the more accessible grails on the November calendar, and pre-release pairs were already listed under retail in some sizes in the weeks leading up to the drop.

If you only buy one Jordan in November, the release calendars are near-unanimous: this is the one.
