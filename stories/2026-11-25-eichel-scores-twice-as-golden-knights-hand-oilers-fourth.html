Eichel scores twice as Golden Knights hand Oilers fourth straight loss

By Matt

Jack Eichel and Trevor Connelly each scored twice as Vegas beat Edmonton 5-3 at Rogers Place, pulling within three points of the Pacific lead.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

Jack Eichel and Trevor Connelly each scored twice as the Vegas Golden Knights beat the Edmonton Oilers 5-3 at Rogers Place on Tuesday night, handing Edmonton its fourth straight loss and tightening the race at the top of the Pacific Division.

It was the first meeting of the season between the division's top two teams, and it lived up to the billing. Vegas pulled within three points of Edmonton for first place with the win.

How it went down

Vegas came out flying and had a two-goal lead before the first period was ten minutes old.

Eichel opened the scoring on the power play at 6:13 of the first, taking a cross-ice feed from Mitch Marner and beating Devon Levi far side with a wrist shot from the left dot. Less than three minutes later, Connelly doubled the lead, stripping the puck from Shakir Mukhamadullin in front of the Edmonton crease and lifting a quick backhand past Levi's glove.

Edmonton answered in the second. Colton Dach got the Oilers on the board, and Connor McDavid tied it 2-2 at 19:41 of the period on a give-and-go.

The Knights had the sharper response after the intermission. Nic Dowd restored the lead from in front at 2:08 of the third, and Eichel finished a 2-on-1 rush with a wrist shot five-hole at 5:29 to make it 4-2. Vasily Podkolzin pulled one back just 16 seconds later, but Connelly sealed it with an empty-net goal at 17:28.

The night's big performances

Trevor Connelly (VGK): 2 goals, 1 assist

Jack Eichel (VGK): 2 goals

Mitch Marner (VGK): 2 assists

Connor McDavid (EDM): 1 goal

Carter Hart (VGK): 21 saves

Devon Levi (EDM): 22 saves

For Connelly, it was a first on two fronts: his first career three-point game and his first career multi-goal game. The rookie's two goals were his eighth and ninth of the season, moving him into the rookie scoring lead with 19 points. He has seven points in his last four games.

Eichel's second goal was the game-winner, and he has seven points in his last five games.

Where the Pacific race stands

The win was Vegas's second in a row after a four-game skid, lifting the Knights to 15-6-5. Edmonton fell to 17-6-4 and is 0-3-1 during its season-long losing streak — the longest skid of the year for a team that has spent most of the season setting the pace in the West.

Vegas now sits three points back of Edmonton with the division lead in sight. The two teams won't have to wait long for the next chapter — and with the gap this tight, every meeting between them carries real weight in the standings.
