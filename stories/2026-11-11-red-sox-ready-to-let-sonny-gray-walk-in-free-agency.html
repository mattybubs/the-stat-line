Red Sox ready to let Sonny Gray walk in free agency

By Matt

Boston is comfortable letting Sonny Gray walk, per Alex Speier — Craig Breslow calls the rotation a strength for 2027, so the money goes to position players instead.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The call

The Red Sox are comfortable letting Sonny Gray walk in free agency, according to Alex Speier on the 310 to Left podcast. Craig Breslow called the rotation a strength for 2027, so Boston plans to put that money toward position players instead.

It's a clear-eyed call, not a shrug. Gray was one of Boston's best arms this year, but the front office is betting the rotation holds up without him — and that the lineup is where the next dollars matter more.

What Gray did in Boston

Gray's one season in a Red Sox uniform was a strong one: 18-5 with a 2.72 ERA and 149 strikeouts over 169 innings in 29 starts. Boston declined his $30 million mutual option for 2027, and he took the $10 million buyout into the open market instead.

Record: 18-5

ERA: 2.72

Strikeouts: 149

Innings: 169

Starts: 29

That line will make him one of the more attractive arms available this winter — and it also explains why Boston isn't chasing him. A pitcher coming off a season like that doesn't come cheap, and the Sox have decided the rotation is already covered.

Where Boston goes from here

The question now is who fills the innings. Boston has already been linked to Freddy Peralta as a possible replacement — The Stat Line covered the $92 million prediction earlier this week — but the message from Breslow is that the front office isn't panicking about the rotation. The priority is the lineup.

That tracks with the rest of Boston's offseason so far. The Sox have already signaled they're open to moving Jarren Duran and Masataka Yoshida, with the outfield set around Roman Anthony, Wilyer Abreu and Ceddanne Rafaela. The money that would have gone to Gray now looks earmarked for bats.
