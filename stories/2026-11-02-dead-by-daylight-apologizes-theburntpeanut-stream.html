Dead by Daylight Apologizes After TheBurntPeanut Stream Sparks a Weekend Backlash

By Matt

Behaviour Interactive apologized November 1 after a staff member gifted streamer TheBurntPeanut free access to Dead by Daylight's full roster during a Halloween stream — a decision the studio says was made without proper approval.

Halloween weekend brought Dead by Daylight an unwelcome kind of attention. Behaviour Interactive apologized on November 1 after a staff member gifted popular Twitch streamer TheBurntPeanut free access to the game's entire roster of Survivors and Killers during a Halloween stream — setting off a community backlash that ended with the studio admitting it handled the whole thing badly.

The sequence, as reported by Dexerto and Games.gg, went like this. TheBurntPeanut, who built his audience largely on extraction shooters like Arc Raiders, played Dead by Daylight for the first time on stream and discovered he had been given instant access to dozens of characters that regular players unlock over time with in-game currency. "Guys! I just got all the guys!" he said on stream, joking about "streamer privileges."

Then the game's official Twitch account weighed in with a blunt message: "WE CAN TAKE THE HEAT! THEY ARE YOURS TO KEEP!"

The apology — and why it did not land cleanly

The backlash had two layers: objections to Behaviour supporting TheBurntPeanut, including criticism of his past on-stream language, and anger at the dismissive response from the official account. Some players posted that they had uninstalled the game as the dispute escalated.

Behaviour's November 1 statement, posted on DbD Pulse, its community account, identified two failures: the decision to award the content was made without the appropriate approvals, and when concerns were raised during the stream, the team should have investigated them instead of dismissing them. The studio also condemned inflammatory language directed at members of the community and said threats against employees were unacceptable, adding that it has begun reviewing its internal processes to prevent a repeat.

Even the apology drew criticism — for appearing on the smaller DbD Pulse account rather than the main Dead by Daylight account, which has 1.3 million followers. For a community that has stuck with the game through ten years of service, the placement read as part of the problem: the loud response went on the big stage, the apology on the small one.

What to watch

Giving creators a sneak peek at content is not unusual in itself — Behaviour's own statement notes that — and this story is really about process: who approved it, how concerns were handled in the moment, and where the accountability shows up afterward. The studio says its review is already underway. The community, judging by the weekend, will be watching whether anything actually changes the next time a big streamer drops into the Fog.
