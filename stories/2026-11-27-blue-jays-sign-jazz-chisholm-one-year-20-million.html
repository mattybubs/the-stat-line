Jazz Chisholm Jr. is a Blue Jay — one year, $20 million, and a $40 million option behind it

By Matt

Toronto adds the first big-name second baseman of the offseason on a bet-on-himself deal — and takes him from the Yankees in the process.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The deal

The Blue Jays are in agreement with Jazz Chisholm Jr. on a one-year contract that guarantees him $20 million, FanSided's Robert Murray first reported, with ESPN's Jeff Passan first with the terms and MLB.com's Mark Feinsand, The Athletic's Ken Rosenthal and the New York Post's Jon Heyman all confirming the structure. The guarantee breaks down as a $6 million signing bonus and a $12.5 million salary for 2027, plus a $1.5 million buyout. Chisholm holds a $16 million player option for 2028; if he declines it, the option converts to a $40 million club option for Toronto. Exercising the player option locks in $34.5 million over two years; the club option would take it to $58.5 million.

The Yankees never made it a contest. They declined to extend Chisholm the $23.15 million qualifying offer, so Toronto signs him without losing a draft pick — and the team that let him walk watches him land with a division rival.

The player

Chisholm is 28 and turns 29 in February, a two-time All-Star who has spent seven big-league seasons putting up one of the strangest profiles in baseball: huge tools, huge variance.

The ceiling showed up in 2025, his first full year in the Bronx. He hit 31 homers, stole 30 bases and posted an .813 OPS across 130 games, good for 4.1 WAR — the second All-Star berth of his career. Then came the platform year. In 2026 he hit 19 homers with 55 RBIs and 41 stolen bases, but his OPS fell to .711 over 137 games, just below league average. The career line sits at .245/.315/.444 with 14.3 WAR.

He is also the first major free-agent second baseman to sign this offseason, and he did not lack for suitors — the Nationals and Padres were both reportedly in the mix before Toronto closed it. The market clearly priced him as a rebound candidate, and the structure of the deal says Chisholm agrees with that read: take the one-year guarantee, bet on the 2025 version showing up again, and hit the market at 30 with a much bigger number attached.

The fit

Toronto needs him everywhere, which is the point. The Jays stumbled to a 79-83 finish in 2026, a year after reaching Game 7 of the World Series, and the lineup was the problem as much as the rotation. Shortly after the season, general manager Ross Atkins told Toronto media that second baseman Ernie Clement — a 2025 playoff hero and a 2026 All-Star — would have to earn an everyday role in 2027. That was the opening, and Chisholm walks straight through it.

He settled in at second base with the Yankees over the past two seasons after struggling defensively at third in 2024, and he has also played shortstop and center field in his career. The Jays have questions at all of those spots: they traded Daulton Varsho out of center field, Addison Barger missed almost all of 2026 injured, and Brett Bateman's late-season audition in center was encouraging without being definitive. Chisholm gives them a starter at second and a fallback in center, with the speed to change a game from either spot — 41 steals in a down year is not a fluke, it is who he is.

What it means

This is the second big swing of Toronto's week, three days after the club landed Japanese right-hander Kaima Taira, and it follows the same logic: buy the upside before the market catches up. The Jays get the 2025 version of Chisholm at one-year money with a club option behind it — if he repeats last season, they can walk away for the price of the buyout; if he repeats 2025, they hold a $40 million option on a 29-year-old entering his prime. Chisholm gets a full-time job in a lineup that needs his bat, a ballpark where his pull power plays, and a clean shot at free agency again next winter.

There is a clock on all of it. The collective bargaining agreement expires Dec. 1, with a lockout expected to freeze player movement the moment it does — which is why the Jays are closing deals on a holiday weekend instead of waiting for the winter meetings. Toronto has now added a front-line starter and a starting second baseman in four days. The team that lost the World Series in seven games a year ago is not waiting around to find out whether the window is still open.
