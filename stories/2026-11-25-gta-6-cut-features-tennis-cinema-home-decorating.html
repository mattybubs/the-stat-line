Rockstar reveals what GTA 6 cut: tennis, cinema trips, home decorating, crafting — and why each one went

By Matt

A week after launch, Rockstar North's Rob Nelson and Scott Mitchell told PC Games what GTA 6 left on the cutting-room floor: tennis that didn't meet the bar, cinema trips that weren't interactive enough, and a home life that didn't fit the story.

Grand Theft Auto 6 is out, and Rockstar is already telling players what is not in it. In an interview published November 25 with the German outlet PC Games, Rockstar North development manager Rob Nelson and design producer Scott Mitchell laid out the features that were planned or prototyped and then dropped before launch — and none of them were cut for time alone. Each one failed a different test.

The list, as the two developers laid it out: tennis, cinema and theatre visits, crawling, home decorating, and crafting.

Tennis is the cut that stings most

Tennis is the one players have actually played before. The sport was in GTA 5, and Rockstar built a version for GTA 6. Then the studio looked at it and decided it was not good enough to ship, so the minigame went entirely. Mini golf, the other country-club pastime, survived and is in the finished game — which makes the tennis decision feel deliberate instead of careless. Rockstar would rather have one good minigame than two where one is a disappointment.

Cinema trips weren't interactive enough

The cinema and theatre visits went for the opposite reason. GTA 5 let players sit down and watch short films in Los Santos, but the developers felt that kind of activity was not interactive enough to earn a place in GTA 6. Rockstar has steered this game toward things players actively do with Jason and Lucia instead — dates at the gym or a bar, missions built around the couple.

There is an irony here, because GTA 6 already has televisions playing cartoons and reality shows in Jason's house, and players can sit down and play entire games on the JoySystem. Rockstar drew a line between watching something on a couch at home, which fits the fantasy of two people living together, and buying a ticket to watch something in a dark room, which does not.

The home life that didn't fit

The home-life ideas are the most revealing cuts of all. At various points Jason and Lucia could crawl across the floor, furnish their safehouse, and craft items. The team also specced out a system where the couple restocked their own fridge with grocery runs, then pulled it to keep daily life simple. None of it fit the story Rockstar wanted to tell or the way the game actually plays, so all of it went.

The developers said they are proudest of the relationship between the two leads, and that Red Dead Redemption 2's slower pacing inspired GTA 6 to make room for quiet moments between the chaos. The cuts were not about removing life from the game. They were about protecting the parts of that life that matter to the story.

What wasn't cut — and what to ignore

One thing to be careful about: several posts on social media added darts to the list of cut activities overnight. The PC Games interview does not mention darts at all, and the claim could not be traced to anything a Rockstar developer has said on the record. Treat it as unconfirmed until Rockstar addresses it directly.

It is the same instinct behind the no-mission-replay call Rockstar explained the same week: if a feature clashed with the story the game is telling, it went, even if players expected it. Cut features do not always stay cut, but Rockstar has made no promises about any of these returning — not in single-player updates, and not in GTA 6's online mode, which still has no official release date.

Until Rockstar says more, the PC Games interview is the clearest official account of what was left out — and the clearest window yet into what Rockstar thinks this game is supposed to be.
