Harry Kane tore ankle ligaments 20 minutes into Bayern's draw with Cologne — and he's out for months

By Matt

Harry Kane tore the syndesmosis ligament in his left ankle 20 minutes into Bayern Munich's 1-1 draw with Cologne. Surgery was a success, but Bayern say the Ballon d'Or winner is out for the next few months.

The diagnosis

Harry Kane is out for the next few months. Bayern Munich confirmed on Friday that the England captain tore the syndesmosis ligament in his left ankle — a high ankle sprain — in the 1-1 Bundesliga draw with Cologne, and he underwent surgery overnight.

"Harry Kane suffered a tear of the syndesmosis ligament in his left ankle in Bayern Munich's Bundesliga home match against Köln on Friday evening," Bayern said in a statement. "This was confirmed by an examination by Bayern Munich's medical department. The 33-year-old will therefore be out for the next few months."

Bayern sporting director Max Eberl put a number on it shortly after the match, telling ARD's Sportschau programme that Kane would be out for three months, likely until February. The operation itself was a success, Bayern said.

How it happened

The injury came 20 minutes into the match at the Allianz Arena, and there was nothing cynical about it. Michael Olise sent a low cross toward the back post, and Cologne defender Joel Schmied got to the ball first — then clattered into Kane's left leg in his follow-through. No foul was called.

Kane went down clutching his lower leg and writhed in pain. He got gingerly back to his feet and tried to carry on, but he was limping, and after treatment he signalled to the dugout and hobbled off alongside two physios. Tom Bischof came on in his place.

It was a brutal night for Bayern's attack all round. Olise was also forced off, at half-time, after a challenge from Tom Krauß caught him in the left leg. He was later seen on crutches, though Bayern said he has only a bruised forefoot and Eberl was hopeful it is nothing serious. "We believe and hope it's nothing serious," Eberl said.

On the pitch, Bayern fell behind before Nathaniel Brown's 37th-minute strike levelled it at 1-1, and that was how it stayed. Bayern remain top of the Bundesliga on 22 points, with Cologne on 10.

What he said

Kane broke his silence on social media after the surgery, and the message was pure Kane — disappointed, but already looking at the calendar.

"The highs and lows of life! Obviously very disappointed to get this injury but as always it's an opportunity to show resilience and improvement going into 2027.

"I'll be back fresh and ready to go for the business end of the season!

"'Tough times don't last, tough people do' — my guy @edelman11 told me this one!

"Thank you for all the messages! Alles gut."

What it means for Bayern

That layoff would rule Kane out of 11 Bundesliga games, the DFB-Pokal round of 16 tie against Hertha Berlin, and all four of Bayern's remaining Champions League league-phase matches. Bayern's next match is against Lille in the Champions League on Wednesday, November 25 — without their No. 9.

The timing could hardly be worse for a player who has been the centre of everything Bayern do since arriving from Tottenham in 2023. He has scored 156 goals for the club, won two Bundesliga titles and the German Cup, and last month became the first Englishman to win the Ballon d'Or in 25 years. Two weeks ago he signed a new contract keeping him in Bavaria until June 2029, reportedly worth more than £400,000 a week — the highest-paid player in Europe before bonuses.

He had gone into the international break without a goal in his previous four matches for Bayern, but he scored twice for England in the 3-2 Nations League win over Spain — the same week he earned his record 126th cap to become England's most-capped player.

What it means for England

The injury reaches beyond Munich. England face Norway in a two-legged Nations League quarter-final in March — in Oslo on March 27, then at Wembley on March 30 — and Kane could be facing a race to be fully fit for it.

As Henry Winter put it on Sky Sports' Back Pages Tonight: "It's three months and, most immediately, it's Bayern Munich's concern. But England have the home and away Nations League quarter-finals against Norway in late March. The timeline would suggest he'll be back in that time, but thoughts with Thomas Tuchel tonight because he'll be absolutely concerned about this. He's such an important player. England's No 9."

Kane's month had been a rollercoaster even before Friday: the Ballon d'Or on October 26, the new contract on November 5, a record-extending 92nd England goal against Croatia on November 12, the record 126th cap against Spain on November 15. Now the last line of the month reads very differently — injured after 20 minutes against Cologne, and out for months.

The hope, for Bayern and for England, is the one Kane wrote himself: back fresh for the business end of the season.
