Zacha's pair powers Bruins' third straight win

By Matt

Pavel Zacha scored twice and five different Bruins found the net as Boston rolled past Pittsburgh 6-2 at PPG Paints Arena for its third straight win.

Pavel Zacha scored twice as the Boston Bruins beat the Pittsburgh Penguins 6-2 on Thursday, November 5, 2026, at PPG Paints Arena. Five different Bruins scored in the win, Boston's third in a row.

The Bruins answered Pittsburgh's early opener with three goals in the final eight minutes of the first period and never trailed again, improving to 12-4-1 while the Penguins fell to 5-9-2 with their fourth straight loss.

How the game played out

Pittsburgh struck first on its first shot of the game. Former Bruin Justin Brazeau dropped to one knee in the right face-off circle and snapped one past Michael DiPietro at 9:04 of the first period, set up by Hendrix Lapierre from below the goal line.

Boston responded with three goals in 6:50. Zacha tied it 1-1 at 12:07, finishing a cross-crease feed from David Pastrnak after JJ Peterka knocked down a bouncing puck and worked it to Pastrnak. Mark Kastelic put the Bruins in front 2-1 at 15:04, deflecting a point shot from Frederic Brunet in the slot. Elias Lindholm made it 3-1 at 18:57, stealing the puck from Erik Karlsson in Penguins territory and scoring unassisted — the eventual game-winner, his sixth goal of the season and his third game-winner.

Nick Robertson cut it to 3-2 at 7:57 of the second, redirecting a Kaedan Korczak shot for his team-leading seventh goal. Zacha answered again at 14:24, burying a wrist shot on a 2-on-1 off a feed from Peterka to restore the two-goal lead. Morgan Geekie pushed it to 5-2 at 16:57, taking a pass from Hampus Lindholm during a Pittsburgh line change and going in alone on a breakaway. Fraser Minten closed it out at 8:39 of the third, snapping one home on a 2-on-1 off a feed from Geekie for the 6-2 final.

Zacha and the top line

Both of Zacha's goals came off the same formula: the Peterka–Zacha–Pastrnak line moving the puck fast through the neutral zone and finishing on the rush. Pastrnak and Peterka each finished with two assists, and Zacha — the night's first star — finished +2 in 18:37 of ice time with five goals on the season.

The depth showed up behind them, too. Geekie scored for the third straight game and added an assist, Hampus Lindholm was +4 in 20:13 with an assist, and DiPietro made 16 saves for his fourth straight win. It was the fifth time this season Boston has put six goals on the board.

The one sour note: rookie James Hagens left early in the third period after taking a crosscheck to the back from Penguins defenseman Samuel Girard.

Why it matters

Boston has now won three straight and five of seven, sitting at 12-4-1 and on top of the Atlantic Division. The encouraging part of this one is that the Bruins didn't need Pastrnak to carry the scoring — he set up two goals without finding the net himself, and the goals came from five different sticks.

Pittsburgh, meanwhile, is sliding the other way. The Penguins have lost four straight (0-3-1), and Arturs Silovs allowed six goals on 24 shots. Boston returns to TD Garden to host the Florida Panthers on Sunday, November 8, at 1 p.m. ET.
