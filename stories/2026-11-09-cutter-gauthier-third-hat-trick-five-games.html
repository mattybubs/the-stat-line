Cutter Gauthier's third hat trick in five games — a run nobody has had since 1993

By Matt

Cutter Gauthier scored his third hat trick in five games as the Ducks beat the Penguins 5-2 on Sunday — the first player in NHL history with three hat tricks in the first 10 days of a calendar month. He has 13 goals in a six-game goal streak, and Anaheim has scored five or more in eight straight games.

What happened

Cutter Gauthier scored his third hat trick in five games and added an assist as the Anaheim Ducks beat the Pittsburgh Penguins 5-2 on Sunday night at Honda Center. The Ducks have won five in a row, and they did it wearing their new green-and-purple District 5 Hometown Remix sweaters, with hats raining onto the ice by the end of the night.

Gauthier has 13 goals during a six-game goal streak and 15 goals on the season — third in the league. He scored just two goals in his first 11 games. Then the switch flipped.

The history

This is not a normal hot streak. Gauthier is the first player in NHL history to score three hat tricks within the first 10 days of a calendar month. The previous record for the fewest days to three hat tricks in a month was 13, set by Phil Esposito in December 1970. He also matched the NHL record for most hat tricks in a calendar month (three) — only Patrik Laine (November 2018) and Alex Kovalev (February 2001) have done it in the past 30 years. And he is the first NHL player with three hat tricks in five games since 1993.

The franchise marks are falling just as fast. Gauthier joined Corey Perry (2010-11 and 2014-15), Frank Vatrano (2023-24), and Teemu Selanne (1997-98) as the only Ducks ever to record three hat tricks in a single season — tying the franchise record before mid-November. He is the fastest Duck ever to five multi-goal games, doing it in 17 games and beating Selanne's 20 from his franchise-record 52-goal campaign in 1997-98. His six-game goal streak is tied with Paul Kariya and Rickard Rakell for the fourth-longest in team history; only Perry (seven) and Selanne (11) have had longer ones.

"I'm just shooting the puck and they're going in, so I'm just going to keep shooting it," Gauthier said. Asked if he has ever felt the puck on his stick like this, he was honest: "Probably not, no. Obviously on a high right now, so pretty speechless."

The team around him

The Ducks are not just a one-man show — they are the first team since the 1988-89 Los Angeles Kings to score at least five goals in eight consecutive games, and only the eighth team in NHL history to do it. Their power play has scored in eight straight games, and it is 17-for-34 (50 percent) over the last six.

Captain Mikael Granlund had a goal and three assists, and he assisted on both of Gauthier's second-period goals. "He's so natural," Granlund said. "You see those shots going five-hole, like that's a goal-scorer's goal. It's impressive and it's fun to watch." Jackson LaCombe had three assists and stretched his assist streak to 10 games, tying Ryan Getzlaf and Perry (both 2009-10) for the longest in team history — and making LaCombe just the fourth American defenseman ever with a 10-game assist streak, after Brian Leetch, Phil Housley, and Quinn Hughes. Tristan Luneau scored on the power play and extended his point streak to eight games. Laurent Brossoit made 32 saves and is 4-0-0 since Anaheim recalled him from AHL San Diego on October 31.

Coach Joel Quenneville, on Gauthier: "He's got magic touch. He scores big goals, big time. I think he's got some deceptiveness with his release and his quickness. He's hotter than a firecracker."

How the game played out

Granlund opened the scoring on a power play at 8:26 of the first, firing home a Gauthier rebound just as the man advantage expired. Gauthier made it 2-0 at 6:28 of the second with a no-look shot between Arturs Silovs' pads from the right face-off circle, then added a wrist shot from the slot at 9:11 for 3-0. Luneau's power-play goal made it 4-0 early in the third. Pittsburgh, which had pulled its goalie for extra attackers, got two 6-on-4 power-play goals from Kris Letang and Rickard Rakell to make it 4-2 — and then Gauthier sealed the hat trick with an empty-netter at 19:26. Hats on the ice, fifth straight win, and a 22-year-old who has already tied a franchise record that took Selanne a full season to set.

The Penguins (6-10-2) have lost five of their last six and eight of 11. Evgeni Malkin had two assists, and Silovs made 20 saves.

What it means

Eight games. Five or more goals in every one. A 22-year-old winger who could not buy a goal in October and now cannot stop scoring in November. The Ducks (10-7-1) are not sneaking up on anyone anymore — they are the loudest team in the league, and Gauthier is the loudest player in it. The question is not whether the streak ends. It is how many records he takes with him before it does.
