Kyle Palmieri tore his other ACL — 364 days after the first one ended his season

By Matt

The Islanders announced Friday that Kyle Palmieri is out six to eight months with a torn ACL and meniscus in his right knee — the other knee from the one that ended his season last November.

The other knee

Kyle Palmieri is out for the season. Again. On the other knee.

The New York Islanders announced on Friday, November 27, that Palmieri tore the ACL and meniscus in his right knee, and he will be sidelined six to eight months. Head coach Pete DeBoer put it plainly: Palmieri is out for the regular season, and the team will see about after that.

The injury came in the second period of Monday's 4-2 loss to the Toronto Maple Leafs. Palmieri got tangled with Toronto forward Easton Cowan, and his right leg caught on the goalpost. He did not return to the game, and the Islanders placed him on injured reserve the next day, listing it only as a lower-body injury.

Friday, the diagnosis came out — and it was the worst version of it.

364 days later

This is the second time in barely a year that Palmieri has torn an ACL. He tore the one in his left knee on November 28, 2025, against the Philadelphia Flyers, after colliding with defenseman Jamie Drysdale — and still recorded an assist while limping his way back to the bench before leaving the game.

That injury ended his season after 25 games. He spent the months after it grinding through rehab to get back for this season, and he did — returning to the Islanders' top line, where he had been playing alongside Bo Horvat.

DeBoer, who watched Palmieri's comeback from the other bench last season before taking over behind it, called the news heartbreaking.

"It's emotional," DeBoer said. "People don't see the work that he put in to come back from what he went through last year, in order to get to the point he got to this year. And then to have it happen like that to the other leg on such a fluky kind of thing, it's heartbreaking, emotional, all those words."

What the Islanders lose

Palmieri had nine points — four goals and five assists — in 23 games this season, a consistent presence on the top line. He is 35 years old, an alternate captain, and a veteran of 948 career games over 17 NHL seasons. He is also in the final year of his contract with the Islanders, a two-year deal worth $4.75 million per year, set to become an unrestricted free agent after this season.

The Islanders acquired him from the New Jersey Devils at the 2021 trade deadline, and he scored seven goals during that spring's run to the Stanley Cup Semifinals. Mathew Barzal, who played on a line with him during that run, called him a warrior — the kind of player who brings it every night, goes to the gritty areas, and scores around the crease.

"He brings an element that's hard to find," Barzal said. "He goes to gritty areas. He forechecks hard and he's got skill along with that and good goal scoring."

Young defenseman Matthew Schaefer said the whole room looks up to Palmieri, and that the veteran was always smiling and checking in on the team even while he was rehabbing last year's injury around the facility.

Now the Islanders will be the ones checking in on him.

"To have it happen again second in a row, it's not fair and it's not fun," Schaefer said. "I know Palms knows we're all with him, and he's gonna do the same thing again. He's gonna keep grinding, and then he's gonna keep coming back."

Two ACL tears, two knees, two seasons in a row. The first one took his season. The second one just did the same.
