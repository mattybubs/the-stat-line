27 Unanswered: Commanders Stun Eagles 27-10 as Philadelphia's Freefall Hits Six Straight

By Matt

Down 10-0 in the first quarter, Washington scored 27 unanswered points and shut Philadelphia out for the final three quarters, handing the Eagles a sixth straight loss and Jayden Daniels his first win in over a year.

For one quarter on Sunday Night Football, it looked like the Philadelphia Eagles might finally stop the bleeding. They led 10-0 in the first quarter at Northwest Stadium. Then Washington scored 27 unanswered points, shut the Eagles out for the final three quarters, and walked off with a 27-10 win that says everything about where both of these teams are.

Jayden Daniels threw for 217 yards and a touchdown, connecting with Stefon Diggs for an 8-yard score in the second quarter that tied the game at 10, and Washington led 13-10 at halftime. Rachaad White added a rushing touchdown, rookie kicker Drew Stevens knocked through four field goals, and the Commanders (2-4-1) picked up their first win in more than a month — and moved ahead of Philadelphia in the NFC East standings.

Daniels' first win in over a year

The personal milestone mattered as much as the standings one. Daniels won as a starter for the first time since beating the Los Angeles Chargers on October 5, 2025. He took a hard hit late in the first half and was slow to get up, but he stayed in the game and finished the job — the kind of night that can steady a season that has felt anything but steady.

Washington's defense did the heavy lifting. The Commanders sacked Tanner McKee five times, intercepted him once, and held Philadelphia to 268 total yards — just 199 of them before a meaningless final drive. After Saquon Barkley's 3-yard touchdown run put the Eagles up 10-0, Philadelphia never scored again.

The Eagles' spiral, by the numbers

McKee completed 23 of 34 passes for 212 yards with an interception, but five sacks and two turnovers told the story of the night. Barkley finished with just 30 rushing yards on 13 carries. Philadelphia converted five of 15 third downs.

The loss drops the Eagles to 2-6 — their worst start through eight games since 1999 — and extends their losing streak to six games, their longest since 2012. A team that opened the season expecting to contend now heads home to face the division-leading New York Giants in Week 9, with the trade deadline conversation already turning toward whether Philadelphia should be sellers.

Washington, meanwhile, is still two games behind the first-place Giants despite opening the season with just one win in its first six games. In a weak NFC East, that is close enough to matter. The Commanders have not played a complete game all season, by their own coach's admission — but on Sunday night, three quarters of one was more than enough.
