Matthew Stafford Now Owns the Passing-TD Record for Two Franchises

By Matt

An 11-yard touchdown to Puka Nacua in the first half at Las Vegas gave Stafford his 155th touchdown pass as a Ram, passing Roman Gabriel. He already owns Detroit's record with 282.

Matthew Stafford now sits atop the passing-touchdown record book for two different franchises.

The record-breaker came with 4:15 left in the first half at Las Vegas: an 11-yard touchdown pass to Puka Nacua, Stafford's third touchdown pass of the first half against the Raiders. It was his 155th touchdown pass in a Rams uniform, moving him past Roman Gabriel's 154 — a record that had stood since Gabriel's Rams career ended in 1972.

How the first half went

Stafford entered today at 152 Rams touchdown passes, two short of Gabriel. He tied the record on a 5-yard touchdown to Davis Allen early in the second quarter, then broke it with the Nacua strike minutes later. His first touchdown of the game was a 3-yarder to Allen in the first quarter. At the time of the record, Stafford was 15-of-17 for 126 yards with 3 touchdowns, no interceptions and a 137.1 rating, with the Rams up 21-3.

The other record he already owns

Stafford threw 282 touchdown passes for Detroit from 2009 to 2020 — the Lions' franchise record, along with 45,109 passing yards. Jared Goff, the quarterback Detroit got back in the 2021 trade, is second in Lions history and still more than 120 touchdowns behind.

Two weeks ago, Stafford became the first quarterback in NFL history to throw 150 or more touchdown passes for two different teams, reaching 150 as a Ram against Buffalo on October 12. Today he went one better: he doesn't just have 150 for two teams — he leads both franchises outright.

The only one — ever

Stafford is the only quarterback in NFL history to hold the passing-touchdown record for two franchises. Every other great who starred for two teams falls short on one of them:

• Peyton Manning leads Indianapolis (399) but trails John Elway in Denver (300 to Manning's 140)

• Tom Brady leads New England (541) but trails Jameis Winston in Tampa Bay (121 to Brady's 108)

• Drew Brees leads New Orleans (491) but trails Philip Rivers with the Chargers (397 to Brees' 80)

• Brett Favre doesn't even lead Green Bay anymore — Aaron Rodgers' 475 passed his 442 — and he trails Fran Tarkenton in Minnesota and Joe Namath with the Jets

• Aaron Rodgers leads Green Bay (475) but trails Namath with the Jets (170 to Rodgers' 28)

Stafford: 282 in Detroit, 155 in Los Angeles. First in both places.

Where he stands all time

Today's three first-half touchdowns pushed Stafford to 437 career passing touchdowns, sixth in NFL history. The five ahead of him: Tom Brady (649), Drew Brees (571), Peyton Manning (539), Aaron Rodgers (528) and Brett Favre (508). At 38, in his 18th season, the gap to Favre is 71 — a question for another season, not this one.

Gabriel reached 154 touchdowns in 130 games as a Ram. Stafford passed him in his 81st.
