Brandon Aiyuk has a new team — the Commanders sign him to the practice squad, two days after the 49ers cut him

By Matt

Two days after San Francisco released him, Brandon Aiyuk agreed to join the Washington Commanders' practice squad — reuniting with Jayden Daniels and giving a receiver-starved team a nearly free flier on a 28-year-old who was an All-Pro two seasons ago.

The move

Brandon Aiyuk has a new team. The free-agent wide receiver is signing with the Washington Commanders' practice squad, his agent Drew Rosenhaus told ESPN's Adam Schefter on Wednesday — hours after Aiyuk visited the team in Ashburn and two days after the 49ers released him.

It is a one-year deal for the rest of the regular season, and it came together fast. Rosenhaus had said earlier Wednesday that a decision was unlikely to happen today. By the afternoon, the two sides had an agreement.

One wrinkle: the signing is not expected to be officially completed today. NFL Network's Ian Rapoport reported it should be official next week — but the agreement is done, and Aiyuk is a Commander.

The reunion

The landing spot was never much of a mystery. Aiyuk reached out to Jayden Daniels about a month ago — the two were teammates at Arizona State in 2019 — and the two have reconciled after a summer rift. Daniels is now his quarterback again.

Washington's general manager Adam Peters knows him, too. Peters spent seven seasons in the 49ers' front office and was the team's vice president of player personnel when San Francisco drafted Aiyuk in the first round in 2020.

And the Commanders need receivers badly. Terry McLaurin is out with a torn patellar tendon suffered in Week 6, and Dyami Brown and Jaylin Lane are on injured reserve. Stefon Diggs is on a one-year deal, and rookie fourth-rounder Antonio Williams has flashed — nine catches for 122 yards against Cincinnati on Monday Night Football — but Washington has been working out receivers for weeks. Aiyuk is the biggest name it has brought in.

The player

Nobody needs a reminder of what Aiyuk was two seasons ago. In 2023 he posted a career-high 1,342 receiving yards and was named a second-team All-Pro during the 49ers' run to the Super Bowl. Across six seasons in San Francisco he caught 294 passes for 4,305 yards and 25 touchdowns in 69 games, averaging 14.6 yards per reception.

Then came the knee injury — a torn ACL and MCL in his right knee seven games into 2024 — and he has not played since October of that year. The recovery never became a comeback in San Francisco. He stopped attending team-directed rehab and meetings, communication with the team broke down, he was placed on the reserve/left squad list last December, and after hiring Rosenhaus and winning reinstatement on November 9, the 49ers released him Monday when their two-week roster exemption expired.

Rosenhaus says Aiyuk is 100 percent healthy, with clean MRIs and X-rays. The Commanders are about to find out.

The cost

The flier costs Washington almost nothing. The Athletic's Nicki Jhabvala noted that if the Commanders signed Aiyuk to the practice squad at the maximum rate for a player of his experience and elevated him for three games, it would owe him roughly $305,000 for the rest of the regular season.

Practice-squad rules mean he can be elevated for Sunday's game against the Cardinals if Washington wants him that fast. If he plays anything like the receiver he was in 2023, the Commanders get a steal for the price of a rounding error. If he does not, they can move on without losing much.

Two days after the ugliest exit in the league this season, Aiyuk has a locker again — and a quarterback he already trusts throwing him the ball.
