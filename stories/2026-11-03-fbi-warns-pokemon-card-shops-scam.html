FBI Warns Pokemon Card Shops About a New No-Payment Scam Hitting Stores

By Matt

The FBI's Newark office warned November 2 that scammers are targeting local card shops and Pokemon vendors with a scheme that lets them walk out with high-value cards without paying — contactless payments that never clear, and a phone app loaded with stolen credit cards.

The FBI is now, officially, a player in the Pokemon card hobby — and not in a fun way. The bureau's Newark, New Jersey, office warned on November 2 that a new scam is targeting local card shops and Pokemon TCG vendors, one designed to let bad actors buy high-value cards without spending any money at all.

How the scam works

According to the FBI notice, reported November 2, seven shops have been targeted so far. The scheme has two faces. First, thieves try to pay for cards via a contactless mobile payment that never actually clears — the transaction looks plausible at the counter and falls apart after the cards are already gone. Second, and more brazen, a cell phone app "operated by a criminal network that stores multiple stolen credit cards" is used to present payment that isn't theirs.

In one example cited in the warning, a man attempted to sell a box of trading cards to a store while an accomplice reached over the counter and stole additional cards — the old distraction play, dressed up for a hobby where a single card can be worth thousands.

Why now, and why Pokemon

The warning lands weeks after the Department of Justice announced an investigation into the biggest Pokemon card heist ever recorded, and it fits a pattern the hobby has felt all year: as card values have climbed, robberies and fraud targeting shops have climbed with them. A shop's singles wall is, functionally, a jewelry case that the public is invited to browse.

For collectors, the practical takeaway is boring and useful: buy from shops you know, be suspicious of deals that route around normal payment, and if you're selling, don't let a "payment" walk out the door with the card before it clears. For shops, the FBI's message is the same one the hobby keeps learning the expensive way — the cards are worth real money now, and the people targeting them know it.

This is also the week the hobby's calendar turns: Pokemon's November slate — the Delta Reign expansion and the 30th Celebration Day & Night Ultra-Premium Collections — lands November 6, which means foot traffic and high-value inventory in shops are both about to spike. That's exactly the moment a warning like this matters most.
