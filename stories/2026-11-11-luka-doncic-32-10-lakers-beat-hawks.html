Luka Doncic returns with 32, 8 and 10 as Lakers hold off Hawks for sixth straight win

By Matt

Luka Doncic came back from a one-game absence with 32 points, 8 rebounds and 10 assists as the Lakers beat the Hawks 124-114 in Atlanta — their sixth straight win, even with Austin Reaves sidelined.

The night

Luka Doncic missed one game. The Lakers did not miss a beat.

Back in the lineup after sitting out Sunday in Detroit with lower-back tightness, Doncic put up 32 points, 8 rebounds, 10 assists, 5 steals and 2 blocks in 39 minutes as Los Angeles beat the Atlanta Hawks 124-114 at State Farm Arena on Monday night. The win is the Lakers' sixth in a row, and it pushes them to 10-2 — the best record in the league.

He did it the way he has been doing it all season: everywhere. Doncic shot 11-of-22 from the field and 7-of-7 from the line, had 8 assists before halftime, and scored 18 of his points after the break as Atlanta kept charging. The Hawks cut a double-digit lead to 115-111 with about three and a half minutes left, and the Lakers still closed it out — their bench doing most of the closing.

That bench was the story of the night. With Austin Reaves out with an illness one night after his season-high 43 in Detroit, the Lakers' reserves scored a season-high 49 points. Sandro Mamukelashvili led the way with 23 points, 8 rebounds and 3 assists in his best game as a Laker. A night after the bench put up 45 in Detroit without Doncic, it put up 49 without Reaves. Coach JJ Redick praised the depth afterward, and he had every reason to.

The streak ends

The one thing Doncic did not do: score 40. His run of four straight 40-point games ended in Atlanta, eight points short of a fifth — the number that would have put him alongside Wilt Chamberlain, Kobe Bryant, Michael Jordan, Allen Iverson, Elgin Baylor and James Harden as the only players in NBA history with five consecutive 40-point games.

He will live with it. The Lakers are 10-2, he still leads the league in scoring at just over 39 points a game, and he was named Western Conference Player of the Week on Monday after averaging 45.0 points, 7.0 rebounds and 8.3 assists across a 3-0 week. The scoring streak was fun. The winning streak matters more, and it is still alive.

Atlanta, meanwhile, saw its own four-game winning streak end. The Hawks had a real chance late and could not finish, and now they watch the Lakers head to Milwaukee on Wednesday with Reaves expected back and Quentin Grimes — out Monday with a sprained right finger — listed as questionable.

The draft-night troll

There was one more moment worth keeping. Doncic was drafted third overall in 2018 by Atlanta, then traded on draft night to Dallas for Trae Young and a protected first-round pick. On Monday, with the game in hand, he had words with a Hawks fan in the crowd — the clip made the rounds within the hour — needling the home fans about the team that drafted him and sent him away.

Eight years later, the trade is still one of the most second-guessed deals in modern NBA history, and Doncic is still happy to remind Atlanta of it in person.
