Canadiens Moneyline (-115): The East's hottest team against a slumping rival — even with Tavares back

By Matt

Montreal is 10-3-3, has won six of seven and four straight over Toronto, and starts a goalie at 7-1-1 with a .925 save percentage. The Leafs get Tavares back, but -115 is still the value side.

Game slip

Montreal Canadiens (10-3-3) at Toronto Maple Leafs (8-7-2)

Saturday, November 7, 2026 — 7:00 PM ET — Scotiabank Arena, Toronto, ON — ESPN+, SN, TVAS

Total: 6.5

Moneyline: Canadiens -115 / Maple Leafs +105

Odds source: ESPN via DraftKings, Nov. 7

### Injury report

Montreal: RW Ivan Demidov (ankle) — injured reserve. D Mike Matheson (lower body) — out. G Samuel Montembeault (lower body) — injured reserve, day-to-day, could return after a month out. D Arber Xhekaj (undisclosed) — questionable. Expected starter Jacob Fowler is 7-1-1 with a .925 save percentage and a 2.17 goals-against average.

Toronto: C John Tavares (lower body) — expected to return after missing nine games since October 15. He practiced Friday in a regular jersey, skated on a line with Nick Paul and William Nylander, and rejoined the top power-play unit. RW Kirill Marchenko (lower body) — injured reserve. C Max Domi (back) — injured reserve. F Miles Wood — injured reserve. D Nick Blankenburg — injured reserve. G Elvis Merzlikins (shoulder) — injured reserve.

### Storylines

• Montreal is the hottest team in the East right now: 10-3-3, winners of six of seven, coming off a 5-4 overtime win over Utah on Thursday in which Nick Suzuki returned from a six-game absence with three points and Cole Caufield scored the winner 30 seconds into OT.

• The Canadiens carry a four-game win streak over the Maple Leafs into Scotiabank Arena, and they just showed they can win the hard way — rallying from 2-0 and 4-3 down on Thursday, two nights after blowing a 4-0 lead to Winnipeg.

• Toronto is slumping: 8-7-2, losers of three of four, held below 30 shots in four straight games, and 4-4-1 at home. The middle six has buckled with Tavares and Marchenko out, and the Leafs are starting a stretch of five straight games against teams in a playoff position.

• The goalie matchup tilts Montreal's way. Fowler is 7-1-1 with a .925 save percentage; one starting-goalie service projects Sergei Bobrovsky for Toronto at .889 with a 3.27 goals-against average. That is a big gap in a game lined as a coin flip.

The bet

Montreal Canadiens moneyline (-115)

This is a bet on the better team at a coin-flip price. Montreal is better at even strength, better in goal, deeper down the lineup, and has owned this matchup lately — four straight wins over Toronto. The Leafs' best argument is Tavares returning and three days of rest, and both are real. But Tavares is one player coming back from a three-week absence, likely on the wing for the first time, and rest doesn't fix a team that has been outshot and outworked for a week. At -115, you're getting the 10-3-3 team at the price of a toss-up. Take it.

Why

The market is pricing the name on the front of the jersey, not the team on the ice. Toronto at home, on Hockey Night in Canada, against Montreal — that matchup always draws Leafs money, and it has kept this line near even despite everything in the form guide pointing the other way. Montreal has the better record by a distance, the better goalie by a distance, the head-to-head streak, and the momentum of a team that just stared down a brutal loss and answered with a comeback win. The Habs' blue line is banged up, but Fowler has covered for it all season.

The honest caveat

Tavares matters. He had seven points in seven games before the injury, he is a point-per-game player for his career, and his return immediately fixes Toronto's top power play and its faceoff circle. The Leafs have also had three days off since their 3-2 loss to Utah on November 3, while Montreal played Thursday and had one day of rest. And Montreal is missing Demidov, Matheson, and possibly Montembeault — if Fowler has an off night, there is no safety net. A rested Leafs team with its captain back, at home, on a Saturday night, is a live underdog. The price is fair, not a steal.

The pick

Canadiens moneyline (-115). The better team, the better goalie, the better form — at a coin-flip price.
