Rangers lose Bjorkstrand and Smits for months as the injury pileup reaches a breaking point

By Matt

The Rangers announced Saturday that Oliver Bjorkstrand and Alberts Smits will each miss two to three months with upper-body injuries, the latest blows to a roster already without Igor Shesterkin for the season.

Two more gone for months

The New York Rangers announced Saturday that forward Oliver Bjorkstrand and defenseman Alberts Smits will each miss the next two to three months with upper-body injuries.

It is the latest gut punch in a season that has tested the Rangers' depth at every position. The team has outperformed expectations so far, but the list of unavailable players keeps growing — and Sunday brings a matinee against the defending Stanley Cup champions.

How Bjorkstrand got here

Bjorkstrand's injury did not announce itself all at once. The 31-year-old winger was hurt midway through the third period of a 3-1 win over Montreal on November 16, then played the next game on Monday and practiced on Thursday. He did not take part in Friday's session, and head coach Mike Sullivan said Saturday that Bjorkstrand complaining about the injury prompted "further investigation."

That investigation ended with injured reserve on Friday and a months-long timeline on Saturday. Taylor Raddysh was recalled to take the roster spot.

The loss stings because Bjorkstrand has been one of the Rangers' most productive forwards. He is tied for second on the team in goals, and he was a steady presence in a top six that has had to carry the load while the lineup around it keeps changing.

The rookie who earned his spot

Smits' timeline is the quieter heartbreak of the two. The 18-year-old defenseman was the No. 5 overall pick and played his way onto the roster out of training camp, but he has not skated in a game since a win over Philadelphia on November 5. Until Saturday, there was no clear timeline for his return. Now there is one — and it is measured in months, not weeks.

Losing a rookie who forced his way onto the team is one thing. Losing him for the bulk of the season, when the blue line is already stretched thin, is another.

The list that won't stop growing

Bjorkstrand and Smits join a casualty list that already reads like a roster page. Joe Veleno went down with a lower-body injury four games into the season. Igor Shesterkin suffered a season-ending knee injury in the sixth game. Goaltender Joonas Korpisalo missed 16 games with a foot infection. Adam Sykora's preseason injury carries a timeline of four to five months.

Pavel Dorofeyev landed on injured reserve Saturday as well, with a lower-body injury retroactive to November 13. He could technically return as early as Sunday, but he did not practice Saturday and continues to skate on his own, and Sullivan would not say whether the stint will be short.

There is at least one player coming back: defenseman Sean Durzi, who has missed the past eight games with an undisclosed injury, is expected to return against Carolina.

Sunday is the test

The Rangers entered a five-day break on a three-game winning streak, with wins in six of their last seven games. Sunday's matinee against the Hurricanes — the reigning Stanley Cup champions and the Metropolitan Division leaders — is, in defenseman Braden Schneider's words, a game against "the team to beat."

"We've just got to have more guys step up," J.T. Miller said after practice in Tarrytown. "It's an opportunity for everybody. It's gonna be a chance to have a bigger role for maybe some guys."

Sullivan called it a next-man-up mindset, and the front office has already acted on it, acquiring center Max Sasson from the Canucks on Friday in exchange for a fourth-round pick in 2029 to boost the center depth.

The Rangers have survived every absence so far. Sunday will show whether they can survive two more.
