TSL Bets: Bruins moneyline (-128) vs Canadiens — 8-1 at home against a banged-up rival

By Matt

Boston is 13-4-1, 8-1-0 at home, and allowing 2.3 goals a game. Montreal hasn't lost in regulation in a month — but the Habs are without Demidov and their goalie picture is unsettled. Take the Bruins at home.

The game

Montreal Canadiens (11-3-4) at Boston Bruins (13-4-1) — 7:00 PM ET, TD Garden, Boston, ESPN+

Bruins moneyline -128, Canadiens +106, total 6.5 (FanDuel).

Injuries — Boston: James Hagens is on injured reserve, est. return November 21. That is the whole list — the Bruins are otherwise healthy.

Injuries — Montreal: Ivan Demidov is on injured reserve with an ankle injury, est. return November 14 — he has played two games all season. Mike Matheson is day-to-day with a lower-body injury. Samuel Montembeault is on injured reserve, with a return imminent. Nick Suzuki and Jake Evans both missed Wednesday's practice for treatment, with no diagnosis and no timeline given. Jacob Fowler has taken the number one job in net: 8-1-2, 2.14 goals-against average, .925 save percentage.

Storylines: This is first against second in the Atlantic — Boston with 27 points, Montreal with 26 — and both teams are playing well. The Bruins have won eight of nine home games and are giving up only 2.3 goals a game, with eight forwards at 10-plus points and 5-plus goals. The Canadiens have points in 11 straight games (8-0-3), have not lost in regulation since October 14, and are 6-1-1 on the road. Lane Hutson leads them with 18 points, Cole Caufield has 11 goals, and Nick Suzuki has 16 points. Boston has won seven of the last ten meetings, and five of the last six in Boston. The models split the difference — one has Boston at 65% to win, the market has it closer to a coin flip, and the -128 sits in between.

The bet

Bruins moneyline (-128). Home ice, the healthier roster, the better defense, and a head-to-head edge in this building. Montreal's point streak is real, but it is built on overtime and shootout points as much as wins — and the Habs are doing it without their best young forward, with a day-to-day top defenseman and a goalie situation that is still sorting itself out. Boston at -128 is a fair price for the better team at home.

The honest caveat

Montreal has not lost in regulation in a month, and Fowler is not a backup playing above his head — he is 8-1-2 with a .925 save percentage and has taken the job outright. The Canadiens are 6-1-1 on the road, they score 3.5 goals a game, and this is a rivalry game where the records barely matter. If Suzuki and Evans are fine after their treatment day, the Habs are close to full strength at forward. The -128 is a fair price, not a steal — this is a bet on home ice and health, and both of those edges are real but thin.

The pick

Bruins moneyline (-128). A $10 ticket returns about $17.81. The healthier team, at home, with the better defense.
