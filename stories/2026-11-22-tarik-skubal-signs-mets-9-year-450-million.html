Tarik Skubal signs with the Mets for nine years, $450 million — the richest pitcher deal in baseball history

By Matt

The Mets landed the winter's top free agent on Sunday, agreeing to a nine-year, $450 million deal with two-time Cy Young winner Tarik Skubal — the richest contract ever given to a pitcher.

The move

The biggest free agent of the winter is off the board.

Tarik Skubal agreed to a nine-year, $450 million contract with the New York Mets on Sunday, sources told ESPN's Jeff Passan, who first reported the terms. Jon Heyman of the New York Post was first to report the signing itself. The deal is pending Skubal passing a physical.

The Mets beat out the Dodgers, Padres and Blue Jays to land the Scott Boras client, according to the Post — the same three clubs that had been in the mix all month.

The number

Four hundred fifty million dollars is the richest contract ever given to a pitcher, surpassing Yoshinobu Yamamoto's 12-year, $325 million deal with the Dodgers. The average annual value works out to $50 million a year.

That is a jump from where the bidding started. The Mets' initial offer, reported last week, was nine years and nearly $400 million. The sweepstakes pushed it $50 million higher before Skubal put pen to paper.

The pitcher

The Mets are paying for the best pitcher in baseball, and the resume backs it up. Skubal won the American League Cy Young Award in both 2024 and 2025 with the Detroit Tigers, and his 2024 season included the AL Pitching Triple Crown.

Detroit traded him to the Dodgers at the Aug. 2 deadline, and he started Game 1 of the NLCS for Los Angeles in October. He hit free agency as the clear top prize of the winter — a left-hander with two Cy Youngs, a mid-90s fastball, and no real holes in his game.

What it means

For the second time in three offseasons, Mets owner Steve Cohen went out and landed the top free agent on the market. The rotation now has its ace, and the Mets did it by outbidding the team Skubal just pitched for in the postseason.

The Dodgers, meanwhile, lose the sweepstakes for the pitcher who started their NLCS opener. The winter's biggest domino has fallen, and it fell in Queens.
