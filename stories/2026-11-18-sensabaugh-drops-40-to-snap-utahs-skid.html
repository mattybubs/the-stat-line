Sensabaugh drops 40 to snap Utah's skid

By Matt

Brice Sensabaugh came off the bench and scored 40 points — 25 in the fourth quarter — as the Utah Jazz beat the LA Clippers 132-107 on Tuesday night, snapping a 12-game losing streak and becoming the youngest player in NBA history with 40 points, 10 rebounds and eight threes in a game.

The night

The Utah Jazz had not won a game in nearly a month. Then Brice Sensabaugh came off the bench and took over the fourth quarter.

Sensabaugh scored a season-high 40 points — 25 of them in the final period — as Utah beat the Los Angeles Clippers 132-107 at Intuit Dome on Tuesday night. The win snapped a 12-game losing streak and gave the Jazz just their second victory of the season, improving them to 2-13. It was also their first road win of the season after an 0-6 start away from home, and their first win of any kind since a 120-119 decision over New Orleans on October 23.

The game was tight for three quarters. Utah led just 88-86 heading into the fourth, then blew it open. The Jazz outscored the Clippers 44-21 in the final 12 minutes, shooting 18-for-22 from the floor in the period. Sensabaugh sparked a game-changing 20-2 surge — including a 16-0 run midway through the quarter — with a pull-up jumper when Utah led 93-91, scoring 13 points in that stretch alone.

He finished 16-for-21 from the field and 8-for-11 from three, the eight makes a career high, adding 11 rebounds, five assists and a block. Keyonte George added 30 points and 11 assists, and Utah finished with a season-high 20 three-pointers on 38 attempts, shooting 58.3 percent from the floor as a team. Brook Lopez led the Clippers with 23 points and six rebounds in the loss, dropping Los Angeles to 4-9.

The history

The line put Sensabaugh in the record book. At 23 years and 18 days old, he became the youngest player in NBA history to total 40 or more points, 10 or more rebounds and eight or more three-pointers in a single game.

The 40 was three points shy of his career high — and his third career 40-point game — but this was his first 40-point double-double, and the first time he had done it coming off the bench. For a Jazz team that has been injury-plagued and short on bright spots during a 2-13 start, a 23-year-old reserve rewriting a record that young is the kind of night worth remembering long after the losing streak is forgotten.

What's next

Utah visits the Lakers on Wednesday, with three more games on the schedule this week. The skid is over. The question now is whether the youngest 40-10-8 night in league history was a one-off explosion or the start of something bigger for Sensabaugh.
