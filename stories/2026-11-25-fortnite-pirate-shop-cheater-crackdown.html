Epic is banning Fortnite players who cheated their way to the Pirate Shop token — and their teammates are losing everything too

By Matt

Epic Games announced November 25 that it is banning Fortnite players who used cheats to win the Pirate Shop Access token — and anyone who partied up with a cheater loses the token and every cosmetic they claimed through it, even if they had no idea. Random Squad Fill matches are the only exception.

Epic Games has had enough of the Pirate Shop token hunt. The company announced November 25, through its Fortnite Status channel, that it is banning players who used cheats to obtain Pirate Shop Access in the Treasure Hunters mini season — and revoking the access and every cosmetic claimed through it. The crackdown lands with just over a week left in the season, and it comes with a warning that reaches further than the cheaters themselves.

Your squadmates pay for it too

The part players need to hear is the second half of the announcement. Anyone who won the token by teaming up with a cheater loses the token and all the Item Shop goodies they claimed while it was active — even if they had no idea someone in their party was cheating. They will not be banned themselves, but the rewards are gone. The one exception is random matches: players who were paired with a cheater through Squad Fill are exempt, since they never chose the party.

Epic's target is clear from the design of the punishment. A player who builds a throwaway smurf account, cheats their way to the token, and funnels the free cosmetics to their main account through a party gains nothing now — the whole party's haul gets wiped. The message is aimed at the grey market that has grown up around the token all season, from paid carries to accounts listed for sale.

Why everyone is this desperate

The token is the most coveted item in Fortnite history, and the numbers explain the frenzy. It grants up to 28 days of free Item Shop cosmetics, and it drops from treasure chests at odds of roughly one in 137,500. Some players have opened more than 150,000 chests without ever seeing one, which is why the hunt has driven people to cheats, exploits, and fake tools all month.

This is not the first time Epic has had to shut a shortcut down. On November 21 the company switched off geysers across the Battle Royale map after players used them with SUVs to glide endlessly and farm chests, and earlier in the month it patched a glitch that let players break open chests inside locked vaults without a key. Epic has said throughout the season that there is no special way to get the token and that buying accounts or using fake tools is a scam that can get you banned.

The Squad Fill exemption has already been tested once. A player who won the token in a random fill match posted that he had been banned days later; Epic replied that his account had not been banned at all — it had been locked because it was hacked, a different problem entirely. The company has repeated since that it does not ban players just for being matched with a cheater in fills.

What happens next

Epic has not said how many accounts have been hit in this wave, or whether the bans are permanent. The clock is the real pressure now: Golden Week starts November 28 with better odds on the season's gold rewards, the final Treasure Hours windows run that Saturday with the token's odds boosted, and Treasure Hunters ends December 5 with the "Out of Time" live event. After that the token is vaulted for good — which is exactly why the cheating got this desperate, and why Epic is drawing the line before the final week.
