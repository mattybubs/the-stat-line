Apex Legends: Cipher Is All About Information — Crypto Rework, Akimbo Wingman, and Strongholds Land November 3

By Matt

Apex Legends Season 31, Cipher, launches November 3 at 10 a.m. PT / 1 p.m. ET with a full Crypto rework, dual-wielded Wingmans, high-risk Stronghold loot, and buffs for Revenant and Lifeline.

Respawn and EA pulled the curtain back on the next Apex Legends season on October 29, and the theme is right there in the name. Cipher is about knowing more than the other squad — and then doing something about it before they know you know.

Season 31 launches November 3 at 10 a.m. PT / 1 p.m. ET, free to play as always, on PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Xbox One, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC through the EA app, Epic Games Store, and Steam.

Watch the official gameplay trailer first — it sells the whole season in about a minute: intel first, gunfire second.

Crypto, rewired

The headline change is a full rework for Crypto, and it flips how the legend actually plays. Thanks to a firmware upgrade from his sister Mila, Crypto's role shifts from passive drone-camper to aggressive intel-gatherer.

His Surveillance Drone no longer runs on a cooldown. It runs on batteries, which means you can pick it up and redeploy it without sitting through a long wait. Park it in the right spot and it switches into a new Sentry Mode, automatically tagging any enemy in its line of sight for your whole squad. And his EMP Ultimate can now be fired with a single button press instead of a charge-and-release — a small change on paper, and a massive one in the middle of a fight, when you do not have a spare second to wind anything up.

If you have ever played Crypto as a guy who hides in a corner staring at a screen while his squad fights 2v3, this season is specifically trying to break you of that habit. The drone is now a weapon you push forward with, not a security camera you babysit.

The Akimbo Wingman is exactly as rude as it sounds

The Wingman has always been the revolver that rewards people who do not miss. Cipher gives it a second life — literally a second gun. The Akimbo Wingman lets you dual-wield the classic revolver, trading some of that famous precision for a whole lot more damage up close.

This is not a spray-and-pray weapon. It is a "you had better hit your shots, because now there are two of them" weapon, and in the right hands it is going to end a lot of close-range fights before the other squad figures out what happened. In the wrong hands, it is going to be very loud and very embarrassing. Either way, it will be everywhere in the first week.

Strongholds: loot with an alarm attached

Strongholds are Respawn's evolution of Care Packages, and the pitch is simple: high-tier, high-risk loot in a very loud package. Raid one and you can haul out crate weapons, corrupted attachments, and EVO for your entire squad.

The catch is the whole point. Opening a Stronghold takes time, and it trips an alarm that tells everyone nearby exactly where you are. Strongholds spawn with cover and Hardlight to help you hold your ground, but make no mistake — you are ringing a dinner bell and then daring the lobby to come take it from you.

This is going to change how endgames play out. Squads that used to avoid third parties will now have to decide whether the loot is worth becoming the third party's favorite target, and squads that love a fight will have a glowing invitation to every Stronghold on the map.

Revenant and Lifeline get some love too

Two more legends get meaningful buffs, and both of them are about staying in the fight longer.

Revenant plays more aggressively this season with a shorter Ultimate cooldown and a quicker Tactical. His new Upgrades let you extend your Ultimate with every knock, silence nearby enemies after you land a Shadow Pounce, or refresh your Shadow Pounce automatically while an enemy is marked. That is a Revenant who does not have to disengage to recharge — he just keeps going.

Lifeline, meanwhile, gets a reduced cooldown on D.O.C. and the ability to self-heal while gliding, which is a survivability tool she has never really had. Her new Upgrades push further: an Ultimate cooldown reduction, a shield while you revive a teammate, improved flight mobility — and the return of her revive shield, a piece of her identity that longtime players will recognize immediately.

Ranked gets Legend Bans, and the Mythic Volt goes full Crypto

Ranked players in Diamond lobbies and above get a new wrinkle: Legend Bans. Every player casts one vote to ban a Legend, and the more votes a Legend gets, the greater the chance they are banned for that match. Have a Plan B. Maybe a Plan C.

And because no Apex season is complete without something shiny to chase, Cipher brings the Cryptic Core, a new Mythic Volt SMG covered in Crypto's digital fingerprints. It is a hive-mind of nanites that takes shape as a weapon in Crypto's signature colors, with a built-in PALS assistant rendered in low-res pixel art. You can reprogram it with Exotic Shards, each one slamming in a new retro game cartridge and switching the gun to a new vibe and color. It drops in Cipher Seasonal Packs all season long, alongside new skins for Crypto, Wraith, Gibraltar, Revenant, and Horizon.

Looking past Cipher

EA also used the reveal to look one season further ahead: the major map update planned for Season 32 is Kings Canyon Evolved, a completely reworked Kings Canyon with new, remixed, and touched-up points of interest, planned for early 2027. That is a whole other conversation — but it tells you Respawn is not treating Cipher as a quiet filler season.

For now, the job is simple. Learn the drone, respect the Stronghold alarm, and do not stand still in front of a dual-wielding Wingman. Cipher drops November 3. Get the squad together.
