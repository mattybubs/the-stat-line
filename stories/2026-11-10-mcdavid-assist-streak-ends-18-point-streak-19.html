McDavid's assist streak ends at 18 — but the point streak hits 19, and only three players have ever done that

By Matt

Connor McDavid's assist streak ended at 18 in Edmonton's 3-1 win over the Rangers — but his point streak reached 19 games to open the season, something only Gretzky, Heatley and Sundin have ever done.

The night

The Edmonton Oilers beat the New York Rangers 3-1 at Rogers Place on Monday night, and the box score came with a plot twist. Connor McDavid scored 6:42 into the first period — unassisted, off a Dylan Garand giveaway — and his point streak reached 19 games to open the season. His assist streak, the one everyone was watching, ended at 18.

Well, almost. McDavid was briefly credited with an assist on Leon Draisaitl's empty-net goal, which would have kept the streak alive. Then the NHL pulled it off the scoresheet. Eighteen it is — tied with Adam Oates for the second-longest assist streak in NHL history, behind only Wayne Gretzky's 23 in 1990-91.

Draisaitl scored twice and now leads the NHL with 18 goals in 19 games. Devon Levi made 28 saves for the Rangers, who went 0-for-5 on the power play and got a short-handed goal from Jaroslav Chmelar. The Oilers improved to 13-3-3, first in the West, and swept the season series from New York after a 4-1 win there on November 1.

The history

The point streak is the bigger deal, and it's the one that puts McDavid in a room with three names. He has 11 goals and 37 assists — 48 points — through 19 games, making him the fourth player ever with a 19-game point streak to open a season. The other three are Wayne Gretzky, Dany Heatley and Mats Sundin.

The chase isn't over, it just changes shape. McDavid is two points from 50 on the season — only Gretzky and Mario Lemieux have ever reached 50 points in 19 games or fewer. And three assists in his next three games would tie Gretzky's mark for fastest to 40 assists in a season, 40 in 22 games. The Oilers host Philadelphia on Wednesday.
