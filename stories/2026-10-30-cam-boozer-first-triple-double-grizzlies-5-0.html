Cam Boozer's First Triple-Double Keeps the Grizzlies Perfect at 5-0

By Matt

Rookie Cam Boozer posted 18 points, 12 rebounds and 12 assists in Memphis' 124-103 win at Portland — the first triple-double of his career, and one that makes him the second-youngest player ever to record one.

The Memphis Grizzlies are 5-0, and their rookie just announced himself in the loudest way possible.

Cam Boozer finished with 18 points, 12 rebounds and 12 assists in Memphis' 124-103 win over the previously unbeaten Portland Trail Blazers on Thursday night at Moda Center — the first triple-double of his NBA career, in just his fifth game as a pro.

Second-youngest ever — and the youngest at his position

Boozer is the second-youngest player in NBA history to record a triple-double, and the youngest power forward and the youngest Grizzlies player ever to do it. He did it efficiently, too: 6-for-10 from the field, 4-for-5 from three, in 28 minutes.

The No. 3 pick in the 2026 draft had already opened his career with two double-doubles in his first four games. Thursday was the step up — he controlled the game without forcing it, and Memphis outscored Portland 66-45 in the second half after the teams went to halftime tied at 58.

The Grizzlies keep rolling — and Portland's run ends

Memphis is now one win shy of tying the franchise's best-ever start, 6-0 in 2014, and the Grizzlies have already completed their first undefeated road trip of four games or more since 2013. They did it on a night when the headliner matchup fizzled: Portland's Ja Morant, facing his former team for the first time, had 14 points and three assists in 23 minutes before leaving in the fourth quarter with a left hamstring injury.

Memphis got help everywhere. Reserve Isaiah Stewart scored 18, Cedric Coward added 17 points, seven rebounds and three steals, and Zach Edey chipped in 13. Deni Avdija led Portland with 24 points, nine rebounds and seven assists, but the Blazers' second-half collapse — outscored by 21 after the break — ended their unbeaten start at 3-1.

The Grizzlies are now one of just two unbeaten teams left in the league, alongside Houston. They return home Saturday against Sacramento, with a shot at franchise history on the table.

What it means

Rookie triple-doubles are rare for a reason: they demand a player do everything, against grown men, in real minutes. Boozer just did it in his fifth game, on the road, against an unbeaten team, in a game his team won by 21. The Grizzlies didn't just stay perfect — they found out their rookie can run the show.
