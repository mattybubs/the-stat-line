McDavid ties Oates at 18 — now five games from Gretzky's assist record

By Matt

Connor McDavid picked up three assists in Edmonton's 4-2 win over Vancouver on Saturday, running his season-opening assist streak to 18 games — tying Adam Oates for the second-longest in NHL history. Only Wayne Gretzky's 23 stands ahead of him, and McDavid gets his next crack at it tonight against the Rangers.

What happened

Connor McDavid did not need to score a goal to own the night. The Edmonton Oilers captain collected three assists on Saturday as Edmonton beat the Vancouver Canucks 4-2 at Rogers Place, extending his assist streak to 18 games and tying Adam Oates for the second-longest such run in NHL history.

Leon Draisaitl did the finishing. He scored twice and added an assist, Mattias Ekholm and Jason Dickinson also scored, and Devon Levi made 20 saves in his return from injury as the Oilers (12-3-3) snapped a two-game slide. Edmonton is now 10-0-2 when scoring first this season. The Canucks (3-15-1) have lost five straight and 15 of their last 16.

The streak, in context

Only one player in NHL history has ever had an assist in more consecutive games than McDavid has right now: Wayne Gretzky, who assisted in 23 straight for the Los Angeles Kings in 1990-91. Oates did it in 18 straight for the Boston Bruins in 1992-93 — in the middle of a 97-assist season. McDavid has now caught him.

This is also the longest assist streak to open a season in league history. McDavid set that mark when he reached 13 straight games on October 28, and he has kept adding to it every night since. It is the longest season-opening streak by any active player, passing his own 17-game run from 2021-22 that he shared with Toronto's William Nylander (2023-24). He is also the sixth player ever with a point in each of his team's first 18 games of a season, and he leads the league with 47 points — 10 goals and 37 assists.

The math from here is simple and delicious. If the streak holds, McDavid ties Gretzky on November 17 against the Panthers in Florida, and breaks the record two nights later against the Lightning in Tampa. Tonight is game 19, at home against the Rangers. McDavid had a goal and an assist in Edmonton's 4-1 win at New York on November 1 — the night he became the fastest player in NHL history to reach 30 assists in a season.

How Saturday played out

Ekholm put Edmonton ahead 1-0 at 9:14 of the first, taking a one-time pass from McDavid at the front of the right circle. Max Sasson tied it with nine seconds left in the period. Dickinson regained the lead 49 seconds into the third on a backhand off his own rebound, and four minutes later McDavid sent a cross-ice feed to Draisaitl for a one-timer and a 3-1 lead — the kind of setup the NHL's own account called perfection. Brock Boeser made it 3-2 at 17:25, and Draisaitl added an empty-netter with 45 seconds left for his 16th goal of the season.

It was not a clean night for Edmonton otherwise. Zach Hyman returned after missing five games with a neck injury and had an assist. Ryan Nugent-Hopkins left 45 seconds into the second period after taking a puck to the head off a teammate's shot — coach Mike Babcock said the initial fear was a broken jaw, but Nugent-Hopkins seemed to be doing OK afterward and Babcock does not expect him to miss much time. Evan Bouchard extended his points streak to six games, and he and Draisaitl both have points in 16 of 18 games.

Babcock liked that it was tight. "This is what I expected tonight," he said. "I'd like to beat them 6-0, but the reality is this was way better for us, and it was way better that they pushed back and we had to stick to doing it right."

Why this one matters

Gretzky's records are supposed to be the untouchable ones. McDavid has spent his whole career running into them — and lately, running through them. He already owns the season-opening assist streak outright. Now he is five games from the all-time mark, with the chase playing out in real time: tonight at home, then a road trip that ends in Florida, where the record would fall.

And the Oilers need every bit of it. They are the league's highest-scoring team, but they have been winning with their captain setting the table every single night — 37 assists in 18 games, a point in all 18. The only question left is whether anyone can stop the streak before Tampa. So far, 18 teams have tried. None have.
