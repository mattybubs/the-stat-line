The Warriors' win streak is over — and Curry left after six minutes

By Matt

Houston beat Golden State 115-108 on Wednesday, ending the Warriors' eight-game win streak. Stephen Curry played six minutes before leaving with right abdominal soreness, and the MRI results weren't known after the game.

The night

The Houston Rockets beat the Golden State Warriors 115-108 on Wednesday night in Houston, and the final score only tells half of it. The Warriors' eight-game winning streak — the run that started after an 0-3 opening and made them the first team ever to open a season with three losses and then win eight in a row — is over.

The bigger worry left the floor in the first quarter. Stephen Curry played six minutes before departing with right abdominal soreness. He underwent an MRI after the game, and the results weren't immediately known, coach Steve Kerr said postgame. Golden State played most of the night without the player carrying the streak — Curry was averaging 30.6 points a game, 32.3 over his last five.

Houston did not need a masterpiece to close it out. Alperen Sengun flirted with a triple-double — 19 points, 13 rebounds, nine assists — Kevin Durant led the Rockets with 20, and Amen Thompson added 19 points and seven rebounds. The Rockets pulled away in the fourth quarter for their second straight win, improving to 9-3. Golden State falls to 8-4.

Why it matters

Streaks end. That is the boring part. The interesting part is how this one ended — not with a cold shooting night or a buzzer-beater, but with the best player on the floor walking to the locker room six minutes in and the team holding on for three quarters before Houston's depth took over.

The Warriors have been shorthanded all through the run — Jimmy Butler, Moses Moody and Gary Payton II are all out — and they kept winning anyway. But there is shorthanded and then there is no Curry at all, and Wednesday was the second one. Until the MRI results come back, the streak is the smaller story. The abdominal is the story.

Around the league

Boston pulled off its own 22-point comeback on the same night, beating Toronto 98-93 behind Jayson Tatum's 24 — the Celtics' fifth straight win. James Harden scored a season-high 32 as Cleveland won 115-107 at the Garden.
