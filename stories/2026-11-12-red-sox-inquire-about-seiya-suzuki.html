Red Sox inquire about Seiya Suzuki

By Matt

Boston has checked in on free agent Seiya Suzuki, per Pete Abraham — the Cubs slugger is coming off the best season of his career and would add the power bat the Sox lineup lacked.

The first bat checked

The Red Sox have checked in on free agent Seiya Suzuki, according to Pete Abraham of the Boston Globe. Free agency opened on November 2, and Suzuki is the first premier bat Boston has reportedly asked about.

The inquiry came out of the GM Meetings in Las Vegas, where Abraham also reported the worst-kept secret in the room: Boston is shopping left fielder Jarren Duran. The two moves fit together — if Duran goes, there's an outfield spot and a middle-of-the-order hole to fill, and Suzuki is the top right-handed bat on the market.

What Suzuki did in 2026

Suzuki, 32, just finished the best season of his five-year run with the Cubs. He set a career high with 5.1 wins above replacement while playing 143 games, and he did it as the middle-of-the-order bat Chicago leaned on all year.

Games: 143

Slash line: .270/.368/.473

Home runs: 26

Doubles: 31

RBIs: 86

OPS: .841

That production is exactly what Boston's lineup was missing. The Sox finished 12th in the American League in home runs last season, and the front office has made adding a middle-of-the-lineup hitter its offseason priority. This isn't the first time the two sides have been linked, either — The Athletic's Jen McCaffrey named Suzuki as a possible Boston target last week.

What it would cost

Suzuki just completed a five-year, $85 million deal he signed with the Cubs ahead of the 2022 season. Chicago extended him a one-year, $23.1 million qualifying offer, but his agent, Joel Wolfe, said at the GM Meetings that Suzuki will decline it and test the open market.

The early projections put the price in nine figures: Spotrac projects a four-year deal worth just over $109 million, while The Athletic's Jim Bowden projects five years and $120 million. Boston won't be bidding alone — the Mariners, Padres, Phillies and Blue Jays are all reported to have interest, and the Cubs are expected to make an effort to keep him.

There's also a draft-pick cost attached. Because Suzuki received a qualifying offer, signing him would cost Boston a second- and fifth-round pick in the 2027 MLB Draft plus $1 million in international signing bonus pool space.
