Cutter Gauthier's hat trick makes it 10 straight games with a goal — one shy of Selanne's Ducks record — as the Blues win a wild 8-6 one

By Matt

Cutter Gauthier scored three times in St. Louis — the last one with the goalie pulled and 41 seconds left — to stretch his goal streak to 10 straight games, one shy of Teemu Selanne's Ducks record, in an 8-6 loss to the Blues.

The night

Cutter Gauthier did everything except win the game. The Anaheim Ducks winger scored three times at Enterprise Center on Tuesday night — a slap shot early in the second, a power-play snap shot late in the second, and a wrist shot with the goalie pulled and 41 seconds left — for his fourth hat trick of the season and a goal in his 10th consecutive game, the longest goal streak in the NHL in 29 years. The St. Louis Blues still won, 8-6.

The hat trick goal was the loudest of the night even in a loss. Down 7-4, the Ducks got a power-play goal from Mikael Granlund with 1:25 left — a 6-on-4 with the goalie already pulled — and Gauthier — fed by Troy Terry and Jackson LaCombe — beat Jordan Binnington from the right wing to make it 7-6 with 41 seconds on the clock. The building had three seconds to believe in a comeback before Pavel Buchnevich scored into the empty net to seal it.

Gauthier finished with three goals and an assist on eight shots in 23:54 of ice time. It was his fourth hat trick in 10 games, a run that already included three of them in eight days to start November. Four hat tricks in one calendar month is an NHL record — no player had ever done it before.

The streak

Ten straight games with a goal makes Gauthier the 21st player in NHL history to do it — and the first in 29 years. He entered the night with 16 goals in nine games, and he has 19 across the 10, with five assists for 24 points in the stretch. His point streak is now 12 games.

One more gets him a share of the franchise record. Teemu Selanne scored in 11 straight for Anaheim from October 21 to November 10, 1997, with 17 goals in that span. Gauthier can tie it Thursday night in Colorado, and break it Saturday in Utah.

The company keeps getting more ridiculous. Gauthier already joined Wayne Gretzky and Mario Lemieux as the only players in 100 years with 14 or more goals in the first eight games of a calendar month, and he was the first player in NHL history with three hat tricks inside the first 10 days of a month — eight days, beating Phil Esposito's 13-day mark from December 1970. He entered Tuesday tied with Auston Matthews for the most goals through 20 games of a season by an active American player, at 18. He left St. Louis with 21 goals in 21 games.

November is turning into its own record chase. His 17 goals this month leave him three shy of the NHL record for a calendar month — 20, shared by Selanne (March 1993) and Joe Malone (February 1921) — with six November games left.

The game around him

It took eight different Blues to outscore him. St. Louis got goals from Jonatan Berggren, Connor McMichael, Robert Thomas, Dalibor Dvorsky, Jake Neighbours, Philip Broberg, Logan Mailloux and Buchnevich, with Thomas adding two assists for a three-point night. Mailloux's goal was the first of his NHL career. The Blues led 4-3 after two and 7-3 before Anaheim's late push made the final look closer than most of the night felt.

The Ducks got goals from Alex Killorn, Ian Moore — the first of his NHL career, unassisted — and Granlund alongside Gauthier's three, and they outshot St. Louis 31-28. But Ville Husso allowed seven goals on 24 shots, and even with Lukas Dostal stopping all three he faced in relief, the hole was too deep. Binnington made 25 saves for St. Louis.

Anaheim falls to 11-10. St. Louis improves to 10-9.

What it means

A hat trick in a loss is usually a footnote. This one isn't, because the streak is now the story of the NHL season — a 22-year-old winger scoring at a pace that has him standing next to Gretzky, Lemieux and Selanne in the record book, and doing it while his team keeps losing the games he takes over. The goals haven't been enough.

The record is right there, though. One goal in Denver on Thursday ties Selanne. One more in Utah on Saturday passes him. At this rate, betting against either feels foolish.

Up next: the Ducks visit the Avalanche on Thursday, November 19 at 9:00 PM ET, then the Mammoth in Utah on Saturday.
