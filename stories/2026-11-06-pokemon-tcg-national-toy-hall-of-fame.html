Pokémon TCG enters the National Toy Hall of Fame

By Matt

Thirty years after it debuted in Japan, the Pokémon Trading Card Game is a Hall of Famer — inducted Thursday alongside bingo, Care Bears, and the playground slide.

What happened

The Pokémon Trading Card Game is officially a member of the National Toy Hall of Fame. The Strong National Museum of Play in Rochester, New York, announced the 2026 class on Thursday, November 5, on the Today show — and Pokémon TCG made the cut in its first year as a finalist.

It joins three other inductees: bingo, Care Bears, and the playground slide. The four winners came out of a field of 12 finalists that also included Bop It!, Mahjong, Monster High dolls, Mouse Trap, Polly Pocket, Sorry!, Tickle Me Elmo, and Yahtzee. All four are now enshrined and on display at The Strong.

Thirty years, 85 billion cards

The timing is the story. The Pokémon TCG debuted in Japan in 1996 — thirty years ago this year — after the Game Boy games turned monster-collecting into a phenomenon. The Hall of Fame, established in 1998, recognizes toys that "have inspired creative play and enjoyed popularity over a sustained period." Few inductees can match this one on the sustained part: the game has never really left the cultural conversation since.

The numbers back it up. As of March 2026, more than 85 billion Pokémon cards have been printed and distributed worldwide. The Strong's own write-up calls the game "renowned for its relative accessibility compared to other collectible card games" — a big reason it spread from schoolyards to a global hobby that now supports grading companies, billion-dollar resale markets, and release-day lines at every major retailer.

"Pokémon TCG's widespread appeal and global presence are undeniable," curator Mirek Stolee said in the announcement.

A very good week to be a Pokémon collector

The induction lands on the same week as the biggest release day of the anniversary year. Mega Evolution—Delta Reign hits shelves today, November 6, alongside the final wave of 30th Celebration collections. It is the kind of week that shows exactly why the Hall of Fame came calling: thirty years in, the game is still setting release-day records instead of coasting on nostalgia.

The other inductees tell their own version of the same story. Bingo descends from an Italian lottery played around 1530 and is now played virtually worldwide. Care Bears, like Pokémon, got a boost from an active fan community that mobilized around the nomination. And the slide — the simplest toy on the list — was honored for its role in child development and a century of playground use.

What to watch

The Strong says the induction will likely lead to new exhibits and events around the Pokémon TCG in the new year. For collectors, the near-term watch is simpler: this is the third piece of institutional recognition the hobby has picked up in a year when the cards themselves keep breaking records. The game is not just popular. It is, officially, historic.
