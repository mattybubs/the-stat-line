William Contreras Is the World Series MVP: The Catcher Who Won It Behind the Plate

By Matt

William Contreras was named World Series MVP after hitting .381 with four RBIs — and throwing out four Rays, winning 10 of 13 ABS challenges, and guiding the Brewers' staff to the franchise's first title.

William Contreras won two championships this year. The first came in March, with Venezuela at the World Baseball Classic. The second came Wednesday night in St. Petersburg, and it ended with the Milwaukee Brewers catcher holding the Willie Mays World Series MVP trophy — the first World Series MVP the Brewers have ever had.

That last part is worth sitting with for a second. Fifty-eight seasons, one trip to the Fall Classic before this one (1982, a loss), and the first MVP trophy in franchise history goes to the catcher. Not the rookie shortstop with the record hit streak. Not the ace who won the clincher on a bad calf. The catcher. And once you look at what Contreras actually did in this series, it stops being a surprise at all.

The bat

Contreras hit .381 (8-for-21) with four RBIs against Tampa Bay, and his biggest swing of the series came in the sixth inning of Game 5. With Christian Yelich on after being hit by a pitch, Contreras belted a double off the left-field wall that scored Yelich and made it 3-0. Cooper Pratt followed with a sacrifice fly that scored Contreras himself, and just like that a tense clincher was a 4-0 game.

He also set a franchise record with 19 postseason hits along the way. The bat played, full stop.

The glove, and the brain

But the bat is maybe the third-most impressive thing he did in this series, which tells you how ridiculous the rest of it was.

The Rays led the American League with 164 stolen bases in the regular season. They ran into a wall named Contreras. He threw out four runners trying to steal in the first four games — the first catcher to do that in a World Series since Lance Parrish in 1984 — and finished the series 4-for-9 against Tampa's running game.

Then there's the robot umps. This was the first season of MLB's automated ball-strike challenge system, and Contreras turned it into a weapon. He challenged 185 calls in the regular season and won 105 — both major league highs. In the World Series he went 10-for-13 on challenges, including a perfect 4-for-4 in Game 3 and another won challenge in the clincher. As he put it: "It's not magic. I think it's the experience of playing every day throughout the season."

A catcher who steals strikes with challenges, kills your running game, and hits .381 is not a role player having a nice week. That's the series, right there.

The job got bigger

None of this happened in a vacuum. Last winter the Brewers traded Freddy Peralta — who, in one of the series' stranger subplots, started Game 3 of this World Series for the Rays — and left Contreras with a young pitching staff to guide. He took that on himself.

"I took that upon myself and I knew the responsibility I was going to have that year," he said.

The staff responded by strangling the Rays for five games: Tampa scored 2.4 runs per game in the series, and the Brewers finished the postseason 11-2. The MVP voters noticed who was calling those games.

Over Pratt — and that's fine

This one could have gone the other way, and nobody would have argued much. Cooper Pratt slashed .421/.455/.684 against Tampa and set a World Series rookie record with six straight hits, including the go-ahead homer in extra innings of Game 4. Jackson Chourio piled up 11 hits, a five-game Series record.

Pratt will have his MVPs. This one belonged to the guy who controlled every pitch of the series, then drove in runs with the bat too. "I was playing every day for the fans," Contreras told MLB on Fox. "It had to happen for the city. They were waiting a long time for that."

They were. And now the parade route gets to include the first World Series MVP in Brewers history.
