Quinn Hughes is headed home for evaluation — and the NHL's best team just lost its best defenseman

By Matt

The Wild sent Quinn Hughes home from their road trip for evaluation of an undisclosed injury suffered Thursday in Calgary. The Norris winner is out at least two games, his 13-game point streak is over, and there is no timeline yet.

Sent home mid-trip

Quinn Hughes is headed back to Minnesota.

The Wild ruled their star defenseman out for the rest of the road trip on Saturday, November 21, and sent him home to be evaluated for an injury he suffered Thursday night in Calgary. Head coach John Hynes confirmed the move before Saturday's game in Vancouver, saying Hughes will miss at least the next two games — Saturday against the Canucks and Monday at San Jose.

What Hynes did not say is what is actually wrong. He would not say whether it is an upper- or lower-body injury, only that it is not a concussion. There is no timeline beyond the two games he has already been ruled out for, and no word on whether he will be ready when the Wild return home Wednesday to host Utah.

The hit in Calgary

The injury came late in the third period of Thursday's 4-1 win over the Flames, when Calgary forward Adam Klapka checked Hughes into the boards. Hughes finished his shift, went out for one more power play, and then left the game for good.

It was an ordinary-looking hit. That is what makes the next part concerning: a routine shove into the glass ended the night of the best defenseman in hockey, and a day and a half later the team decided he needed to go home to be looked at properly.

Before he left that game, Hughes had a goal and an assist — extending his point streak to 13 games, with six goals and 15 assists in that stretch. The streak ends now, in a building where he used to be captain.

What Minnesota loses

Hughes is not just the Wild's best defenseman. He is their engine.

He has 34 points in 24 games this season — second on the team in scoring — and leads the entire NHL in average ice time at 27:49 a night. He is the 2023-24 Norris Trophy winner and a U.S. Olympic gold medalist, and he is doing it in the final year of his contract, with no extension signed and unrestricted free agency waiting on July 1.

The timing could hardly be worse for the standings. Minnesota is 17-3-4, the best record in the NHL, riding a five-game winning streak and points in 13 straight. They have been doing it with Hughes playing nearly half of every game.

The Wild do have some cover. Brock Faber and Jared Spurgeon recently returned from injuries of their own, and David Spacek steps into the lineup alongside Zach Bogosian in Vancouver. Minnesota also recalled defenseman Viking Gustafsson Nyberg from AHL Iowa for depth, with Olli Maatta still on injured reserve.

None of that replaces 28 minutes a night from a Norris winner. The question now is what the evaluation in Minnesota finds — and whether two games is the whole story, or just the start of it.
