Josh Allen passes Peyton Manning — 325 total touchdowns, the most ever through a player's first 10 seasons

By Matt

Josh Allen entered Sunday tied with Peyton Manning at 322 career total touchdowns. A one-yard run in the first quarter put him past the Hall of Famer — and he finished the day at 325, with a season and a half still to add to the record.

The night

Josh Allen needed one touchdown. He got it early, and then he kept going.

Allen entered Sunday's game against the New York Jets tied with Hall of Fame quarterback Peyton Manning at 322 career total touchdowns — the most by any player through his first 10 NFL seasons. On Buffalo's second possession, after a defensive holding call gave the Bills first-and-goal at the one-foot line, Allen called his own number and punched in a one-yard rushing score. Career touchdown No. 323. Manning, passed.

Allen wasn't done. He added another one-yard rushing touchdown in the third quarter and a 45-yard touchdown pass to DJ Moore, finishing the day with three total touchdowns and a career total of 325 — 233 passing, 90 rushing and two receiving — in a 31-12 Bills win at MetLife Stadium that improved Buffalo to 8-1.

The gap in the numbers

The record is impressive on its own. The way Allen got there is the jaw-dropper.

Manning needed 160 regular-season games to pile up his 322 touchdowns across his first 10 seasons in Indianapolis. Allen passed that total in just 137 games — and he did it before completing his ninth season. He still has the rest of this season and all of 2027 to add to a record that now belongs to him alone.

The one-yard run that broke the record was also his 10th rushing touchdown of the 2026 season, extending his streak to four consecutive years with at least 10 rushing scores — tying Jalen Hurts (2021-24) for the longest such streak by a quarterback in NFL history. His second rushing touchdown of the day was the 90th of his career, tying Eric Dickerson and Curtis Martin for 14th in NFL history; his next one ties Jerome Bettis and Franco Harris for 12th.

Allen's own assessment was characteristically blunt: he was critical of his own performance, an 11-of-21 passing day for 196 yards and an interception. The record didn't care. Neither did the scoreboard.

The team around him

Buffalo's win was its fifth straight, and the Bills are 8-1 for the first time since 1991 — the year they finished 13-3 and reached the second of four consecutive Super Bowls. They've won eight consecutive road games dating to last season and are the NFL's only unbeaten team on the road in 2026.

The defense did its part: four sacks, 12 quarterback hits, two takeaways, and zero touchdowns allowed over the last eight quarters — the Jets managed four field goals and went 0-for-2 in the red zone. Geno Smith left the game briefly but cleared concussion protocol and returned, finishing 13-of-21 for 148 yards. Garrett Wilson caught seven passes for 106 yards in the loss.

Hall of Fame quarterback Jim Kelly posted a short message for Allen on Instagram after the game. He didn't need many words. Nobody does, when the number is 325 and counting.
