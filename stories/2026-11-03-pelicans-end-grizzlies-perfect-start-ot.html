Pelicans Hand Grizzlies Their First Loss, 135-134 in Overtime — Jerome's 40 Wasted

By Matt

Memphis was the NBA's last undefeated team, and Ty Jerome nearly saved it single-handedly — nine straight points to force overtime, 40 in all. But Jeremiah Fears' two free throws with 7.3 seconds left gave New Orleans a 135-134 win, ending the Grizzlies' franchise-record-matching 6-0 start.

The NBA's last undefeated team is undefeated no more — and it took one of the wildest finishes of the young season to do it. The New Orleans Pelicans walked into FedExForum, led for the entire fourth quarter, watched Ty Jerome drag Memphis back from the dead, and still walked out with a 135-134 overtime win on Monday night.

Jeremiah Fears did the damage at the line. With 7.3 seconds left in overtime and New Orleans down one, Fears sank both free throws off a second-chance scramble, capping a 12-point night and a 25-of-30 night for the Pelicans at the stripe. Memphis put the ball in Jerome's hands on the final possession, and for once this season, his contested 15-footer missed.

Jerome's one-man rescue — and its limit

This was Jerome's game until it wasn't. He scored nine of his game-high 40 points in the final 1:43 of regulation — all of Memphis' points in a 9-0 run that erased a nine-point deficit and tied it at 124. He scored eight of the Grizzlies' 10 points in overtime. He finished 14-of-22 from the field.

And it still wasn't enough, because the rest of the night kept slipping away from Memphis in the margins. New Orleans attempted 101 field goals to Memphis' 91, took 30 free throws to Memphis' 27, and won points off turnovers 21-7. The Pelicans led 124-115 with 2:36 left in regulation on a Karlo Matkovic three — Matkovic finished with a career-high 25, matching Zion Williamson's team high.

Williamson, for his part, did his best work when it mattered most: 25 points, 11 rebounds, and nine of New Orleans' 11 overtime points. Fears' free throws were the only Pelicans points in the extra period that didn't come from Williamson.

The streak, and what it meant

Memphis' 6-0 start matched the best season-opening streak in franchise history. That's the part that stings: the Grizzlies weren't just unbeaten, they were the league's last unbeaten team, and a seventh straight win would have stood alone in franchise history. Instead, the streak ends at six, on their own floor, in a game they trailed for the entire fourth quarter.

For New Orleans, the arc bends the other way. The Pelicans opened the season 0-4 and have now won three straight. Six Pelicans scored in double figures, including Derik Queen with 20 points, seven rebounds and five assists off the bench, and Trey Murphy III and Saddiq Bey with 18 apiece. Rookie Cameron Boozer had 18 points, nine rebounds and five assists for Memphis, and Jaylen Wells added 16.

Memphis coach Tuomas Iisalo put the ending down to inches — a tipped rebound that found a Pelican instead of a Grizzly. Jerome was blunter: "The minute we start to think we won a few and can relax a little bit, we're not going to beat anybody doing that." The Grizzlies get their next shot at a reset Wednesday against Indiana. The Pelicans, suddenly, look like a team that figured something out.
