Ja'Marr Chase puts on a show in Madrid as Bengals beat Falcons 34-17

By Matt

Ja'Marr Chase caught 10 passes for 155 yards — 142 of them in the first half — as the Bengals beat the Falcons 34-17 in Madrid, their fourth straight win and first ever outside the United States.

Madrid got the full Ja'Marr Chase experience on Sunday. The Bengals wide receiver caught 10 passes for 155 yards and a touchdown — 142 of those yards before halftime — as Cincinnati beat the Atlanta Falcons 34-17 at Real Madrid's Santiago Bernabéu Stadium in Week 9.

The win was the Bengals' fourth straight, pushed them to 6-2, and pulled them into a tie with the Pittsburgh Steelers atop the AFC North. It was also the first win outside the United States in franchise history.

Chase took over before halftime

Atlanta scored first. Michael Penix Jr. found Charlie Woerner for a one-yard touchdown on the Falcons' opening drive, the first touchdown ever scored in an NFL game in Madrid, and the Falcons led 7-0.

Cincinnati answered in five plays. Joe Burrow hit Chase on a 47-yard touchdown strike to tie it, and the Bengals never trailed again.

Chase's first half was the story of the game:

Receptions: 8; 10

Receiving yards: 142; 155

Touchdowns: 1; 1

The 142 first-half yards were the most of Chase's career in a half, and the most by a Bengal in a first half since 1988. They were also more than any player had managed against Atlanta in a full game all season. He finished with 10 catches on 12 targets.

Burrow kept dealing

Joe Burrow completed 24 of 29 passes for 271 yards and three touchdowns, and ran in a fourth score himself. It was his fourth straight game with at least three touchdown passes, and his fifth in the last six games.

Tee Higgins caught five passes for 32 yards and scored twice, both on Burrow throws. Evan McPherson added a 57-yard field goal just before halftime and a 43-yarder late to close out the scoring.

The Bengals led 24-10 at halftime and scored on all four of their first-half possessions. They have now scored 30 points or more in four straight games, something the franchise hasn't done since 1997.

The Moss company

Chase's touchdown in Madrid put him alongside Randy Moss as the only receivers in NFL history with seven or more touchdowns in each of their first six seasons.

That's the kind of company that says everything about a career, not just a game. Moss did it across his first six years in Minnesota. Chase is doing it in an era where the Bengals' offense runs through him and Burrow — and on Sunday, in front of a Madrid crowd that had never seen an NFL game before, he made it look easy.

Atlanta's side

The Falcons fell to 3-6. Bijan Robinson ran for 147 yards, and Atlanta ran for 157 as a team, but the Falcons couldn't keep pace once Cincinnati's offense got rolling. Two pass interference flags on cornerback DJ Turner II set up both Atlanta touchdowns — the only real blemish on a Bengals defensive day that ended with Barrett Carter's diving interception sealing the win.

Atlanta had scored on its first two drives. After that, Cincinnati's offense simply outscored them, and the Falcons never got closer than seven points again.

What it means

The Bengals host the Steelers next Sunday night at Paycor Stadium, with first place in the AFC North on the line. Cincinnati is playing its best football of the season, and the offense that put up 34 points in Madrid has now done it four weeks running.

The Madrid game was billed as a showcase. Chase turned it into a statement.
