Rosas Jr. Taps Covington in 43 Seconds in Rome Shocker

By Matt

Raul Rosas Jr. needed just 43 seconds to submit Colby Covington with a rear-naked choke in the Hype Italy main event in Rome.

Raul Rosas Jr. just pulled off one of the wildest results of the year. The UFC bantamweight submitted former interim welterweight champion Colby Covington with a rear-naked choke just 43 seconds into their submission-only grappling main event at Hype Italy in Rome on Tuesday night.

The finish

It was over almost before it started. Rosas pressured Covington immediately, secured an early takedown, and quickly worked his way onto the bigger man's back. Covington couldn't clear the choking arm before Rosas locked up the rear-naked choke and forced the tap 43 seconds into a match scheduled for 10 minutes.

The size gap made it even more stunning. Rosas competes at 135 pounds in the UFC; Covington used to fight at 170. None of it mattered once the action started.

One timing note: Hype's own announcement and the ESPN graphic going around tonight round it to 40 seconds. The 43-second figure comes from MMA Fighting's report of the finish, and it's the number most outlets have settled on.

Three days' notice

Rosas wasn't even supposed to be in this match. Covington was booked to face Dillon Danis in the main event, but Danis withdrew — MMA Mania reports it was an injury — and at Sunday's press conference, the promoter asked if anyone in attendance would step in. Rosas, sitting in the audience, raised his hand.

"I'll do it, f—k it," he said. "Chiwiwis!"

That gave the 22-year-old roughly three days to prepare for a much larger opponent with elite NCAA Division I wrestling credentials. He needed less than a minute to finish the job.

The reaction

The broadcast cut to Conor McGregor at ringside laughing as the tap came, and both men referenced him afterward. McGregor, a teammate of Danis, was a special guest at the show.

Covington owned it. He said he started slowly and had been focused mainly on his wrestling rather than jiu-jitsu — he hadn't trained Brazilian jiu-jitsu in years — but offered no excuses for the loss.

Rosas thanked Covington for accepting the last-minute matchup and reiterated that his primary goal remains becoming the youngest champion in UFC history. He rides a six-fight MMA winning streak into whatever comes next, fresh off his first UFC main-event win over Raoni Barcelos in September.

What's next

Covington isn't slowing down. He wrestles Khamzat Chimaev for the RAF Cruiserweight Crossover Championship in Abu Dhabi on November 14, and his next Hype match is already set against UFC lightweight Ignacio Bahamondes on December 20.
