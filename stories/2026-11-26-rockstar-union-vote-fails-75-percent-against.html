Rockstar's UK staff vote against union recognition — 75% say no, a week after GTA 6 launched

By Matt

The ballot that could have made Rockstar's UK workforce the biggest union in British games is over: three in four staff voted against formal recognition, with 93% turnout across five studios.

Rockstar Games will not be recognizing a union. The UK staff at the studio behind Grand Theft Auto 6 voted overwhelmingly against a formal recognition agreement with the IWGB Game Workers Union, ending a ballot that ran through the biggest launch week in gaming history.

The result was decisive: 75.3% against, 24.7% in favor. Of the 1,544 employees who voted — a 93.2% turnout across Rockstar's offices in Edinburgh, Leeds, Lincoln, Dundee, and London — 1,163 voted no and 381 voted yes.

What was on the ballot

The vote was over formal recognition of the Rockstar Game Workers Union, part of the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB). Recognition would have forced Rockstar to collectively bargain with the union over pay and conditions. Had it passed, the union would have become the largest in the UK video game sector, covering more than 1,600 workers at a company of roughly 4,700 employees worldwide.

The ballot opened on November 12 and closed on November 26 — meaning it ran directly through the launch of GTA 6 on November 19, the week the game demolished sales records. The recognition bid itself was announced last month, and the vote was first reported by Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, who broke the result on Thursday.

What both sides said

Rockstar's head of publishing, Jennifer Kolbe, said in a statement to IGN that the company was "extraordinarily proud of our team, the culture we've built together and the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI." Rockstar said it had worked with the union to facilitate a fair and transparent ballot.

The IWGB, for its part, said its Rockstar workers would keep organizing without formal recognition — the union's campaign doesn't end with the vote, even if the legal status it was seeking does.

Why it matters

This was the highest-profile union vote the games industry has seen. Rockstar is the studio behind the biggest entertainment launch ever, and the vote was watched as a test of whether unionization could take hold at a major AAA developer — especially one whose crunch culture was the subject of intense scrutiny during the Red Dead Redemption 2 era. The answer, from the workforce itself, was no, and by a wide margin.

There's also unfinished business hanging over the result: the ballot overlapped with the closing submissions of an employment tribunal, with judgment still pending. That case, not the vote, is the next thing to watch on the labor side of Rockstar.

One week after launch, GTA 6 is still breaking records. The studio that made it just voted, decisively, to stay as it is.
