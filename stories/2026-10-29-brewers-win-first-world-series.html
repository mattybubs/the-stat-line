Brewers Win the World Series: First Title in Franchise History

By Matt

The Milwaukee Brewers beat the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 in Game 5 to win the World Series 4-1, the first championship in franchise history.

The Milwaukee Brewers are World Series champions. They beat the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 in Game 5 at Tampa to take the series 4-1, the first title in franchise history — in the franchise's 58th season, after a journey that started as the Seattle Pilots in 1969.

Fifty-plus years of waiting, and they closed it out on the road, in a dome, in Florida. Not exactly the storybook Milwaukee setting — the storybook version probably involves snow — but nobody in Wisconsin is complaining about the weather this week.

The clincher

Cooper Pratt was the bat of the night, going 2-for-3 with 2 RBIs and a run scored. The 22-year-old rookie set a World Series rookie record with six straight hits during the series before a sacrifice fly in the sixth inning of Game 5. William Contreras drove in the other Milwaukee run, and Christian Yelich scored after reaching base.

Jacob Misiorowski, the Game 5 starter, held Tampa scoreless for 5.2 innings on just 2 hits before leaving with a right calf cramp — and a standing ovation from the Brewers fans who had taken over the section behind Milwaukee's dugout for the last three games. Trevor Megill closed it out with 2 innings and 3 strikeouts, giving up the Rays' only run. Chandler Simpson drove in Tampa's run.

Jackson Chourio, whose 10 hits through the first four games tied Babe Ruth's mark for the most through four games of a Fall Classic, went 1-for-4 in the clincher to finish with 11 hits — a five-game Series record.

How the series went

• Game 1 (Oct 23): Brewers 3, Rays 2

• Game 2 (Oct 24): Brewers 5, Rays 4

• Game 3 (Oct 26): Brewers 10, Rays 1

• Game 4 (Oct 27): Rays 4, Brewers 3 (12 innings)

• Game 5 (Oct 28): Brewers 4, Rays 1

Milwaukee took the first three, Tampa staved off elimination with a 12-inning walk-off win in Game 4, and the Brewers finished it one night later.

What it means

Start with the numbers, because they are silly: the smallest market in baseball won an MLB-best and franchise-record 103 games, went 11-2 in the postseason, and finished with 114 wins overall. No longer can anyone say the last baseball title in Milwaukee belongs to the 1957 Braves, or that the franchise that began as the Seattle Pilots is without a championship.

Then there is Pat Murphy. The 67-year-old manager survived a heart attack in 2020 and was the NL Manager of the Year in his first two full seasons at the helm, in 2024 and 2025. Before the champagne started flying in the clubhouse, he told his team: "You boys oughta be awfully proud to know that you're the first ever Milwaukee Brewers team to be World Champs." Hard to argue with that.

And one more presence hung over the whole night. Bob Uecker called Brewers games for over 50 years and died in January 2025 at 90. He was on the minds of fans, coaches and players as the Brewers closed it out, and FOX put together a tribute soundtracking his iconic voice with 2026 Brewers action. As the FOX intro put it: as Ueck looks down from above, proud of his boys, what would this title run have sounded like in his words?

The Brewers, who lost their only previous World Series trip in 1982, are champions. The parade planning can finally start. Yes, there will be brats.
