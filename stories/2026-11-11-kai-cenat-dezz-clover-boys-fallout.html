Kai Cenat says he's 'genuinely terrified of human beings' as the Clover Boys fallout spills over

By Matt

Dezz says Kai Cenat cut off the Clover Boys. Kai answered on his GTA marathon stream — then admitted on camera that he's 'so scared to meet new people.' Here's what we actually know.

What happened

The Clover Boys are the group Kai Cenat built — Dezz, Tylil, Punga, RaKai, Ray and Reggie, a streaming circle that goes back to college. This week, one of its founders said it was falling apart in public.

Dezz hosted a TikTok livestream on November 9 and accused Kai of cutting off communication with him and the other Clover Boys streamers. His line, as it made the rounds: Kai "don't speak to none of us gang." He also said Kaiya Cenat — Kai's sister — had stopped talking to him as well.

Kai addressed it the same day, during his GTA marathon stream. He was not having it. He told his chat he was "tired of the bull**" and that people should "leave him alone" — the kind of answer that says more by what it does not say than by what it does.

Then, on November 10, a different clip started circulating — shared by X user @scubaryan_ — where Kai talked about why he pulls back from people at all. "I'm genuinely terrified of human beings," he said. "I'm so scared to meet new people." It was not a response to Dezz, exactly. It was the context underneath the whole thing.

Why this one stings

Kai and Dezz go back further than most people in this scene. They met in college, and Dezz helped produce Kai's early videos — he was there before the fame, before the numbers, before the marathon streams. The Clover Boys were supposed to be the answer to the lonely-at-the-top problem: a group that grew together instead of one star outgrowing everyone around him.

But the fractures have been visible for a while. Reggie distanced himself from the group in September, and Kai said in October that he has invested $3.7 million into the Clover Boys. When the money and the distance both get that big, a group of friends starts to look like a business — and businesses have arguments that friendships do not survive.

What we don't know

The important part: neither Kai nor Kaiya has confirmed Dezz's account of being cut off, and the full picture is murkier than one livestream makes it sound. Kai streamed with Ray in Japan recently, which cuts against the idea that he has shut everyone out. The "cut off everyone" claim, in other words, is one person's telling of it — and Kai's own words on stream read less like a denial of the distance and more like an explanation of why it exists.

This is not a story with a villain. It is a story about a streamer who got famous faster than anyone in his circle expected, and a group that is finding out what that does to a friendship.
