GTA 6 tops the Japan charts — and hands PS5 a rare win over Switch 2

By Matt

GTA 6 sold more than 200,000 physical copies in its first week in Japan — and the launch pushed PlayStation 5 past Nintendo's Switch 2 in hardware sales, something that almost never happens there.

Grand Theft Auto 6 didn't just conquer the US and UK at launch — it did it in Japan too, in a market where Western games and PlayStation hardware usually play second fiddle to Nintendo. Famitsu's charts for the week of November 16–22 tell the story: GTA 6 debuted at No. 1, and the PS5 outsold the Switch 2.

The game sold 200,759 physical copies in its first week in Japan, debuting at the top of the software chart and finishing more than five times ahead of the No. 2 game, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time on Switch 2, at 35,295. It was the only new release to crack the top 10.

A rare hardware win

The bigger shock is on the hardware side. The PlayStation 5 family sold 41,472 units for the week, against 36,817 for the Switch 2 — a PS5 win in a market where Nintendo's machine dominates nearly every week. The PS5 Digital Edition did almost all of the damage on its own, moving 40,362 units, with the PS5 Pro adding 1,031 and the standard PS5 just 79.

For context, the week before, the Switch 2 sold 42,402 units while the PS5 Digital Edition managed only 10,448. GTA 6 flipped the chart in seven days — and, as Push Square put it, this is an incredibly rare scenario that likely won't be repeated again soon.

The Japan caveat

The number comes with a wrinkle: 200,759 physical copies is actually below the Japanese debuts of earlier GTA games — GTA 5 opened with 304,752 in 2013, Vice City with 223,933 in 2004, and San Andreas with 209,974 in 2007. But the comparison isn't clean. Buying habits have shifted hard toward digital in 13 years, and GTA 6's physical release is a code in a box, not a disc — which makes the retail number a floor, not the full picture.

The broader point stands: analysts had questioned before launch whether GTA 6 would land in Japan at all, with one telling Reuters the franchise was a niche pursuit there and the trends didn't fit. Instead it debuted at No. 1, moved the hardware chart, and did it in a week when the game was also lifting console sales in the US and topping the physical charts in the UK.

Launch week keeps finding new ways to be historic. This one just happened in the market everyone said it wouldn't.
