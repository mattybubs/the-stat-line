GTA 6 runs better on Xbox Series X than base PS5 — Digital Foundry's verdict flips Sony's marketing

By Matt

Digital Foundry's full console comparison is in, and the verdict is awkward for Sony: GTA 6 holds its frame rate better on Xbox Series X than on the base PS5 — the console Rockstar's marketing says it plays best on. The PS5 Pro is still the best way to play, and the Series S is the one to avoid.

Sony paid to make the PlayStation 5 the face of GTA 6. Digital Foundry just tested all four consoles — and found the game runs better on Xbox.

The tech outlet's full cross-platform comparison, published Tuesday, ranks the consoles like this: PS5 Pro first, Xbox Series X second, base PS5 third, and Xbox Series S last. The surprise is in the middle of that list. On the two base machines, which render the same image at the same internal resolution with matched settings, Microsoft's box holds the target frame rate more reliably than Sony's — and not by a little.

What Digital Foundry actually found

Both the base PS5 and the Series X render GTA 6 internally at 1440p, upscale to 4K with temporal anti-aliasing, and target 30 frames per second, with the same ray-traced lighting and reflections. In ordinary play both hit the target. The split opens in the heavy scenes — fast driving through packed traffic, the crowded airport sequence, big explosions — where frame rates fall into the mid-to-upper 20s and, in the worst moments, toward 20.

The PS5 falls off that cliff substantially more often than the Series X, Digital Foundry found, matching the same sequences on both machines and watching the PlayStation drop frames where the Xbox held on. The gap widens further because the Series X supports 120Hz VRR, which smooths out the dips that do happen; the base PS5 has no such option, so every stutter lands raw. Digital Foundry's summary is blunt: "Series X is meaningfully better off."

The outlet puts it down to hardware. The Series X GPU carries about 44% more compute units than the PS5's, and while the PlayStation chip runs at a higher clock speed, it can't fully compensate in a game pushing this much detail. It's the rare case where the two consoles' usual near-parity breaks in Microsoft's favor.

The Pro is still king — and the Series S is the one to avoid

None of this dethrones the PS5 Pro. It takes the same 1440p base image and rebuilds it into a proper 4K picture with Sony's PSSR upscaler, adds ray-traced shadows for the sun and local lights, and holds 30 frames per second more steadily than anything else. It is, by Digital Foundry's judgment, the definitive console version of the game.

The catch is that even the Pro is locked to 30 frames per second. CPU limits rule out 40 or 60 fps modes, and the Pro doesn't support 120Hz or VRR either. If you want a higher frame rate, no console will give you one — that's a story for the still-unannounced PC version.

At the bottom sits the Xbox Series S. It renders at 720p internally, upscaled to 1080p with FSR, keeps the ray-traced lighting but with lower-resolution shadows and softer clouds, drops below the frame-rate target frequently in vehicle travel, and suffers a bug where the audio sporadically cuts out entirely. It does support 120Hz VRR, which helps, but it's the weakest way to play the biggest game of the year.

The awkward part for Sony

Rockstar signed a marketing agreement with Sony, and the game's own official page carries a "Plays Best On PlayStation 5" badge. That slogan is technically defensible if you count the Pro — the most expensive PlayStation is indeed the best console for the game. But for the standard PS5, the console most of the 39 million-plus players who jumped in over launch weekend are actually using, the claim doesn't survive contact with the testing. The base PlayStation is third out of four.

None of this changes what the game is — the biggest entertainment launch ever, with more than 39 million players on PS5 alone in its first four days. But if you're choosing where to play it and you don't own a Pro, the answer Digital Foundry gives is not the one on the poster.
