Saints waive starting safety Justin Reid at his request

By Matt

The Saints granted captain Justin Reid's request for his release on Friday, ending a two-season stint in New Orleans and handing the starting safety job to Jordan Howden.

The move

The New Orleans Saints officially waived starting safety Justin Reid on Friday afternoon, granting a release the veteran had requested earlier in the week. Head coach Kellen Moore confirmed the move after Reid posted a goodbye message on social media, thanking the city and the team.

"I love Justin, I felt like this was the best for everyone," Moore said.

Reid, 29, is a defensive captain and a nine-year veteran. His farewell was gracious and brief: "In life and sports, things don't always go the way you planned or hoped they would. Unfortunately this is one of those moments."

Why now

The reason, by all accounts, is playing time. Reid played nearly every defensive snap early in the season, but his share dropped over the past three games as the Saints began turning to Jordan Howden at safety. Howden, 26, has earned those opportunities, in Moore's words, and he will start when the 3-6 Saints visit the 7-2 Chicago Bears on Sunday.

Moore declined to say whether Reid's request came because New Orleans was planning to replace him in the lineup. He did not have to. Reid had not practiced Wednesday or Thursday, listed on the injury report for what the team termed a personal matter, and he was still listed as the starter on the Saints' official depth chart on Friday morning. By Friday afternoon, he was gone.

Reid's Saints tenure never matched the heights of the resume he arrived with. He was a key contributor on the Chiefs' Super Bowl LVII and LVIII championship teams and started the Super Bowl LIX loss to the Eagles. In New Orleans, he started all 24 games he played and posted 118 tackles with one interception — one takeaway in two seasons for a player brought in to make plays. A concussion in the October 25 game in Paris seemed to mark the turn; his snap share declined afterward and never came back.

The money

Because the release came after the trade deadline, Reid goes through waivers, which clear at 4 p.m. ET on Monday. Any claiming team would inherit roughly the remaining $578,000 of his 2026 salary, plus a non-guaranteed $9.25 million option year for 2027. The Saints absorb about $5.2 million in dead cap this season and $13.26 million in 2027.

If nobody claims him, he becomes a free agent on Monday afternoon — and a two-time Super Bowl champion safety with starting experience should not stay unemployed long.

What's next

For New Orleans, this is the second time in two seasons a team captain has asked out. Wide receiver Brandin Cooks was granted his release a year ago under similar circumstances. That is a pattern, and it is not a flattering one for a 3-6 team already looking toward next year.

For Reid, the ending is abrupt but not bitter, at least publicly. Moore kept the conversations private, praised Reid as a player and a person, and framed the split as mutual. Reid thanked the organization and the city. Everybody said the right things. The football truth is simpler: the Saints decided Jordan Howden was their safety of the future, and Reid decided he would rather find his future somewhere else.
