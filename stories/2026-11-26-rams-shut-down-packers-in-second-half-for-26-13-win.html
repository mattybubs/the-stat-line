Rams shut down Packers in second half for 26-13 win

By Matt

Blake Corum ran for a career-high 184 yards on his 26th birthday as the Rams shut out Green Bay after halftime, winning 26-13 in the NFL's first Thanksgiving Eve game for their fifth straight victory.

Blake Corum ran for a career-high 184 yards and a touchdown as the Los Angeles Rams beat the Green Bay Packers 26-13 on Wednesday night at SoFi Stadium, shutting Green Bay out after halftime in the NFL's first-ever Thanksgiving Eve game.

The win was the Rams' fifth in a row, lifting them to 7-4 and ahead of the Packers in the NFC wild-card race. Green Bay fell to 6-5 with its second straight loss and now sits outside the playoff picture with six games to play.

A tie at halftime, then all Rams

The first half was even. Brandon McManus kicked two first-quarter field goals for a 6-0 Rams lead, and after Trey Smack answered from 54 yards, Kyren Williams' 7-yard touchdown run made it 13-3 midway through the second. Green Bay fought back to 13-13 by halftime on Christian Watson's 6-yard touchdown catch and another Smack field goal.

The second half belonged entirely to Los Angeles. The Rams opened the third quarter with a 15-play, 68-yard drive, capped by Corum's 1-yard touchdown run, and the Packers never scored again. McManus added field goals of 40 and 30 yards in the fourth to close it out.

Corum's career night

Corum carried 32 times — the most by a Rams running back in the Sean McVay era and the most by any player in the NFL this season. He did most of the damage after the break, with 25 carries for 161 yards in the second half alone, and added two catches for 21 yards.

The touchdown was his seventh straight game with a score. It also came on his 26th birthday, and his yardage total was the second-most ever by a player on his birthday in NFL history.

The ground game as a whole was overwhelming: the Rams ran 47 times for 241 yards and held the ball for 40:40, more than double Green Bay's 19:20. The Packers managed just 244 yards of total offense.

The playoff picture

The win was also McVay's first over his former assistant Matt LaFleur in six meetings, and it vaulted the Rams ahead of Green Bay in the race for the NFC's final wild-card spot. Thursday's Lions-Bears game will shape the race further for all four teams involved.

Green Bay did get star edge rusher Micah Parsons back — he recorded a sack in his first game of the season — but the Rams made sure he wasn't much of a factor, and Jordan Love's late interception to Emmanuel Forbes with 3:39 left ended any comeback hopes. Love finished 17 of 29 for 198 yards with one touchdown and two picks, while Matthew Stafford went 16 of 23 for 140 yards with one interception.

The Packers also lost Watson to a concussion late in the second quarter, on a hit that got Rams safety Kamren Kinchens ejected. They now get extended time off before visiting New Orleans on December 6, while the Rams turn around quickly for a Thursday night home game against the Chiefs in Week 13.
