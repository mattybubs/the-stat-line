Rockstar explains why GTA 6 judges how you commit crimes — the Criminal Profile, from Professional to Irredeemable

By Matt

Rockstar's Rob Nelson says GTA 6's Criminal Profile grew out of Red Dead Redemption 2 — five tiers that track whether you're a clean professional or a permanent Irredeemable, and change how the cops and your Focus meter treat you.

GTA 6 doesn't just count your crimes. It judges how you commit them. That's the Criminal Profile — a system that follows Jason and Lucia separately across Leonida, grading every robbery, every takedown, every body you leave behind, and sorting each of them into one of five tiers: Professional, Hardened, Violent, Psycho, and Irredeemable. Now Rockstar has explained why it's there, and the answer starts with Red Dead Redemption 2.

Rob Nelson, Rockstar North's co-studio head and head of development, told Eurogamer the studio wanted what players do in the open world to line up with how Jason and Lucia are portrayed in the story. The team was aiming to "build up the sophistication of the NPC population, and also allow for more player choice" — and to add "more options in terms of the way you interact with the world beyond just violence."

The five tiers

The profile is a long-term record of how you consistently approach crime, not a simple body count. A clean job — mask on, alarm disabled, hostages alive, clean getaway — pushes a character toward Professional. Using force that still spares lives lands around Hardened. Harming civilians, killing cops, and finishing off downed enemies slides toward Violent. Sustained, extreme violence earns Psycho. And if you stay there long enough, you hit Irredeemable — the point of no return. There is no natural way back from it; the only fix is reverting to an earlier save.

The tiers aren't just a label on a menu screen. They change how aggressively the police respond to you, and they change what refills your Focus meter — the slow-motion aim that matters in every gunfight. Professional and Hardened refill it through stealth and non-lethal takedowns. Violent and Psycho refill it through headshots. Irredeemable refills it with nothing.

One thing the system doesn't do: it doesn't punish you for the story. Violence inside main story missions doesn't count toward your profile, and neither does genuine self-defense. The profile is about what you choose to do when the game isn't telling you to do it.

Crime fights back now

Nelson also laid out the other half of the design: the world pushes back. In GTA 6, the person you're robbing might not just hand over the cash.

"It's still a world of violence, but you could threaten somebody by aiming a gun at them and just robbing them, and they have a chance to comply. So you don't have to beat them to death to get their money," Nelson explained. "What if, instead of whipping an RPG out of nowhere, you have to call your car. You have to pop a car trunk and get it, and now I'm walking, and now people are noticing, but they're sort of sidling away, and it's more believable."

Try to hold somebody up and they might pull a gun of their own, or grab yours, and then you have to deal with that. It's the same philosophy that ran through Red Dead Redemption 2's Honor system, taken further: crime in Leonida is supposed to feel dangerous and unpredictable, not free.

The reception has been mixed — some players love that their choices carry weight, others just want to cause chaos without a permanent record of it. There's currently no way to turn the system off, and Rockstar hasn't said whether that option is coming. But the intent is clear now: in GTA 6, the game remembers not just what you did, but how you did it. Play clean and the world treats you like a professional. Go on a rampage long enough, and it decides you're something else entirely — permanently.
