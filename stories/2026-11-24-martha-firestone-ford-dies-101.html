Martha Firestone Ford dies at 101 — the matriarch who held the Lions for six decades

By Matt

The Detroit Lions announced Tuesday night that owner and chair emeritus Martha Firestone Ford has died at 101 — two days before the 8-2 Lions host the Bears on Thanksgiving at Ford Field, the stadium she helped rebuild.

The news

The Detroit Lions announced Tuesday night that Martha Firestone Ford, the team's owner and chair emeritus, has died at her home in Grosse Pointe, Michigan, surrounded by her family. She was 101.

The team posted the announcement to its official website on Tuesday evening. The Associated Press, USA Today, NBC Sports and Fox News all confirmed the death within the hour. No cause of death was given.

She died two days before the Lions host the Chicago Bears on Thanksgiving at Ford Field — a game that will decide first place in the NFC North, with both teams at 8-2. She was still attending games this season, including a win over the Minnesota Vikings earlier this month.

The steward

Ford's connection to the Lions spanned more than six decades. In 1963, she and her husband, William Clay Ford, purchased full control of the franchise. When he died in 2014, she assumed sole control of the team, and in 2020 she transitioned to emeritus status, with her daughter Sheila Ford Hamp taking over as principal owner and chair.

Her six seasons as principal owner were the steadiest stretch the franchise had known in years. The Lions reached the playoffs twice under her watch, in 2014 and 2016, and in 2017 she made a significant investment in Ford Field that the team credits as the foundation of the gameday environment Lions fans enjoy today.

The family succession is already in place. Her four children — Sheila Ford Hamp, Martha Ford Morse, William Clay Ford Jr. and Elizabeth Ford Kontulis — all hold ownership roles with the franchise, and the team said Tuesday that the family "proudly carries forward the legacy established by Mr. and Mrs. Ford."

The life

She was born Martha Parke Firestone in Akron, Ohio, on September 16, 1925 — the granddaughter of Harvey S. Firestone, founder of the Firestone Tire and Rubber Company. She graduated from Vassar College in 1946 and married William Clay Ford the following year. The couple moved to the Detroit area in 1949 and became fixtures of the city for the next 77 years.

Her work outside football was quiet and constant. She expanded Detroit Lions Charities, served as a longtime trustee of Henry Ford Health, held leadership roles with the Merrill Palmer Skillman Institute, University Liggett School, the Children's Home of Detroit and the Edsel and Eleanor Ford House, and founded the Henry Ford Maplegrove Center. In January 2019 she helped launch Detroit Lions Inspire Change, the team's social justice initiative in the city.

In March 2019, as part of the NFL's 100th-season celebration, she starred in the NFL Films documentary A Lifetime of Sundays alongside the league's other matriarchs — Virginia Halas McCaskey of the Bears, Patricia Rooney of the Steelers and Norma Hunt of the Chiefs.

What it means

Ford never got to see the Lions reach a Super Bowl — the one milestone that eluded the franchise across her entire tenure, through the lean years and into the current revival. She did live to see the turnaround: the team she handed to her daughter is 8-2, contending for the NFC's top seed, and playing its biggest Thanksgiving game in years on Thursday.

She is survived by her four children, 14 grandchildren and 17 great-grandchildren.
