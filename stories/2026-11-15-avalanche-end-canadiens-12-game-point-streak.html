The Avalanche are missing MacKinnon and Makar — and they just ended Montreal's 12-game point streak anyway

By Matt

No Nathan MacKinnon. No Cale Makar. No problem. The Avalanche walked into Bell Centre and beat the Canadiens 4-1, ending Montreal's 12-game point streak and handing the Habs their first regulation loss since October 14.

The night

The Colorado Avalanche showed up in Montreal on Saturday night without their two best players — and handed the Canadiens their worst result in a month.

Colorado beat Montreal 4-1 at Bell Centre, ending the Canadiens' 12-game point streak (9-0-3) and handing them their first regulation loss since October 14, a 4-3 defeat at Washington. The Avs did it without Nathan MacKinnon, out at least a month, and Cale Makar, out roughly two weeks — both sidelined with lower-body injuries suffered in the second period of Thursday's 4-3 overtime loss to Ottawa.

How it happened

The Avalanche's depth did the work. Matvei Svechkov opened the scoring at 3:57 of the first, Brock Nelson — who took MacKinnon's place on the top line — added a goal, and Artturi Lehkonen scored his team-leading 15th of the season (a power-play goal on his own rebound at 4:14 of the third, his third straight game with a goal) to make it 3-1. Nicolas Roy's empty-netter finished it off.

Mackenzie Blackwood made 25 saves behind them, and the Avs stretched their own point streak to five games (3-0-2) at 12-6-3. Colorado's next-man-up act extended to the blue line too: Sam Malinski stepped onto the top pair alongside Devon Toews and logged 24:19 with an assist.

Montreal's lone goal was worth the price of admission — captain Nick Suzuki beat Blackwood with a spin-o-rama at 10:25 of the second. But the Canadiens, already short-handed (Chris Kreider is out six to eight weeks with a lower-body injury suffered Thursday against Boston), couldn't solve Blackwood again. Jakub Dobes made 22 saves in the loss.

What it means

A point streak is a fragile thing — 12 games of never leaving the rink empty-handed, undone in one night by a team missing its two best players. Montreal (12-4-4) still has plenty of cushion, and the Habs get a quick chance to start a new one: they visit the Rangers on Monday night. Colorado heads to Utah on Wednesday, still without its two stars, still winning.
