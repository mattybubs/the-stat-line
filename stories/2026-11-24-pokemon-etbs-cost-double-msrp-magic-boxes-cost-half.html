Pokémon ETBs cost double MSRP; Magic boxes cost half

By Matt

NerdSnack's Black Friday price guide, updated November 24, puts 40 sealed products against MSRP — and the two biggest games point in opposite directions: Pokémon boxes run double list price while Magic booster boxes sell for about half of it.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

Black Friday is Friday, November 27, and this year's card market is split in two. NerdSnack's Black Friday guide, updated November 24, lists the price to beat for 40 Pokémon, Magic, Lorcana and One Piece products — and the two biggest games in the hobby point in opposite directions. Magic booster boxes already sell for about half their MSRP, while a Pokémon 30th Celebration Elite Trainer Box costs more than twice its list price.

The rule the guide uses is simple: a real deal beats two numbers, the product's MSRP and its current TCGplayer market price. If a sale price only beats a crossed-out "list price" the seller chose, it isn't a deal.

The split at a glance

MTG Marvel Super Heroes Play Booster box: $209.70; $102.64; 51% under MSRP

MTG Star Trek Play Booster box: $209.70; $110.82; 47% under MSRP

Lorcana Hyperia City booster box: $143.76; $128.45; 11% under MSRP

Pokémon Delta Reign Elite Trainer Box: $49.99; $74.95; 50% over MSRP

Pokémon 30th Celebration Elite Trainer Box: $49.99; $113.99; 2.3× MSRP

One Piece OP-18 booster box: $119.76; $180.88; 51% over MSRP

Pokémon: the premium side

The 30th Celebration set is this year's big Pokémon gift release, and it's the hardest to find at list price. The ladder runs from a roughly $10 Mini Tin — which still sells for $20.88 to $25.16 — up through a $66.27 Booster Bundle and the standard Elite Trainer Box. The Pokémon Center version of that box is the steepest climb of all: $249.93 against a $59.99 MSRP.

Delta Reign, which came out November 6, is the newest set and the most reasonable of the current Pokémon releases. Its standard ETB sits at $74.95, and the guide's advice is to hold out for an MSRP restock rather than pay the marketplace price — the Pokémon Center, Target and Best Buy all sell at MSRP when stock drops. Older main sets have settled closer to list: Pitch Black, Chaos Rising and Perfect Order booster boxes now sit only 6–9% above MSRP, so a Black Friday box under about $165 is a strong price. The outlier is Phantasmal Flames, whose ETB is $138.02 — nearly three times MSRP almost a year after release.

A second guide, Tabletop Meta's Pokémon Black Friday roundup, tells the same story from a different snapshot: on November 18, every recent set's Elite Trainer Box and Booster Bundle sold above MSRP on TCGplayer, with the 30th Celebration box at $110.58.

Magic: the bargain side

Magic is the opposite of Pokémon this year. Most sealed product already sells below MSRP, and even Star Trek — which came out November 13 — trades well under it. The Marvel Super Heroes Play Booster box is the cheapest box in the guide at $102.64, about half its list price, and the Star Trek Play Booster box has kept falling since launch, from $132.48 on October 22 to $110.82.

The one Magic exception is the Star Trek Beam Me Up Bundle, which still sells above its $99.99 MSRP at $152.73. A Star Trek Commander deck, at $32–$73 against a $74.99 MSRP, is a ready-to-play gift for a Commander player.

Lorcana and One Piece

Lorcana's newest set, Hyperia City (out October 16), is a rare case where the booster box sells below MSRP. The gift products go the other way: the Beast Gift Box sells for $66.59 against a $29.99 list price, more than double. The guide's advice is to look for it at big-box stores before paying marketplace prices.

One Piece is the priciest side of the market. Boosters list at $4.99 a pack, so a 24-pack box costs $119.76 at MSRP — but every recent box trades far above that, from OP-18 at $180.88 up to OP-17 at $266.12. A box at or near MSRP usually means a local game store on release day, not a Black Friday sale.

How to tell a real deal from a fake one

Presale prices are the biggest trap in the guide. When it was first published on October 22, a Delta Reign Elite Trainer Box cost $106.29 on presale; today it's $74.95, about 29% less. An OP-18 booster box dropped from $353.77 to $180.88 once it was in stores. The same warning applies to what's coming next: Lorcana's Into the Inkdark booster box already lists at $294.05 on TCGplayer, about double MSRP, more than two months before its February 2027 release.

TCGplayer itself hasn't announced a 2026 sale yet, as of November 24. Last year it ran a four-day "Cyber Weekend" with 10% back in store credit on everything, and subscribers got 13% or more. If the timing holds, the 2026 sale would run from Friday morning, November 27, to Monday night, November 30 — but store credit only saves money on a later TCGplayer order, so it's worth the most if you'll buy again before Christmas.

Three big card releases landed in the past three weeks — Delta Reign on November 6, MTG Star Trek on November 13, and One Piece OP-18 on November 20 — and all three are now past launch day, with prices cooling since presale. That's the window Black Friday sits in: new enough that stock exists, old enough that the launch-day markup has started to fade. The numbers above are the line to beat.
