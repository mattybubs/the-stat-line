McDavid Does Something No One's Ever Done Faster: Fastest to 20 Assists in NHL History

By Matt

Connor McDavid dished his 20th assist in just his 9th game — the fastest any player has ever reached 20 helpers in a season — capping a 4-3 OT win over Minnesota with the primary assist on Evan Bouchard's winner.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

Connor McDavid is rewriting the record book in real time. On Tuesday night, the Oilers captain became the fastest player in NHL history to reach 20 assists in a season, doing it in just 9 games — and he did it by setting up the overtime winner in Edmonton's 4-3 win over the Minnesota Wild.

The record

McDavid recorded 3 assists against Minnesota, with No. 20 coming on Evan Bouchard's power-play goal nearly four minutes into overtime at Rogers Place. Per NHL Public Relations: "Connor McDavid (6-20—26 in 9 GP) required the fewest games in NHL history to reach 20 assists in a single season."

The previous record — 10 games — was shared by three of the greatest playmakers ever:

• Pat LaFontaine (1992-93)

• Mario Lemieux (1988-89)

• Wayne Gretzky (1986-87)

McDavid beat all three of them by a full game. It was also his 66th career three-assist game, moving him to sixth all-time in that category behind Gretzky (224), Lemieux (108), Paul Coffey (82), Adam Oates (71) and Sidney Crosby (67).

The game

Edmonton's 4-3 OT win had a little of everything:

• McDavid's first two assists came on Leon Draisaitl power-play goals — including a no-look, cross-ice feed that froze Minnesota's penalty kill.

• Bouchard's OT winner came on his 27th birthday, roofing one past Jesper Wallstedt after a faceoff.

• Devon Levi made 27 saves for the win.

• The Oilers went 3-for-5 on the power play and killed all three Wild chances.

Edmonton is now 7-1-1, winners of seven of its last eight, sitting second in the Pacific behind the 8-2-0 Golden Knights.

The pace is absurd

McDavid sits at 6 goals, 20 assists, 26 points through 9 games — the league lead by a mile. That projects to roughly 242 points over the 84-game season, which would shatter Gretzky's single-season record of 215 (1985-86). His career high is 153 (2022-23).

It's October. Nobody is penciling in 242. But the fact that the math even works — that McDavid could be held pointless in his next game and still be tracking 215 — tells you everything about this start.

For reference, McDavid's career line now reads 415 goals, 831 assists, 1,246 points in 803 games across nine 100-point seasons. He's 29. This is his 12th NHL season, and he's somehow still finding new gears.

Next up: the Carolina Hurricanes visit Edmonton on October 22.
