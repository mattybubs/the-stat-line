GTA 6 sold an estimated 53 million copies in its first week — $4.7 billion, and it's already the 13th best-selling game ever

By Matt

Alinea Analytics estimates Grand Theft Auto 6 sold 53 million copies in its first week on sale, for roughly $4.7 billion in revenue — a week-one total that would already put it 13th on the all-time best-sellers list, before GTA Online or a PC version even exist.

One week in, Grand Theft Auto 6 has sold an estimated 53 million copies. That's the week-one figure from Alinea Analytics, published Friday, November 27, and it works out to roughly $4.7 billion in revenue — from a single-player game, on two consoles, with no PC version and no online mode yet.

The estimate is not an official number from Rockstar or Take-Two, and it matters to say that plainly: the only figures the publisher has confirmed are $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales and 32.5 million units sold before release. Alinea's number is an independent estimate of sell-through to players, built from its own market data. But it is consistent with everything else we've seen this week — the same firm's estimate through the launch weekend was 44.9 million, and the jump to 53 million means roughly 8 million more copies moved in the four days after that.

The PlayStation split, and why it matters

Of the estimated copies sold, 75.7% were on PlayStation 5 — about 40.1 million — with the remaining 24.3%, roughly 13 million, on Xbox Series X|S. The revenue split tilts even further toward Sony, with PS5 accounting for 76.1% of the money, a gap Alinea attributes to regional pricing.

That is the payoff of Rockstar's PlayStation marketing deal, which positioned GTA 6 as playing best on Sony's console. PlayStation does hold roughly 75% of the console market, so the split is close to proportional — but the deal still delivered, and Sony has already struck a similar arrangement with CD Projekt Red for future releases, including The Witcher 4, according to Polygon.

The Xbox share has been shrinking steadily since launch day, from 25.8% on November 21 to 24.3% now.

The comparison that puts it in perspective

GTA 5 sold 29 million copies in its entire first month. GTA 6 nearly doubled that in seven days. GTA 5 has since gone on to sell more than 230 million copies across its lifetime — a number that took thirteen years and three console generations to reach. At this pace, the conversation about whether GTA 6 catches it is a question of when, not if.

On the all-time best-sellers list, the week-one estimate would already place GTA 6 13th, ahead of games that have been selling for a decade or more.

People aren't just buying it — they're living in it

The same Alinea report found nearly 7 million players have already logged more than 50 hours in the game, with around 250,000 past 100 hours. The game has been out for eight days.

The launch also swallowed the rest of the industry whole. On PlayStation, sales of several other major releases — including 007 First Light, Resident Evil Requiem and Marvel's Wolverine — fell 34% during GTA 6's release week, though Alinea notes typical post-launch sales decay accounts for part of that drop. Call of Duty's daily active users fell 42% around the launch, from an average of 7.5 million to 4.4 million.

What comes next

None of this includes GTA Online, which hasn't launched yet, or a PC version, which Rockstar still hasn't announced. The unofficial PC port scene keeps making headlines — the latest is a 209GB torrent repack of the leaked Xbox build — but there's no official word on when PC players get in. Whenever those two things arrive, they'll be selling into a player base that is already tens of millions strong.

For now, the first week is done — and the next chapter, Online and PC, hasn't even started.
