Call of Duty lost 42% of its daily players to GTA 6 — and four days later, it still hasn't come back

By Matt

Alinea Analytics estimates the Call of Duty app's daily players fell from 7.6 million to 4.4 million during GTA 6's launch weekend — PlayStation took the worst of it, and the lobbies still haven't filled back up.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

GTA 6 did not just break sales records on its launch weekend. It emptied the lobbies of the biggest shooter on the planet. New estimates from Alinea Analytics show Call of Duty losing nearly half of its daily players the moment Rockstar opened the gates of Leonida — and the players have not come back yet.

The weekend by the numbers

Call of Duty HQ daily active users, pre-launch baseline: 7.6 million (est.)

Call of Duty HQ daily active users, launch weekend: 4.4 million (est.)

Drop in daily active users: 42% (est.)

Drop on PlayStation 5: 44% (est.)

Still below baseline four days later: 41% (est.)

Fortnite daily active users, same weekend: Down 28%, 17.8M to 12.8M (est.)

The figures come from Alinea Analytics' Rhys Elliott, as reported by IGN, WCCFTech and Pro-Gamer. One important caveat runs through all of them: these are third-party estimates, not official numbers. Activision has published no player figures for Modern Warfare 4 at any point since launch, and Alinea does not publish its method — so read the direction and the rough size as solid, and the decimals as approximate. The "Call of Duty app" here is the whole HQ ecosystem — Modern Warfare 4, Warzone and Black Ops 7 behind one login — not a Modern Warfare 4 multiplayer figure on its own.

PlayStation took the worst of it

The drop was not spread evenly. PlayStation 5, Call of Duty's biggest platform, saw the steepest fall of any ecosystem. That matters because PlayStation is also where GTA 6 sold hardest — roughly three-quarters of the game's launch-weekend copies moved on PS5, and about the same share of Sunday's players logged in there. The overlap between the two audiences is the story: the same players who grind Call of Duty every night are the ones who disappeared into Leonida on Thursday.

Fortnite felt it too, losing more than a quarter of its daily players in the same estimates. EA FC 27 slipped as well, though it recovered faster than either shooter. Call of Duty got the worst of the weekend — and the slowest recovery.

The quote that didn't age well

Earlier this fall, Activision chief marketing officer Tyler Bahl said he believed GTA 6 would actually help Call of Duty — that the open-world epic would drive more attention toward gaming as a whole, and players would turn their attention to other titles once they were done with it. In the short term, the opposite happened. Four days after launch, Call of Duty was still sitting well below its pre-launch baseline, with no sign of the bounce Bahl predicted.

This is also the second straight year Call of Duty has fought a rival launch headwind. Battlefield 6 did the same damage in 2025. The difference now is the scale of what's pulling players away: a game that sold an estimated 44.9 million copies in four days and drew 40.8 million players on Sunday alone — numbers we covered earlier today — and one that still has its PC release and its online mode to come.

What decides whether they come back

The test is the calendar. The mid-season update is expected around December 2, and that is the first real chance for Call of Duty to pull its audience back with fresh content. The longer test is GTA 6 itself: the story campaign will eventually run its course for most players, and the question is whether they drift back to their old grind — or whether Rockstar's online mode, expected next year, lands right on top of Call of Duty's next annual cycle and does this all over again.
