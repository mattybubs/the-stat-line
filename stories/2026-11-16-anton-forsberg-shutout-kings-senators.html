Anton Forsberg blanks his old team — 17 saves, 1-0 — as the Kings win their fourth straight in Ottawa

By Matt

Anton Forsberg stopped all 17 shots he faced in his return to Ottawa — his first shutout of the season and ninth of his career — as Los Angeles blanked the Senators 1-0 for its fourth straight win.

The night

Anton Forsberg spent parts of five seasons in Ottawa. On Saturday night, he went back to Canadian Tire Centre wearing a different jersey and made sure his old team remembered exactly what he can do.

Forsberg made 17 saves for the Los Angeles Kings in a 1-0 win over the Senators — his first shutout of the season, the ninth of his NHL career, and a win against the franchise he played for from 2020 to 2025. He improved his career record to 3-0-0 against his former club, and he preserved the shutout in style, stopping Ridly Greig on a breakaway with 11:21 left in the third.

Alex Laferriere scored the game's only goal, tipping a Mikey Anderson point shot at 9:19 of the first period. Linus Ullmark made 17 saves at the other end for Ottawa. That was the entire scoring line. One goal, 34 combined saves, and a goaltender who spent five seasons learning that building deciding he wasn't going to let anything in.

The road Kings

The win was Los Angeles' fourth in a row and its ninth road win of the season — the most in the NHL — and the sixth straight win on the road this fall. The Kings improved to 10-5-4 and moved into first place in the Pacific Division for the first time this season, opening a six-game Eastern road trip with four straight wins.

Ottawa, meanwhile, saw its seven-game point streak (4-0-3) come to an end, falling to 9-6-4. Laferriere extended his point streak to four games, and the Kings' second shutout win in as many weeks against the Senators in Ottawa underscored how comfortable this team is playing away from home.

The win came at a cost: top defenseman Drew Doughty left the game in the second period after blocking a shot, a loss the Kings will be watching closely as the trip continues to Washington.
