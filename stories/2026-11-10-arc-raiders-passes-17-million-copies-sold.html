ARC Raiders passes 17 million copies sold

By Matt

ARC Raiders has sold more than 17 million copies, Nexon said in its third-quarter earnings release — up from 16.3 million at the end of June, with the October Frozen Trail update pulling lapsed players back a full year after launch.

ARC Raiders has sold more than 17 million copies. Nexon put the total in its third-quarter earnings release on November 10, 2026, and credited the October Frozen Trail update for pulling lapsed players back a full year after launch.

The numbers

Copies sold to date: More than 17 million

Copies sold at the end of June 2026: 16.3 million

Copies sold by May 2026: 16 million

Matches played in year one: Six billion

Average raids per day in year one: 16 million

Frozen Trail did the work

Nexon's earnings letter calls Frozen Trail, released by Embark Studios on October 8, "the largest update since the 2025 launch" — and says it was "successful in re-engaging lapsed players and driving incremental unit sales." That is the story behind the new total: the game did not just hold its audience, it won back players who had drifted away and sold new copies to players coming in fresh.

The pace tells the rest of it. The gap between the May figure and this one is roughly a million copies over about six months, after the launch rush did the heavy lifting in the first seven. Nexon also said third-quarter revenue from the game came in within its outlook, and that it expects fourth-quarter revenue to stay broadly flat against the third quarter while falling year over year — an honest comparison, since the same quarter last year was launch season.

What a year looks like

The game launched October 30, 2025, on PC, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X|S. By its first anniversary it had six billion matches played — an average of 16 million raids a day — and Nexon said in August it had generated more than 88 billion yen since launch. It is a buy-to-play game, so every active player is a paying player, which makes the retention story matter more than it would for a free-to-play title: nobody is padding the numbers with free downloads.

A year after launch, the question for an extraction shooter is never whether the launch was big. It is whether anyone is still raiding. Frozen Trail answered that, and the sales total is the receipt.
