Jamie Benn's 1,000th point lifts the Stars past the Sabres — and Oettinger's shutout is the bounce-back

By Matt

Jamie Benn became only the second player in Stars franchise history to record 1,000 points in a Dallas uniform, scoring the winner in a 2-0 blanking of Buffalo — two days after Jake Oettinger was pulled for allowing six goals.

The night

Some milestones arrive with fireworks. Jamie Benn's arrived the way most of his career has gone — in the middle of a scoreless grind, with a captain's goal, in a game his team had to win.

Benn scored late in the second period for the 1,000th point of his 18-year NHL career, and the Dallas Stars beat the Buffalo Sabres 2-0 on Saturday night at American Airlines Center. He joins Hall of Famer Mike Modano (1,359) as the only players in franchise history to record 1,000 points in a Stars uniform.

The goal itself was pure Benn: a snap through traffic with 3:37 left in the second to break a scoreless tie, his fourth goal of the season and third in his last seven games. Wyatt Johnston added a power-play insurance goal with 40 seconds left, on assists from Mikko Rantanen and Roope Hintz, and that was that.

The other half of the story

If Benn's milestone was the headline, the other end of the ice was the redemption. Jake Oettinger turned aside all 22 shots he faced for his fourth shutout of the season and the 20th of his career — two days after he was pulled for allowing six goals on 27 shots in an 8-3 loss to Winnipeg.

It had been a rough stretch for the Dallas netminder: 14 goals allowed in his past three starts before Saturday, and no shutout since Oct. 17, after he'd posted three of them in the team's first six games — including a 4-0 win against these same Sabres on Oct. 8. Saturday was the answer. He denied Noah Ostlund from point-blank range on a power play early in the third to preserve the 1-0 lead, and the Stars killed all five Buffalo power plays on the night.

Dallas outshot Buffalo 30-22, improved to 9-3-1 in its last 13 games, and completed a season sweep of the Sabres. Buffalo's winless streak hit four games, with Alex Lyon making 28 saves in the loss.

The company

The 1,000-point club in Dallas is now two men deep, and the gap between them tells you everything about what Benn has meant to this franchise. Modano's 1,359 points came across 21 seasons and a Stanley Cup. Benn has spent all 18 of his seasons in Dallas, wearing the C, and Saturday's goal put him in the one piece of company in franchise history that matters most.

He didn't make a fuss about it. That's also on brand. The goal came in a 2-0 win that Dallas needed after Thursday's embarrassment in Winnipeg, and the captain treated it like another night at the office — which, for him, it mostly was. The milestone will get its ceremony later. The two points mattered now.
