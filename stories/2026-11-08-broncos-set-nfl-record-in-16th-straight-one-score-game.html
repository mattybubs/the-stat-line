Broncos set NFL record in 16th straight one-score game

By Matt

Denver beat Carolina 26-21 in Charlotte on Sunday for its 16th straight one-score win — a new NFL record, passing Peyton Manning's 2008-09 Colts — behind a fourth-quarter rally and Nik Bonitto's game-sealing strip-sack.

The Denver Broncos just keep finding ways to win the close ones. Denver beat the Carolina Panthers 26-21 on Sunday at Bank of America Stadium in Charlotte, and the win was the Broncos' 16th straight in a one-score game — a new NFL record.

Denver is now 7-2, on a five-game winning streak, and alone in first place in the AFC West. Carolina fell to 3-5 with its third straight loss.

The record

One-score games are decided by eight points or fewer, and nobody in NFL history has survived more of them in a row than these Broncos. The previous mark was 15 straight, held by Peyton Manning's 2008-09 Indianapolis Colts — a team that went to a Super Bowl on the back of exactly that kind of late-game nerve.

Bo Nix has started all 16 games of the streak. Denver's last loss in a one-score game came in Week 3 of the 2025 season, a 23-20 defeat at the Chargers. Since then, the Broncos have won every close game they've played.

NBC Sports research noted before the game that Denver's run — counting last season's playoff overtime win over Buffalo — already tied the 2023-24 Kansas City Chiefs for the longest one-score win streak since 1950. Either way you count it, Sunday made the regular-season record Denver's alone.

What makes the streak stranger is how Denver keeps doing it. The Broncos have a minus-658 yardage differential on the season, third-worst in the NFL, per NBC Sports. They get outgained, they fall behind, and they still walk out with the win.

How the 16th one went down

Denver dominated the first quarter and then spent the rest of the afternoon digging itself out.

First quarter — Broncos 10, Panthers 0. Denver held the ball for almost 11 minutes. The opening drive stalled at the three after a delay-of-game penalty, and Wil Lutz opened the scoring with a 26-yard field goal. On the next drive, a 22-yard catch by Jaylen Waddle and a 20-yard screen to Evan Engram set up Bo Nix's three-yard touchdown pass to Courtland Sutton.

Second quarter — Broncos 13, Panthers 7. Nix's streak of 180 passes without an interception ended when Carolina cornerback Corey Thornton picked him off in the red zone — his first pick since Week 3. The Panthers turned it into points, converting a third-and-17 on a long march that ended with Bryce Young's touchdown pass to tight end Tommy Tremble. With 1:27 left and no timeouts, Denver still got Lutz a 48-yard field goal before halftime.

Third quarter — Panthers 14, Broncos 13. Carolina opened the half with a 65-yard drive capped by Young's 15-yard touchdown to tight end Darren Waller, and the Panthers took the lead. Then they marched from their own 12-yard line and looked ready to go up by eight — until Young's pass was tipped up and safety Devon Key snatched it in the end zone for a critical turnover.

Fourth quarter — Broncos 26, Panthers 21. Down one, Denver put together a 14-play, 80-yard drive. A third-and-six completion from Nix to Sutton got the Broncos to the 20, and running back Jonah Coleman capped it with a nine-yard touchdown run. The two-point try failed, leaving it 19-14. Jonathon Cooper's first sack of the season forced a three-and-out, and Nix found wide receiver Pat Bryant wide open for a 26-yard touchdown to make it 26-14.

Carolina wouldn't go away. The Panthers converted a fourth-and-18 to keep their final touchdown drive alive, and Young's four-yard pass to Tetairoa McMillan cut it to 26-21 with 3:05 left. Then Coleman bobbled a toss from Nix, lost 18 yards, and Denver had to punt from deep in its own territory just after the two-minute warning.

The Panthers took over just shy of midfield with a real shot at the comeback. On the second play, Nik Bonitto batted the ball out of Young's hand — a strip-sack, recovered by Bonitto — and that was the game. Denver picked up a first down, knelt out the clock, and the record was theirs.

The cost

The win wasn't free. Receiver Jaylen Waddle left late in the first quarter and cornerback Riley Moss left midway through the second, both to be evaluated for possible concussions, and both were ruled out at halftime. Key briefly exited in the fourth quarter to be evaluated but returned to the game. Denver's defense was already down three of its four starting defensive backs at times in this one, per Mile High Report, and it showed — Carolina moved the ball all afternoon.

What it means

The Broncos head into the bye in Week 10 at 7-2 and in first place in the AFC West, with the NFL trade deadline on Tuesday before they host the Las Vegas Raiders in Week 11. The record is the headline, but the pattern underneath it is the real story: this team does not lose close games anymore. In 2024, Denver was 1-6 in one-score games. Now it's 16 straight wins in them, and the record book belongs to Bo Nix and a defense that keeps producing one more play than the other team.
