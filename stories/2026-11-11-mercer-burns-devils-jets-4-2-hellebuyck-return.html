Dawson Mercer burns his old team as Jets beat Devils 4-2 in Hellebuyck's return to Winnipeg

By Matt

Dawson Mercer scored twice against the team that traded him — including the power-play winner — and Nico Daws got the win in the other net as Winnipeg beat New Jersey 4-2 on the night Connor Hellebuyck came home and never played.

The night

Tuesday in Winnipeg was supposed to be about Connor Hellebuyck. The former Jets goalie came back to Canada Life Centre for the first time since his trade request was granted on October 23, and the building was ready for him — the fans booed him like he had started all 60 minutes.

He never played a second. The Devils went with Jake Allen in net, and the two players New Jersey sent the other way in the deal decided the game instead.

Dawson Mercer scored twice against his former team, including the tiebreaking power-play goal with 8:02 left in the third period, and Nico Daws — the other half of the return package — made 19 saves for his first win as a Jet as Winnipeg beat New Jersey 4-2. Kyle Connor scored twice as well, adding an empty-netter with 58 seconds left to seal it.

The goals told the story of the night. Connor put Winnipeg up 2-0 in the first. Jesper Bratt and Cody Glass answered 44 seconds apart early in the second to tie it, and the building went quiet for a while. Then the Devils took a too-many-men penalty in the third, Mercer buried the power play at 11:58, and the Jets had their fourth straight win — this one after a nine-game skid that had buried their season before it started.

The trade, in one night

This is what a revenge game is supposed to look like. Mercer and Daws were not bit players in the Hellebuyck deal — they were the package — and on the night Hellebuyck returned to Winnipeg, they were the two best Jets on the ice. Mercer has now scored three goals since changing teams. Daws outdueled Allen, who made 21 saves in the loss.

Hellebuyck, for his part, watched from the bench as the crowd chanted at him. Devils general manager Sunny Mehta had announced on Sunday that Hellebuyck would not start, and head coach Scott Arniel said afterward that he hopes the win brings closure for his team — the Jets still have to go to New Jersey on November 28, but the home half of the saga is done.

The loss ended New Jersey's five-game winning streak and dropped the Devils to 13-6-0. Winnipeg improved to 8-8-3, and after nine straight losses, four straight wins has them back in the conversation.

The bigger picture

The Jets spent the first month of the season in a hole of their own making. Four straight wins does not erase a nine-game skid, but beating the team that took your franchise goalie — with the two players you got back doing the damage — is the kind of night that changes how a room feels about itself.

Winnipeg visits Dallas on Thursday. New Jersey hosts Washington the same night.
