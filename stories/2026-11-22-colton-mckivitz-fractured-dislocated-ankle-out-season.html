Colton McKivitz's season is over — the 49ers' starting right tackle fractured and dislocated his ankle in Mexico City

By Matt

The 49ers beat the Vikings 35-10 on Sunday night to move to 9-1, but they lost starting right tackle Colton McKivitz for the season — a fractured and dislocated left ankle, confirmed by Kyle Shanahan after the game.

McKivitz is done for the year

The San Francisco 49ers lost starting right tackle Colton McKivitz for the rest of the season on Sunday night, after he suffered a fractured and dislocated left ankle in their 35-10 win over the Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City.

Head coach Kyle Shanahan confirmed the diagnosis after the game, telling reporters McKivitz's ankle was broken and dislocated, per ESPN's Nick Wagoner. Shanahan added that the injury is similar to the one linebacker Fred Warner suffered last year.

How it happened

The injury came in the second quarter, on a four-yard Christian McCaffrey run. McKivitz was blocking when he got rolled up in the pile, and McCaffrey landed on the back of his left leg. When McKivitz came out of the pile, his left foot was facing the wrong direction.

The reaction on the field told the story before any diagnosis did. McCaffrey dropped to a knee beside him. Left tackle Trent Williams spiked his helmet into the turf. Teammates gathered around as the medical staff put McKivitz's leg in an air cast and carted him off, to an ovation from the crowd at Estadio Banorte.

What the 49ers lose

McKivitz is not a name that shows up in highlight reels, but he is the kind of loss that changes a season. The 30-year-old has been San Francisco's starting right tackle since 2023, and Sunday night was his 61st consecutive start. He had not given up a sack all season, and Pro Football Focus had him graded as the seventh-best tackle in the league through Week 10 — second-best in run blocking.

The 49ers know it, too. They signed him to a three-year, $45 million extension in September 2025, with $27 million guaranteed, keeping him under contract through the 2028 season. He was playing the best football of his career.

Now the job falls to swing tackle Vederian Lowe, who had played only 36 offensive snaps all season before checking in on Sunday night. Lowe will get the first crack at the job, but there is no replacing a lineman who had not missed a start in three years — not in November, not with the playoffs coming.

The win came at a cost

None of this takes away from what the 49ers did on the field. Brock Purdy completed 21 of 26 passes for 255 yards and three touchdowns against a Vikings defense that had been the stingiest in the league, and San Francisco's 35-10 win moved them to 9-1 — one game clear of Detroit and Chicago in the race for the NFC's top seed.

But the 49ers were already the most banged-up contender in football, with 14 players on injured reserve before Sunday night. McKivitz makes 15, and he is the one they could least afford to lose on the offensive line.

What comes next

San Francisco hosts Seattle in Week 12, and holding the No. 1 seed means winning that game with a backup right tackle on Purdy's right side. The 49ers have survived injuries all season — that is how they got to 9-1 — but this one tests the depth at the exact spot where depth is thinnest.

McKivitz will have surgery, and the recovery from a fracture-dislocation is a long one. The 49ers' Super Bowl push now runs through Vederian Lowe.
