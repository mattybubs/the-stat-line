Astros part ways with pitching coach Josh Miller after 16 years — the Ausmus overhaul rolls on

By Matt

Houston told Josh Miller in October that he'd be back for 2027. Then Brad Ausmus arrived as manager, and the 16-year Astro is out — the latest piece of a full teardown in Houston.

The move

The Houston Astros will have a new pitching coach in 2027. Josh Miller will not return to the staff under new manager Brad Ausmus, MLB.com's Brian McTaggart first reported on Monday, with The Athletic confirming the same through team sources.

It ends a 16-year run with the organization for Miller, who joined the Astros in 2010 and worked his way up from scout and minor league coach to bullpen coach and, since 2022, major league pitching coach. He was in the organization for both World Series titles.

The reversal

This one caught people off guard because Houston had already said the opposite. Last month, the club announced Miller was among the coaches who would return for the 2027 season, alongside first-base coach Dave Clark, field coordinator Jason Bell, hitting coach Victor Rodriguez and bullpen catcher Javier Bracamonte.

That announcement came before Ausmus was hired as manager on November 13. Once the new skipper arrived, the plan changed. Miller is now the second coach from that supposedly returning group to leave, after Bell took a job on the Boston Red Sox staff.

Whether Ausmus drove the decision or Miller had a change of heart himself is unclear. Either way, the message is the same: the new manager gets his own staff.

The backstory

Miller, who turns 48 in February, was a minor league pitcher who also pitched professionally in Taiwan before moving into coaching. He spent two years as Houston's bullpen coach under Brent Strom, then took over the pitching coach job when Strom left after the 2021 season. Strom, notably, returned to the organization as a pitching consultant a few months ago — but that relationship did not keep Miller on Ausmus' staff.

The results in 2026 did Miller no favors. Houston finished 81-81, and the pitching staff posted a 4.45 ERA, 24th in baseball, while issuing 650 walks. A staff that used to be the organization's calling card spent the season leaking runs and free passes.

The overhaul

Miller's exit is one piece of a teardown that now touches every level of the organization. Owner Jim Crane fired general manager Dana Brown in August and announced after the season that manager Joe Espada would not return. Randy Flores, hired away from the Cardinals, is the new general manager after the Astros' first choice, Brewers assistant GM Matt Kleine, turned down the job.

On the coaching staff, the vacancies are piling up. Beyond pitching coach, Houston still needs a third base coach, a bullpen coach, and assistant hitting and pitching coaches. The club announced last week that longtime big league catcher Bobby Wilson, who had been the Nationals' catching and game-planning coach, will be the new bench coach. Rodriguez and Clark are still expected back, though that was also true of Miller until Monday.

What's next

Houston now has to find a pitching coach who fits Ausmus' vision and can rebuild a staff that lost its way in 2026 — and do it before a December 1 lockout freezes the sport. Miller, meanwhile, becomes one of the more experienced pitching coaches on the market, with a World Series ring on his resume and sixteen years inside one of baseball's model organizations.

The Astros spent a decade as the standard. This offseason, they are starting over.
