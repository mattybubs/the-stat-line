Darcy Kuemper's 40th career shutout — 29 saves and a penalty-shot stop — blanks the Bruins and ends the Kings' skid

By Matt

Darcy Kuemper stopped all 29 shots he faced, including Morgan Geekie's third-period penalty shot, for his 40th career shutout as the Kings beat Boston 3-0 at TD Garden, snapping a four-game losing streak.

The night

Darcy Kuemper picked the right night to be perfect. The Los Angeles Kings goaltender stopped all 29 shots he faced — including a third-period penalty shot — for his 40th career shutout as Los Angeles beat the Boston Bruins 3-0 at TD Garden on Tuesday night, snapping a four-game losing streak.

It was his first shutout of the season, and it made him the fifth active goalie to reach 40. He got there faster than any of the other four: 501 games played, passing Connor Hellebuyck's 516.

"The guys were doing a great job in front of me, and I felt like I was able to see every shot," Kuemper said. "When we're boxing out like that it makes the goalie's job a lot easier."

The save

The shutout nearly ended at 9:51 of the third. Morgan Geekie was hooked by Drew Doughty on a breakaway and awarded a penalty shot with the Kings up 2-0. Geekie tried a forehand shot from in close. Kuemper made a glove save.

"I was just trying to be as big as I could," Kuemper said. "Obviously, he's a great shooter, so I wanted to take away as much of the net as I could and got a piece of it."

Penalty shots barely dent him: he has allowed only one goal in seven career penalty shots against.

The Bruins had a penalty shot of their own earlier — Quinton Byfield was tripped by Mason Lohrei on a breakaway at 5:56 of the second — but Michael DiPietro made a sprawling glove save on Byfield's forehand-backhand deke. DiPietro finished with 19 saves in the loss.

The goals

Mats Zuccarello opened the scoring at 15:46 of the first, beating a screened DiPietro short side from the left circle. It was the second consecutive game Zuccarello has scored — at 39, he joins Jarome Iginla, Luc Robitaille and Corey Perry as Kings who have scored in consecutive games at age 39 or older.

Andre Lee made it 2-0 at 16:35 of the second, redirecting a Scott Laughton centering pass in with his knee after Hampus Lindholm's clearing attempt was intercepted at the hash mark. Erik Gustafsson — back after missing three games with an undisclosed injury — scored into the empty net at 16:53 of the third for the final margin, with Kuemper picking up an assist on the play. Gustafsson also had a goal and an assist on the night.

The context

The Kings needed this one badly. They entered the night 6-11-2, losers of four straight, and got a road game exactly the way coach Peter Laviolette drew it up: desperate, blocking shots, backchecking, and a goalie who saw everything.

"We played a really good 40 minutes, and we didn't want to play the next 17 minutes like we did the first three minutes of the third period," Laviolette said. "I thought we responded well to both. I thought we did a really good job right in front of Darcy, and Darcy made some really big saves."

Boston, meanwhile, lost its third straight for the first time this season and fell to 13-7-1. Coach Marco Sturm was blunt: "We got more and more frustrated and made some mental mistakes and lost the game. I knew if we scored one, we would score two, but that goal never came."

What it means

Forty shutouts is a career milestone, but the timing is the story: a struggling team, a four-game skid, a road game in a building where the last eight meetings between these teams had all gone to overtime or a shootout. Kuemper ended that pattern with a clean sheet — and did it faster than any active goalie in the league.
