NBA admits the refs missed it — Embiid stepped out of bounds, and the Bucks should have had the ball with 3.5 seconds left

By Matt

A day after Tyler Herro's career-high 50-point night ended in a 132-129 loss, the NBA's last-two-minute report confirmed what the Bucks bench saw in real time: Joel Embiid stepped out of bounds in the final seconds, and possession should have gone to Milwaukee with 3.5 seconds left.

The league confirmed it a day later

The NBA admitted Sunday that the officials missed the call that decided Saturday's game between the Philadelphia 76ers and the Milwaukee Bucks.

In its daily last-two-minute report, the league acknowledged that Joel Embiid stepped out of bounds in the final seconds of Philadelphia's 132-129 win — and that "possession should have been awarded to Milwaukee." The report also noted there should have been 3.5 seconds left for the Bucks to try and win the game afterward.

The result stands. The admission changes nothing in the standings. But it confirms the Bucks were robbed of a chance, not just a call.

How the final seconds actually went

Milwaukee had stormed back from 10 points down with just over two minutes left. A Brayden Burries three-pointer cut the deficit to one, 130-129, with 4.8 seconds on the clock. Philadelphia called timeout.

LeBron James inbounded the ball deep toward the opposite end, to Embiid. Herro chased him down to commit the intentional foul — and as Embiid tried to evade him along the baseline in front of the Bucks bench, his foot went over the line. Out of bounds, plain as day on the replay.

Only nobody with a whistle was there to see it. Every official had been watching James inbound the ball, then positioned themselves around the Philadelphia basket. The Bucks bench saw it. The fans in that corner saw it. The referees did not.

Herro saw it too — and for a split second, he pulled up, expecting the whistle. When none came, he committed the foul with 1.5 seconds left. Embiid sank both free throws, stretching the lead to three, and Herro's desperate buzzer-beating three was ruled no good — the ball was still on his fingertips when the horn sounded.

Why Milwaukee couldn't challenge

The cruelest part: the Bucks had no way to fix it. They had already used their coach's challenge with 1:42 remaining, and an uncalled out-of-bounds violation cannot be initiated by the officials. Had they still possessed a challenge, they could have challenged Herro's foul on Embiid, which would have let officials review whether Embiid committed a boundary-line violation immediately beforehand. They were out of challenges, so the play stood.

Herro, who finished with a career-high 50 points to go with 11 rebounds and 7 assists, was diplomatic about it afterward.

"Well, Embiid, he did step out of bounds," he said. "But for ref, it is a tough play. 'Cause the ball was being taken out on the other side of the court. For him to see that you've got to have a ref on that baseline. So it was a tough call, a tough play to see as a ref. So, I get that one."

One call the league says was right

The last-two-minute report did not hand Milwaukee everything. The league also determined that 76ers guard VJ Edgecombe did not foul Herro while the Bucks guard tried to get off his game-tying shot before the buzzer — a correct non-call, in the NBA's view.

So the record is now this: the Bucks should have had the ball, down one, with 3.5 seconds left and Herro — the hottest hand on the floor — ready to take the shot. Instead they got a foul, two Embiid free throws, and a 13th straight loss that dropped them to 1-16, the worst record in the league.

Philadelphia, meanwhile, survived again. The 76ers sit atop the Eastern Conference, and they keep finding ways to win the close ones — this time with a little help from the officials, who the league now admits missed the one that mattered most.
