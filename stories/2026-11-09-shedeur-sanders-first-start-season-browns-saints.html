Shedeur Sanders nearly steals one in New Orleans — 297 yards, two touchdowns, and a QB question Cleveland can't dodge

By Matt

Shedeur Sanders made his first start of the season for Cleveland and threw for 297 yards and two touchdowns without a turnover — nearly beating the Saints in overtime before a failed two-point try ended it 23-22. Deshaun Watson was healthy enough to dress. He never played. Now the Browns have a question to answer.

What happened

Shedeur Sanders could not quite finish what would have been a dramatic overtime victory for the Cleveland Browns. But he took solace in how his play improved during the course of his first start this season — and he gave the Browns something they did not have before Sunday: a quarterback controversy.

The New Orleans Saints beat Cleveland 23-22 in overtime at the Caesars Superdome. Sanders, starting in place of Deshaun Watson, finished 19-of-31 for 297 yards and two touchdowns without a turnover. It was his second-highest yardage total in eight NFL starts. All but 38 of those yards came after halftime.

The comeback

For three quarters, Sanders played like a backup who had three days of practice reps and a walkthrough — which is exactly what he was. The Browns punted three times and settled for a 42-yard field goal on their first four series. They stumbled on a lineman's foot on a failed fourth-and-goal from the 1. It was 3-3 at halftime and 6-6 going into the fourth quarter, and the game looked like it was going nowhere.

Then the fourth quarter happened. Sanders hit rookie KC Concepcion on a deep pass that turned into a 64-yard touchdown, giving Cleveland a 13-9 lead with less than six minutes left. After the Saints regained the lead, Sanders found Jerry Jeudy for 30 yards on a third-down scramble, setting up Andre Szmyt's 51-yard tying field goal with eight seconds left.

New Orleans scored a touchdown on the first series of overtime. Sanders answered. He went 6-of-8 on the final drive, hit Denzel Boston on a crossing pattern to convert a third down, and watched tight end Harold Fannin rumble 39 yards to the end zone, breaking two tackles along the way. The two-point conversion attempt — Sanders flushed right, throwing for Boston — fell incomplete. A completion would have been nullified anyway: tackle Tytus Howard was flagged for holding, though the Browns would have gotten another play.

The context nobody can ignore

Sanders started the Browns' final seven games last year as a rookie fifth-round draft pick, going 3-4 with more interceptions (10) than touchdowns (seven). He lost the preseason battle to Watson under first-year coach Todd Monken, threw only three passes in relief over the first eight games, and had not taken snaps with the starters in 10 weeks. Watson started the first eight games and went 5-3. He self-reported concussion symptoms on Tuesday, entered the protocol, cleared it on Saturday — and stayed in street clothes on Sunday while Sanders took the snaps.

"For a guy that really hasn't taken snaps with (starters) in 10 weeks, I'm really proud of him," Monken said. "He had a great week of practice, and he gave us a chance at the end. He came through when we needed it with some big plays."

Sanders, for his part, sounded like a quarterback who knows what he just did. "To even be able to get in that position was amazing," he said. "I was truly thankful to get to that point. The moment never felt big, so that's how I know I'm growing as a quarterback in this league. The next time we get in that situation, I will have a better plan on what to do."

Asked after the game whether he had earned the right to start again, Sanders called it "a set-up question." Monken declined to answer, too: "How long has it been — 30 minutes since the game ended? I'm not getting into the quarterback."

The noise around him

Sanders made his biggest mistake earlier in the week. Asked what he had seen while studying the Saints' defense, he said, "Yeah, I've seen a lot. When I smile, just know that means something." Some Saints took offense. "It's disrespectful for someone to laugh at our defense, you know, take us as a joke," said New Orleans edge rusher Carl Granderson, who had two of the Saints' five sacks. Sanders insisted the comment was misinterpreted and apologized if his words were taken out of context: "I was never, ever throwing a shot."

He took five sacks on Sunday and never turned the ball over. He also threw a 60-yarder to Boston, who outjumped cornerback Kool-Aid McKinstry down the sideline. The Browns are 5-4, and they host the Houston Texans next. Watson is in the final season of his five-year contract. Somebody has to decide who starts that game, and after Sunday, "the starter" is no longer an automatic answer.
