GitHub celebrated a PS5-to-PC tool's GTA 6-fueled boom — then deleted the post

By Matt

GitHub's own X account toasted GTA 6's launch for pushing the AnyPS5 project past a big milestone, signed off 'Happy GTA VImas!' — and asked whether changing where games run crosses a legal line. Then the post vanished, two days after GitHub wiped thousands of unofficial GTA 6 PC-port repos for Take-Two.

GitHub spent Thanksgiving morning celebrating the exact thing it had just spent the week deleting. The Microsoft-owned platform's official X account posted that GTA 6's launch had sparked "one of the biggest weeks in gaming and open source on GitHub," congratulating the AnyPS5 project on passing 58,000 stars since its August debut — and signed off with a festive "Happy GTA VImas!" Then, after the screenshots started circulating, the post disappeared.

The deleted post didn't just celebrate a star count. It posed a question to its developer audience that reads very differently coming from the company that hosts the code: "Does changing where games run cross a legal line?" The video attached to the post drove the point home, spotlighting a comment from an AnyPS5 contributor that read, "We have 3 days until GTA 6 everyone […] get your agents or deevs and work." One X user downloaded and re-uploaded the video before the deletion, so the whole thing is preserved anyway.

What AnyPS5 actually is

The headlines calling AnyPS5 a PlayStation 5 emulator are only half right, and the distinction matters. It isn't a traditional emulator that spins up a virtual PS5 operating system. It's an open-source C++ toolkit for static porting — converting PS5 executables into native Windows and Linux binaries. That doesn't make it a GTA 6 PC port on its own, and celebrating its star count isn't the same as endorsing an unauthorized port of the game. But the timing made the celebration land badly: the whole reason AnyPS5's community was sprinting was GTA 6, a game with no PC version, and GitHub's own video said so out loud.

The takedown two days earlier

The deletion looks even stranger against what happened just before it. Two days earlier, GitHub processed a DMCA notice from Take-Two covering the network of repositories behind Project Leonida, the unofficial GTA 6 PC port built from the leaked Xbox executable — removing 3,007 repositories in one sweep. So within the same week, GitHub took down thousands of repos for trying to bring GTA 6 to PC, then used GTA 6 to promote a tool whose community is trying to do the same thing from a different angle, then deleted the promotion.

GitHub and Microsoft have not publicly explained how the post cleared internal review or why it was pulled. IGN noted that GitHub had not responded to its request for comment at the time of publication. The likeliest reading is the simplest one: a social team saw a big open-source milestone tied to the biggest launch in gaming history and posted before anyone in legal had a look. The question it asked — whether changing where games run crosses a legal line — is one Take-Two has already answered for itself, with takedown notices. For now, the post lives on only in screenshots and re-uploads, which is usually how deleted posts end up reaching more people than the original ever would.
