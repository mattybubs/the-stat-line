GTA 6's unofficial PC port just hit 20 FPS — and modders already built a mod menu for it

By Matt

Three days ago the unofficial GTA 6 PC port needed one to two seconds to draw a single frame. Now community builds are running the open world at around 20 frames per second, holding a 41-minute session without a crash — and the first mod menu and dedicated PC launcher have already shown up.

Three days ago, the unofficial GTA 6 PC port needed one to two seconds to draw a single frame of the game world. Now community builds are running Leonida at around 20 frames per second, holding a session for 41 minutes without a crash — and the first mod menu and dedicated PC launcher for the game have already shown up.

It is still not playable in any real sense. But the pace of this is now the story: every day since the leaked Xbox executable surfaced on November 23, the port has jumped to a new milestone, and Take-Two's takedowns have not slowed it down.

From seconds per frame to 20 FPS

The numbers come from two separate forks of VIctory, the open-source compatibility layer that runs the leaked Xbox build's code directly on Windows. TweakTown reported on November 26 that footage posted to X by MrPoulpy shows the game running through VIctory with a live frame counter at 30 FPS in the menus and 19 to 23 FPS while riding a jet ski and driving along the coast. In town, it falls to about 12 FPS. The clip carries a watermark from another user, AzRage, and TweakTown's author wrote that he was unable to independently verify who made the build or what hardware it ran on — so treat that clip as a claim with video attached, not a benchmark.

The second report is easier to pin down. VGTimes covered a fork called VIctory-7900XTX, built by AvatarManz and tested on a Windows 11 PC with an Intel i9-12900K, 64 GB of RAM, and a Radeon RX 7900 XTX, running at 1440p on the Series X graphics profile. Its longest reported session stretched to 41 minutes without a crash. The prologue's indoor scenes reached 13 to 20 FPS, and the first boat mission ran at 19 to 22 FPS. A street chase full of cars and people dropped to 6 to 10 FPS — but the same scenes had taken 280 milliseconds per frame two days earlier, roughly 3 to 4 FPS. The fork used 40 to 46 GB of private memory, and its documentation recommends 64 GB of RAM.

A third data point comes from PC Games Hardware, cited by TweakTown: a public fork called VIctory-NVIDIA averages 20 to 22 FPS indoors on an RTX 5070 and a Ryzen 7 9800X3D. The catch is in the settings — that fork uses the Xbox Series S graphics profile, rendering internally at 960 by 540 before upscaling, with ray tracing largely switched off.

The main VIctory project's own README still lists the first mission intro at 1 to 2 seconds per frame. The forks are moving faster than the project documenting them.

The mod menu already exists

The port is not just getting faster — it is getting tooling. TweakTown reported on November 26 that community developers have demonstrated an in-game mod menu and a dedicated PC launcher, three days after the leaked executable surfaced.

Footage shared by X user cccurrenccc shows an in-game mod menu running on an unofficial PC build, with an interface resembling the mod menus previously built for GTA V. The current implementation was created primarily for debugging purposes, and it is unclear whether it has been made publicly available. Separately, Brazilian streamer Suco demonstrated a dedicated GTA VI launcher for PC, reporting an average of around 10 FPS on a system with a Radeon RX 7900 XTX and 64 GB of memory, with frame rates occasionally dropping to just 2 FPS.

That is the shape of this race now: the builds get faster every few days, the tooling shows up alongside them, and the takedowns keep landing. Take-Two's DMCA notice on November 24 took down the Leonida project's network of 3,007 GitHub repositories, and TweakTown noted several other port repositories have since vanished with no public takedown notice. VGTimes reported two more unofficial ports were taken down as the VIctory-7900XTX fork arrived.

Still not a game you can play

None of this is an official port, and Rockstar has announced no PC version of GTA 6. The visual bugs are not subtle: black holes sit where shoreline water should be, magenta streaks run along a road, and outdoor trees, plants, and distant buildings can flicker while textures arrive late. At 12 FPS in town on one build and 6 to 10 FPS in a street chase on another, this is a technical experiment, not a way to play the game.

And the warning from every previous post in this saga still stands: there is no playable PC version of GTA 6, and there is no safe way to get one. The leaked files have not been independently authenticated. Downloading them is piracy, and files circulating from strangers carry real malware risk — the port tracker is already flagging fake repositories distributing malware under the guise of GTA 6 port tools. The projects themselves do not distribute the game's files; users are expected to supply their own, and the only source in circulation right now is the leak.

The builds get faster every few days. The takedowns keep landing. And 20 FPS at 540p is still a long way from playable.
