Jaguars rout short-handed Ravens

By Matt

Jacksonville beat Baltimore 33-10 on Thursday night behind Trevor Lawrence's 242 yards and a defense that held the Ravens to 227 total yards — Baltimore's fourth straight loss with undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano making his first career start.

What happened

Jacksonville beat Baltimore 33-10 Thursday night at M&T Bank Stadium, in a game that was decided less by what the Jaguars did than by who the Ravens could not put on the field.

Joe Fagnano's first career start never got going. The undrafted rookie threw for 60 yards with two interceptions, and Baltimore managed just 227 total yards all night. Trevor Lawrence, meanwhile, threw for 242 yards and a touchdown, and the Jaguars improved to 6-2. The Ravens fell to 4-5 — their fourth straight loss, and below .500 for the first time this season after starting 4-1.

The mismatch at quarterback

This was the game Baltimore had been dreading all week. Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley are both on injured reserve — Jackson with a right thumb injury, Huntley with a right toe injury — leaving the Ravens down to their third quarterback on a short week in prime time.

Fagnano finished 10 of 18 for 60 yards and two interceptions. Baltimore took the ball out of his hands as much as possible, leaning on Derrick Henry and the run game, but the Ravens never found a passing attack. The plan to load up on Henry faded fast: he carried 17 times for 57 yards, and Baltimore's only touchdown came on a 65-yard Justice Hill run in the fourth quarter, long after the game was decided.

It was not just a one-night problem. The Ravens have now been outscored 84-10 in the second half during this four-game slide. Baltimore is finalizing a trade for veteran Jacoby Brissett, but he cannot arrive soon enough — and Thursday night showed exactly why.

How it unfolded

Jacksonville took the lead for good on the opening drive, when Bhayshul Tuten powered in from the 1-yard line. The Jaguars' lead never dipped below double digits after halftime.

The decisive blow came just before the break. With under a minute left in the first half and Jacksonville up 10-3, Lawrence hit Parker Washington on a 50-yard catch-and-run touchdown — Washington juggled the ball, corralled it, and finished the play to make it 17-3. Baltimore never got closer.

From there it was Cam Little's night as much as anyone's. The Jaguars kicker hit four field goals — 36, 45, 47, and 33 yards — and Chris Rodriguez Jr. added a 2-yard touchdown run in the fourth quarter to stretch the lead to 33-3. Hill's 65-yard score, the second-longest run of his career, was Baltimore's only answer.

Jacksonville outgained Baltimore 319-227. The Jaguars' defense picked Fagnano off twice and recovered a fumble.

Final score: Jaguars 33, Ravens 10

Trevor Lawrence: 16-of-26, 242 yards, 1 TD

Joe Fagnano: 10-of-18, 60 yards, 2 INT

Jaguars record: 6-2

Ravens record: 4-5 (four straight losses)

Henry's milestone in a lost night

The one piece of history on Baltimore's side belonged to Derrick Henry. His 57 rushing yards moved him into seventh place on the NFL's all-time rushing list with 13,707 yards, passing Hall of Famers Jerome Bettis and LaDainian Tomlinson.

It is a milestone worth pausing on: Henry passed two of the most decorated backs in league history on a night when his team could barely move the ball. He did it in his 11th season, on a team that started 4-1 and has now lost four straight — a reminder that individual greatness and team collapse can share the same box score.

What the win means

Jacksonville has now won five of its past six games and sits at 6-2, firmly in the AFC mix. The Jaguars have outscored their last two opponents 75-17. This was not a statement win in the usual sense — beating a third-string rookie at quarterback is the result the standings said to expect — but the way Jacksonville handled it, cleanly and without drama, is what good teams do with games like this.

Baltimore, meanwhile, is in real trouble. Four straight losses, a quarterback room held together by a rookie and a trade that has not closed yet, and a second-half scoring drought (10 points across four games) that no defense can keep covering for. The Ravens host the Chargers next, and the season's direction depends on how fast Brissett — or Jackson's thumb — can get them a functioning passing game again.
