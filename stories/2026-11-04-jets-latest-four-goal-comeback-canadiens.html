Jets Pull Off the Latest Four-Goal Comeback in NHL History, Stunning the Canadiens 5-4 in OT

By Matt

Down 4-0 with 2:39 left in Montreal, the Winnipeg Jets scored four goals in 2:06 and won 5-4 in overtime — the latest four-goal comeback in NHL history, snapping a nine-game winless skid.

Down 4-0 with 2:39 left on the clock, at the Bell Centre, against a Montreal team riding a five-game winning streak, the Winnipeg Jets did the impossible. Four goals in 2:06 of regulation, then Dylan Samberg's overtime winner, and a 5-4 victory that will live in the record book — and in Canadiens fans' nightmares — for a very long time.

The 2:06 that changed everything

The comeback began at 17:21 of the third period, with 2:39 remaining. Tyrel Bauer scored his first NHL goal, on a Dylan DeMelo dump-in that took a funny bounce off the left boards, to make it 4-1 and end Jacob Fowler's shutout bid. Fowler had stopped the first 37 shots he faced and was 2:39 from his second shutout in three games.

It only accelerated from there. Morgan Barron made it 4-2 at 18:03, shooting on a 2-on-1 and beating Fowler glove side off the crossbar. Kyle Connor then scored twice on one-timers from the right circle with Stuart Skinner pulled for the extra attacker — cutting it to 4-3 with one minute left, then tying it 4-4 with 33 seconds left on a shot that deflected in off Canadiens defenseman Noah Dobson.

In overtime, Gabriel Vilardi took the puck away from Lane Hutson along the boards, Adam Lowry fed a cutting Samberg, and Samberg scored blocker side from the right circle at 3:30 to complete it.

Samberg, 3:30 of overtime: 5-4 Jets

Why this one goes in the record book

Winnipeg began its comeback 57 minutes and 21 seconds into the game, making it the latest four-goal comeback in NHL history, per NHL Stats cited by ESPN. The previous mark belonged to the Los Angeles Kings, who began their rally against Dallas at 52:46 on November 10, 2007 — with 7:14 remaining. The Jets beat that record by nearly five minutes.

The four goals in 2:06 were also the fastest four goals in Jets franchise history. The previous franchise record was four goals in 2:16, set on November 9, 1984.

And it ended a miserable stretch. Winnipeg entered the game on a nine-game winless skid (0-7-2) and had just been beaten 7-0 at Carolina on Sunday. This was the Jets' first win since October 13. "We needed something to happen, some kind of crazy thing to happen, and four goals in 2:06, that doesn't happen very often, if ever," Winnipeg coach Scott Arniel said. "It was amazing. That felt like a playoff win in there."

The other side of it

Montreal had done everything right for 57 minutes. Owen Beck opened the scoring at 3:05 of the first, Josh Anderson made it 2-0 at 18:18, and Cole Caufield scored twice on the power play — at 4:00 of the second and 46 seconds into the third — to build the 4-0 lead. Caufield has now scored against every NHL team.

Then it all came apart, and Caufield did not hide from it. "You look at that game, we were winning but we weren't beating them," Montreal coach Martin St. Louis said. Caufield was blunter: "The guys thought the game was over, I think. Listen, a four-goal lead, there's no excuse for this. Yeah, it was terrible."

The loss ended Montreal's five-game winning streak, though the Canadiens (9-3-3) extended their point streak to eight games (6-0-2). Fowler finished with 42 saves. Skinner made 18 for Winnipeg (5-8-3), and Bauer and Barron each finished with a goal and an assist.

What it means going forward

One win does not fix a 5-8-3 start, and the Jets know it. But a team that had lost nine straight, that had been shut out 7-0 two days earlier, that trailed 4-0 with under three minutes left — that team now has a night nobody in that room will ever forget. "That's a long, hard stretch, but this group believed in each other," Bauer said. "We're proud of that. We had each other's backs in that room. We're going to run with that and we're not looking back."
