Tom Brady's Card Vault Opens Its 20th Store — First in Pennsylvania, in Pittsburgh's Strip District

By Matt

Card Vault by Tom Brady opened its first Pennsylvania location Sunday at The Terminal in Pittsburgh's Strip District — its 20th store nationwide, with fans lining up before the doors opened.

The hobby's retail boom has a new address. Card Vault by Tom Brady opened its first Pennsylvania store on Sunday at The Terminal in Pittsburgh's Strip District, its 20th location nationwide, with fans lining up before the opening to get a first look.

The timing was deliberate: the grand opening was scheduled to coincide with a major Pittsburgh sports weekend, and the Strip District — already one of the city's busiest foot-traffic corridors — gave the store a ready-made crowd.

What the store offers

The Pittsburgh location carries singles, sealed boxes, packs and collectibles from Topps, Panini, Upper Deck, Pokémon and Fanatics Authentic, according to the store announcement. Customers can also submit cards for professional grading through PSA, Beckett and SGC — the three names that matter most to anyone trying to turn a pull into a graded asset.

The location also features Card Vault's signature vault: a rotating selection of autographed and authenticated memorabilia, including helmets, bats, balls and other pieces. That vault concept is the brand's calling card — part retail floor, part museum case — and it is a big reason the chain has scaled to 20 stores.

Why this opening matters

Brady did not attend the grand opening, though he is expected to visit the Pittsburgh location in the near future. His name on the door is the point, though: Card Vault is the most celebrity-backed retail bet in the hobby, and its expansion into Pennsylvania — a state with deep sports-card roots and a Pittsburgh collector scene that has been waiting for a flagship — is a signal of how mainstream the modern card shop has become.

Twenty stores is not a pop-up experiment anymore. It is a chain, and Pittsburgh is the latest proof that the card boom did not end when the pandemic-era frenzy cooled. It just got a storefront.
