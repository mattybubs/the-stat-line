Kayla Harrison chokes out Amanda Nunes in the fourth — the GOAT's comeback ends in a tap

By Matt

Amanda Nunes came out of a three-year retirement to take her belt back. Kayla Harrison caught a kick in the fourth round, took her back, and ended the comeback with a rear-naked choke at 1:29 — keeping the women's bantamweight title at UFC 334.

The night

The comeback lasted three rounds and 89 seconds of a fourth.

Kayla Harrison retained the UFC women's bantamweight title on Saturday night at Madison Square Garden, submitting Amanda Nunes with a rear-naked choke at 1:29 of the fourth round in the co-main event of UFC 334 — spoiling the return of the woman widely regarded as the greatest female fighter of all time.

Nunes, the former two-division champion, was fighting for the first time since retiring as champion in June 2023 — more than three years out of the cage. Harrison, the two-time Olympic judo gold medalist, was herself returning from a lengthy layoff after neck surgery. The buildup was the biggest women's fight the UFC could make. The ending was sudden and total.

The finish

Harrison had dominated the fight before the finish. She outstruck Nunes 169 to 62 in total strikes, landing at 88 percent accuracy, and spent long stretches pinning the Brazilian to the canvas and punishing her with ground-and-pound — at one point, two straight minutes of punches to the head while Nunes was flattened out underneath her.

The end came fast. In the fourth, Nunes landed a left hook in the clinch — and Harrison caught a kick, tripped her to the mat, chased her down, took her back, and had the choke in before the two had even really hit the ground. Nunes tapped, and that was that.

Harrison improves to 20-1 (4-0 in the UFC) and takes home a $100,000 Performance of the Night bonus. Nunes falls to 23-6 (16-3 in the UFC), and the question now is whether the greatest women's fighter ever tries again — or whether this was the last time we see her in the cage.
