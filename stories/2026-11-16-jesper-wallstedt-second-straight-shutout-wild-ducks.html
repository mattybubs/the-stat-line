Jesper Wallstedt's second straight shutout — 28 saves, zero answers — lifts the Wild past the Ducks

By Matt

Jesper Wallstedt stopped all 28 shots he faced for his second consecutive shutout — and the third of his career — as Minnesota blanked Anaheim 2-0, making him the first Wild rookie ever to post back-to-back shutouts.

The night

The Minnesota Wild are starting to look like a team that's turning a corner, and the clearest sign is the 23-year-old in their net who won't let anything past him.

Jesper Wallstedt made 28 saves for his second straight shutout on Saturday night as the Wild beat the Anaheim Ducks 2-0 at Grand Casino Arena in St. Paul. It was his second shutout of the season and the third of his young career — and it made him the first Wild rookie ever to post back-to-back shutouts.

Wallstedt blanked Calgary 2-0 on 36 saves in his previous start, then did it again six days later against a Ducks team that came in as the second-highest scoring team in the NHL at 3.88 goals per game. His shutout streak reached 141:09 and counting. His biggest save of the night came late in the second period, when he robbed Frank Vatrano alone in front to keep the game scoreless.

Marcus Johansson scored the goal Minnesota needed 55 seconds into the second period, Matt Boldy added a goal and an assist — including the empty-netter with 55 seconds left — and Petr Mrazek stopped 29 shots at the other end for Anaheim. It didn't matter. Nobody was beating Wallstedt on this night.

The Wall of St. Paul

Wallstedt turned 23 on Friday. He is a first-round pick from 2021, the backup to Filip Gustavsson — the fellow Swede who signed a five-year, $34 million extension on October 4 — and he is playing like a goaltender who intends to make the tandem question uncomfortable. He improved to 4-0-2 as a starter, has two shutouts in six starts this season, and three in 11 starts across his three-year NHL career.

The Wild needed him. Minnesota climbed back above .500 for the first time in a month at 8-7-4, has gone 5-1-1 in its last seven games while allowing just 12 goals in that stretch, and is now 19-1-0 against the Ducks dating to the 2020-21 season, including six straight wins. The power play went 0-for-7 and the offense managed just enough — but when your goalie hasn't allowed a goal in 141 minutes, just enough is plenty.

Anaheim, for its part, is still dangerous — just not on this night, and not against this goalie. The Ducks will get their rematch in Minnesota to close the regular season on April 14. Wallstedt will be waiting.
