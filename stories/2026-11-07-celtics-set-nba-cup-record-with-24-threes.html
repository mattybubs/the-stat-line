Celtics set NBA Cup record with 24 threes

By Matt

Jayson Tatum dropped 34 points in 29 minutes and Boston buried an NBA Cup-record 24 threes in a 119-80 rout of the Hornets — a wire-to-wire blowout that pushed the Celtics to 7-2 and 2-0 in Cup play.

What happened

The Boston Celtics routed the Charlotte Hornets 119-80 on Friday night at Spectrum Center, and the score barely tells the story. Boston led 40-20 after the first quarter, by 31 at halftime, and by as many as 46 points on the way to its second NBA Cup group-play win.

Jayson Tatum scored a game-high 34 points in just 29 minutes — he did not play a minute in the fourth quarter — and the Celtics made 24 three-pointers on 54.5% shooting from deep. That is a new NBA Cup record.

The win moved Boston to 7-2 on the season and 2-0 in East Group C, where the Celtics now lead the group by record and own a +53 scoring differential — 44 points clear of the next team.

The three-point blitz

The record was not a late-game accident. Boston hit 17 threes in the first half alone, tying a franchise record, and kept firing until the 24th fell. The Celtics finished 24-of-44 from deep (54.5%) with 33 assists as a team.

They were even flirting with bigger history. The NBA record for most threes in a game is 29 — a mark the Celtics themselves tied on Opening Night in 2024 against the Knicks. They settled for the Cup record instead, and with the game long decided, there was no reason to chase it.

The contrast on the other end was stark. Charlotte shot 11-of-50 from three (22%) and 27-of-92 from the field overall (29.3%) in its second-lowest scoring performance of the season. The loss was the Hornets' sixth straight, dropping them to 3-7.

Tatum's night

Tatum did most of his damage before halftime: 24 of his 34 points came in the first two quarters, including 18 in the first. He finished 9-of-16 from the field, 5-of-8 from three, and a perfect 11-of-11 from the free-throw line, with 8 rebounds, 9 assists, a steal, and a team-best plus-36.

The road crowd in Charlotte even gave him MVP chants — fitting for a player who averages nearly 30 points per game against the Hornets, the most he averages against any of the 29 other teams.

It is also worth remembering how recently this version of Tatum was in doubt. He returned from a torn Achilles tendon on March 6 of last season, and nights like this one — efficient, unhurried, done by the fourth quarter — are the clearest sign yet that the explosion and the shot-making are fully back.

He had help. Derrick White added 18 points, including 10 in the first quarter, with 4 threes, 5 assists, and 4 rebounds. Payton Pritchard came off the bench for 12 points and 4 more threes. Tatum was the only player on either side to reach 20 points.

Around the game

• Brandon Miller: Charlotte's night got worse when Miller left the game with a groin injury.

• First stop on the road: the game opened a four-game road trip for Boston.

• Cup math: point differential matters in NBA Cup group play, and Boston's +53 through two games is the best in East Group C by 44 points.

• Next Cup game: Boston's next group-play game is not until Tuesday, Nov. 24, against the Wizards.

What it means

This was a statement in the exact form the NBA Cup rewards: win big, and the tiebreaker takes care of itself. The Celtics did not just beat an injury-riddled, struggling Hornets team — they buried them from three, sat their star for the fourth quarter, and still walked out with the best point differential in their group.

The bigger signal is Tatum. A 34-point night in 29 minutes, with the efficiency and the bounce, against a team he has always torched, says the Celtics' best player is rounding into form at the same time the team is stacking wins. Boston's next test is a real one: at Milwaukee on Sunday night.

What's next

Boston visits the Milwaukee Bucks on Sunday, Nov. 8, at 8 p.m. at Fiserv Forum.
