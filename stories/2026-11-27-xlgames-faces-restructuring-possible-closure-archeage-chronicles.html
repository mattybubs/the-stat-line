ArcheAge Chronicles studio XLGames faces 'extensive restructuring' — and possible closure

By Matt

XLGames, the studio behind ArcheAge Chronicles, is preparing for large-scale restructuring and has told staff it must even consider going out of business — weeks after the MMO's early-access launch fell short of the sales it needed.

XLGames is in trouble. The Korean studio behind ArcheAge Chronicles is preparing for what its own CEO calls "extensive restructuring" — and has told employees it must even consider the possibility of going out of business entirely, weeks after the MMO's early-access launch fell short of what the company needed it to be.

According to reporting from Korean outlet MTN, covered in English by Massively Overpowered, CEO Choi Kwan-ho announced the plan in a letter to staff. Complaints about the early-access launch keep coming, sales are trending downward, and layoffs are likely as the company shrinks toward what the letter calls "minimum viability" — drastically cutting business operations and keeping only essential functions and a minimal workforce. Choi also said the company will try to find additional funding so there are no disruptions to severance pay and benefits for those who leave.

"We must prepare for extensive restructuring by drastically reducing business operations and focusing on essential functions and a minimal workforce," the letter reads. "The company is currently at a critical crossroads where we must even consider the possibility of going out of business."

The launch that was supposed to save the studio

The numbers tell the story of why this happened now. ArcheAge Chronicles sold 100,000 copies in its first 10 days of release, bringing in about 4 billion KRW — just under $3 million. For most games that would be a fine start. For XLGames, a studio that had already sold off most of its flagship ArcheAge IP to stay liquid, it wasn't enough of a tailwind.

The game started life as ArcheAge 2, in development as far back as 2020, and went through a long run of delays and problems before finally reaching early access. In October, XLGames sold the ArcheAge IP and the separate ArcheAge S development business to Align Studio, a developer funded by Kakao Games, for roughly 39.2 billion KRW — a deal the company said was about securing liquidity and improving its financial structure. That effectively put the company's entire existence on Chronicles doing numbers at launch. It didn't.

The game isn't done yet

For players, the immediate picture is less bleak than the headlines. The game is still being updated — a November 25 patch raised monster gold and experience and opened a loot trade route after a shop-price cut that had angered players — and a major update is still due December 10, with the Azhra raid, a female Warrior class and the Gladius weapon, according to a November 26 letter from executive producer Yongjin Ham. The Steam page still lists an expected early-access run of nine to twelve months.

But the studio behind it is now openly talking about its own survival, and that's the part that matters. A game can keep getting updates while its developer shrinks to a skeleton crew — the question is for how long, and at what cost to the roadmap. XLGames is betting that a December update and new funding can steady the ship. The CEO's own letter says the alternative is on the table.
