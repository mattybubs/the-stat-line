Story of Seasons maker Marvelous is cutting console jobs — even as its profits soar

By Matt

Marvelous is offering voluntary retirement to 65 people on its console team, even after reporting operating profit up more than tenfold this year — a restructuring that leans harder on outsourcing for its Story of Seasons and Rune Factory games.

Marvelous — the Japanese publisher behind Story of Seasons, Rune Factory and Daemon X Machina — is cutting jobs on its console team, and the timing is what makes it sting. The company announced a voluntary early-retirement program on Friday that aims to shrink its console business by 65 people, weeks after reporting a huge jump in profits.

The program, which Marvelous calls its External Career Transition Support Program, is part of a restructuring of its console division — the part of the company that makes full-price, non-mobile games. Applications open December 14 and run through January 8, 2027, and anyone who takes the offer will leave by March 31, 2027, the end of the company's fiscal year. Those who go get a lump-sum retirement payment on top of standard severance, plus help finding a new job through an outside firm if they want it.

Why now — and why it looks strange

Marvelous says the move is about the changing shape of game development: bigger projects, longer cycles, and a need for a more flexible structure that mixes in-house development with outsourcing to external studios. Console development stays a core business, the company insists — the team is just getting smaller and more reliant on partners.

The awkward part is the money. For the six months from April through September 2026, Marvelous reported operating profit up 1,105.3% year on year, according to Automaton — a surge driven by its games business. The company still expects the retirement program to cost it about ¥220 million, roughly $1.37 million, which it will book as an extraordinary loss in the third quarter.

What it means for the games

For players, the near-term question is what happens to the pipeline. Marvelous is still working on Eternal Anima, its upcoming title, and the Story of Seasons and Rune Factory series remain the company's best-known console brands — Grand Bazaar, the latest Story of Seasons, only just arrived. A smaller in-house team with more outsourced work doesn't automatically mean fewer games, but it does mean the next ones will be made differently.

This is the same pattern the industry has been running all year: even healthy publishers are trimming headcount while they restructure around longer, more expensive development. Marvelous just did it in the most Japanese way possible — nobody is being fired, the door is open for volunteers, and the company is paying for the privilege.
