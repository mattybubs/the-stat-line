Aidan Hutchinson tore his ACL celebrating a sack — and his season is over

By Matt

The Lions' best defender tore the ACL in his right knee jumping to celebrate a sack of Drake Maye in Munich. Detroit placed him on injured reserve, and Hutchinson says the leap that ended his season was his last one.

The diagnosis

Detroit Lions defensive end Aidan Hutchinson tore the ACL in his right knee, and his 2026 season is over. The Lions placed him on injured reserve on Wednesday, November 18, three days after the injury in Munich.

It is the second season-ending injury of his five years in Detroit. In 2024 he broke the tibia and fibula in his left leg against the Dallas Cowboys and missed the rest of that season. He came back and played all 17 regular-season games in 2025, then opened this season as the anchor of one of the league's best pass rushes.

How it happened

The play itself was a highlight. On the opening drive of Detroit's 35-10 win over the New England Patriots on November 15, Hutchinson beat the tackle, caught Drake Maye from behind on third down, and dragged him to the turf. It was a sack that forced a field goal.

Then came the celebration. Hutchinson leapt in the air, and when he landed his right knee buckled. He recoiled immediately, collapsed, and grabbed the knee before heading to the locker room. Detroit ruled him out in the third quarter. The same play cost New England its quarterback too — Maye stayed in Germany overnight for testing and was later diagnosed with a fractured right fibula.

There was no hit on the play that hurt Hutchinson. No pile, no twist, no contact at all after the whistle. He jumped, he landed wrong, and his season ended on the landing.

What he said

Hutchinson broke his silence on Thursday, November 19, with an Instagram post that was equal parts disbelief and defiance.

"I honestly can't believe it. I've jumped hundreds of times on a football field and never in a million years did I think this would happen to me. But even something like this won't hold me back. I can ask why over and over again, but it'll never make sense. All I can do is surrender to the path. Only God knows why and I trust in Him. Very grateful for all of your prayers and well wishes. Two things I know for sure: That's the last time I go airborne and I always come back stronger. Hold down the fort til I'm back, boys."

Head coach Dan Campbell did not dress it up when he addressed the injury: "It stinks." Campbell said he expects Hutchinson to attack rehab the same way he did after the broken leg in 2024, and that surgery is expected within the next week or two.

What it means for Detroit

The timing is brutal for a team in first place. Detroit is 7-2 and tied for first in the NFC North, and Hutchinson was the headliner of a pass rush that defensive coordinator Kelvin Sheppard called not "a one-man band" — but he also told his unit plainly that they will miss him.

The numbers back up both halves of that. Through nine games Hutchinson had 24 combined tackles, six sacks and one forced fumble. The Lions are still the only team in the league with three players at six sacks, with Alim McNeill at six and a half and D.J. Wonnum at six, so the rush has depth. It just lost its best player, and its most feared one, for the rest of the year.

Detroit hosts Tampa Bay on Sunday, the first game of life without Hutchinson. Kerby Joseph was activated from the physically unable to perform list in the same round of moves that sent Hutchinson to injured reserve, so the defense gets a starter back even as it loses its star.

The celebration question

Hutchinson's injury has already changed behavior around the league. New York Giants running back Cam Skattebo, known for his backflip celebrations, said after hearing about it that fans may not see the flip much anymore, if at all. "It's hard to see," he said. "I'm saying yeah [no more]. Mentally, I'm like, 'I'm not doing that.' That ain't going to happen to me."

Celebrating big plays is part of the game and always will be. Hutchinson jumped hundreds of times and nothing happened — until the one time it did. That is what makes this one land the way it has: it was not a dirty hit or a freak collision. It was a sack, a leap, and a landing, and a season ended in between.

He has done this comeback before. The broken leg in 2024 was supposed to be the setback that defined him. Instead he came back, played every game in 2025, and put up 14.5 sacks. Now he has to do it again, and his own words say he will.
