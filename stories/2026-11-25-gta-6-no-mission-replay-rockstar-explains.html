Rockstar explains why GTA 6 has no mission replay — replaying 'diminished the choices you had made'

By Matt

GTA 6 has no way to replay a finished story mission, and Rockstar says that is a deliberate choice, not an oversight. Senior design director Scott Butchard told Kotaku the feature 'felt at odds with the story we were trying to tell' — because in Leonida, your choices stick.

If you have finished a story mission in Grand Theft Auto 6 and gone looking for the replay button, you already know the answer: there isn't one. Rockstar has now explained why — and the reason is baked into how the game is built.

Senior design director Scott Butchard told Kotaku that mission replay "was certainly something we looked at early on in development, and we do not currently support it." The problem, as he put it, was that replaying missions outside the story "felt at odds with the story we were trying to tell and felt a bit alien and disconnected."

"Sitting outside of the game as it were and replaying missions really diminished the choices you had made," Butchard said.

A break with 17 years of GTA tradition

Mission replay has been part of the series since The Ballad of Gay Tony, the GTA 4 expansion, introduced it. GTA 5 brought it back with a medal system that graded how well you completed each mission's secondary objectives — and plenty of players went back again and again chasing the highest rating.

GTA 6 drops both. There is no replay menu and no gold medal objectives at all. Challenges still exist, but they live in their own section of the progress menu rather than being attached to individual missions.

Why your choices stick this time

The design makes more sense once you know how GTA 6's story works. Missions are not self-contained episodes that reset when they end. They change the world around you, and the outcomes can be permanent — relationships shift, open-world events get set in motion, and a decision you made hours ago can still be echoing through Leonida.

Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson made the same point to IGN during the review period: because story missions affect the world and its open-world events, you cannot simply replay a mission and choose a different outcome once those events are in motion.

It is a different philosophy from GTA 5, and it has more in common with Red Dead Redemption 2 — a game where the story is the point, and where getting a perfect outcome matters less than living with the one you got. Kotaku's piece makes the comparison explicit, likening GTA 6's missions to quests in an RPG like The Witcher 3, where the outcome you do get is what tells the story of your time in the world.

The one way back in

There is a workaround, and it is the old-fashioned one: manual saves. GTA 6 gives you 15 manual save slots, and if you save before a story mission, you can load that save to play it again. The catch is that you have to plan ahead — a save made after the mission does not help, and the autosave will overwrite itself as you keep playing.

The practical advice is simple: rotate manual saves before the big turning points. If a mission looks like it might matter, bank a save first.

What is repeatable, without any of that, is most of the rest of the game. Races, stunts, pool and other minigames can be played again, and shop robberies and bank jobs reset after a cooldown once the story unlocks them. It is only the story itself that is one and done.

Don't expect it in a patch

Rockstar has not said the feature is coming later, and Kotaku's reporting suggests not to hold your breath. The omission is a creative decision, not a missing feature waiting on an update — the studio looked at replay, tried it, and decided it clashed with the game it was making.

The one exception might be PC. GTA 6 has no PC version yet, and whenever it arrives, modders will almost certainly build a mission replay of their own. Until then, Leonida is a one-way street: every mission happens once, and the choices you make are the story you get.
