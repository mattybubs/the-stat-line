Spurs win their 10th straight after erasing a 22-point hole in Chicago

By Matt

Victor Wembanyama scored 32 and Dylan Harper added 18 points, 10 assists and four steals as the Spurs came back from 22 down to beat the Bulls 124-119 — their 10th straight win after an 0-2 start.

The night

The San Antonio Spurs trailed by 22 points midway through the second quarter in Chicago on Wednesday night. The Bulls were 10-for-12 from three, the United Center was loud, and the Spurs looked like a team about to take its first loss in two weeks.

Then they did what they have done all month: they won anyway.

Victor Wembanyama scored 32 points and Dylan Harper — 18 points, 10 assists, four steals — ran the comeback as San Antonio beat the Bulls 124-119, closing out a 4-0 road trip. It was the second night in a row the Spurs had to dig out of a hole, and the second night in a row Wembanyama delivered: he dropped 40 with nine blocks in 25 minutes against Phoenix two days earlier.

The Bulls led 64-42 before the Spurs started chipping away, and Chicago's collapse is its own story. The Bulls have now lost eight straight to fall to 1-9, winless in four home games. Their biggest lead of the season lasted about a quarter.

The history

The record book cares about how the run started, not just how long it has gone. San Antonio opened the season 0-2. Since then, ten wins in a row — and that makes the Spurs just the second team in NBA history to start 0-2 and then win ten straight. The only other club to do it: the 2017-18 Boston Celtics, who turned an 0-2 start into a 16-2 stretch.

That is a very different kind of streak than an undefeated start. Nobody remembers the two losses in October because the response has been perfect since. Ten in a row, and the last one came from 22 down on the road.

What's next

The test gets harder immediately. Golden State comes to San Antonio on Friday night — the same Warriors team that just saw its own eight-game win streak snapped in Houston, and the same team the Spurs beat to start this run's momentum. A win there makes it eleven, and the conversation changes from "nice streak" to "how far can this go?"
