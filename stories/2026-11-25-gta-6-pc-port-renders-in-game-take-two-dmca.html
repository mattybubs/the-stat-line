GTA 6 is now rendering in-game on PC — and Take-Two just DMCA'd 3,007 repositories trying to stop it

By Matt

Two days ago the unofficial GTA 6 PC port could only reach the menus. Now the game is rendering its actual world on Windows — at under two frames a second, but rendering. Take-Two answered with a DMCA takedown that wiped 3,007 GitHub repositories. The race is still running anyway.

Two days ago, the unofficial GTA 6 PC port could only reach the menus. Now it is rendering the actual game world on Windows — and Take-Two has answered with a DMCA takedown that wiped out more than three thousand GitHub repositories.

The port is still nowhere near playable. But the speed of this is the story: in less than 48 hours, the effort went from a settings screen to 3D models, shadows, and physics running on a PC, while the publisher's lawyers took down the biggest project in the scene and the work scattered to a half-dozen successors instead of stopping.

From menus to the actual world

The project leading the race now is called VIctory, an open-source compatibility layer that runs the leaked Xbox build's code directly on Windows. According to reporting from TweakTown and XDA, it has moved past the menus entirely: saves work, the intro video plays with audio, and the world loads in about 50 seconds on a Windows 11 PC with an RX 6700 XT graphics card. The catch is the frame rate — most of the scene still fails to render, and each frame takes between half a second and 1.8 seconds to draw.

That is roughly 0.6 to 2 frames per second. You can watch the game load. You cannot play it.

A private build called CGBJ, based on VIctory, reportedly goes further — a player moving with a controller, talking to NPCs, and opening the in-game menu at about 4 frames per second, with no lighting and flat, mostly untextured graphics. That claim comes with a warning even from the outlets reporting it: the tracker behind it has reportedly confirmed the footage, but it could still be fake. Treat it as a rumor with a receipt, not a fact.

The public fork moving fastest is victory-fork, which landed a commit of about 32,000 lines on the evening of November 24 and was tested on an RTX 4070. Its own front page that night read "Not playable yet: about 3 frames per second, and shadowed areas are black." By Wednesday morning, 63 strangers had run it on their own machines, and the fork had drawn 483 pull requests in its first nine hours. One tester's log line, from an RTX 5060 Ti, measured a single frame at 390.6 milliseconds — 2.6 frames per second. A single run on an RTX 4090 reportedly reached 7.6.

Take-Two's answer: 3,007 repositories, gone

Take-Two confirmed it was behind the takedown of Project Leonida, the first and biggest port project, with a DMCA notice that GitHub published on November 24. The notice went after far more than the main repository: it covered Leonida and its forks, a network of 3,007 repositories in total, all disabled in one action.

The notice calls the project a derivative work of GTA 6 and cites Section 2.3 of Rockstar's terms of service, which bans decompiling, decrypting, or reverse engineering its games. It also fills in the timeline: the stolen Xbox build files were publicly distributed on November 20, and the Leonida repository opened the following day.

That is the confirmation that was missing when Leonida's Discord and GitHub vanished on November 23. At the time, Rockstar had not commented and the shutdown rested on creator NotRexed's own account. Now the publisher's fingerprints are on the paperwork.

The takedown did not end the race — it renamed it. A successor called Leonida Continued is live on GitHub carrying the project's full commit history, though its README says the game "does not boot yet." VIctory's status page says it now loads into the world and draws the first mission's intro at 1 to 2 seconds per frame. And the enforcement is spreading beyond GitHub: clips of the port running have been disappearing from X, and a YouTube video tracking the project was removed following a copyright request by Take-Two Interactive.

Do not download the files

This needs saying plainly, again: there is no playable PC version of GTA 6, and there is no safe way to get one. The leaked files have not been independently authenticated. Downloading them is piracy, and files circulating from strangers carry real malware risk — the port tracker is already flagging fake repositories distributing malware under the guise of GTA 6 port tools. The projects themselves do not distribute the game's files; users are expected to supply their own, and the only source in circulation right now is the leak.

There is still no official PC version of GTA 6 announced and no confirmed release date. Reports have pointed to early 2027 — a much shorter wait than Rockstar's usual year-plus gap between console and PC. Until Rockstar announces a date, the unofficial race is the only PC story there is — and after this week, it is very much still running, just under new names.
