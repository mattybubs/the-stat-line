The Mets just locked up A.J. Ewing for eight years and $95 million — the biggest pre-arbitration deal in franchise history

By Matt

The Mets agreed to an eight-year, $95 million extension with 22-year-old center fielder A.J. Ewing on Saturday — the largest pre-arbitration commitment in franchise history, and a bet that their rookie-year outfielder is a cornerstone.

The deal

The New York Mets and center fielder A.J. Ewing have agreed to an eight-year, $95 million contract extension, with a club option for a ninth season that would keep him in Queens through 2035.

The agreement was first reported Saturday by SNY's Chelsea Janes and confirmed by The Associated Press, which cited a person familiar with the negotiations. The Athletic's Will Sammon reports the option year is worth $25 million, and the New York Post reports the deal includes a $2 million signing bonus plus escalators that could push the total value higher.

The biggest pre-arb bet the Mets have ever made

The $95 million guarantee is the largest pre-arbitration commitment in Mets history, surpassing the six-year, $55 million deal third baseman David Wright signed in 2007, per Spotrac. It is also the eighth-largest guarantee ever given to a player with less than a year of big league service time, according to MLB Trade Rumors.

That is the scale of the bet. Ewing made his major league debut in mid-May and stayed with the big club for the rest of the 2026 season, finishing with a .252/.324/.376 slash line, 11 home runs, 43 RBI and 25 steals in 33 attempts across 492 plate appearances. He is 22 years old.

Under baseball's labor rules, Ewing could have been eligible for arbitration after the 2028 season and free agency following the 2032 season. Instead, the extension buys out all of his arbitration years plus three free-agent seasons — and if the Mets pick up the option, they control him through 2035.

What it says about the Mets' plan

Ewing slots into a young outfield the Mets are clearly building around: Carson Benge in right field and star slugger Juan Soto in left. And the Ewing deal may be the first of several. SNY's Janes reports the Mets are open to more pre-arbitration extensions with young players like Benge — who finished second in National League Rookie of the Year voting after a 20-homer, 22-steal campaign — and pitcher Nolan McLean, who owns a 2.93 ERA across 39 career starts.

It also lands in the middle of the Mets' biggest offseason pursuit. The club is chasing free-agent ace Tarik Skubal, and locking up Ewing now sends a clear message about how the Mets intend to spend: keep the young core, then add the star on top of it.

For a franchise that has spent the last few years buying stars at the top of the market, this is a different kind of move — a $95 million vote of confidence in a 22-year-old who has played one season. The Mets are betting the best version of A.J. Ewing is still years away, and that by the time it arrives, he will already be theirs.
