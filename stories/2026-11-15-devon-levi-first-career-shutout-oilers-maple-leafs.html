Devon Levi's first career shutout blanks the Leafs — and McDavid's streak hits 21

By Matt

Devon Levi stopped all 19 shots he faced for his first NHL shutout as Edmonton blanked Toronto 1-0, and Connor McDavid's assist ran his season-opening point streak to 21 games — with 50 points already, the fifth-fastest start to a season in NHL history.

The night

Devon Levi has been dreaming about this one since he was a kid. He said so himself, minutes after it happened.

The 24-year-old stopped all 19 shots he faced for his first career NHL shutout on Saturday night, and the Edmonton Oilers beat the Toronto Maple Leafs 1-0 at Scotiabank Arena to open a six-game road trip with their fourth straight win.

"It feels good, super special," Levi said. "Since I was a kid, I was thinking about getting a shutout in the NHL, so I'm just soaking it in. The guys played unbelievable tonight. I don't know how many shots I saw in the third, but we really locked it down. That's just like a textbook win right there."

The only goal of the night came at 15:34 of the second period, when Evan Bouchard took a backhand pass from Connor McDavid at the point and beat Anthony Stolarz glove side with a slap shot. It was Bouchard's 12th goal of the season — and the assist did something else, too.

The streak

McDavid's helper extended his season-opening point streak to 21 games. He's now one away from tying Dany Heatley's 22-game run in 2005-06 for the fifth-longest season-opening point streak in NHL history.

The raw totals are getting silly. McDavid leads the NHL with 50 points — 11 goals, 39 assists — in 21 games, remaining on pace for 200 points over a full season. He became just the fifth different player in league history to reach 50 points in 21 games or fewer, joining Wayne Gretzky (who did it three times), Mario Lemieux (three times), Bernie Nicholls and Mike Bossy.

"That's pretty good company," Ryan Nugent-Hopkins said. "Not really [do I have the words to put it into perspective], I've seen it for so long and he always finds another level to get to. It's like the older he gets, the more he figures out the game a little bit more. It's hard to wrap your mind around, but he is driving it for us every night."

Leon Draisaitl added an assist of his own to extend his point streak to eight games — six goals and eight assists in that span.

The save that almost wasn't

Toronto thought it had tied the game 1:00 into the third period, when John Tavares appeared to deflect one past Levi. Video review confirmed the deflection came with a stick above the crossbar, and the goal came off the board. Levi said he knew right away.

"I saw the tip and it went over my head, and I thought there's no way the puck should be going over my head like that," he said. "I knew it was a high stick."

The Leafs pulled Stolarz for the extra attacker in the final 1:48, and Auston Matthews had a chance alone in the slot with four seconds left — but shot wide before Nugent-Hopkins blocked the follow-up attempts from William Nylander. Levi's last word on the night was pure goalie: "I saw Matthews in the slot with the puck and I was like, 'Bring it on.' You've got to love it. As a goalie, you always want an opportunity to make a key save at a big moment. That's why I'm there."

The other side

This was a rough one for Toronto, and the sixth straight loss says it plainly enough. The Leafs went winless on their entire six-game homestand, losing each game in regulation while being outscored 23-9, and they've now been shut out twice in the span of a week. Stolarz made 22 saves in his first back-to-back starts of the season, but the Leafs — who routed Edmonton 7-2 on Oct. 24 — couldn't solve the other end.

"It's difficult because I can't just go and tell you all these good things and then we get on the wrong side of it again, so that's the hard part," Toronto coach Jim Hiller said. "The guys played their tail off, they did, so I don't think anybody is going to question that. I'd like to see them get rewarded for that, I really would."

For Levi, the night adds another line to what's becoming a remarkable start to life in Edmonton. Acquired from Buffalo on July 1, he improved to 9-1-0 in 2026-27 — the best 10-game start to a career with the Oilers in franchise history, two wins better than the next closest (Calvin Pickard at 7-2-0 in 2023-24, and Ron Low at 7-2-1 in 1979-80). He didn't play a single NHL game last season. Now he has a shutout, a 9-1 record, and a team that sits at 15-3-3.

Oh, and one more subplot: it was Oilers coach Mike Babcock's first game in Toronto since being fired as Maple Leafs coach on Nov. 20, 2019.
