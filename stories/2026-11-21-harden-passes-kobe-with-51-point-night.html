Harden passes Kobe with 51-point night

By Matt

James Harden dropped 51 on the Pacers to pass Kobe Bryant for third-most 50-point games in NBA history — at 37, in a season where he'd barely cracked 30 all year.

James Harden turned back the clock on Friday night. The 37-year-old guard scored 51 points to lead the Cleveland Cavaliers past the Indiana Pacers 128-119 in an Emirates NBA Cup game at Rocket Arena — and in doing so, he recorded his 26th career 50-point game, moving past Kobe Bryant into third place on the all-time list.

Harden shot 15-of-26 from the field, 7-of-11 from three, and a perfect 14-of-14 from the free-throw line, adding six rebounds and six assists in a season-high 40 minutes. It was his first 40-point game since November 22, 2025 — when he dropped 55 for the Clippers against Charlotte, two days shy of a full year earlier — and his best scoring night since joining Cleveland near last season's trade deadline, topping the 36 he put up against Chicago.

How the night unfolded

Harden signaled what was coming early, scoring 15 points in the first nine minutes as the teams fought to a 29-29 draw. He had 24 by halftime, but Cleveland still trailed early in the third before flipping the game for good: up 74-73 four minutes into the period, the Cavs ripped off a 21-4 run, with Harden scoring 13 of those points on a perfect 4-of-4 from the field.

The third quarter belonged to him outright — 18 points on 6-of-8 shooting — and his driving layup at the end of the period pushed the total to 42 and the lead to 105-83. Cleveland outscored Indiana 43-22 in the quarter.

The Pacers made it interesting late, cutting a 23-point lead down to six with 2:05 left after a flagrant foul on Harden gave them two free throws and possession. Donovan Mitchell answered with a driving floater, and Harden closed it himself: a three with 57.9 seconds left made it 124-113, and two more free throws with 42.3 seconds left carried him past 50 and sealed the win. He scored seven of Cleveland's final nine points.

Mitchell added 20 points, 3 rebounds and 3 assists. The win moved Cleveland to 2-1 in East Group B of the NBA Cup and 9-8 overall, with three wins in its last four.

Where the milestone sits in history

Only two players in NBA history have more 50-point games than Harden now does. Wilt Chamberlain leads the way with 118, and Michael Jordan sits second with 31. Bryant finished his career with 25, and Elgin Baylor rounds out the top five with 17.

The number is even more striking in context: Harden is one of only six players age 37 or older to ever score 50 in a game, joining Bryant, Jordan, LeBron James, Jamal Crawford and Steph Curry. At 37 years and 86 days, he is one of the oldest players ever to do it.

The night also moved him up another all-time list. It was his 346th career 30-point game, passing Allen Iverson and tying Dominique Wilkins for the 10th-most in NBA history. Only four guards have more: Jordan, Bryant, Oscar Robertson and Jerry West. And he has now scored 50 or more against 18 different teams, passing Jordan and Bryant, who each did it against 17.

In Cavaliers history, it was the 15th 50-point game by any player — Harden is just the sixth Cavalier ever to do it, and the first since Donovan Mitchell's 71-point night against Chicago on January 2, 2023.

Why this one hit different

This wasn't a star padding a hot streak. Harden entered Friday averaging 16.2 points per game on 38.5% shooting — a career low — and had cracked 25 points just once all season, a 32-point night at Madison Square Garden on November 11. His previous season high was that 32. He blew past it by the third quarter.

The milestone clearly meant something to him afterward. Asked about passing Bryant, Harden called him "my idol" — "the person I watched growing up. The way he cared about the game and the effort he put in is something special. So, we miss him. We miss him a lot."

Harden signed a three-year, $96 million deal to stay in Cleveland this past summer, so the chase isn't over — Jordan's mark of 31 is now five away, and a healthy season of nights like this one puts it within reach. For now, though, the story is simpler: a 37-year-old in his 18th season, in a year where the scoring had mostly dried up, went out and reminded everyone what the Beard used to do every other week.
