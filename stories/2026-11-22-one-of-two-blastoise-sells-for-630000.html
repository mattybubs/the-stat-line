A one-of-two Blastoise sells for $630,000

By Matt

Fanatics Collect sold the 1998 Commissioned Presentation Galaxy Holo Blastoise — one of only two in existence, graded CGC 8.5 — for $630,000, a new record for the card.

The second-most famous starter is closing the gap on Charizard. Fanatics Collect sold the 1998 Commissioned Presentation Galaxy Holo Blastoise, graded CGC 8.5, for $630,000 in its November Premier auction — a new record for any Blastoise card, after 59 bids.

The card

Only two copies of this card are known to exist, and the location of the other one is currently unknown. That scarcity is the whole story: this isn't a pack pull, it's a piece of Pokémon history that was never meant to be sold at all.

Wizards of the Coast printed it in 1998 as a presentation piece — a pitch to Nintendo to show what an English-language Pokémon TCG could look like, ahead of the Base Set launch in 1999. The tells are obvious once you know them: the font on the name and text is different from the Base Set release, the Blastoise artwork by Ken Sugimori is a different image from the final printing, the corners were kept square, and the back is blank.

The copy that sold carries a CGC 8.5 NM-Mint+ grade, with 8.5s on centering, surface, and corners and a 9.5 on edges.

The price, in context

This is the same copy that sold at Heritage Auctions in January 2021 for $360,000 — so the card is up 75% in under six years. That 2021 sale was already a record at the time; the new result nearly doubles it.

The rest of the Blastoise leaderboard shows how far ahead this card sits:

1998 Commissioned Presentation Galaxy Holo: CGC 8.5; $630,000 — Fanatics Collect, Nov 2026

1998 Prototype Test Print (Magic back): CGC 6.5; $241,083.60

1996 Japanese Base Set, No Rarity Symbol: PSA 10; $231,800 — Goldin, Dec 2025

1999 Base Set 1st Edition: PSA 10; $138,880 — Goldin, Feb 2026

The gap between the top two rows tells you everything about what collectors are actually paying for: not the character, and not the grade — the story. The presentation card is the artifact that brought Pokémon cards to the English-speaking world. The next-best Blastoise is a test print that never left the factory floor in any real sense either, but it doesn't carry the same origin-story weight.

The market around it

The sale came out of Fanatics Collect's November Premier auction, which totaled $39.3 million across 604 lots. Pokémon accounted for 89 lots and $7.05 million — 18% of the auction's dollars. The Blastoise was the sixth-highest lot in the sale, sitting just below a $780,000 Jordan/Kobe dual patch auto.

Charizard is still the king by a significant margin — SI's words, and the numbers back it up. But Blastoise is comfortably in second place among the Kanto starters, and the gap is narrowing with every record result like this one.
