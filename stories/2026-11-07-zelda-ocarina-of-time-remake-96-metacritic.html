Ocarina of Time remake is 2026's highest-rated game — a 96 on Metacritic

By Matt

Nintendo's ground-up Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time launched November 5 to a 96 Metascore — the highest-rated game of 2026, and one of the 30 highest-rated games in Metacritic history.

The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is back, and the critics have spoken about as loudly as critics can speak. Nintendo's ground-up remake for Switch 2 launched Thursday, November 5, 2026, carrying a 96 on Metacritic — the highest-rated game of 2026, built from 87 critic reviews with zero mixed or negative notices among them. Players agree so far: the user score sits at 9.6.

The remake is a Switch 2 exclusive, $59.99 digital and $69.99 physical. It is, by the numbers, one of the best-reviewed games ever made — sitting inside Metacritic's all-time top 30, one rung below the original it remakes.

The original is still the one to beat

Here is the beautiful problem with remaking Ocarina of Time: the 1998 Nintendo 64 original holds a 99 on Metacritic, still the highest aggregate score the site has ever recorded for any game. The remake's 96 doesn't topple its own legend. It does, however, clear Nintendo's last attempt by a comfortable margin — Ocarina of Time 3D on the 3DS sits at 94 — and it puts the remake ahead of every other 2026 release, including Resident Evil Requiem, Phantom Blade Zero, Forza Horizon 6, and The Witcher 3 Remastered.

The review spread tells the story. The Guardian, Eurogamer, GamesRadar, and Destructoid all handed out perfect scores. IGN, GameSpot, and Forbes each gave it 9 out of 10. One outlet's verdict, quoted widely this week, called it "the gold standard for any remake of any franchise going forward."

What Nintendo actually built

This is not a remaster. Nintendo rebuilt the game from the ground up for Switch 2, with overhauled visuals across every dungeon and landmark, reworked camera and movement controls, new character dialogue, and a symphonic orchestral soundtrack. It targets and holds 60 frames per second in both docked and handheld modes, and it integrates with the Nintendo Switch App's Zelda Notes service for interactive maps and progress tracking.

There are new additions for returning players, too. A new Hall of Fallen Foes lets players view statues of the enemies they have defeated — a small touch, but the kind that tells you the developers were thinking about people who have already beaten this game three times and want a reason to do it a fourth. Nintendo's own Ask the Developer interview, published around launch, goes deep on just how much work the rebuild took.

Not everything made the cut. The lack of Master Quest — the harder alternative dungeon mode included in previous releases — is the most common complaint in otherwise glowing reviews.

Launch week had its wrinkles

No launch this big goes perfectly. Within hours of release, the remake had been dumped and run on a PC emulator, continuing a pattern that has shadowed Nintendo's biggest Switch 2 launches. And some Amazon physical pre-orders were delayed by up to a week after launch day, leaving buyers waiting while the digital version went live on schedule.

Neither wrinkle has dented the reception. At 96, the remake is also the most critically acclaimed game on Switch 2 to date — a remarkable achievement for a game that first came out before a lot of today's Switch 2 owners were even born.
