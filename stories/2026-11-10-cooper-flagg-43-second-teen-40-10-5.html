Cooper Flagg drops 43 — the second teenager ever with a 40-10-5 game

By Matt

Cooper Flagg dropped 43 points, 10 rebounds and 6 assists as the Mavericks beat the Pacers 125-122 — becoming just the second teenager in NBA history with a 40-10-5 game, after Kevin Durant.

The night

Cooper Flagg is 19 years old, and he just had the kind of night that makes the rest of the league check the calendar. Flagg scored a career-high 43 points on 16-of-25 shooting — 3-of-6 from three, 8-of-8 from the line — with 10 rebounds, 6 assists and 3 steals as the Dallas Mavericks beat the Indiana Pacers 125-122 at American Airlines Center on Monday night.

Thirty of the 43 came after halftime. Sixteen came in the fourth quarter, including two 18-foot pull-up jumpers in the final 62 seconds — the second a go-ahead bucket with 26.3 seconds left. Dallas trailed by 13 late in the first half and still found a way, winning its third straight to move to 7-4. It was Flagg's fourth straight double-double.

He didn't do it alone. Kyrie Irving scored 35 points on 7-of-10 from deep, and the two of them combined for 78. Pascal Siakam led Indiana with 29 — 26 of them in the first half, just 3 after the break — and the Pacers still had seven players in double figures and lost. That's the kind of night it was.

The history

The line puts Flagg in company that barely exists. He is the second teenager in NBA history to post 40 points, 10 rebounds and 5 assists in a game. The first was Kevin Durant, who went for 42 points, 13 rebounds and 6 assists for Seattle at Golden State on April 16, 2008 — the final game of the 2007-08 season. That was 18 years ago.

It was also Flagg's fifth 40-point game as a teenager, the most in NBA history, and it tied him with LeBron James for the most 35-point games by a teenager, with seven. His 14th 30-point game before turning 20 — LeBron had 20 — and per StatMuse, he's the first teenager ever with 40 points, 10 rebounds, 5 assists and 3 steals in a game. He turns 20 on December 21.

The mid-range work is the part that should scare people. Flagg has been doing his damage in tight spaces, off limited dribbles, and Monday night he closed a game from 18 feet — the shot defenses are supposed to be happy to give up. Dallas coach Dusty May called the performance unbelievable for a 19-year-old, and honestly, there isn't a better word for it.
