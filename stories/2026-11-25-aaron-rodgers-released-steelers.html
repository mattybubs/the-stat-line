Steelers release Aaron Rodgers hours after his scorched-earth Pat McAfee interview

By Matt

About an hour after Aaron Rodgers went on the Pat McAfee Show and torched Mike McCarthy, the Steelers released him — ending a two-year tenure in Pittsburgh and sending the four-time MVP to waivers.

The release

Aaron Rodgers is a free man — or about to be one. The Pittsburgh Steelers released the four-time NFL MVP on Wednesday afternoon, about an hour after Rodgers went on ESPN's "Pat McAfee Show" and publicly torched head coach Mike McCarthy, according to multiple reports.

The Pittsburgh Post-Gazette's Gerry Dulac first reported the release, and ESPN's Adam Schefter amplified it within the hour. USA Today, the New York Post, Fox Sports and Bleacher Report all confirmed it shortly after.

It ends a two-year tenure in Pittsburgh that was supposed to be a storybook finish. Rodgers joined the Steelers in June 2025, reuniting with the coach he won a Super Bowl with in Green Bay, and said all year that the 2026 season would be his last in the NFL. Instead, his final act in black and gold was a benching, a tell-all interview, and a pink slip — in that order, inside about 18 hours.

The about-face

The speed of the decision is the tell. NFL Network's Ian Rapoport reported that the Steelers had initially planned to keep Rodgers after benching him — inactive, fourth string, but still on the roster — and changed course after the bombshell interview.

That matches what our earlier post on the interview reported: as of Wednesday morning, there were no immediate plans to release him. The interview changed that. Rodgers used the appearance to accuse McCarthy of falling asleep in meetings, skipping walk-throughs, and failing to prepare — and to say he would never play another snap for Pittsburgh. The team answered by making that permanent.

What happens now

Rodgers goes on waivers. Any team can claim him until 4 p.m. ET on Thursday, and if no team does, he becomes a free agent who can sign anywhere.

Whether anyone will is an open question. The New York Post floated the Patriots and Ravens as teams that could use a quarterback, and NFL insider Dianna Russini reported that Rodgers likely won't draw much interest if he hits the open market. He turns 43 next week and has said this is his final season — so any team that claims him is getting a bridge, not a building block.

Friday's game goes on without him either way. Mason Rudolph starts against the Denver Broncos on Black Friday, with Rodgers inactive — the same arrangement the Steelers announced late Tuesday night, before any of this exploded.

How it ended

The collapse took less than a day. McCarthy publicly backed Rodgers as his QB1 on Monday. On Tuesday night, the Steelers announced Rudolph would start and Rodgers would be inactive. Rodgers learned of the decision from tight end Pat Freiermuth, not from the team, and on Wednesday morning he went on McAfee and said everything he had been sitting on for a decade — the snoring in meetings, the missed walk-throughs, the "1-2-3 Packers" huddle break at halftime of Sunday's loss in Philadelphia.

"At this point, I'm not going back to Pittsburgh," he said. "That ship has definitely sailed."

He was right. The Steelers just made sure of it.

The Pittsburgh numbers

Rodgers started 10 games for Pittsburgh and led the Steelers to a 6-4 record, completing 60.9% of his passes for 2,241 yards with 16 touchdowns and six interceptions. The team sits in an AFC wild-card spot with seven weeks left, and its season now belongs to Rudolph.

The reunion was the whole point of the signing. Rodgers and McCarthy spent 13 seasons together in Green Bay, winning the Super Bowl at the end of the 2010 season — against the Steelers. Rodgers vouched for McCarthy to get the Pittsburgh job after Mike Tomlin stepped down last January, hoping it would be different in McCarthy's hometown, in his dream job, in Rodgers' final year.

It wasn't. And now the most decorated quarterback of his generation is on waivers in the final weeks of his final season, waiting to see if anyone wants the ending to happen somewhere else.
