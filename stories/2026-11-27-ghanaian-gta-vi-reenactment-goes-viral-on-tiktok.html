Ghanaian GTA VI reenactment goes viral on TikTok

By Matt

A group of Ghanaian creators staged GTA VI scenes in a real car park — and the clip passed 1.5 million TikTok likes in a day, with viewers arguing over whether it was real life, gameplay, or AI.

A group of Ghanaian creators turned a car park into Vice City — and the internet couldn't tell the difference. Their real-life GTA VI reenactment passed 1.5 million TikTok likes by Friday, November 27, a day after the Daily Mail shared the clip and declared that the cameraman deserved an Oscar.

The clip is a straight-faced imitation of the game's most familiar sights. A man in a white singlet, blue cap, and denim shorts walks across the car park with the swagger of a GTA protagonist before approaching a black Dodge Challenger. Around him, bystanders play along as non-player characters, mimicking the stiff, repetitive movements the games are famous for. An Intercity STC bus sits in the background, placing the scene firmly in Ghana.

Why it took off

The numbers piled up fast. By Friday the clip had gathered more than 1.5 million likes, over 12,600 comments, and more than 226,000 shares — all within about a day of the Daily Mail posting it.

The reaction was the giveaway that the bit worked. Commenters from around the world said they genuinely couldn't tell whether they were watching real footage, actual gameplay, or AI. That confusion is the whole joke, and the creators nailed it: the walk, the NPC extras, the car, the framing — all of it reads like a phone video of a game that happens to be happening in real life.

The timing helped

The reenactment landed a week after GTA VI itself arrived. Rockstar released the game on Thursday, November 19, and Ghana has been part of the launch story from the start — Ghanaian music features in the game, putting the country's sound in front of a global audience of players. The creators built on that moment, staging their own version of the game's famous scenes rather than just playing it.

It's a reminder of how the launch has played out beyond the sales numbers: the game is so embedded in the culture now that a car park, a Challenger, and a few friends acting like NPCs can pass for Vice City — at least long enough for 1.5 million people to tap like.
