Murray's 45 and Jokic's triple-double set the NBA record for a duo — their eighth together

By Matt

Murray scored a season-high 45 and Jokic posted his 202nd career triple-double as the Nuggets outlasted the Bulls 151-145 in overtime — the eighth time the pair has combined a 40-point night and a triple-double, the most by any duo in NBA history.

The record

Jamal Murray and Nikola Jokic now own a piece of NBA history no duo has ever held.

Sunday was the eighth time in their careers that Murray has scored 40 or more points while Jokic has recorded a triple-double in the same game — passing Wilt Chamberlain and Guy Rodgers (seven) and Tom Gola and Wilt Chamberlain (seven) for the most such games by a duo in NBA history, according to the NBA.

Together they scored 82 points against Chicago, the second-most the pair has ever combined for in a game, regular season or playoffs. The record is 91, set last Christmas against Minnesota.

The night

The Nuggets needed all of it to outlast the Bulls 151-145 in overtime at the United Center. Denver improved to 10-5 and won three straight for the first time this season; Chicago fell to 4-12.

Murray scored a season-high 45 points on 13-of-22 shooting, 6-of-11 from three and 13-of-14 at the free-throw line, adding seven assists, five rebounds, three steals and two blocks. He played every minute of the fourth quarter and overtime and scored 17 points in that stretch, opening overtime with a 27-foot step-back three as Denver scored the first eight points of the extra session and never trailed again.

It was Murray's fifth 40-point game of the season and the 13th of his career — fifth in franchise history, behind Jokic (35), Alex English (33), Carmelo Anthony (19) and David Thompson (18).

Jokic had 37 points, a season-high 18 rebounds and 11 assists for the 202nd triple-double of his regular-season career, seven shy of Russell Westbrook's NBA record of 209.

The 151 points were Denver's most since it put up 157 at Portland on February 20, and they tie for the fourth-most the Nuggets have ever scored on the road. The starters scored 137 of them — Christian Braun had 21 points and eight rebounds, Aaron Gordon had 18 points and eight rebounds, and Cameron Johnson scored 16 on 6-of-9 shooting.

The other side

Chicago nearly stole it anyway. Josh Giddey had 23 points, 14 rebounds and a career-high 22 assists — the first Bulls player with more than 20 assists in a game since 1985, and the first with a 20-point, 20-assist game since December 6, 1969, when Clem Haskins had 23 points and 21 assists against Boston.

The Bulls made 25 three-pointers, tying a franchise record, led by Buddy Hield's 33 points and seven threes. Jalen Smith added a career-high 26.

Chicago wiped out a seven-point deficit in the final 40 seconds of regulation — Giddey's layup tied the score at 131 with 2.1 seconds left, and Jokic's 31-footer missed at the buzzer. Then Denver's starters took over the extra session, and the Bulls never got closer than four.
