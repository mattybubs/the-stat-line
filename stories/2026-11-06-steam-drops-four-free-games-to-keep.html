Steam Drops Four Free Games to Keep

By Matt

Four very different indie games — led by the 99%-rated Heartfall — are free to download and keep on Steam starting today.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

Steam's newest round of free games is live starting today, November 6, 2026 — four games, free to download and keep forever. No catch, no subscription, no time limit on claiming them. Just add them to your library and they're yours.

This is part of a steady stream of free games Steam has delivered throughout 2026, and this week's set is a strong one: a story-driven visual novel RPG, a cosmic-horror cooking game, a found-footage horror short, and a post-apocalyptic tactical RPG. Four very different experiences, and every one of them carries an overwhelmingly positive rating from Steam users.

The four free games

Heartfall: A Tale in Three Lives: 99% positive; Story-driven visual novel RPG

Cucina Stellata: 97% positive; Psychological horror cooking game

Playable Archive: 97% positive; First-person found-footage horror

Outcast Tales: 95% positive; Post-apocalyptic tactical RPG

Heartfall: A Tale in Three Lives — 99% positive

The headliner of the set, and the highest-rated game of the four. Heartfall is a story-driven visual novel RPG from developer Kazehita that follows a character named Grian after he wakes up on a mysterious island. Players explore different storylines, form alliances, uncover secrets, and make decisions that change how the adventure unfolds.

The scale is what stands out: it's a fan project inspired by the YouTube series 3rd Life, featuring over 400,000 words of dialogue, 450 illustrations, and more than 50 original music tracks. A 99% positive rating puts it among the highest-rated free games on Steam — the kind of number usually reserved for established classics, not a free fan project.

Cucina Stellata — 97% positive

The weirdest game in the set, and maybe the most interesting. Developed by Filthy Eden and published by CRITICAL REFLEX, Cucina Stellata is a psychological horror experience that combines traditional Italian cooking with cosmic horror. Players prepare authentic 19th-century Italian recipes in what the game describes as "a kitchen haunted by hunger."

It starts out looking like a straightforward cooking game, then descends into disturbing imagery and strange supernatural elements. It's currently sitting at 97% positive across more than 1,100 Steam reviews, and it's short — about an hour — which makes it an easy weekend pickup.

Playable Archive — 97% positive

For horror fans looking for something different, Playable Archive is a first-person adventure from DarkStone Digital built around mysterious footage of a New England couple who discover a strange object while renovating their home. Players investigate disturbing events — demonic rituals, communicating with the dead — across multiple timelines.

The whole game runs 30–60 minutes and has to be completed in a single sitting, which fits the found-footage format perfectly. It also holds a 97% positive rating on Steam.

Outcast Tales — 95% positive

The final game in the set is something completely different: a post-apocalyptic tactical RPG with turn-based combat and roguelite mechanics. Developed by Hardlane Studio, it has players leading a dysfunctional crew of survivors through a dangerous wasteland filled with mutants, robots, and hostile raiders.

The hook is a personality system that lets characters develop friendships, rivalries, and even romantic relationships based on player decisions. Add in customizable character classes, strategic combat, and Twitch integration, and there's plenty here to keep you busy. It carries a 95% positive rating on Steam.

Why this batch is worth grabbing

Free-game drops on Steam are nothing new, but a set where the lowest-rated game still sits at 95% positive is rare. These aren't throwaway titles — they're four polished indie games across four genres, and the price of admission is zero. Whether you're hunting for something to play this weekend or just adding to the ever-growing backlog, all four are worth claiming while they're free.
