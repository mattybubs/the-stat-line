Montgomery took the whole Texans backfield

By Matt

David Montgomery returned from a four-game groin absence and carried 26 times for 107 yards as the Texans rolled the Colts 26-3 — Houston's first 100-yard rusher of the season.

David Montgomery didn't ease back in. Returning Thursday night after four games out with a groin injury, the Houston Texans running back carried 26 times for 107 yards in a 26-3 rout of the Indianapolis Colts — season highs in both categories, and the first time any Texan has topped 100 rushing yards this season.

There was no ramp-up plan. With starter Woody Marks inactive after suffering a concussion last week, Montgomery took the backfield outright from the opening drive and never gave it back, finishing with 29 touches and 124 yards from scrimmage.

The backfield split

Houston leaned on the run all night, and the split tells the story of who owned it:

David Montgomery: 26; 107; 3; 17; 29

Emari Demercado: 5; 33; 2; -4; 7

Montgomery's 70% share of the team's rushing attempts was the heaviest single-game workload of his season, and the most carries he's logged in a game since Week 4 of 2023. Demercado, claimed off waivers from Dallas last month, handled the change-of-pace role — and that was it. Nobody else in the backfield touched the ball.

The efficiency matched the volume. Montgomery averaged 4.1 yards per carry with a long of 15, and per NFL Next Gen Stats, about 92% of his yardage came inside the tackles — steady, grinding runs that kept the chains moving and the clock running. His workload only grew in the fourth quarter as Houston bled the clock rather than chasing more points.

Why it matters

This was the ground game Houston had been missing all season. The Texans hadn't produced a 100-yard rusher in ten weeks, and the 167 team rushing yards against Indianapolis were their best of the year. Montgomery's return gave C.J. Stroud the balance the offense had lacked since Week 5 — Stroud threw three touchdowns (to Nico Collins, Kayshon Boutte, and rookie Marlin Klein for his first NFL score) on a night he only needed 194 passing yards.

The performance also marked just the third time since 2024 that Montgomery has topped 120 yards from scrimmage, and his best outing since a Week 1 hat trick against the Buffalo Bills. For a back who arrived in Houston as a veteran addition, it was the first game that looked like the role the Texans signed him for.

The test gets harder fast. Houston travels to Baltimore next weekend to face a Ravens defense that has been solid against the run all season, and Marks' return would split the work again. But for one night, the backfield belonged to one man — and the Texans finally looked like a team that can run the ball.
