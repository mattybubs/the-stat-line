Bruins end skid with 4-1 win in Detroit

By Matt

Four unanswered goals, a Pavel Zacha power-play winner and a three-game skid snapped — Boston beats Detroit 4-1 on the 100th anniversary of the Red Wings' first NHL game.

The Boston Bruins snapped their three-game losing streak the hard way — falling behind early in Detroit, then answering with four unanswered goals to beat the Red Wings 4-1 on Wednesday night at Little Caesars Arena.

It was a fitting stage for a bounce-back. The game landed exactly 100 years to the day after Detroit's first NHL game, a 2-0 loss for the then-Cougars to the Bruins at Border Cities Arena in Windsor, Ontario. A century later, Boston spoiled the centennial night the same way.

How it played out

Lucas Raymond put Detroit in front late in the first period, finishing a 2-on-1 rush with a one-timer at 16:58. Boston answered early in the second, when JJ Peterka kept the puck on a 2-on-1 of his own and beat Daniil Tarasov over the glove to tie it at 2:49.

The turning point came on the power play. Pavel Zacha buried a net-front rebound at 11:48 of the second to give Boston its first lead, and the Bruins never gave it back. David Pastrnak added insurance at 5:11 of the third, taking a drop pass from Mason Lohrei on a rush, cutting into the slot and beating Tarasov glove side. Casey Mittelstadt sealed it with an empty-net goal at 19:05.

The top line delivered — and so did the depth

Pastrnak, Zacha and Mittelstadt each finished with a goal and an assist, and the scoring was spread across the lineup: four different Bruins found the net, and six players recorded a point. Morgan Geekie and Lohrei each picked up two assists, with Lohrei setting up both the tying goal and Pastrnak's insurance marker.

Pastrnak's goal was his 10th of the season. With the two points he now leads Boston in goals, assists (18) and points (28). Zacha's winner was his sixth goal of the year, and he sits at 17 points.

In net, Jeremy Swayman stopped 20 of 21 shots for his 10th win of the season. Tarasov made 27 saves for Detroit. Boston also did the dirty work in front of its goalie, blocking 23 shots.

Why this one mattered

Three straight losses had put the Bruins in a corner, and the response was a complete road game: a comeback from an early deficit, a power play that converted on its only chance, and a third period that stayed locked down until the empty-netter. Detroit, meanwhile, has now lost six of its past seven despite coming off a 2-0 win at Tampa Bay on Saturday.

Boston (14-7-1) returns home to TD Garden to host the Washington Capitals on Saturday night. Detroit falls to 8-11-3.
