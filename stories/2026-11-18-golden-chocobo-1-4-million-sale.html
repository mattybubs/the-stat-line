A serialized Golden Chocobo just sold for a reported $1.4 million — more than five times the card's previous record

By Matt

A PSA 10 Golden Chocobo, copy 47 of 77, reportedly changed hands in a $1.4 million private sale — shattering the card's previous confirmed record of $250,000. The buyer is not identified, and no settlement documents have been published, so the figure is an attributed private-sale price, not an auction result.

The sale

A serialized Golden Chocobo from Magic: The Gathering's Final Fantasy set reportedly sold for $1.4 million in a private sale — a new reported high for the card, and more than five times its previous confirmed record.

The sale was reported by Collectr, the card-tracking app, and covered by MTG Rocks on November 17. The card is copy 47 of 77, graded PSA Gem Mint 10, and it also carries an MBA Gold Diamond evaluation — a second independent grading that marks it among the best examples of its kind.

The seller was identified as Adam Cai, known as Pristine_Collectibles, who was tagged in Collectr's announcement. MTG content creator Ben Bateman posted on November 17 about meeting Cai at MagicCon: Atlanta and discussing the deal.

The caveat

This is a reported private sale, not a public auction. No buyer identity has been published, and no settlement document or payment record has been made public. The $1.4 million figure is attributed — real reporting, from a named seller and a named source, but with less public price discovery than a fully documented auction result.

That matters for what the number means. With only 77 serialized copies in existence, one sale cannot establish a reliable market price for the other 76. The previous confirmed Golden Chocobo sale was $250,000 — less than a quarter of this new figure. If the $1.4 million holds up, it resets the ceiling for the card overnight. If it does not, it is a rumor with a big number attached.

The card

The Golden Chocobo — the serialized gold-stamped version of Traveling Chocobo — is the premier chase card of the Final Fantasy set, Magic's best-selling release of all time. Only 77 copies were printed, each uniquely numbered, and they appear only in English-language Collector Boosters. The card carries no rules text at all, a first for a serialized Magic card, and no borders — just the golden bird, the number, and the art.

It is the set's answer to The One Ring: a single serialized chase card that became the face of the entire release, with every Collector Booster opened in part because it might be inside.

What it means

Whether or not $1.4 million becomes the card's settled price, the report itself is the story: a modern Magic card, printed in 2025, is now being talked about in the same breath as the game's most expensive pieces ever. The serialized-chase era that started with The One Ring has a new headline number — and every other Golden Chocobo owner just watched their card's ceiling move.
