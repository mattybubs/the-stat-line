Ravens finalizing trade for Jacoby Brissett after losing Jackson and Huntley

By Matt

With Lamar Jackson headed to injured reserve and Tyler Huntley out for the season, Baltimore is finalizing a trade for Cardinals veteran Jacoby Brissett — though an undrafted rookie still starts Thursday night.

What happened

The Baltimore Ravens are finalizing a trade for Arizona Cardinals quarterback Jacoby Brissett, according to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport and ESPN's Adam Schefter, who both reported the move on Wednesday, November 4.

The deal is not done yet. Compensation for Arizona has not been announced, and the trade is expected to be completed this weekend — which means Brissett will not be available for Thursday night's game against Jacksonville. His earliest possible debut would be Monday, November 16, when Baltimore hosts the Los Angeles Chargers.

How Baltimore got here

This is a quarterback room that has been wiped out in the space of a week.

Lamar Jackson returned to the lineup last Sunday against Buffalo after missing three games with an ankle injury, then hurt the thumb on his throwing hand on the first play from scrimmage. He needs surgery, is headed to injured reserve, and may not play again this season.

His backup, Tyler Huntley, came in and went down with a turf toe injury in the second quarter. Tests revealed the injury is season-ending, and Huntley needs surgery too.

That left undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano, who completed 10 of 21 passes for 91 yards with two turnovers in his relief debut as Baltimore lost 36-10 to the Bills. Fagnano will make his first NFL start on Thursday night against the AFC South-leading Jaguars. He has been dealing with an ankle injury himself, but he was a full participant in practice on Tuesday and Wednesday and carries no injury designation.

Who Brissett is

Brissett, 33, is the definition of a veteran insurance policy. He has started for four different franchises across his career, and he opened this season as Arizona's starter before losing the job to Gardner Minshew on Sunday — benched during the Cardinals' 33-30 come-from-behind win over Dallas, then demoted to emergency third quarterback this week.

He has thrown for 1,719 yards with 10 touchdowns and 7 interceptions in 2026. He is on a one-year, $15.5 million deal he negotiated to remain Arizona's starter this season.

Baltimore had also been linked to Chicago backup Tyson Bagent, but the Ravens moved for Brissett instead. He is not a savior — he is a steady hand, and right now that is exactly what this team needs. A 4-4 club that has lost three straight and sits last in the AFC North cannot keep sending out a quarterback room held together by a rookie and a practice squad.

What it means

The trade tells you everything about how the Ravens view the rest of their season. They are not waving a white flag on a year that started with real expectations. They are buying a bridge.

Fagnano gets Thursday night, on a short week, against one of the league's best defenses. Brissett gets the weekend to learn the offense, and then the job is presumably his until Jackson can return — if Jackson can return.

For a team that has already lost its two best quarterbacks to season-altering injuries, "steady" is the whole plan now.
