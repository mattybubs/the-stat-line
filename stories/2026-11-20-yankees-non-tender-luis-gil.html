Yankees non-tender Luis Gil — two years after he won Rookie of the Year, the 2024 AL ROY is a free agent

By Matt

The Yankees non-tendered former AL Rookie of the Year Luis Gil on Friday, making him a free agent two years after his breakout 2024 season — and the Red Sox are already interested.

The move

The New York Yankees non-tendered Luis Gil on Friday, making the 2024 American League Rookie of the Year a free agent. The Yankees also cut loose reliever Fernando Cruz, leaving both pitchers free to sign anywhere after the club declined to offer them contracts by the tender deadline.

Gil was the headliner of a 44-player non-tender class, and his name stands out for how fast the fall came. Two years ago he was the Yankees' breakout starter. This winter, New York decided he wasn't worth the roughly $2.3 million he was projected to earn in arbitration.

The door isn't fully closed — both pitchers can still negotiate a new deal with the Yankees — but for now, Gil is on the open market, and he won't be short of suitors.

The 2024 season

Gil's rookie year was the real thing. He went 15-7 with a 3.50 ERA in 29 starts, striking out 171 in 151⅔ innings with a four-seam fastball that averaged 96.6 mph. The walks were the flaw — a major league-high 77 — but the stuff was undeniable, and he ran away with the Rookie of the Year vote.

He looked like a long-term piece of the Yankees' rotation. He was 26, cheap, and throwing 97.

The fall

It unraveled from there. A lat strain sidelined Gil for much of 2025, when he went 4-1 with a 3.32 ERA in 11 starts. Then right shoulder inflammation derailed his 2026: he spent much of the season in the minors, was optioned to Triple-A Scranton/Wilkes-Barre in April after struggling in four starts, and finished the year 1-2 with a 4.61 ERA across nine appearances, four of them starts.

There was a flicker at the end. Gil returned on August 31 and posted a 1.13 ERA across eight innings in five appearances. But he didn't pitch in the postseason, and the Yankees had seen enough to walk away from the arbitration number.

His 2026 salary was $2,162,500. The club chose the open roster spot over the raise.

Cruz, too

Cruz's exit is its own story. The 36-year-old right-hander went 4-6 with a 3.13 ERA and two saves in 61 appearances, striking out 67 in 54⅔ innings — but he hasn't pitched since August 25 because of a partially torn ulnar collateral ligament, and he has said he will try to return without surgery.

New York tendered the rest of its arbitration class — Anthony Volpe, Clarke Schmidt, Austin Wells, Ryan Weathers, José Caballero, Luis García Jr. and Heliot Ramos — and earlier Friday agreed with Jonathan Loáisiga on a two-year deal. Closer David Bednar is also back, having accepted the $23.15 million qualifying offer.

What's next

Gil won't wait long. The Red Sox are already interested, according to the Boston Globe's Tim Healey, and a 28-year-old with a Rookie of the Year trophy and a 97 mph fastball will draw a crowd — even one coming off two injury-marred seasons.

The team that signs him is betting on the 2024 version. The Yankees just decided that bet wasn't theirs to make anymore.
