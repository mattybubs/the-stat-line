Devils' Brenden Dillon suspended four games for the illegal check to the head that put Denton Mateychuk in concussion protocol

By Matt

The NHL's Department of Player Safety suspended Devils defenseman Brenden Dillon four games without pay for an illegal check to the head of Columbus defenseman Denton Mateychuk on Wednesday night. Mateychuk left the game and entered concussion protocol.

The league moved fast

The NHL's Department of Player Safety announced Thursday that New Jersey Devils defenseman Brenden Dillon has been suspended for four games, without pay, for an illegal check to the head of Columbus Blue Jackets defenseman Denton Mateychuk.

The hit came early in the first period of Wednesday night's game in Columbus, with the Devils leading 1-0. Dillon was assessed a match penalty on the play, ending his night after just 3:23 of ice time, and the league scheduled a hearing for Thursday before handing down the four-game ban.

Under the collective bargaining agreement, Dillon will forfeit $82,474.24 in salary. The money goes to the Players' Emergency Assistance Fund.

What the league saw

The Department of Player Safety released its usual explainer video with the ruling, and it did not leave much room for debate. The hit satisfied both elements of the illegal check to the head rule: the head was the main point of contact, and the contact was avoidable.

"Once the puck was gone, Dillon finished a high, hard check, elevating upward and unnecessarily and making Mateychuk's head the main point of contact on a hit where such head contact was avoidable," the league said in the video.

Mateychuk had just moved the puck to a teammate near the blue line and continued into the Columbus zone when Dillon launched his right shoulder into the side of the 22-year-old's head. Mateychuk left the game and did not return, entering concussion protocol. He has been ruled out for the Blue Jackets' game Friday against the Detroit Red Wings.

This is not Dillon's first trip through the process. The 36-year-old veteran had previously been fined once and suspended twice by the league for infractions, which is part of why a four-game ban landed on a hit that, for a first-time offender, might have drawn less.

What it costs New Jersey

Dillon will miss Friday's home game against Calgary, Saturday's home game against Winnipeg, Tuesday's home game against San Jose, and Thursday's trip to Ottawa. He is eligible to return Saturday, Dec. 5, at Pittsburgh.

The timing stings for a Devils team in the middle of a four-games-in-seven-days stretch. New Jersey gutted out a 3-2 shootout win in Columbus on Wednesday despite losing Dillon minutes into the game, but the blue line now has to cover four games without one of its most physical defensemen — and do it while the schedule offers no breathing room.
