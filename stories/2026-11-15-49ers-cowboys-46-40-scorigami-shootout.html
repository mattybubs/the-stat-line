46-40! The 49ers and Cowboys just played the first game in NFL history to end that way

By Matt

San Francisco outlasted Dallas 46-40 at AT&T Stadium in the first 46-40 final in NFL history — a 36-point fourth quarter, 584 yards of 49ers offense, and four stars rewriting record books on the same night.

The first 46-40 ever

The San Francisco 49ers beat the Dallas Cowboys 46-40 on Sunday at AT&T Stadium, and the final score had never happened before in NFL history. No game had ever ended 46-40 — a genuine Scorigami, on a day when the offenses made it look easy.

The fourth quarter alone produced 36 points. San Francisco piled up 584 total yards, the fifth-most in 49ers history and the third-most Dallas has ever allowed — and the most the Cowboys have ever given up in a home game. The win moved San Francisco to 8-1, still atop the NFC West. Dallas fell to 4-6.

This was the 42nd meeting between the two franchises, and San Francisco's fifth straight win in the rivalry. The 49ers now lead the all-time series 22-19-1. It was also, per the Associated Press, the first time in Cowboys history that a defense allowed both a 70-plus-yard touchdown run and a 70-plus-yard touchdown pass in the same game. And per the Elias Sports Bureau, San Francisco's three touchdowns of 60-plus yards tied the most by any team in a game over the last 50 years.

49ers: 7; 10; 14; 15; 46

Cowboys: 10; 6; 3; 21; 40

Dallas actually led for much of the first half, going up 16-10 in the second quarter before San Francisco answered with a 28-3 run that built a 38-19 fourth-quarter lead. The Cowboys then scored 21 in the final frame, twice cutting the deficit to six, but never got the ball back with a chance to tie. The last Dallas drive ended at its own 9-yard line with nine seconds left, after a deep shot fell incomplete and a series of laterals went nowhere.

Brock Purdy: a career day, under pressure all night

Purdy finished 22-of-28 for 389 yards with three touchdowns and no interceptions, a 154.5 passer rating, and added 21 yards on two carries. The yardage total is the best of his career.

It did not come clean. Dallas sacked him five times, four of them in the first half, and still he completed 78.6 percent of his throws. The signature play came with 2:26 left in the second quarter: on third-and-8 from his own 26, Purdy escaped the blitz, bought time, and found Christian McCaffrey releasing out of the backfield. McCaffrey caught it at the 40 and took it 60 more yards for the score that gave San Francisco a 17-16 halftime lead.

Purdy is now 4-0 all-time against Dallas. He entered the game with five touchdowns and no interceptions in the rivalry, and added three more scores to that ledger on Sunday.

Christian McCaffrey: the most dangerous catch in football

McCaffrey caught all four of his targets for 161 yards and two touchdowns, and ran 13 times for 62 yards and a score. That is 223 scrimmage yards and three total touchdowns, with the receiving yardage a career high.

Both receiving touchdowns were career-long plays: a 74-yarder just before halftime and a 66-yarder late in the fourth, when Dallas inexplicably had linebacker Von Miller in coverage on him. His rushing score was a 16-yard burst off left tackle that stretched across the goal line and made it 38-19.

The 74-yarder was also his 38th career receiving touchdown, extending the running-back record he set a week ago in Las Vegas by passing Marshall Faulk. San Francisco's ground game as a whole ran for 214 yards at 7.4 yards a carry, and rookie Kaelon Black added a 25-yard touchdown in the third quarter, running clean through two defensive backs. Keenan Allen, in his 49ers debut, caught five passes for 61 yards.

Dak Prescott: five touchdowns, two records, and still a loss

Prescott went 30-of-51 for 343 yards with five touchdowns and one interception, a 103.6 rating. The five touchdown passes tie his career high, the second time he has done it.

The fifth of them made history. His fourth-quarter touchdown to CeeDee Lamb was the 249th passing touchdown of his career, passing Tony Romo's 248 for the Cowboys' franchise record. Prescott set a franchise mark on a night his team lost by six — the cruelest kind of milestone game.

Lamb's touchdown, a 1-yarder with 47 seconds left, plus Prescott's two-point conversion run, cut the lead to 46-40. Ryan Flournoy had caught an 11-yard score earlier in the rally and finished with 71 yards. But the onside kick was recovered by San Francisco, and Dallas never saw the ball again with a real chance.

George Pickens: the return, the milestone, the hat trick

Pickens caught 11 passes for 145 yards and three touchdowns on 18 targets, with a long of 39. All three marks tell the same story: a career high in touchdown catches, a season high in yards, in his first game back from the hamstring injury that cost him Week 9.

The first score did the historical work. His 35-yard first-quarter touchdown catch pushed him past 2,000 receiving yards as a Cowboy — and made him the third Cowboy ever to reach that mark in his first 26 games with the franchise, joining Terrell Owens (2,208) and Bob Hayes (2,136). His other two scores went for 39 and 5 yards. Lamb finished with seven catches for 72 yards and the record-setting touchdown.

What it means

San Francisco stays on the road this week, practicing in Colorado Springs before a Sunday night showdown with the 7-2 Minnesota Vikings in Mexico City on November 22. Dallas, now 4-6, hosts the Tennessee Titans next Sunday. The 49ers look like the NFC's team to beat; the Cowboys look like a team that can score with anyone and still lose, because on Sunday even a franchise-record night from their quarterback and a hat trick from their No. 1 receiver were not enough.
