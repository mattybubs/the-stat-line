Fat Perez, Riggs and Bobby Ray win the Internet Invitational 5 & 3

By Matt

Blue never trailed at Payne's Valley — Fat Perez, Riggs and Bobby Ray closed out Big John, Ryan Whitney and Salim 5 & 3 to split the $4 million purse and lift the Cody 'Beef' Franke Trophy.

Blue never trailed

The Internet Invitational is over, and it belongs to Team Blue. Fat Perez, Sam "Riggs" Bozoian and Bobby Ray beat Big John, Ryan Whitney and Salim Sirur 5 & 3 in the 18-hole alternate-shot final at Payne's Valley, closing the match out on the 15th hole with three still to play.

The margin tells the whole story. Blue went 1 up on the first hole and never gave it back — 2 up after three, 3 up at the turn, 4 up after ten, and 5 up after fifteen. Red never led a single hole all day. In a format built to swing wildly, with three wildly different handicaps sharing every shot, this one never really swung at all.

Each of the three winners takes home $1,338,333 from the $4 million purse, and Bobby Ray's total runs $25,000 higher — the 9th-hole money he banked alongside The Duke back on Day 2. On top of the split, the trio also takes home a Tracker boat from Bass Pro Shops. The trophy they lifted is the Cody "Beef" Franke Trophy, named for last year's winner.

The drama: Salim, and the finale nobody could look away from

The score says Blue won 5 & 3. The internet says something else decided it — or at least ruined the watch. Salim Sirur, Red's third man, drank through the episode on camera: a tequila shot on just about every hole, beers on top of it, asking the cart girl for another round. Australian Golf Digest's episode recap named him "the drunkest man alive" and described him as "blackout drunk" going into the final, showing no signs of stopping.

It bled into the team. At one point Salim took a beer out of Big John's hand, causing real friction inside the group, and Ryan Whitney spent part of the match coaching Big John on keeping Salim's vibes up rather than letting it show. Big John, for his part, came out of it as the sympathetic figure of the finale — viewers calling him "all heart" and the MVP of a losing team.

The audience reaction has been brutal. Across Barstool's, Portnoy's and the Invitational's own posts, the comments are dominated by viewers calling the finale unwatchable, blaming Salim's drinking for costing his teammates $1.3 million each, and calling for him not to be invited back. Some of it is cruel; a smaller thread defends him or jokes about it. The dominant tone is anger that a $4 million match came down to a guy who could barely stand up straight.

There is a twist that complicates the whole thing: Salim himself has said he's on Day 87 of a 90-day sober challenge — he knew before the episode aired that he needed a lifestyle change. Golf Digest called the whole thing "an all-time drunk golf performance" while noting he's since reeled the drinking in. Whether the finale becomes a turning point for him or just the episode everyone remembers him for is the open question hanging over next year.

The trophy means something

That name is the emotional center of this whole series. Cody "Beef" Franke won the first Internet Invitational in 2025 alongside Brad Dalke and Francis Ellis, beating Paige Spiranac, Frankie Borrelli and Malosi Togisala 1 up in the final. He died suddenly at 31 in October 2025, the day before the series aired. Barstool and the Fore Play crew helped set up a scholarship fund in his name at his alma mater, Ferris State, and his family opened this year's first episode to introduce the Beef Franke Foundation, which funds golf-management scholarships and a Heart of the Course Award for the people who keep courses running. They finished by teaching the room Beef's signature send-off: two claps and a woo.

So when the trophy got handed over at Payne's Valley, it wasn't just a prop with a funny name. It was the whole point of the week.

How Blue got here

Nobody had this trio winning it when the field of 64 was drafted. All six finalists came out of Ryan Ruffels' Team White, which survived a wild three days at Big Cedar Lodge in Ridgedale, Missouri.

Day 1 at Ozarks National ended with Ruffels and Peter Finch's team tied 8–8, and Ruffels going through on a sudden-death playoff — Big John, nearly left out of the event entirely, holing the putt on the second extra hole. Defending champions Dalke's Red lost 10.5–5.5 to Wesley Bryan's Green and went home. Day 2 at Buffalo Ridge was sixteen singles matches, and Ruffels closed it out 12.5–6.5, with Salim winning the 17th and 18th to clinch it on the last.

Then came the twist: four of Ruffels' sixteen were going home that night. The captains played a sudden-death hole under the lights, and Grant Horvat's team went out when Ryan Whitney's par beat Jake Adams on the next hole — taking Horvat, Adams, Spiranac and Jon Gruden with it. The last twelve were split into four teams of three for nine holes of alternate-shot stroke play at Payne's Valley, top two through. Blue won it at two over. Red got through at four over only because Salim holed a bogey putt on the last that would have tied Green if he'd missed.

On paper, the final was close: Blue's combined handicap averaged 2.1 (Fat Perez 0.3, Riggs 4.1, Bobby Ray 2.0) against Red's 2.9 (Big John +3.1, Whitney 2.1, Salim 9.8). A million simulations gave Blue a 57% edge. On the course, it wasn't close at all.

What this event is

For anyone still catching up: the Internet Invitational is Barstool Sports and Bob Does Sports' creator golf tournament, hosted by Dave Portnoy, who runs the show as host and rules commissioner alongside Big Cat and former PGA Tour pro Kevin Kisner. The second edition filmed August 10–17, 2026 at Big Cedar Lodge and rolled out in six episodes on YouTube — Mondays and Thursdays, October 26 through today — free on the Fore Play Golf, Bob Does Sports and Internet Invitational channels, presented by Dunkin'.

The first two episodes alone drew more than 16 million combined views. It's the rare YouTube golf series that plays like a real sports event: a draft, team play, a rules committee with actual teeth — the "Gruden Rule" lets them veto any pairing that looks like a thrown match — and side pots everywhere, including a $500,000 Reese's hole-in-one prize on the par 3 each day and $25,000-a-man payouts on the Day 2 9th hole. The production team cut it from more than 24 terabytes of raw footage, and the 2025 series was a Shorty Awards finalist for Best Video Series.

It also never stops being messy, which is half the appeal. The Duke, the weakest golfer in the 64-man field, became the story of Episode 5 when his play cost Ruffels and Marissa Wenzler their shot at the final — then took to Instagram to apologize to his teammates. Portnoy spent Wednesday addressing whether The Duke gets invited back at all.

What's next

Portnoy has confirmed the Invitational will be an annual event, and the 2026 edition proved the format has legs: a bigger field, a purse four times the size of year one, and a final that delivered a clean, decisive winner. The champions list now reads two lines long — Dalke, Ellis and Beef in 2025; Fat Perez, Riggs and Bobby Ray in 2026. Three guys who started the week as long shots walk away as millionaires, and the trophy they lifted carries the name of the guy who won it first.
