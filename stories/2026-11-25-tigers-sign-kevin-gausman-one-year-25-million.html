Tigers land Kevin Gausman on a one-year, $25 million deal — the latest strike in Detroit's pre-lockout spending spree

By Matt

Detroit agreed to a one-year, $25 million deal with veteran right-hander Kevin Gausman on Wednesday — the fifth significant move of a Tigers offseason that refuses to wait for the lockout.

The deal

The Detroit Tigers are not waiting for the lockout. On Wednesday, they agreed to a one-year, $25 million contract with veteran right-hander Kevin Gausman, first reported by ESPN's Jeff Passan and confirmed by the Detroit Free Press, theScore, MLB Trade Rumors and TSN. The deal is pending a physical.

It is the fifth significant move of a Tigers offseason that has been all gas since the World Series ended: a six-year extension for Troy Melton, a one-year deal for former AL Rookie of the Year Luis Gil, a one-year deal for infielder Ha-Seong Kim, a deal for reliever Emilio Pagán — and now a $25 million bet on a 35-year-old workhorse starter.

The 2026 season

Gausman split last season between two teams. He opened the year in Toronto, going 5-10 with a 4.38 ERA in 23 starts, before the Blue Jays traded him to the Chicago Cubs at the August 2 deadline for prospects Brett Bateman and Ty Southisene. In nine starts for Chicago he went 4-2 with a 4.83 ERA.

Put it together and the line reads 9-12 with a 4.51 ERA, a 1.27 WHIP and 184 strikeouts across 177⅔ innings in 32 starts. The ERA was his worst since 2019, and the 26 home runs he allowed were a career high. But the underlying numbers tell a kinder story: his 3.74 FIP sat nearly eight-tenths of a run below his ERA, and his strikeout rate held steady at roughly 24.5 percent — the splitter is still missing bats.

Why Detroit is buying

The Tigers are buying the one thing Gausman has never stopped providing: innings. He has made at least 30 starts in every season from 2016 through 2026, except for the COVID-shortened 2020 campaign, when he made all 12 of his scheduled starts. He turns 36 in January, and Detroit is paying him like a pitcher it expects to take the ball every fifth day.

The résumé backs up the price. Gausman was the No. 4 overall pick in the 2012 draft out of LSU, is a two-time All-Star, and owns a 121-125 career record with a 3.87 ERA and 2,138 strikeouts in 354 starts. He just finished a five-year, $110 million contract with Toronto and pitched in the 2025 World Series with the Blue Jays. The $25 million is a raise on his old deal's average annual value — and a one-year commitment, so Detroit carries no long-term risk if the age-35 season turns out to be the start of a decline rather than a blip.

The lockout clock

The timing is the story as much as the player. Major League Baseball's collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 1, and the owners are expected to lock the players out the moment it does — a fight that reached federal court on Wednesday, when MLB sued the players' association. Once the lockout starts, trades and free-agent signings freeze.

Detroit is racing that clock like everyone else. MLB Trade Rumors notes the Tigers have a full 40-man roster and still need to make the Gil and Kim deals official alongside Gausman's, meaning three roster spots have to open before any of the three can be announced. The Gausman agreement lands with six days to spare — and the Tigers' rotation, fronted by Melton and Jackson Jobe, now has a veteran anchor who has done this for a decade.
