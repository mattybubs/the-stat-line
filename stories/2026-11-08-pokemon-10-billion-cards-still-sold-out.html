Pokémon printed 10 billion cards a year — and still sold out

By Matt

The Pokémon Company printed roughly 10 billion cards in the fiscal year ending March 2026 — past 85 billion lifetime — and it still wasn't enough. Output has been flat for three years because the printers are already at maximum capacity, and the next big plant doesn't open until 2028.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

What happened

The Pokémon Company printed roughly 10 billion Pokémon TCG cards in the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026. The lifetime total is now past 85 billion cards since the game launched in 1996. The cards are printed in 16 languages and sold in more than 90 countries.

And it still was not enough. Shops are still selling out on day one.

That is the number at the center of the story: 10 billion sounds like a flood, but it is a ceiling. Output has been flat at around 10 billion a year for three years — the high-water mark was about 11.9 billion in fiscal 2023/24 — because the company has been printing at maximum capacity since January 2025. There is no more room on the presses. The only way to make more cards is to build more presses, and that takes years.

The sellout problem

The demand side is not a mystery. A London shop's allocation rose 40% and still sold out on day one, according to a Brit Brief report from a London Comic Con panel on November 6. That is the pattern everywhere: allocations go up, demand goes up faster, and the shelves are empty by the afternoon.

The timing makes it worse. Mega Evolution – Delta Reign and the 30th Celebration set launched on November 6, right into the holiday season, when demand is at its peak. New sets used to be a restock event. Now they are a race, and the race is over before most people get to the store.

The fix is coming — slowly

The Pokémon Company is building a new printing campus: 1.27 million square feet, due for completion in 2027, with full operation expected in late 2028. When it is running, it would roughly double the company's printing capacity.

That is a real fix, and it is also two years away. Between now and then, the math does not change: 10 billion cards a year, flat, against demand that keeps growing. The company is not holding back supply to create hype — it is printing as fast as it physically can, and it is still not enough.

What it means

For collectors, the takeaway is simple: the scarcity is structural, not artificial. The cards are not hard to find because the company is being cute with supply. They are hard to find because the printers are maxed out and the next plant is two years from full operation. That means day-one sellouts are the normal state of things until 2028, and the secondary market is where the action is — for better and for worse.

Ten billion cards a year. Eighty-five billion lifetime. And still sold out. That is the Pokémon economy right now.
