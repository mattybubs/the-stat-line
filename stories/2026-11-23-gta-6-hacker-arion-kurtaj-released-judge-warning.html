The hacker who leaked GTA 6 in 2022 just walked free — with a warning from the judge

By Matt

Arion Kurtaj, 21, was sentenced to seven years at Woolwich Crown Court on Monday and released the same day on licence. The judge called him 'a very talented young man' — then told him: 'Don't let this happen again.'

Four days after Grand Theft Auto VI finally launched, the teenager who leaked it to the world in 2022 walked out of court a free man.

Arion Kurtaj, 21, was sentenced at Woolwich Crown Court on Monday to seven years in prison — and released the same day on licence, because the time he had already spent in a secure hospital and on remand covered the term. The judge reduced the sentence from nine years to account for that time. Kurtaj pleaded guilty on November 16 to the Rockstar hack and a string of other cybercrimes, carried out as a member of the Lapsus$ hacking group.

The judge, Mr Justice Turner, had two things to say to him. "You are a very talented young man," he told Kurtaj. Then the warning that sums up the whole hearing: "Don't let this happen again."

The leak that showed the world GTA 6

In September 2022, Kurtaj broke into Rockstar's internal systems and stole 90 clips of unfinished GTA 6 gameplay — nearly an hour of footage — then published it online under the username TeaPotUberHacker after threatening to release the game's source code unless Rockstar contacted him on Telegram. The leak was the world's first real look at the game: it confirmed the Vice City setting and revealed that Lucia would be one of the playable protagonists, a detail Rockstar had been keeping under wraps.

Rockstar has said the breach cost it around $5 million to recover from, plus thousands of hours of staff time. The studio called itself "extremely disappointed" at the time, then released the game's first trailer later that year, officially confirming the female lead the leak had exposed.

The hack itself has become almost as famous as the leak. Kurtaj pulled it off from a Travelodge hotel room in Bicester, England, while under police protection and on bail for other hacking offenses — with his computer confiscated. He used an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into the hotel television, along with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, to get into Rockstar's systems.

A long road to sentencing

Kurtaj's path to Monday's hearing was unusual. In late 2023, a judge determined he was unfit to stand trial because of his acute autism and ordered him held in a secure hospital, finding he posed a high risk of serious harm through his hacking skill. Earlier this year he was assessed as fit to stand trial and moved to a conventional prison in January. His defense team argued his autism spectrum disorder played a role in his offending, saying he found the online world more comfortable than face-to-face interaction.

His release comes with a five-year Serious Crime Prevention Order restricting his online activity — including a ban on using Telegram and Discord, the apps at the center of his offending.

The leak era isn't over

Kurtaj's release lands in the same week GTA 6 finally reached players, and the leak saga around the game hasn't stopped. A leaker going by Cyberleek posted GTA 6 clips in the months leading up to launch, and on Monday released the missing executable from its leaked Xbox build — the final piece unofficial PC port projects have been waiting for.

Kurtaj, for his part, can now go home and play the game he leaked four years ago. The judge's warning goes with him.
