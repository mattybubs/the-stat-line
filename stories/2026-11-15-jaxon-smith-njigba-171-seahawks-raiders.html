Jaxon Smith-Njigba goes for 171 as the Seahawks spoil Fernando Mendoza's first start

By Matt

Jaxon Smith-Njigba had one catch at halftime. He finished with 171 yards and the game-breaking touchdown — and Seattle's backup quarterback and defense did the rest in a 21-13 win over the Raiders.

The day

The Seattle Seahawks beat the Las Vegas Raiders 21-13 at Allegiant Stadium on Sunday, spoiling the first NFL start of No. 1 pick Fernando Mendoza. Jaxon Smith-Njigba caught eight passes for 171 yards and a touchdown on 10 targets, including a 71-yard score. The touchdown came from backup quarterback Drew Lock, who went 14-of-17 for 240 yards with two touchdowns, no interceptions and a 158.0 passer rating — but lost two fumbles. Sam Darnold was out with ribs and quad injuries for a second straight week. Mendoza finished 17-of-29 for 143 yards and was sacked six times. Zach Charbonnet carried 20 times for 94 yards.

A quiet first half, then the third quarter

Smith-Njigba had exactly one catch for 11 yards entering halftime, and Seattle trailed 10-7. The Raiders had struck first after Lock fumbled on the game's second play from scrimmage, and Mendoza capped an 11-play drive with a 10-yard touchdown run on third-and-goal. Lock answered with a 34-yard touchdown pass to Cooper Kupp, but a Matt Gay field goal sent Las Vegas into the break ahead.

Then the third quarter happened. Smith-Njigba piled up 144 yards on six catches in the quarter alone — more than the Raiders' entire offense managed in the second half. Seattle scored on its first two drives after the break and never looked back.

The 71-yarder

The game-breaker came on third-and-3 midway through the third quarter. Lock escaped pressure, fired deep over the middle, and Smith-Njigba did the rest, out-running the Raiders defense for a 71-yard touchdown that made it 21-10. It was the second long scoring throw of the day for Lock after scrambling to buy time — and it was Smith-Njigba's 10th touchdown of the season, matching his career high.

The go-ahead score had come one drive earlier, when rookie Jadarian Price ran in a 12-yard touchdown to cap an 87-yard march. Lock hit Smith-Njigba twice for 53 yards on that drive.

The defense closed the door

Seattle's league-leading defense took the game over after halftime. The Raiders went three-and-out on five straight possessions to open the second half, managing minus-17 yards of total offense in that stretch, and punted seven times in a row before finally adding a late field goal. Las Vegas finished with 163 total yards — 27 of them after halftime — and the six sacks kept Mendoza under constant pressure. A second Lock fumble in the fourth quarter kept the Raiders alive, but Gay's 43-yarder with 2:04 left only cut it to eight, and a 15-yard naked bootleg by Lock helped Seattle run out the clock.

What it means

Seattle heads into its bye week at 6-4, winners of three of four, while Las Vegas fell to 4-6. Smith-Njigba's season total now leads the league at 1,207 receiving yards, and he has seven games this season with 100-plus yards and a touchdown — matching Randy Moss in 2007 for the most such games through the first 10 games of a season. He is also just the third player ever, along with Tory Holt in 2003 and Kupp in 2021, to record at least 75 catches, 1,100 yards and 10 touchdowns in his team's first 10 games.

Lock's 158.0 rating was the highest of his career and the second-highest in franchise history. For Mendoza, the debut still had bright spots: the Heisman winner who led Indiana to a national title was 11-of-13 for 90 yards in the first half and scored on the ground. He got the start after Las Vegas traded Kirk Cousins to Minnesota on November 10. Jason Myers missed a 44-yard field-goal attempt in the fourth quarter that would have stretched Seattle's lead.

By the numbers

Raiders second-half drives: Five straight three-and-outs to open the half
