Broncos Stun Chiefs 28-24: Fifteen Unanswered Points and a Goal-Line Sack to Take the AFC West

By Matt

Down 24-13 in the fourth, Denver scored 15 unanswered points and sacked Patrick Mahomes on the final play to beat Kansas City 28-24 and take sole possession of the AFC West.

The Denver Broncos were down 24-13 in the fourth quarter to Patrick Mahomes. Then they scored 15 unanswered points, survived a goal-line stand for the ages, and walked off with a 28-24 win over the Kansas City Chiefs that gives them sole possession of first place in the AFC West.

It was only the third loss of Mahomes' career in 83 games in which his team led by 10 or more points in the final period. This one had everything: a wiped-out game-winning touchdown, a missed field goal, and a sack on the final play.

The comeback

Mahomes had been brilliant. He finished 30-of-41 for 321 yards and three touchdowns, and his third, a 4-yard pass to Noah Gray, made it 24-13. Kansas City had led most of the way, and Denver's offense had gone quiet for long stretches.

Then the fourth quarter belonged to the Broncos. Bo Nix, who finished 20-of-32 for 222 yards and two touchdowns, led a drive capped by a touchdown on fourth-and-goal, plus a two-point conversion. Harrison Butker missed a critical fourth-quarter field goal that opened the door wider, and Denver took over near midfield. On the first play of the next drive, Jonah Coleman leaked out and took a pass 51 yards inside the five, then punched it in on the next play for a 5-yard touchdown run with 1:56 left. Denver led 28-24.

Emmett Johnson added five catches for 128 yards and a touchdown for Kansas City in the loss.

The finish

Kansas City drove to Denver's 1-yard line with a chance to win it. Mahomes appeared to throw a game-winning touchdown on a quick slant to Rashee Rice in the waning seconds, but it was nullified by a false start on offensive lineman Jaylon Moore. Pushed back five yards, the Chiefs got one more play: fourth-and-goal with four seconds left, and Malcolm Roach sacked Mahomes to end it. Mahomes' desperation pass fell into the hands of one of his own linemen, but he was ruled down, and Mile High erupted.

Chiefs coach Andy Reid called the flag "one man's opinion" afterward. Mahomes pointed to penalties and miscommunication. None of it changed the result.

What it means

Kansas City falls to 5-2 with its second straight loss, its first losing streak after a 5-0 start. Denver improves to 6-2, alone atop the AFC West for the first time this season, with revenge for its Week 1 loss to the Chiefs. The Broncos have made a habit of fourth-quarter comebacks this season. This was their best one yet.
