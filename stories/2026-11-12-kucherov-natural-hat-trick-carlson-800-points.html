Kucherov's natural hat trick buries Buffalo — and Carlson hits 800 points on the same play

By Matt

Nikita Kucherov scored a natural hat trick in the third period as Tampa Bay beat Buffalo 8-3. John Carlson had five points in the win, and his assist on Kucherov's third goal was the 800th point of his career.

The night

The Tampa Bay Lightning led Buffalo 4-0 midway through the second period on Wednesday night, and then nearly gave the whole thing away. The Sabres scored three straight — Josh Doan and Jason Zucker late in the second, Tage Thompson 33 seconds into the third — and a blowout was suddenly a one-goal game at KeyBank Center.

Then Nikita Kucherov did what he does.

Fifty-two seconds after Thompson's goal, Kucherov went in alone on a breakaway and beat Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen with a backhand under his left arm to make it 5-3 — ending Luukkonen's night in the process. At 4:11 he one-timed a John Carlson feed from the right circle to make it 6-3. At 14:13, from the same spot, on the power play, off another Carlson feed, he completed the natural hat trick: three goals, one period, no one else scoring in between. Brandon Hagel added his second of the night at 15:06 for the 8-3 final.

Kucherov finished with three goals and an assist. Carlson finished with a goal and four assists — five points — and the assist on the hat-trick goal was the 800th point of his NHL career. Jake Guentzel added four assists of his own, Anthony Cirelli had three, and Andrei Vasilevskiy made 28 saves. Tampa Bay improved to 12-4-2 and is 5-1-2 in its last eight. Buffalo fell to 8-7-3, losers of three of four.

The history

The natural hat trick was the third of Kucherov's career — the most in Lightning history. It was his eighth hat trick overall, tying Martin St. Louis for the second-most in franchise history. Steven Stamkos, with 13, is the only name ahead of him.

Carlson's milestone landed on the same play, which is fitting: 800 career points (176 goals, 624 assists) for a defenseman who has spent his whole career setting up exactly that kind of goal. "Nothing he does surprises me now," Carlson said of Kucherov afterward. Coach Jon Cooper was blunter: "'Kuch' did a Kuch thing."

It is worth remembering where this story was a week ago. Kucherov scored the overtime winner against Minnesota on Sunday, seven days after his trade request became public — and then made it clear, again, that he intends to keep playing for the Lightning. A natural hat trick in a game his team nearly let slip is the loudest possible version of that message.

The fine print

Two footnotes from a wild night in Buffalo: rookie Sam O'Reilly, making his NHL debut, picked up his first career point with the secondary assist on Yanni Gourde's goal. And defenseman Erik Cernak left the game in the second period after colliding with the curved glass — Cooper said he is "probably out tomorrow" when Tampa Bay visits Columbus on Thursday. Cirelli played his 600th NHL game.
