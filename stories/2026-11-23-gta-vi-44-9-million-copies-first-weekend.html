GTA VI sold 44.9 million copies in its first weekend — and cleared $4 billion in four days

By Matt

Alinea Analytics estimates Grand Theft Auto VI sold through 44.9 million copies by the end of its first weekend — more than $4 billion in four days, with 40.8 million players logging in on Sunday alone.

Grand Theft Auto VI did not slow down after launch day. It sped up. New estimates from Alinea Analytics put the game's first weekend at a scale no launch has ever reached — a weekend total that already sits alongside the biggest box office weekends in movie history, and a player count on Sunday alone that would fill every NFL stadium in America several times over.

The weekend by the numbers

Copies sold through to players, Nov 19–22: 44.9 million (est.)

Gross revenue in four days: Over $4.0 billion (est.)

Players logged in on Sunday, Nov 22: 40.8 million (est.)

PlayStation 5 share: 33.7 million copies, 75.1%

Xbox Series X\: S share; 11.2 million copies, 24.9%

Players already past 20 hours: About 9 million

The figures come from Alinea Analytics, as reported by GameSpot, Insider Gaming and WCCFTech. The important distinction: these are sell-through estimates — copies actually bought by players, not units shipped to stores and sitting on shelves. And they are third-party estimates, not official Take-Two figures. Take-Two has not published its own weekend numbers, and won't report net bookings until its February earnings. Until then, Alinea's read is the best public picture of the weekend — and it moved in only one direction.

How the weekend built on launch day

Take-Two's own official day-one figures set the floor: 32.5 million units sold before release, and $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales, which the publisher called a record for entertainment in any medium. The weekend estimates show what happened next. Roughly another 12 million copies moved between Friday and Sunday, and revenue pushed past the four-billion mark — with more than three billion of it coming from PlayStation alone.

The split barely moved all weekend. PlayStation held about three-quarters of sales from the first day through Sunday, and the same share showed up in Sunday's player count, with about 76% of the day's logins on PS5. The audience was not just buying the game and putting it down, either. About one player in five had already logged more than 20 hours by Sunday night — four days after launch.

The movie comparison

The number that puts the weekend in perspective is not from gaming at all. Alinea's Rhys Elliott pointed out that the four-day revenue is roughly equal to the combined box office of this year's two biggest movies — Spider-Man: Brand New Day and The Odyssey. Two of the year's biggest theatrical releases, added together, are worth about what one game did in four days on two consoles.

The comparison to the old record is even starker. GTA V earned roughly $800 million in its first 24 hours in September 2013, on 11.21 million copies — a mark Guinness World Records recognizes as the best single-day haul for any video game. It needed three days to pass $1 billion. The new game cleared that bar before most players had finished downloading it, then kept going until the weekend total was five times the old game's first day.

What's still to come

The most remarkable part of the weekend is what it does not include. There is no PC version yet, and GTA Online has not launched. Both are expected in 2027, which means the game that just posted the biggest launch weekend in entertainment history has two more selling cycles ahead of it — before the online mode that kept GTA V earning for more than a decade even begins.

Critics have matched the commercial response. The game sits at 99 on Metacritic, tied as the highest-rated game of all time — a mark no game has reached since The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time in 1998.
