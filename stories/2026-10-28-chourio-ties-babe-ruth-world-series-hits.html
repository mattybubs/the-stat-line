Jackson Chourio Ties Babe Ruth for Most Hits in the First Four Games of a World Series

By Matt

Jackson Chourio's 10th hit of the World Series — a two-out double in the top of the 12th in Game 4 — tied Babe Ruth's 1928 mark for the most hits through the first four games of a Fall Classic.

The record

Jackson Chourio now shares a line in the record book with Babe Ruth. With Milwaukee leading 3-2 in the top of the 12th inning of Game 4 on Tuesday night, Chourio lined a two-out double off Rays reliever Ian Seymour. It was his second hit of the game — and his 10th of the World Series.

That ties Ruth's mark from 1928 for the most hits by one player through the first four games of a World Series, a record that had stood alone for 98 years. MLB flagged the moment itself, posting a side-by-side graphic of Chourio and Ruth as the hit landed.

The series so far

Chourio has had multiple hits in all four games of this Fall Classic. He finished Game 4 2-for-6, pushing his series line to 10-for-20, a day after a 4-for-5 night in Game 3 helped Milwaukee move within one win of its first championship.

The historic hit came in a losing cause — Tampa Bay scored twice in the bottom of the 12th to win Game 4 4-3 and avoid the sweep — but the 22-year-old has been Milwaukee's most consistent bat all series. The Brewers still lead 3-1 heading into Game 5 on Wednesday night, when Chourio gets his first chance to pass Ruth outright.

Why it matters

• Ten hits in four World Series games is a Ruth-level start, full stop — and Chourio did it at 22, in his first Fall Classic.

• He has multi-hit games in all four contests; this is not one hot night, it is a series-long run.

• Game 5 is the next chapter: one more hit and the record is his alone.
