The best GTA 6 PC port just vanished — 25 FPS, then gone minutes after going public

By Matt

CGBJ was the furthest along of any unofficial GTA 6 PC port — reportedly running the game at 25 to 30 frames per second and nearly finished. Hours after it went public on GitHub, the repository was gone. Nobody has confirmed who removed it.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The unofficial race to play GTA 6 on PC just lost its leader. CGBJ, one of the furthest-along fan-made PC port projects, reportedly reached a playable 25 to 30 frames per second — and then its GitHub repository disappeared minutes after going public. As of Thursday evening, nobody has confirmed who removed it.

That uncertainty is the story. Take-Two's lawyers spent this week taking down the Leonida project and its 3,007 associated repositories, so the obvious suspect is Rockstar's parent company. But no DMCA notice against CGBJ has surfaced in GitHub's public record, and the team behind the project may have pulled it down themselves to avoid becoming the next takedown. Both explanations are live, and neither is confirmed.

How far it got

CGBJ was a fork of VIctory, the open-source compatibility layer that runs the leaked Xbox build's code directly on Windows, combining VIctory's runtime with XCL's shader translation technology. That is the same family of projects that spent this week climbing from one frame every two seconds to around 20 frames per second — but CGBJ had gone further than the rest.

XDA Developers reported on November 26 that CGBJ hit a playable 25 to 30 FPS just as its GitHub page vanished. GameGPU, citing the community tracker PortWatch, put the build at an average of 25 FPS on NVIDIA hardware and estimated the project was 91.5 percent complete overall, with the story campaign roughly 80 percent complete. Those are tracker estimates, not benchmarks, but they explain why this one mattered: of all the fan ports, CGBJ was the closest to something a person could actually play.

It was also, until Thursday, one of the more private efforts. XDA notes the project had previously been kept private before going public — and that public window lasted only minutes.

Who took it down

Nobody will say, because nobody can prove it yet. Fextralife, reporting Thursday evening, wrote that there is no publicly verified DMCA notice or official statement confirming that Rockstar or Take-Two targeted CGBJ, and that a missing repository does not necessarily mean a copyright takedown took place. XDA reached the same conclusion: it is unclear whether the team made the page private to avoid a potential takedown or whether Rockstar's legal department hit it. It usually takes a few days for GitHub DMCA notices to become public, so the paper trail may still appear.

GameGPU went further, reporting outright that Rockstar removed the repository minutes after it went public — but that reads as an inference from the timing, not a confirmed notice, and the other outlets covering the same disappearance would not go that far. Reddit users noted the speed of the removal suggests round-the-clock monitoring of the platform by Take-Two's lawyers, who had already secured the removal of Leonida and its forks earlier in the week.

What is confirmed is the 404: navigating to CGBJ's GitHub page now returns an error, and several forks of the project remain active, which means the code is already out in the wild whether the team pulled it or someone else did. In the short time the project was openly accessible, thousands of users copied the source code, and alternative mirrors have begun appearing on third-party hosting services, according to GameGPU.

The crackdown keeps working — and keeps not working

This is the third beat in the same week-long pattern, and it keeps cutting both ways. Take-Two's DMCA notice on November 24 took down the Leonida network — 3,007 repositories in one filing, the main project plus every fork, because GitHub processes a whole fork network when a complainant alleges most of it infringes. Leonida's 15-year-old creator quit the same day. Then the VIctory forks kept climbing anyway, hitting 20 FPS and sprouting a mod menu and a dedicated launcher.

CGBJ is the sharpest version of that contradiction yet: the most advanced build, the fastest disappearance, and a takedown — or a self-pull — that arrived after the code had already spread to forks and mirrors. The projects do not distribute the game's files; users are expected to supply their own, and the only source in circulation is the leak. The code can be taken down. The leak cannot be un-leaked.

And the warning from every previous post in this saga still stands: there is no official PC version of GTA 6, Rockstar has announced no PC release date, and there is no safe way to get one. Fake GTA 6 PC downloads are already spreading malware, and the port tracker is flagging fake repositories distributing malware under the guise of port tools. The builds get faster every few days. The takedowns keep landing. And 25 FPS on a project that no longer exists is still a long way from playable.
