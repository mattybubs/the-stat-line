Nagoshi Studio Is Officially Dead — and So Is Gang of Dragon

By Matt

Toshihiro Nagoshi's NetEase-backed studio was dissolved by shareholder vote on October 31, ending Gang of Dragon, the Yakuza-style action game announced at The Game Awards last December.

It is official now, in the least ceremonious way possible: Nagoshi Studio is gone, and the game it was built to make is gone with it. A notice in Kanpo, the Japanese government gazette, confirms the studio was dissolved by a shareholders' resolution on October 31, 2026 — ending development of Gang of Dragon, the melee-based action-adventure set in Shinjuku, Tokyo, that was announced at The Game Awards in December 2025.

The news had been expected for months, but the quiet of it is striking. There was no farewell statement, no social post, no final trailer. The studio simply faded: its YouTube channel and official website disappeared over the summer, co-head Daisuke Sato changed his bio to "Ex-Nagoshi Studios," and its Google Business listing was marked permanently closed.

How it fell apart

Nagoshi Studio was founded by Toshihiro Nagoshi, the creator behind Yakuza and Like a Dragon, under NetEase's umbrella — an attempt to build a new contender and evolution for that series. Gang of Dragon starred Ma Dong-seok (Don Lee) and was pitched as a hard-hitting, street-level action game in the tradition Nagoshi built his name on.

The money ran out first. Reports dating back to March 2026 said NetEase pulled funding after it emerged that another ¥7 billion — roughly $44 million — would be needed to finish the game and bring it to market. For all of NetEase's size, that was apparently the line.

Why it stings

This is not just another cancelled game. Nagoshi is one of the most important creators in Japanese games, and Gang of Dragon was supposed to be his second act — a new studio, a new publisher backer, a new world, with one of Korea's biggest action stars attached. Instead, it becomes a cautionary tale about how quickly even a legendary name can lose its runway when the funding math stops working.

The game was only revealed a few months before the money stopped. Fans never got a release date, never got a playable demo, never really got more than the promise. Now they will not get the game at all.
