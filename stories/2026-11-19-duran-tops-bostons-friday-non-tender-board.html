Duran tops Boston's Friday non-tender board

By Matt

Jarren Duran's $11.1 million projected salary and crowded outfield make him Boston's biggest non-tender decision before Friday's 5 p.m. deadline.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The Red Sox have until Friday at 5 p.m. ET to decide whether Jarren Duran is still one of theirs. MLB's contract tender deadline hits then, and Boston has to tender contracts to every unsigned player on its 40-man roster or watch them become free agents immediately. Duran, the 2024 All-Star Game MVP, is the name at the top of the board — and a trade looks likelier than a straight non-tender.

The deadline

The mechanics are simple: tender a contract and the player stays under team control, with the two sides free to keep negotiating until an arbitration hearing. Don't tender, and the player hits the open market the same day. Duran is arbitration-eligible for 2027 and 2028, so the decision isn't just about next season — it's about whether Boston wants to pay a projected $11.1 million (via MLB Trade Rumors) for a player who no longer has a clear everyday role.

He isn't the only name on Boston's list. First baseman Triston Casas is among the other potential cuts, per NBC Sports Boston, but Duran is the one with real trade value still attached.

The case against paying him

Duran's 2026 season was the worst of his career, and it wasn't close. The full line:

Games: 151

Average: .207

On-base: .266

Slugging: .361

OPS: .627

Home runs: 20

Stolen bases: 21

RBI: 69

Strikeouts: 165

Walks: 41

wRC+: 69

That wRC+ — 31 percent below league average — was the second-worst mark among qualified hitters in baseball, per MLB Trade Rumors. The strikeout rate climbed to 27.1 percent, up from 24.3 percent in 2025 and 21.8 percent in his breakout year. The one thing that didn't crater was the power-speed combination: he still reached 20 homers and 21 steals, the second 20-20 season of his career, which is what makes him a change-of-scenery candidate rather than a straight release.

From All-Star MVP to the bubble

The fall is what makes this one sting. Two years ago Duran was one of the best players in baseball: a .285/.342/.492 line in 2024, an All-Star selection, and the Ted Williams Award as All-Star Game MVP after a tiebreaking two-run homer off Hunter Greene lifted the American League to a 5-3 win. He led the majors in doubles (48) and triples (14), stole 34 bases, and finished eighth in AL MVP voting. He followed that with a 4.0 fWAR season in 2025 — a step back, but still a useful everyday player.

Then came the collapse. The extra-base hits dried up — 15 doubles and five triples after 62 combined in 2024 — and the strikeouts piled up. Boston's outfield, meanwhile, got younger and more settled around him: Roman Anthony, Ceddanne Rafaela, and Wilyer Abreu are the projected starters, and the club just signed Jake Bauers to a four-year, $58 million deal to be the primary designated hitter. With Bauers at DH, Duran and Masataka Yoshida — due $18.6 million next season — are left fighting for bench at-bats at starter money.

The trade market

Boston has been shopping Duran since the GM Meetings, and the interest is real even at his lowest value. The San Diego Padres have long admired him and are doing background work again. The Seattle Mariners and Philadelphia Phillies have each poked around, per MassLive's Chris Cotillo. The Chicago Cubs evaluated him as a potential change-of-scenery addition this week, The Athletic's Patrick Mooney reported Thursday, and the Cubs are also in on posted Japanese outfielder Teruaki Sato.

The leverage is thin. Boston's own reporting has it that the Red Sox don't want to pay $11.1 million in arbitration for a player who is, at best, a fourth outfielder — USA Today's Bob Nightengale put it bluntly: "He should be as good as gone." But letting him walk for nothing would sting for a front office already criticized for not trading him when his value peaked after 2024. A trade, even for a modest return, beats a non-tender.

What happens Friday

If no deal materializes by 5 p.m., Boston faces a straight choice: tender him and swallow the arbitration number, or non-tender him and let the market set his price — almost certainly well below $11.1 million. Either way, it would be a surprise to see Duran back in a Red Sox uniform in 2027. The deadline forces the answer.
