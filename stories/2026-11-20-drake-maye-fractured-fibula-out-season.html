Drake Maye fractured his right fibula, likely out for the rest of the regular season

By Matt

Tests revealed the ankle injury Drake Maye suffered in Munich was a fractured right fibula, and the defending-champion Patriots' season now rests on Tommy DeVito.

The diagnosis

New England Patriots quarterback Drake Maye suffered a fractured right fibula in Sunday's loss to the Detroit Lions in Munich, ESPN's Adam Schefter and Mike Reiss reported Friday. The injury is expected to sideline him deep into the regular season, if he is able to return this year at all.

There is a small silver lining in the news. Maye will not need surgery, and tests showed no additional ligament damage, which leaves the door open — barely — for a late return if the Patriots are still in playoff contention. The Patriots have not officially announced the diagnosis themselves, and they are on their bye this week, so no injury status has to be reported yet.

How it happened

Maye went down on the opening drive of the 35-10 loss to Detroit on November 15, sacked by Lions defensive lineman Aidan Hutchinson. What was initially feared to be an ankle injury turned out to be worse. Maye stayed overnight in Germany for testing to determine whether he would need surgery.

It was a strange week of silence before Friday's news. The Patriots said almost nothing about their franchise quarterback for five days, and the vacuum did the talking. Now we know why.

Who plays quarterback now

Head coach Mike Vrabel has already made the call: backup Tommy DeVito takes over as the starter, with 2026 seventh-round pick Behren Morton serving as the No. 2 option. DeVito replaced Maye for the remainder of the Lions game.

There is a roster wrinkle behind the decision. The Patriots have only one injured-reserve return designation left to use this season, and Maye is a candidate for it under normal circumstances. New England may choose not to place him on IR at all, preserving the designation — and the possibility, however slim, that he plays again this year.

What it means for the season

The Patriots are 5-5 and one game out of a playoff berth, which makes the timing brutal. Their remaining schedule is unforgiving: four games against winning teams in the Bills, Vikings, Chiefs and Broncos, starting with a November 29 trip to face the Chargers in Los Angeles. They also just lost wide receiver A.J. Brown for multiple weeks with a hamstring injury, so DeVito inherits a thinner offense than the one Maye was running.

Maye's season line is the reason this hurts as much as it does. In 10 games he completed 65.6 percent of his passes for 2,330 yards with 18 touchdowns and 11 interceptions, good for a 97.0 passer rating. The third-year quarterback finished as the runner-up for the 2025 NFL MVP Award, and he becomes eligible for a contract extension after the 2026 season — a negotiation that just got a lot more complicated.

For now, the defending champions are a 5-5 team with a backup quarterback, a banged-up receiving corps, and a closing schedule full of contenders. The season is not over. It just got a lot harder to see how it ends well.
