Cody Garbrandt says his UFC farewell is set — one last fight in Cleveland on Feb. 13

By The Stat Line

Former bantamweight champion Cody Garbrandt told MMA Junkie his Feb. 13 fight in Cleveland will be his last in the UFC — twelve years after he debuted, and with one fight left on his deal.

Cody Garbrandt is picking his ending. The former UFC bantamweight champion told MMA Junkie on Friday that he plans to fight one last time in the UFC — at UFC Fight Night 299 in Cleveland on Feb. 13, 2027 — and then walk away from the promotion for good.

"We're looking for February in Cleveland. No opponent yet, but that looks to be the date and my farewell from the UFC. It's bittersweet," Garbrandt said.

The plan

Garbrandt has one fight left on his UFC contract, and he has already talked with matchmaker Sean Shelby about filling it on the Cleveland card at Rocket Arena in his home state of Ohio. No opponent has been confirmed, and his place on the card has not been made official yet. The event is headlined by Joe Pyfer vs. Bo Nickal.

The 35-year-old's record stands at 15-8 in MMA, 10-8 inside the octagon.

Why now

Garbrandt framed the decision around family and timing. He has a 10-month-old daughter and an 8-year-old son, and his wife is expecting their third child in February — the same month as the planned farewell.

"At the end of the day, I competed at the highest level, became a world champion and I wasn't supposed to make it this far in life and where I'm at. I'm OK with it," he said. "You can't fight forever."

The run

Garbrandt debuted in the UFC as an undefeated fighter in January 2015 and climbed to the bantamweight title in under two years — a rise he still measures himself against. "A lot of people spend their whole career chasing what I was able to accomplish in under two years in the UFC," he said.

The back half of his run was harder. His most recent fight was a first-round TKO loss to Adrian Yanez at UFC 329 in July, and he has not fought since. His dream final opponent, he said, is a third fight with longtime rival T.J. Dillashaw — though he acknowledged that fairytale endings don't always happen.

What's next

Garbrandt is not leaving combat sports entirely. He makes his Real American Freestyle wrestling debut on Saturday at RAF 15 in Chicago, facing former Bellator titleholder Sergio Pettis — and wrestling, his first sport, is where he sees his future.

"I would love to jump in and wrestle as much as I can. It was my first love and the first sport I did," he said. "There's some unfinished business I want to do in wrestling, and I'll want to compete until I can't anymore."

He left the door slightly open on fighting MMA again outside the UFC, but he was clear about the direction: "I don't see myself really wanting to compete any more after that."

What it means

Twelve years after he arrived as an undefeated prospect, Garbrandt is choosing to close his UFC chapter on his own terms, at home in Ohio, with a third child on the way. The opponent is still to be determined — but the date, and the decision, are set.
