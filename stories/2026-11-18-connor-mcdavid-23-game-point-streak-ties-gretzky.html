Connor McDavid's point streak is at 23 — tied with Wayne Gretzky for the fourth-longest season-opening run in NHL history

By Matt

Connor McDavid scored a power-play goal in Edmonton's 5-2 win at Florida to extend his season-opening point streak to 23 games, tying Wayne Gretzky's 1988-89 run for the fourth-longest in NHL history — and passing Dany Heatley's 22.

The night

Connor McDavid does not need long to make history. Three minutes and 33 seconds into the Edmonton Oilers' game against the Florida Panthers on Tuesday night, he scored a power-play goal — his 13th of the season — and extended his season-opening point streak to 23 games.

The Oilers won 5-2 at Amerant Bank Arena, their sixth straight victory. McDavid finished with a goal and an assist, giving him 55 points on the season — 13 goals and 42 assists — still the league lead by a wide margin.

The list

Twenty-three games ties Wayne Gretzky's 1988-89 run for the fourth-longest season-opening point streak in NHL history. The full list ahead of him now reads like a shrine:

• Wayne Gretzky, 51 games (1983-84)

• Wayne Gretzky, 30 games (1982-83)

• Mats Sundin, 30 games (1992-93)

• Wayne Gretzky, 23 games (1988-89)

• Connor McDavid, 23 games (2026-27)

One game ago, McDavid was tied with Dany Heatley (22 games, 2005-06) for fifth. Tuesday's goal moved him past Heatley and level with Gretzky — the fourth name on the list who is not Gretzky or Sundin, and the only active player anywhere near it.

The goal also moved McDavid past Gretzky for the most power-play points in Oilers franchise history.

The season

This is the best start of McDavid's career, and the numbers keep stacking up behind the streak. He was the first player to reach 50 points in 21 games or fewer since Mario Lemieux did it in 18 games in 1995-96. He tied Gretzky as the fastest player ever to 40 assists in a season, doing it in 22 games. He is on pace for a season that would challenge Gretzky's single-season scoring record of 215 points, set in 1985-86.

Kasperi Kapanen opened the scoring for Edmonton on Tuesday and finished with a goal and two assists. The Oilers are 16-3-3.

What it means

The next milestone is seven games away: 30 would tie Gretzky's 1982-83 run and Sundin's 1992-93 mark. The 51-game record is not a realistic target for anyone — Gretzky's 1983-84 run is the most untouchable number in the sport. But McDavid is now sitting at a table with exactly two other names on it, and he gets his next chance on Thursday against the Tampa Bay Lightning, where a point would move him into sole possession of fourth place all by himself.
