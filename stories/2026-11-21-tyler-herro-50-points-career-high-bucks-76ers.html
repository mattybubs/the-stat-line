Tyler Herro dropped a career-high 50 — then his buzzer-beater was waved off by a fingertip

By Matt

Tyler Herro scored a career-high 50 points for the Bucks in Philadelphia, including 15 in the first quarter. He appeared to tie the game at the buzzer — but replays showed the ball was still on his fingertips when the horn sounded, and the 76ers escaped 132-129.

The night

Tyler Herro had the game of his life on Saturday afternoon in Philadelphia — and it still wasn't enough.

Herro finished with a career-high 50 points on 15-of-23 shooting, including 7-of-12 from three and a perfect 13-of-13 from the line, plus 11 rebounds and seven assists in 40 minutes. His previous career high was 41. The Bucks noted afterward that he is the first player in NBA history to record at least 50 points, 11 rebounds, seven three-pointers and 65 percent shooting in a single game.

He set the tone immediately, scoring 15 points in the first quarter to help Milwaukee take a 30-29 lead after 12 minutes. He poured in 35 more after halftime, and by the closing minute he was carrying the Bucks' entire comeback on his back.

The ending

The final 20 seconds were as cruel as a basketball ending gets.

Herro converted a running layup with 19.2 seconds left — the basket that got him to 50 — and cut the Sixers' lead to 128-126. Rookie Brayden Burries, who had a career-high 26 points off the bench, then drilled a three with 4.8 seconds to go to make it a one-point game. Joel Embiid answered with two free throws on the other end, and with 1.5 seconds left the Bucks inbounded the ball to Herro.

His fallaway three went in at the buzzer. The building erupted. Then the officials went to the replay.

Replays showed the ball was still on Herro's fingertips as the horn sounded. The basket was overturned, the game was over, and Philadelphia walked away with a 132-129 win. Herro finished with 50 — not 53, and no overtime.

What it means for the Bucks

The loss was Milwaukee's 13th in a row, dropping the Bucks to 1-16. Herro is the first Bucks player to score 50 points since Giannis Antetokounmpo did it on November 13, 2024, against the Pistons — and Giannis now plays for Miami, which is how Herro ended up in Milwaukee in the first place.

There is no silver lining in a 13-game losing streak. But a career night like this one, in a game the Bucks had no business being in, is the kind of performance that reminds everyone what Herro is: one of the purest scorers in the league, and the one player on this roster who can win a game by himself on any given night.

The other side

Philadelphia survived because its stars did just enough. Embiid had 26 points, 17 rebounds and nine assists — one assist short of a triple-double — and LeBron James added 21 points, six rebounds and six assists. Tyrese Maxey chipped in 17, and all five Sixers starters scored in double figures. The win moved Philadelphia to 14-3.

Embiid made four late free throws in all, and everyone on the Sixers' sideline was relieved to see that Herro hadn't quite released his jumper in time.
