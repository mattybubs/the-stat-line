CoreJJ leaves Team Liquid after eight years — the end of an LCS era

By Matt

Team Liquid confirmed on Tuesday that Jo 'CoreJJ' Yong-in is leaving after eight years, seven titles and two MVP awards — ending one of the longest and most decorated runs any player has had with a single North American team.

Eight years, seven titles, and one of the defining runs in North American League of Legends is over. Team Liquid announced on Tuesday that Jo "CoreJJ" Yong-in is leaving the organization, ending a partnership that began in November 2018 and turned a Korean import into the face of the LCS.

The announcement came in a farewell video posted to Team Liquid's X account, with a message that said everything about what he meant to the org: "Eight years of leadership and dedication. You helped shape who we are today." The official LoL Esports account shared the news soon after, under the line "AFTER 8 YEARS."

What eight years looked like

CoreJJ arrived in North America already a world champion. He won the 2017 World Championship with Samsung Galaxy, then switched from AD carry to support and joined Team Liquid on November 20, 2018. He won both LCS splits in his debut season and was named Spring Split MVP — and he never really stopped winning.

Across eight seasons, CoreJJ won seven titles with Team Liquid: LCS championships in Spring and Summer 2019, Spring and Summer 2024, and Summer 2026, plus the LCS Lock-In tournament in 2021 and 2022. He was named LCS MVP a second time in Summer 2020, and he earned eight First-Team All-Pro selections — the most recent in Summer 2026, his final split with the team.

He was also the shotcaller and the steady hand. Through roster turnover, coaching changes and the league's rebrand from LCS to LTA, CoreJJ was the constant — the player Liquid built its bot lane, and often its whole identity, around.

The one thing he never got

For all the domestic dominance, the international breakthrough never came with Liquid. CoreJJ never reached the knockout stage of Worlds with the organization, despite winning the league five times. His final season told the same story: a fourth-place finish at the LCS Lock-In, a Spring Split grand final swept 3-0 by LYON, a 3-0 loss to T1 in the MSI 2026 Play-In stage, and a 1-3 Swiss-stage exit at Worlds 2026 in front of a home crowd. His last match in a Liquid jersey was the 2-1 defeat to Brazilian champions LOS that knocked North America's top seed out of the tournament.

That gap is the only blemish on the résumé — and it's the reason the departure lands the way it does. He didn't leave because he declined. He left as the reigning Summer Split champion, still playing at an All-Pro level, because after eight years the run had simply reached its end.

Where he goes next

Neither Team Liquid nor CoreJJ has said where he will play next, and Liquid has not named a replacement. The reporting, though, has been consistent for weeks: Sheep Esports reported on November 12 that CoreJJ and longtime bot-lane partner Sean "Yeon" Sung are set to join Cloud9 for the 2027 LCS season, and followed up on November 23 that Cloud9 has finalized its planned 2027 lineup, with CoreJJ and Yeon joining returning players Robert "Blaber" Huang and Park "Loki" Jong-hoon. That move has not been confirmed by either team, and Yeon's departure has not been announced either — but if it lands, Cloud9 would be getting the most proven support in the league's history, already paired with the ADC he has played beside since 2023.

Liquid, meanwhile, is reportedly turning to the team that ended its Worlds run. Sheep Esports reported on November 18 that Argentine support Gabriel "Ackerman" Aparicio — who won the 2026 CBLOL title with LOS — is set to take over as Liquid's starting support.

Eight years is an eternity in esports. Most players don't get eight years in the league, let alone eight years with one team. CoreJJ got both, won nearly everything there was to win at home, and walks out the door still at the top of his game. Wherever he lands next, the LCS just lost one of the players who defined it.
