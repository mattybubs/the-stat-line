Jaxon Smith-Njigba Over 99.5 Receiving Yards: The Seahawks' Whole Passing Game Runs Through Him

By Matt

JSN is averaging 123.9 receiving yards per game — nearly 25 clear of his 99.5 line. Chicago's defense will have to stop him to stop Seattle, and nobody has yet.

The game slip

Matchup: Chicago Bears (5-2) at Seattle Seahawks (4-3)

Kickoff: Monday, November 2, 8:15 PM ET — ESPN, Lumen Field, Seattle

Line: Seahawks -3 | Total 46.5 | Moneyline: Seahawks -166, Bears +140 (via DraftKings, November 1)

Bears injury report: OUT — Kyle Monangai, plus Cole Kmet, Braxton Jones and Coby Bryant on injured reserve. Questionable — D'Marco Jackson, Malik Muhammad II. Chicago's offense is down its tight end and a starting lineman, and its backfield depth took a hit with Monangai out.

Seahawks injury report: OUT — Charles Cross (ankle), Rashid Shaheed, Velus Jones. Doubtful — Uchenna Nwosu. Questionable — Anthony Bradford, Drew Lock, Patrick O'Connell, Brandon Pili. Cross is the big one: Seattle's left tackle is out, which matters for Sam Darnold's time to throw — and for this bet, which is why the number is 99.5 and not 110.5.

Storylines driving the bet:

1. Jaxon Smith-Njigba has 867 receiving yards in seven games — 123.9 per game, nearly 25 yards clear of this line. He is not just Seattle's top target; he is the passing game. Defenses know it and still have not stopped it.

2. Caleb Williams is playing well since returning — 71.0 percent completions, 1,020 yards, five touchdowns and one interception in four appearances, plus 128 rushing yards and two scores — and Chicago is 5-2 against the spread, the NFL's best cover record. This should be a competitive game, which means Seattle keeps throwing late. A blowout either way is the enemy of a receiving prop; a close game is its friend.

3. Seattle just beat Kansas City 20-3 in Week 7 behind its defense, and Chicago arrives with extra rest after beating New England on Thursday night in Week 7. Both teams have something to prove in primetime, and primetime games lean on stars. JSN is Seattle's star tonight.

The bet

Jaxon Smith-Njigba over 99.5 receiving yards (-114). The math is simple: a receiver averaging 123.9 yards a game, in a game projected to be close, at a line almost 25 yards below his average. Even a below-average JSN night clears this if Seattle throws 35-plus times, which it will if Chicago's offense keeps it close — and Williams' recent form says it will.

The honest caveat: Cross being out is real. Darnold has already thrown eight interceptions and faced the highest pressure rate per dropback of any quarterback this season, and without his left tackle that pressure gets worse. If Darnold is running for his life, JSN's targets get shorter and scarcer. But JSN's game is built on quick separation and yards after the catch — the exact profile that survives a shaky pocket. Take the over, and expect him to clear it in the fourth quarter, not the first.
