Tuesday Night Belonged to the Goalies: Fowler's 46-Save Shutout, Varlamov's Comeback, Schaefer's Full OT, Hellebuyck's Devils Debut

By Matt

Jacob Fowler stopped a career-high 46 shots in a shutout at St. Louis, Semyon Varlamov won for the first time in 712 days while Matthew Schaefer skated all five minutes of overtime, Connor Hellebuyck won his Devils debut — and Colten Ellis posted his second career shutout.

Jacob Fowler: 46 saves, zero goals

The night's biggest goaltending performance came in St. Louis, where rookie Jacob Fowler made a career-high 46 saves for the shutout in Montreal's 2-0 road win over the Blues. St. Louis outshot Montreal 46-20, including a 24-3 edge in the third period, and still could not solve him.

It was Fowler's second shutout of the season and his third in 24 career starts. He is now on a six-game winning streak with a .942 save percentage in seven games this season. Zack Bolduc — scoring against his former team — and Florian Xhekaj, with his first NHL goal, supplied the offense, and Mike Matheson had two assists. The Blues have now been shut out in two straight games.

Semyon Varlamov: first win in 712 days

In Nashville, Semyon Varlamov's wait ended. The 38-year-old made 20 saves and was perfect in the shootout as the Islanders beat the Predators 4-3, earning his first NHL win since November 14, 2024 — a gap of 712 days that included two partial knee replacements and a long rehabilitation, with conditioning starts for Bridgeport last April.

"A year ago, to be honest with you, I didn't see this coming," Varlamov said afterward, calling it "a very special moment." Head coach Pete DeBoer called him "our best player tonight" after he poke-checked the puck away from Ryan O'Reilly to open the shootout.

Matthew Schaefer: all five minutes of overtime

The same game produced a record of its own. Nineteen-year-old Islanders defenseman Matthew Schaefer stayed on the ice for the entire five-minute overtime period — the first skater on record to do so since the NHL began tracking real-time ice data in 2009-10. The shift topped the Islanders' previous mark of 4:50, held by Casey Cizikas.

Schaefer drew a tripping penalty on Filip Forsberg 2:17 into overtime, rested only at the stoppage, then stayed out through the power play and the final 43 seconds — and still had the legs to break up a possible Forsberg breakaway late. He finished with a career-high 32:16 of ice time, plus an assist that extended his point streak to five games. He now has 11 points in 12 games.

Connor Hellebuyck: a winning Devils debut

And in Dallas, the Connor Hellebuyck era began with a win. The three-time Vezina Trophy winner stopped 16 of 17 shots as New Jersey beat the Stars 5-1 in his first start four days after being acquired from Winnipeg — his first game action since April 13 and his first win since April 9.

Captain Nico Hischier scored twice, Jack and Luke Hughes each had a goal and an assist, and the Devils extended their winning streak to four games while snapping Dallas' five-game run. Note the record straight: this was a debut win, not a shutout — Dallas' Justin Hryckowian scored the Stars' lone goal.

Also: Colten Ellis, second career shutout

The night's fourth shutout came in San Jose, where Colten Ellis made 23 saves in Buffalo's 2-0 win over the Sharks — his second career shutout and first of the season, in his first appearance since being pulled early on October 12 against Florida. Olen Zellweger and Zach Benson (empty net) scored, Owen Power assisted on both, and Yaroslav Askarov made 38 saves in defeat.

Why it matters

• Four goalie storylines in one night, and none of them routine: a career-high shutout, a 712-day comeback, a first-ever full overtime shift, and a blockbuster debut win.

• Fowler's six straight wins and .942 save percentage are turning a rookie season into a Calder-adjacent conversation.

• Schaefer's 32:16 is the workload of a No. 1 defenseman at 19 — and the Islanders keep leaning on him heavier.
