Deep Space 9 is Star Trek's breakout card

By Matt

Five Magic: The Gathering Star Trek cards have spiked since prerelease — and Deep Space 9 leads the way, climbing from $14.50 to $40 after Worlds put the new set through its paces.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The spike

Five cards from Magic: The Gathering | Star Trek have spiked in price in the few days since prerelease — and the headliner is the station itself. Deep Space 9 went from $14.50 at prerelease to $40, a jump of roughly 176% in under two weeks. A nine-mana mythic was always going to cost real money — but $40 caught everyone off guard.

The timing tells the story. Worlds, the biggest tournament of the year, kicked off on the set's release day, immediately putting the new cards to the test in high-level competitive play. The ones that performed spiked; the ones that didn't fell back. Five passed the test.

The five risers

Deep Space 9: $14.50; $40

Jadzia Dax, Living Legacy: $7.70; $23.50

Captain James T. Kirk: $6.50; $22

Anthropological Mission: $0.50; $8

The Naked Time: $0.50; $2.90

The foil market agrees with the direction: NerdSnack's TCG Market Movers board has the foil printing at $43.83, up from $18.60 over the November 10–17 window — a 136% move.

Why the station is worth it

Deep Space 9 is a nine-drop that pays you back the moment it lands. When it enters — and at the beginning of your upkeep — it exiles the top four cards of your library and puts every permanent exiled that way onto the battlefield. Up to four free permanents a turn is the kind of effect that ends games, and it's one you can cheat into play rather than hard-casting for nine.

It shines in the new spacecraft decks built around Captain Picard, sure — but the price is being driven by something wider: unlike most cards of its type, it doesn't need your deck to be all about rockets and charge counters. It fits Eldrazi shells and artifact decks, or really anything that can get to nine mana and wants a game-ender. A finisher that slots into existing shells instead of demanding a new one is exactly the kind of card that holds a price.

The Worlds effect

The other four risers all have the same fingerprint: they showed up at Worlds and did real work.

Anthropological Mission is the real shocker out of Atlanta. Stefan Schütz rode it to the Worlds title with an Izzet list running a full playset of a fifty-cent uncommon nobody had on their radar. Once it has three counters, it stops looting and starts netting a card every time you play a non-creature spell. Schütz's list leaned all the way into that: thirty instants and sorceries, zero creatures, and a deck that just buries opponents in cards once the Mission is online. A fifty-cent uncommon that won the biggest tournament of the year doesn't stay fifty cents.

The Naked Time caught the wave of Boros Pixie, the most-played deck of the weekend. It pumps a creature, gives it haste, and ramps you in red — strong enough that some players were openly asking if it should be banned. It isn't, and it's not even the set's best uncommon — but a card that strong in the format's most-played deck was never going to stay at prerelease pricing.

Captain James T. Kirk is the other half of that Boros story: an efficient, aggressive creature that generates a lot of value when your back is against the wall, and a key part of the deck that dominated Worlds weekend.

Jadzia Dax, Living Legacy is a useful ramp creature — and a beater in her own right in a long game — that has proven popular in a rising Selesnya Landfall deck, an archetype that appeared en masse at the recent tournament.

What it means

Prerelease pricing is a guess; Worlds is the answer key. The Star Trek set's early market was soft — most cards came down once packs started opening — but the five that proved themselves in competitive play went the other way, and the station went furthest of all. If the Worlds decks stay relevant, these prices have room; if the meta moves on, the uncommons that rode one tournament are the first to give it back.
