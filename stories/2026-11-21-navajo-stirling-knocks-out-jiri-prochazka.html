Navajo Stirling knocks out Jiri Prochazka in round two

By Matt

Navajo Stirling starched former champion Jiri Prochazka with a right hook at 4:58 of round two in the UFC Qatar main event — his second straight knockout of a former champion.

Navajo Stirling is no longer a prospect. The undefeated New Zealander knocked out former light heavyweight champion Jiri Prochazka with a right hook at 4:58 of round two in the main event of UFC Fight Night 294 on Saturday night at ABHA Arena in Doha, Qatar.

It was the biggest win of Stirling's career, and it came the hard way. Stirling briefly hit the deck in the opening round on what the replay showed was a slip, not a knockdown, and the 29-year-old reset and found the shot that ended the fight.

The finish

Stirling fought patient for most of the night, working leg kicks and jabs while Prochazka pressed with his usual unorthodox striking. The round looked headed for the scorecards.

Then Prochazka rushed in for an exchange, Stirling slipped a punch and came over the top with a counter right hook that landed flush on the jaw. Prochazka crumpled to the canvas, and Stirling followed with punches before referee Marc Goddard stepped in. None of the follow-ups were needed — Prochazka was out on the first shot.

Second former champion in a row

This was not a one-off. Stirling's previous fight was a first-round knockout of former champion Jan Blachowicz in August, so the Doha finish makes it two straight knockouts of former light heavyweight champions.

The run goes deeper than that. Saturday was Stirling's fourth win of 2026, and all four have come by knockout — a sharp turn from the start of his UFC run, when his first three wins all went to the judges. The record now reads 12-0 overall and 7-0 in the UFC, and he did it in his first UFC headlining assignment.

Navajo Stirling: 12-0; 7-0

Jiri Prochazka: 32-7-1; 6-4

The UFC agreed with the eye test: Stirling took home a $100,000 Performance of the Night bonus for the finish.

What it means

Prochazka came into Doha ranked near the top of the division and fighting to get back to the title after losing the vacant title fight to Carlos Ulberg in April. Instead he has now lost two straight, and the second one was the kind of knockout that resets a career arc.

For Stirling, the question is now the title — and it comes with a wrinkle. The belt belongs to Ulberg, his City Kickboxing training partner. Neither man has said whether they would fight each other, but after knocking out two former champions in his last two fights, Stirling has forced the conversation. He did not call out a specific name after the win, but he is one step from a shot either way.
