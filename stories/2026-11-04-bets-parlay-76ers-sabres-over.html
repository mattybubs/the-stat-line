2-Leg Parlay (about +292): 76ers Moneyline + Senators-Sabres Over 6.5

By Matt

Two plays, no filler: the 7-1 76ers at plus money in Detroit, and seven goals in a game where both nets are leaky. Together at +112 and -118, they pay about +292.

The legs

Leg 1 — Philadelphia 76ers moneyline (+112 at FanDuel), at Detroit, 7:00 PM ET on ESPN. Philadelphia is 7-1, unbeaten on the road, and getting plus money against a 6-1 Detroit team missing starting center Jalen Duren (hamstring) and Isaiah Joe (hip). Joel Embiid is off the injury report after managing plantar fasciitis, and the 76ers' only loss this season came against Detroit in a game Embiid rested. This time he plays, Detroit's frontcourt has to handle him without Duren, and the better team is the underdog. Full game slip, injury reports for both teams, and the honest caveats are in the standalone 76ers post.

Leg 2 — Ottawa Senators at Buffalo Sabres, Over 6.5 goals (-118 at FanDuel), 7:30 PM ET on ESPN+. Ottawa's No. 1 goalie, Linus Ullmark (.945 in eight starts), is on injured reserve; his replacements sit at .866 and .848. Buffalo's Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen is at .874. Both penalty kills rank in the bottom ten (Buffalo 28th at 68.6 percent, Ottawa 23rd at 72.9 percent), and both teams have the scorers to punish that — Tim Stutzle leads Ottawa with 21 points, Tage Thompson leads Buffalo with 10 goals. Seven goals is a low bar for this matchup. Full game slip and injury reports are in the standalone Senators-Sabres post.

Why these two, together

A parlay only works if each leg clears on its own, and both of these do. The 76ers leg is a value moneyline on the better team at plus money. The total leg is a structural play on goaltending and penalty kills, not a guess about which team wins. Neither leg depends on the other, neither is a long shot dressed up as a lock, and there is no third leg added just to juice the payout.

At the morning prices (+112 and -118), the two legs combine to about +292. Books vary, so check your book's parlay price before placing it — the legs are the bet, not the exact combined number.

The honest caveat, stated plainly: parlays need everything to go right. Embiid's minutes could stay restricted and turn the Detroit game into a coin flip, and a hockey total can die on a single hot period from either goalie. If either leg makes you uncomfortable on its own, play it straight instead of parlaying it. That is always the right call.
