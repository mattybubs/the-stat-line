Scheifele sets Jets points record in comeback win over Blackhawks

By Matt

Mark Scheifele passed Dale Hawerchuk for the Jets' all-time points lead — then kept going, factoring in on all four Winnipeg goals as the Jets erased a 4-0 deficit and beat Chicago 5-4 in a shootout.

The night

Mark Scheifele picked the loudest possible way to make Jets history.

The Winnipeg center had a goal and three assists on Saturday afternoon as the Jets rallied from 4-0 down to beat the Chicago Blackhawks 5-4 in a shootout at the United Center — and in the process, he passed Dale Hawerchuk to become the franchise's all-time points leader.

The record-breaking point came at 16:05 of the second period. With Chicago up four and the game slipping away, Josh Morrissey carried the puck in from the point and found Scheifele, who beat Spencer Knight to make it 4-1 — and move past Hawerchuk on the franchise list. He finished the afternoon with 933 career points, and he wasn't done: he also scored in the shootout, as did Kyle Connor, whose tally stood up as the winner.

The record

Scheifele entered the day tied with Hawerchuk after a three-point night two days earlier — two goals and an assist in an 8-3 win at Dallas on Nov. 12 had brought him even. One more point in Chicago gave him sole possession.

The final tally reads 379 goals and 554 assists across 982 career regular-season games, all of them in a Jets sweater. Hawerchuk's mark stood at 929 points — 379 goals and 550 assists in 713 games for the original Winnipeg franchise from 1981-82 through 1989-90.

Saturday was a two-record afternoon. The milestone goal also tied Hawerchuk's franchise record for goals, and his third assist of the game — the setup on Gabriel Vilardi's tying goal — moved him past Thomas Steen (553) for the franchise assists lead. He now stands alone atop both franchise leaderboards.

Points: 933; Dale Hawerchuk, 929

Goals: 379; Tied with Hawerchuk

Assists: 554; Thomas Steen, 553

The context matters: the record only became reachable this season. The NHL announced in September that the statistical history of the two Winnipeg franchises would be merged — the original Jets (1979-96) and the current club, which arrived from Atlanta in 2011 and includes the Thrashers years. The merger came after the Arizona Coyotes went dormant in 2024. Before the merge, Scheifele had already passed Blake Wheeler in October 2025 as the points leader of the current Jets era.

He is the fourth player to hold the franchise points record, and the fourth NHL player in two seasons to claim a franchise points mark.

The mentor connection

The man whose record fell was also Scheifele's junior coach. Hawerchuk coached him with the Barrie Colts in the OHL, and the two stayed close until Hawerchuk's death in 2020.

Scheifele was the first draft pick of the current Jets era — seventh overall in the 2011 NHL Entry Draft, the franchise's first selection after the move from Atlanta. Fifteen years later, in his 16th NHL season, the Kitchener, Ontario native owns the record book of a franchise that spans two eras and 44 seasons.

The comeback

The record would have meant less without the win, and for most of the afternoon that looked unlikely. Chicago built a 4-0 lead — Tyler Bertuzzi opened the scoring 1:48 in, and Frank Nazar's power-play goal at 14:44 of the second made it four.

Then Scheifele started the rally himself, and 1:56 later he set up Connor for the goal that made it 4-2 — a bizarre sequence in which Knight's stick got wedged in the boards behind his own net, leaving Scheifele free to feed Connor in front. Connor's goal was his 17th of the season and the 700th point of his career.

Cole Perfetti scored on the power play 55 seconds into the third to cut it to one. With Nico Daws — who had replaced Stuart Skinner after the first period — pulled for the extra attacker, Vilardi buried Scheifele's pass at the net front with 1:13 left to tie it.

Shootout: Scheifele and Connor score; Jets win 5-4

Nobody scored in overtime — Connor Bedard hit the post for Chicago — and in the shootout, Patrick Kane kept the Blackhawks alive in the second round before Daws stopped rookie Roman Kantserov to seal it.

It was Winnipeg's sixth straight win, and the second time in 11 days the Jets have erased a four-goal deficit and won — the first came against Montreal on Nov. 3. The Jets improved to 10-8-3. Chicago fell to 5-13-2.
