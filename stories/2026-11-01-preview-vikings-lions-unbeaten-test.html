Unbeaten Vikings Walk Into Ford Field: Minnesota's Perfect Season Meets Detroit's Home Field

By Matt

The NFL's last unbeaten team visits Detroit on Sunday at 1:00 PM ET on FOX, with the 6-0 Vikings catching three points against the 4-2 Lions.

The NFL's last unbeaten team gets its loudest test yet. The Minnesota Vikings (6-0) visit the Detroit Lions (4-2) at Ford Field on Sunday at 1:00 PM ET on FOX, and the market is not buying the perfect record at face value: Detroit is a 3-point favorite, with a total of 46.5 and a moneyline of Lions -170 / Vikings +142, via the ESPN scoreboard's DraftKings odds.

That line is the story of this game in one number. Minnesota has not lost, and yet it walks into Detroit as an underdog. The Lions are the team the market trusts at home, and the Vikings are the team that has to prove the record is real against a division rival that knows exactly what it wants to do.

The matchup

Minnesota's season so far has been efficient rather than explosive. The Vikings are the league's last unbeaten team, and Justin Jefferson just caught his 600th career pass in a 16-10 win over Indianapolis, becoming one of just seven players since 1993 to reach 600 receptions in his first 99 games. But the offense has been grinding: Jefferson had four catches for 44 yards against Indianapolis, and quarterback Murray attempted just 24 passes last week. This is a team winning with defense, field position, and just enough offense, not by lighting up scoreboards.

Detroit, at 4-2, is the opposite kind of problem. The Lions score, they score at home, and Jared Goff has hit the passing-yards over in 13 of his last 16 games. Ford Field is a fast track and a loud building, and a 46.5 total says the books expect points even against a Minnesota defense that has kept this team unbeaten.

Injury report

Minnesota: Running back Aaron Jones is listed questionable with a knee issue, a status that could change the Vikings' rushing plan and several prop markets if he is inactive.

Detroit: The Lions' secondary is banged up — Joseph is out and McCreary's status was in doubt heading into the weekend. The good news for Detroit: Amon-Ra St. Brown, who picked up an ankle tweak after catching nine passes for 153 yards against Green Bay in Week 7, is not on the Lions' latest injury designations and is set to play.

Storylines to watch

1. The unbeaten tax. Minnesota is 6-0 and still getting points. If the Vikings win this one on the road, the "yeah, but who have they played" conversation ends. If they lose, the market will say it told you so.

2. Jefferson at Ford Field. Jefferson has historically been exceptional in Detroit, with 831 receiving yards in six career games there. Against a thinned-out Lions secondary, this is the spot where Minnesota's passing game could finally look like Minnesota's passing game.

3. Goff at home. Detroit's offense runs through Goff at Ford Field, and Minnesota's defense has to deal with a quarterback who has been clearing his yardage lines for most of the last two seasons. On the other side, Detroit's pass rush — 21 sacks, tied for the league lead — is exactly the kind of thing that wrecks this Minnesota offense: Murray was sacked nine times in his first three starts. If Detroit gets home early, comfortable Goff at home is how 46.5-point totals get threatened.

No bet pick in this preview — the picks live in their own posts. This one is just the lay of the land: the last unbeaten team, a home favorite, and a division game that should tell us which one of those things is real.
