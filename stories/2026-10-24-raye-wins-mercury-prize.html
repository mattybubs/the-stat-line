RAYE Wins the 2026 Mercury Prize for 'This Music May Contain Hope'

By Matt

RAYE took British music's most respected prize on Thursday in Newcastle, beating Paul McCartney, Suede and Dave for her 73-minute second album.

RAYE has won the 2026 Mercury Prize. The British singer-songwriter took the award on Thursday at a ceremony in Newcastle's Utilita Arena for her second studio album, "This Music May Contain Hope" — beating a shortlist that included Paul McCartney, Suede, Dave, Olivia Dean, JADE and Florence + The Machine.

The album

The judges praised the record's "scale, structure, ambition and sheer charm." The 73-minute album blends jazz, orchestral pop, funk and more, and RAYE performed "I Hate The Way I Look Today" at the ceremony to a standing ovation. The prize comes with £25,000.

The speech

"Hope is a very cheesy word … but hope is just something that we all need," RAYE said while accepting the award. "Music is medicine, so I just hope as well that message comes across to anyone who needs it."

The context

The Mercury Prize, first handed to Primal Scream in 1992, shortlists 12 albums by British and Irish acts from the past year and is regarded as less mainstream — and more genre-open — than the BRIT Awards. RAYE was previously nominated in 2023 for her debut, "My 21st Century Blues"; Sam Fender won last year for "People Watching." This time, the trophy is hers.
