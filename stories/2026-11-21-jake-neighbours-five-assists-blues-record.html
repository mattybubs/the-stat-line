Jake Neighbours ties a Blues record with five assists — and breaks a Brett Hull mark in under six minutes

By Matt

Jake Neighbours tied the St. Louis franchise record with five assists in a 9-6 win over the Devils — and needed less than six minutes to pass Brett Hull's mark for the fastest three points to start a game in Blues history.

A night for the record book

Jake Neighbours tied a St. Louis Blues franchise record on Saturday night, recording five assists in a 9-6 win over the New Jersey Devils at Enterprise Center.

He is the seventh Blues player to record five assists in a game, and the first since Torey Krug did it against the Montreal Canadiens on Feb. 11, 2024. Neighbours assisted on all three of St. Louis' opening goals, then added two more as the Blues piled up nine in one of the wildest games of the season.

"I started to hear some rumblings (of breaking records), but I'm not too sure of what I tied or what I beat or anything like that," Neighbours said. "Just happy we pulled out the win and I can contribute."

Six minutes to pass Brett Hull

The record that actually fell belonged to a Hall of Famer. Neighbours had three assists in the opening 5:53, making him the fastest player in Blues history to record three points in a game — passing Brett Hull, who did it in 6:57 on Oct. 26, 1996.

It was nearly an NHL record, too. Only Guy Lafleur (5:37 on March 27, 1979) and Pavol Demitra (5:43 on Nov. 13, 2005) have ever reached three points faster from the start of a game.

"It's cool to be mentioned in the same company as those names," Neighbours said. "I'm not going to read into it too much. Those guys are Hall of Famers and a higher pedigree than I am, but obviously just one of those nights where things were working for me."

The helpers

Neighbours set up Jimmy Snuggerud, Robert Thomas, Brandon Carlo, Logan Mailloux and Mason McTavish. Thomas added a goal of his own for a three-point night — his third straight game with at least three points, the first Blues player to do that since Keith Tkachuk in 2005-06 — and Jonatan Berggren scored twice.

The Devils never recovered from the opening burst. Connor Hellebuyck was pulled in the first period after allowing goals on four consecutive shots, facing just six in total, and Jake Allen allowed four more on 16 shots in relief. Jordan Binnington made 37 saves at the other end.

"The season's a long year, you've got to win games all kinds of different ways," St. Louis coach Jim Montgomery said. "Sometimes the game gets sloppy. Tonight's game was sloppy by both teams."

A heater, not a fluke

The five-assist night capped a three-game run in which Neighbours has nine points, on one goal and eight assists. He now has 18 points in 23 games this season, a pace that would smash his career high.

It is also the kind of production the Blues have been waiting on from the 24-year-old winger, who is in the final year of the bridge deal he signed in October 2024 and will be a restricted free agent next summer. Nights like this one change the conversation around what that next contract looks like.

What it means

The win was St. Louis' third straight, the first time the Blues have won three in a row this season. They also tied a franchise record with 22 goals over a three-game stretch, matching a mark set in January 1991, and became just the second team in 30 years to score eight or more goals in consecutive home games.

Edmonton is next, and the Oilers just watched the Blues hang nine on one of the league's best goaltenders. If Neighbours keeps setting the table like this, the record book is going to need another look.
