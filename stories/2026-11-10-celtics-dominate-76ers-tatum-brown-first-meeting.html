Celtics dominate 76ers in first Tatum-Brown meeting as Paul George torches his old team

By Matt

The summer trade's grudge match went to Boston — and the new guy in green stole the show.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The game everyone had circled since the summer ended the way Boston drew it up. The Celtics beat the 76ers 123-101 in Philadelphia on Tuesday night, in the first regular-season meeting since the blockbuster that sent Jaylen Brown to the Sixers and Paul George to Boston — and they never trailed. The headliner was supposed to be Jayson Tatum against his former running mate. The story turned out to be the other guy in the trade.

The night belonged to George

George went off against the team that dealt him away, pouring in a game-high 36 points on 14-of-22 shooting, including 6-of-11 from three, with 5 rebounds. He did his loudest damage late: the broadcast graphic during the run said it plainly — an 18-3 Celtics burst over 5:21 that turned a game into a rout. Every time Philadelphia threatened to make it interesting, the ball found George, and the ball went in.

This was the revenge-game script in its purest form. George spent the summer as the footnote to the Brown-Tatum storyline, the other piece of the deal. In his first game back in Philadelphia wearing green, he was the best player on the floor by a distance, smiling all the way through it. He'd told reporters before shootaround that it feels like "a regular game." It did not look like one.

Round one: Tatum over Brown

Tatum got his win over his old running mate, finishing with 21 points, 6 rebounds, and 4 assists, plus a third-quarter poster dunk on LeBron James that had the Boston bench on its feet. The two spent nine seasons together in Boston, won a championship together, and became the identity of the franchise — then the trade split them up, and the week leading in had all the makings of a feud. The scoreboard backed Tatum up.

Brown's first game against his old team was a quiet one: 13 points on 4-of-9 shooting, 5 rebounds, 2 assists, 4 turnovers, and a minus-27 in 26 minutes. He never found a rhythm, and by the fourth quarter he was watching from the bench as the lead stretched past 20. The building that used to cheer George spent the night watching him cook his old team.

Philly's side of the ledger

Tyrese Maxey kept the Sixers in it as long as anyone could, leading Philadelphia with 24 points on 8-of-12 shooting. Joel Embiid added 16, but needed 13 trips to the line to get there on a 2-of-9 night from the field. And LeBron James finished two boards and five dimes short of a triple-double, closing on 11 points, 8 rebounds, and 5 assists — the watch that had some life in the fourth quarter never got there.

Boston's bench buried them

The starters set the tone, but the bench put the game away. Payton Pritchard came off the bench for 18 points, 6 rebounds, and 5 assists and was a plus-26; Sam Hauser knocked down four threes for 12; Hugo Gonzalez added 10. Philadelphia's best stretch was the third quarter, the only frame it won — and even that only trimmed two points off a double-digit halftime deficit. Boston won the other three quarters going away.

What it means

The winner was always going to take sole possession of first place in the East, and now that belongs to Boston alone: the Celtics sit at 9-2, the Sixers at 9-3. It is one game in November, and the standings will move a dozen times before spring — but the trade's grudge match was not close out of the gate, and the team that supposedly lost its second star looks better for the swap so far. Boston doesn't wait long to build on it: the Celtics are in Toronto tomorrow night, Wednesday, November 11, at 7:00 PM ET against the Raptors. The rematch with Philly isn't until Jan. 21, at TD Garden.
