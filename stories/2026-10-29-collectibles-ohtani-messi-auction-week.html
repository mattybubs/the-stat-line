Ohtani's $3.24M Logoman Leads a Monster Week for Sports Cards

By Matt

Shohei Ohtani's 1/1 Logoman patch auto sold for $3.24 million — the most expensive Dodgers card ever — headlining a week that also saw a $2.16M Cooper Flagg, a record Messi Topps NOW, and a $1.47M vintage football set.

It's been a monster week for sports cards, the kind where the top of the market moves in a bunch of directions at once: modern superstars, a farewell record, and vintage all putting up numbers in the same seven days.

Ohtani's $3.24 million Logoman

The headline is Shohei Ohtani's 2025 Topps MLB MVP The Game Dynasty Black Auto Patch 1/1, graded PSA 9, which sold for $3.24 million through Fanatics Premier on October 23. It's the most expensive Los Angeles Dodgers card ever sold at auction, and the third-biggest Ohtani sale of all time.

Logoman cards are the hobby's ultimate chase for a reason: the patch is cut from a game-worn jersey's actual MLB logo, so there's exactly one of each. A 1/1 Logoman auto of the game's biggest star, in a set built to honor one of the greatest performances in North American sports history, is the kind of card that doesn't come around twice. The $3.24 million price says the market agrees — even in a week where Ohtani's on-field postseason was quiet, the cardboard didn't care.

Flagg and the dual Logoman right behind it

The same Fanatics Premier run put two more seven-figure cards on the board. A 2025 Topps Motif Logoman Auto 1/1 Cooper Flagg (unscratched redemption) sold for $2.16 million — the week's second-biggest sale and a statement about where the hobby thinks Flagg's market is going. And a 2026 Topps Chrome Dual MVP Gold Logoman Auto 1/1 of Aaron Judge and Shohei Ohtani went for $1.23 million. Two of the game's biggest names on one card, one copy in the world, seven figures. That's the modern high-end market in one line.

Messi's farewell sets a different kind of record

The record that isn't an auction at all might be the most telling one. Lionel Messi's Topps NOW "Final Farewell" card is now the best-selling Topps NOW card of all time at 664,325 copies sold, passing Shohei Ohtani's 50/50 card (653,737). No bidding, no 1/1 — just an enormous number of people buying the same farewell card in the same week.

That's the other side of the modern hobby: retirements and historic moments have become instant mass-market events. One buyer also gets a 1-of-1 autographed White OpalFractor version, and the card has Chrome and Opal Chrome parallels including a 1-of-1 SuperFractor — so the chase exists inside the mass release. Fanatics, which now controls the exclusive trading card rights for MLB, NBA, NFL, and FIFA World Cup properties, has turned the farewell card into its own category.

Vintage: the $1.47 million football set

And vintage had its moment too. A nearly complete 1962 Topps football set — 175 of the 176 cards, the top set on PSA's All-Time Finest registry at a 9.337 grade point average — sold for $1,473,638 in Mile High Card Company's Fall Auction, the highest price ever paid for a football card set. Only about 1% of all submissions from the set have ever earned a PSA 9, and 101 of those are in this collection. The auction also moved a $360,913 baseball collection and an $833,138 Babe Ruth card (a 1933 Sport Kings, SGC 9, the auction's highest-selling individual card).

Put it together and the week tells one story: the hobby's top end is not cooling off. Modern 1/1s are doing millions, farewell cards are doing hundreds of thousands of copies, and the best vintage sets are setting records of their own. Different corners, same heat.
