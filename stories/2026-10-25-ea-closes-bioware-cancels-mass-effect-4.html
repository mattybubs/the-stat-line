EA Closes BioWare and Cancels the Next Mass Effect

By Matt

Electronic Arts shut down BioWare on October 21, ending early development on the next Mass Effect — closing a 31-year run for the studio behind Baldur's Gate, Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age and Mass Effect.

The studio that defined the western RPG is gone. Electronic Arts shut down BioWare and cancelled the next Mass Effect, the studio confirmed in a statement on X on October 21, 2026.

What happened

BioWare's farewell was short: "Now, after more than three decades of shared adventures, BioWare is closing and early development on the next Mass Effect project is ending." In a letter to staff, EA chief operating officer David Tinson and chief studios officer Cam Weber said a small team at BioWare had been working on early development for Mass Effect, and "we've made the decision not to move forward with this development, which means we're closing the studio and our Edmonton workspace."

The next Mass Effect was first teased in 2020 and never shown beyond teasers. The last new entry in the series was Mass Effect: Andromeda in 2017.

The wider cuts

Bloomberg's Jason Schreier reports around 400 EA employees — roughly 3% of the company's staff, including about 50 at BioWare — are being laid off. The restructuring is the first sweeping change since EA went private in August 2026 in a $55 billion buyout led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund.

The legacy

BioWare was founded in Edmonton in 1995 and acquired by EA in 2007. Its run: Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter Nights, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age and Mass Effect. Its most recent release, 2024's Dragon Age: The Veilguard, underperformed by EA's own account.

Why it matters

This isn't just a studio closing — it's the end of the road for one of gaming's most beloved series, at least in this form. EA says the Mass Effect project will not move to another internal studio. For a generation of players, Shepard's story now ends where Andromeda left it.
