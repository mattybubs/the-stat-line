Dead by Daylight Removes TheBurntPeanut's Free Content in a Second, Longer Statement

By Matt

Behaviour Interactive has removed the Dead by Daylight content gifted to streamer TheBurntPeanut, saying it moved too fast and broke trust — and its second statement, posted November 3, lays out exactly what it says went wrong and what changes now.

Dead by Daylight's Halloween-weekend controversy has a second chapter. Behaviour Interactive has now removed the in-game content it gifted to Twitch streamer TheBurntPeanut, and its longer follow-up statement — posted November 3 on the main Dead by Daylight account — admits the studio moved too fast, skipped its own checks, and broke trust with its community.

This is a follow-up to our November 2 post on the first apology. That first statement, posted November 1 on the smaller DbD Pulse account, said the gift was made without appropriate approvals. The new statement, a four-page post, is Behaviour's attempt to answer the questions players were actually asking.

What happened

On October 30, during a Halloween stream, TheBurntPeanut played Dead by Daylight for the first time. Members of Behaviour's team noticed a very large audience in the game's Twitch directory watching a streamer who had not played it before, and saw a chance to welcome new players.

A staff member then gifted TheBurntPeanut access to the game's DLC characters, perks, and in-game currency during the livestream. When TheBurntPeanut offered to give the content back, concerned about the backlash to come, the official account replied in stream chat: "WE CAN TAKE THE HEAT! THEY ARE YOURS TO KEEP!"

The backlash that followed had two layers. Critics raised concerns about Behaviour partnering with TheBurntPeanut, pointing to clips from his past streams, and many players objected to the dismissive tone of the official account's reply. Some players said they uninstalled the game as the dispute escalated.

What Behaviour says it got wrong

The November 3 statement breaks the failure into three parts, in the studio's own framing:

Due diligence. The decision to give the creator in-game content and join his chat was made before taking the necessary time to look at whether it was a good idea. It happened quickly and outside the team's usual discussions. Behaviour says it normally looks thoroughly at a new creator's past content to judge whether they represent the values of inclusion its community expects. In this case, it says, that was not taken into proper consideration or discussed as a group.

Internal process. The studio says it did not have stringent enough processes and guardrails to prevent in-game items from being granted directly to players without larger consensus and approval. A team member acted without a supporting system behind them to guide them to another outcome.

Communication standards. The "WE CAN TAKE THE HEAT!" reply, along with some other messages in that session, showed a disregard for the context the team was suddenly in and crossed the line of appropriate responses from the brand, Behaviour says. That reply escalated the situation for the studio and for the community, especially for content creators on the frontlines of those conversations, and that was unacceptable.

Behaviour also states its values in the post: respect, transparency, diversity, and creating a space where people from different backgrounds and experiences feel welcome. Diversity is one of Dead by Daylight's core principles, it says, and when it chooses to work with a creator, it is putting its name next to theirs.

What is changing now

• The content is gone. Behaviour says it has already removed the content that was granted to the creator.

• Creator guidelines are being rewritten. That includes how the team interacts with creators playing Dead by Daylight, who on the team can make those calls, and how to identify creators whose past conduct is in direct opposition to the inclusive community Behaviour says it is trying to build.

• Approval methods are being updated. Request and approval methods for granting content and currency to players are being reworked, with checks and balances at the right time, and the team will be trained on the new processes.

• Creator Program rules are being enforced directly. Creators found to have violated Creator Program policy will result in removal. Any hold or removal will be communicated directly to the creator involved, with no additional communications on social channels or in program Discords. All removals are at the team's discretion.

• Internal action is confidential. Behaviour says it takes the situation very seriously and is taking appropriate action internally, but as a confidential matter it will not share further details.

The statement closes with a direct admission: "This incident has broken trust with our community who hold expectations and regards for the types of people we partner with, but more importantly expectations of the conduct and actions taken by our team in our daily work within this community. We understand it will be a slow road to regain that trust but we are committed to doing the work necessary to get there."

The response, by the numbers

The November 3 statement post itself drew a large reaction on X, with 11K comments, 4.4K reposts, and 28K likes at the time of the screenshots shared for this story — a measure of how widely this one is still being watched, not a verdict in itself.

TheBurntPeanut has also responded on stream, saying he saw the reaction online and noting a refund request that cited the collaboration and the communication team's response. He said he did not think he had said anything about transgender people in the last two to two-and-a-half years of streaming, and asked whether he ever had. That response is his, not Behaviour's, and the underlying allegations from critics remain allegations about past clips, not findings in Behaviour's statement — which focuses on its own process, not on adjudicating the streamer's history.

Why this one matters professionally

Strip away the noise and this is a process story, which is exactly why it is worth covering straight. Giving a creator a sneak peek at content is not unusual in itself — Behaviour's own first statement notes that. What made this one different, by Behaviour's own account, was speed without approval, a check that never happened, and a public reply that poured fuel on it instead of slowing it down.

The test now is not the statement. It is the next time a big streamer drops into the Fog: who approves the gift, what check happens first, and whether the public response matches the values the studio just restated.
