BLG beats Gen.G 3-1 to win Worlds 2026 — China's first Summoner's Cup in five years

By Matt

Bilibili Gaming dropped the opening game, then won three straight to beat Gen.G 3-1 in the Worlds 2026 final at Barclays Center — the organization's first title, the LPL's first since 2021, and the end of Korea's four-year run.

The final

Gen.G won the opening game emphatically — 19 kills to 4 in 39 minutes — and for a moment it looked like another Korean coronation. Then Bilibili Gaming did what it had done to every Korean team in its path: it adjusted, it absorbed, and it won three straight.

BLG beat Gen.G 3-1 in the League of Legends World Championship final at Barclays Center in Brooklyn, claiming the organization's first world title in only its third Worlds appearance. Game 2 stretched to 53 minutes — the longest game of the tournament — before BLG leveled the series. Game 3 ended in 37 minutes. Game 4 closed it in 42, with top laner Chen "Bin" Ze-Bin on Gwen and BLG seizing Baron before destroying Gen.G's Nexus.

The result ends a five-year drought for China's LPL — the first Chinese team to lift the Summoner's Cup since EDward Gaming in 2021 — and a four-year run of Korean Worlds titles. BLG took home $1 million of the $5 million prize pool; Gen.G received $800,000 as runners-up.

One piercing four

The final was the culmination of a knockout run the Chinese press dubbed "one piercing four" — BLG personally eliminated all four Korean seeds on its way to the trophy. It beat Hanwha Life Esports in the qualifying stage, swept Dplus KIA 3-0 in the quarterfinals, edged defending champion T1 3-1 in the semifinal, and then overcame Gen.G in the final.

Mid laner Zhuo "Knight" Ding was named Finals MVP, winning his first world title after years as one of the best players never to lift the Cup. Bin, Xun and ON claimed their first titles alongside him. And marksman Park "Viper" Do-hyeon won his second — five years after his first with EDward Gaming in 2021 — becoming a two-time world champion as an LPL player.

What came next

Hours after the final, Riot Games dropped a teaser for League of Legends: New Dawn, with a full reveal planned for January 2027 and a release later in 2027. The timing was no accident: the biggest stage in the sport, a new champion, and a first look at what's next for the game itself.

One footnote on the night: the final peaked at 3.98 million viewers, the lowest since 2020, per Esports Charts — a number that leaves out Chinese streaming platforms entirely, and one shaped by a 3 AM start in China, a 4 AM start in Korea, and a final without T1. The audience that counts most for this result — the one in China, watching a Chinese team end a five-year wait at 3 in the morning — isn't in that number at all.
