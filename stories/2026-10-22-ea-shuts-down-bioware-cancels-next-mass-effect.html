EA Shuts Down BioWare After 31 Years, Cancels Next Mass Effect

By Matt

Electronic Arts is closing BioWare — the studio behind Mass Effect, Dragon Age and Knights of the Old Republic — and cutting around 400 jobs. The next Mass Effect, still in early development, is cancelled with it.

Electronic Arts is shutting down BioWare, the legendary studio behind Mass Effect, Dragon Age and Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, as part of a restructuring that cuts roughly 400 jobs — about 3% of EA's workforce.

What happened

Gaming publication Gematsu reported on October 21 that EA had informed staff of the decision, citing company communication and Bloomberg reporting. Around 50 of the cuts hit BioWare itself, which had already shrunk dramatically — Bloomberg's Jason Schreier reported the studio was down to roughly 50 employees before the shutdown.

BioWare confirmed the news in a statement shared on X, bidding farewell after more than 30 years and confirming that early development on the next Mass Effect project is ending.

"There is nothing easy about writing those words," the studio wrote. "Our focus right now is on our incredibly talented team who put their hearts into these games, and supporting one another through this transition."

The legacy

BioWare's statement looked back over three decades: Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter Nights, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age, Mass Effect — and yes, Anthem. The studio defined narrative-driven RPGs: companion relationships, player choice, worlds that reacted to you.

The next Mass Effect is gone. Your existing library isn't — previously released games stay in players' libraries; support details live on the official storefronts.

The wider fallout

The cuts go beyond BioWare. EA's memo to staff said the company will concentrate on fewer, bigger bets: its sports titles, Apex Legends — and Battlefield is confirmed safe. But a Sims project codenamed Project Lotus was also cancelled, per Insider Gaming.

The memo framed it as focus: "deepening its core, expanding its reach, and transforming the business." For the people who built some of the most beloved RPGs ever made, it's the end of an era.
