FaZe are out of the PGL Singapore Major at 1-3 — HEROIC end their season in Stage 1

By Matt

HEROIC beat FaZe 2-1 in the elimination series on Thursday, dumping one of Counter-Strike's biggest names out of the PGL Major Singapore at 1-3 — 28th to 30th in a 32-team field, and FaZe's worst Major finish since IEM Rio 2022.

FaZe Clan are out of the PGL Major Singapore. HEROIC beat them 2-1 in a Stage 1 elimination series on Thursday — Mirage 13-3, Ancient 13-11 to FaZe, Nuke 13-3 — and the 1-3 record leaves FaZe 28th to 30th in a 32-team Major, their worst finish at a Major since IEM Rio 2022. PGL confirmed the exit on X at 08:42 UTC, minutes after HEROIC closed out the deciding map.

It is the end of a season that started with promise and fell apart. FaZe were not even at the IEM Cologne Major in June — they missed out through Valve's standings — and Singapore was their only Major of 2026. A year earlier they reached the grand final of the StarLadder Budapest Major, losing 3-1 to Team Vitality. This time they never left the first stage.

How HEROIC knocked them out

The series opened on FaZe's pick of Mirage, and it was over almost before it started. HEROIC won it 13-3, and FaZe were one map from elimination inside the first hour. FaZe answered on HEROIC's pick of Ancient, taking it 13-11, and the team account quickly posted that FaZe still had life.

Nuke, the map left over after both sides made their bans, ended the series in one direction. HEROIC won it 13-3, the same score as the opener, and FaZe managed only six rounds across the two maps they lost. HEROIC wasted little time on X, pointing FaZe toward the airport minutes after the win.

It is not the first time HEROIC have ended a FaZe Major either — they also beat FaZe 2-1 in the quarterfinals of the 2023 Paris Major.

A week that never got going

FaZe's Major began badly on November 24, when Wildcard beat them 13-9 on Nuke in the opening round. The first three rounds of Stage 1 are best-of-one matches, so a single bad map was enough to push FaZe straight into the 0-1 pool. They recovered later that day with a 13-3 win on Mirage over GOSH, the former BC.Game roster that includes s1mple, after s1mple's team had already lost to Team Liquid.

The second day brought another best-of-one defeat, this time 13-10 to PARIVISION on Anubis. That loss dropped FaZe into the 1-2 pool, and the Stage 1 Day 2 results paired them with HEROIC. Across six maps in Singapore, FaZe won 51 rounds and lost 66.

The numbers tell the same story as the scorelines. David "frozen" Čerňanský led FaZe with a 1.06 rating in the elimination series — top of the board for his team — but he went missing on the decider with a 0-9 start on the offense. Captain Russel "Twistzz" Van Dulken finished at 1.00, and AWPer Jason "JBOEN" Boe Nielsen at 0.76. On the other side, HEROIC's Linus "nilo" Bergman led all players at 1.24, with Tim "susp" Ångström and Ludvig "Brollan" Brolin both at 1.22.

Twistzz took the blame — and the roster's future is open

Twistzz did not hide after the loss. Speaking on the PGL broadcast, he said FaZe were "not a team that felt extremely competitive coming into this event" and that "throughout the season we regressed a lot as a team and maybe even individually." He took responsibility as captain and said he needed more out of a young lineup that features two Major rookies in JBOEN and Ryan "Neityu" Aubry.

"I'm not the most experienced captain, and I do my best work when I have a second voice to ping-pong with," he said. "I can tell people are pressured, nervous, I have to speak and I have to create ideas for them to move on the map."

He said the same thing on X after the match: "Said most of my thoughts in the post game. Disappointing to see where we are at and it's a shame to see the faze name far down in the ranks. See everyone next season."

frozen was shorter: "Shameful. Nothing else to say, see you next season."

The questions now are real ones. Twistzz's future as in-game leader is under scrutiny after a season in which he took over following Finn "karrigan" Andersen's surprise departure in April — karrigan went on to lift the Cologne trophy as a Falcons stand-in. JBOEN is only on loan from BIG, a loan that runs until the end of 2026, and he told Dust2.dk after the exit that his situation "has been uncertain in FaZe the whole time" and that he feels like he has "already got one and a half legs out of the camp, unfortunately." frozen, meanwhile, has reportedly been pursued by several organizations over the last year, and signed an extension only weeks before karrigan left.

The season that led here

The early exit ends what HLTV called another disappointing season for FaZe. It started in promising fashion despite karrigan's exit, with back-to-back top-four finishes in the team's first two outings in China and Malta with Twistzz taking over in-game leadership. Then it fell apart: FaZe could not make it past the top 16 in any of their top event outings, finishing 13th to 16th at the Esports World Cup, FISSURE Playground 3, and IEM Beijing at the start of November, where they lost both series 2-1 to FURIA and Falcons. On November 15 they lost to fnatic and HEROIC in the closed qualifier for PGL Cluj-Napoca 2027.

The early exit also cuts into FaZe's share of the Major Shop money. Valve's Singapore Major update says half of the proceeds from tournament items go to players, teams and the organiser, shared according to each team's initial seed and final placement — and a finish near the bottom of the field puts FaZe close to the bottom of that split, at a time when sticker money for teams is already under scrutiny.

HEROIC move to 2-2 and play again on November 27 for a place in Stage 2, alongside a Day 3 that also saw Team Liquid, Eternal Fire and PARIVISION through. Stage 1 finishes on November 27, and the Major moves to the Singapore Indoor Stadium for the playoffs from December 10 to 13. FaZe had not announced any roster changes by the time of writing.
