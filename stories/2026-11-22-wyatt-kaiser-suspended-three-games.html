Wyatt Kaiser suspended three games for the illegal check to the head that knocked Martin Pospisil out of the game

By Matt

The NHL's Department of Player Safety suspended Blackhawks defenseman Wyatt Kaiser three games without pay for an illegal check to the head of Calgary's Martin Pospisil on Saturday night. Pospisil left the game and did not return.

The league wasted no time

The NHL's Department of Player Safety announced Sunday that Chicago Blackhawks defenseman Wyatt Kaiser has been suspended for three games, without pay, for an illegal check to the head of Calgary Flames forward Martin Pospisil.

The hit came late in the second period of Saturday night's game in Calgary, with the Blackhawks leading 3-2. Kaiser was assessed a match penalty on the play and a game misconduct, and the league scheduled a phone hearing for Sunday — which caps any suspension at five games — before handing down the three-game ban.

Under the collective bargaining agreement, Kaiser will forfeit $26,288.67 in salary. The money goes to the Players' Emergency Assistance Fund.

What the league saw

The Department of Player Safety released its usual explainer video with the ruling, and it did not leave much room for debate. The hit satisfied both elements of the illegal check to the head rule: the head was the main point of contact, and the contact was avoidable.

"First, the head is the main point of contact," the video states. "Kaiser takes a poor angle of approach and cuts across the front of Pospisil's body, missing his core and making direct contact with his head. And, it is Pospisil's head that absorbs the vast majority of the force."

The league also noted that Pospisil did nothing to contribute to the contact — while stickhandling he pulled the puck toward his body, but his head stayed level and moved predictably. He did not change the position of his head or body just before or simultaneously with the hit.

Pospisil was carrying the puck out of the Flames' zone when Kaiser stepped in at the blue line and drove his shoulder into Pospisil's head. Pospisil went down, left the game with an upper-body injury, and did not return. He is 27 and has a history of concussion problems, which makes any head shot land harder in Calgary.

Kaiser didn't see it coming

Speaking to reporters after Saturday's game, Kaiser sounded like a player expecting a fine at most — not a multi-game ban.

"Not really, to be honest," he said when asked if he was concerned about supplemental discipline. "Because at the end of the day, if they really look at it and say, hey, that's really dangerous, (then) you're suspended for a game or two, take a fine, and then you're back."

The league disagreed, and went a game longer than even Kaiser's own guess. It is the first suspension of the 24-year-old's NHL career — he had never been fined or suspended before Sunday.

What it costs Chicago

Kaiser will miss Sunday night's game at Vancouver, Wednesday's trip to Seattle, and Friday's home game against the New York Rangers. Kevin Korchinski is expected to take his spot on the blue line.

The timing is rough for a team that is already shorthanded. Captain Connor Bedard is out after shoulder surgery this week, and center Anton Frondell left the third period in Calgary with an undisclosed injury and is still being evaluated. Chicago gutted out a 4-3 overtime win on Saturday — Sam Rinzel scored with 15 seconds left in the extra frame for the Blackhawks' first road win of the season — but the blue line keeps getting thinner.

Flames coach Ryan Huska had no official update on Pospisil after the game. "I think he's doing fine, but I don't really have anything official yet," he said. For a player with his concussion history, "doing fine" the night of the hit is the start of the story, not the end of it.
