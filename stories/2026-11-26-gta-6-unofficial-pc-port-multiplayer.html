GTA 6's unofficial PC port just got multiplayer — two Jasons in one Leonida, before GTA Online even exists

By Matt

The unofficial GTA 6 PC port now has working multiplayer: two players sharing one Leonida, driving cars at each other, talking over proximity voice chat — at a claimed 25 to 30 frames per second. The build is private, the footage is verified, and Rockstar still hasn't announced a PC version at all.

The unofficial GTA 6 PC port has multiplayer now. Not a menu, not a launcher — two players, in the same Leonida, at the same time.

French developer TeK posted footage on November 26 showing two Jason Duvals meeting at an airport, driving cars toward each other and interacting in one shared game world, with proximity voice chat running. TeK says the build runs at a stable 25 to 30 frames per second. The community tracker PortWatch VI says it has verified the footage is genuine — though it has not verified the frame-rate figure, which comes from the developer.

One week after launch, modders have built the thing Rockstar hasn't even announced.

What the footage actually shows

The clip is short and the demo is small: two players, one airport, cars, and voice. Notebookcheck, VideoCardz, Dexerto, and WCCFTech all covered the same footage on November 26, and the details line up across them. The build supports character and vehicle synchronization plus proximity voice chat, according to TeK. PortWatch's report notes the menu also allows teleporting around the map and spawning vehicles — this is still a developer's debugging playground, not a server browser.

There is a limit worth stating plainly. The demo shows two players. It does not establish how the build performs with more players, in busier parts of the map, or over a longer session. And some skeptics have suggested the second Jason could be a spawned NPC using Jason's model — PortWatch's verification is the counterweight there, but nobody outside TeK's group can test the build themselves, because the code is not public.

A fork of the build Take-Two already killed

TeK's multiplayer runs on a private fork of CGBJ's build — the same CGBJ whose repository vanished from GitHub on November 26, minutes after going public. Take-Two's DMCA notice on November 24 took down the Leonida project's network of 3,007 repositories, and CGBJ was one of the first projects the publisher targeted. The code TeK is building on was public only briefly before going offline.

That is the pattern of this whole saga now. The work spreads into private forks faster than the takedowns can reach it, and each private fork pushes the state of the art a little further. Three days ago the port needed one to two seconds to draw a single frame. On November 26 the public forks were running the open world at around 20 frames per second with a mod menu already built. Now a private fork has two players in the same world.

For scale, PortWatch's leaderboard puts the fastest verified public build — Belobug's AMD fork — at about 22 frames per second on a Radeon RX 9070 XT with 64 GB of RAM. The private CGBJ build that TeK's group forked averages about 25 frames per second in single-player. TeK's 25 to 30 claim for multiplayer sits just above that, and it is unverified.

Still not a game you can play — and still not legal to get

None of this is an official port, and Rockstar has announced no PC version of GTA 6. The game is listed only for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Reports have pointed at an official PC release in early 2027 with GTA Online to follow, but that is speculation, not an announcement.

And the warning from every previous post in this saga still stands: there is no playable PC version of GTA 6, and there is no safe way to get one. The leaked files have not been independently authenticated. Downloading them is piracy, and files circulating from strangers carry real malware risk — the port tracker is already flagging fake repositories distributing malware under the guise of GTA 6 port tools. TeK's build is private. Nobody can download it, test it, or verify the frame rate but TeK's group.

What is verified is the direction of travel. The port went from a single frame every two seconds to two players sharing a world in four days. The takedowns keep landing. The private forks keep moving anyway.
