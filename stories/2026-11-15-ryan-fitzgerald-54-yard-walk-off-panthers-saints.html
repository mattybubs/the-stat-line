54 yards, as time expires: Ryan Fitzgerald walks off the Saints in New Orleans

By Matt

Charlie Smyth tied it from 55 with 41 seconds left. Ryan Fitzgerald answered from 54 as time expired — his fifth career game-winner, and first place in the NFC South for Carolina.

The day

The Carolina Panthers beat the New Orleans Saints 20-17 at Caesars Superdome on Sunday, and the ending was as wild as a division game gets. Ryan Fitzgerald hit a 54-yard field goal as time expired to win it — 41 seconds after Charlie Smyth tied the game with a 55-yarder of his own. Fitzgerald finished 2-for-3 on field goals. Chuba Hubbard carried 27 times for 92 yards and two touchdowns and added five catches for 80 yards, for 172 total yards. Bryce Young went 17-of-30 for 200 yards with no touchdowns and no interceptions. Tyler Shough finished 25-of-35 for 187 yards with one touchdown and one interception.

The final minute

With 1:43 left, Shough drove New Orleans 41 yards in eight plays to set up Smyth, who drilled the longest field goal of his young career to tie it at 17 with 41 seconds on the clock. The Superdome had barely finished roaring when Carolina got the ball back — and a defensive pass interference call on Saints cornerback Kool-Aid McKinstry against Jalen Coker moved the Panthers to the New Orleans 36-yard line with 21 seconds left.

One Hubbard rush later, Fitzgerald walked on with two seconds remaining and knocked it through right down the middle. It was the longest walk-off field goal in Panthers history, and the fifth game-winner of his young career — a franchise record for a kicker in his first two years with the team, and the third-most game-winners by any Panthers kicker overall. He knew it was good the second it left his foot.

Hubbard carried them

None of it happens without Hubbard, who was Carolina's offense all afternoon. His one-yard touchdown run at 1:49 had given the Panthers the lead in the first place, capping a day where he scored twice on the ground and did damage as a receiver, including a 34-yard catch-and-run that put Carolina on the doorstep. The pregame speech was his, the carries were his, and when the game was on the line, the ball kept finding him.

The kicker's day

Fitzgerald's afternoon started badly. He missed a 48-yarder in the second quarter, sailing it just outside the left upright — an overcorrection, by his own admission, after spending the week fixing a run of misses to the right. He gives himself about two minutes to be upset about a miss, then gets back in the net and moves on. The next opportunity took an hour to arrive: a 55-yarder late in the third quarter, attempted from the Saints' 37 on fourth-and-5, that put Carolina up 10-7. He was already halfway onto the field before head coach Dave Canales had finished deciding to try it.

The track record is why the trust was there. Fitzgerald hit four game-winners as a rookie last season, tying the modern NFL rookie record, and his teammates have a standing rule about him in those moments: nobody talks to the kicker. Just let him work.

The stakes and the mess

The winner took sole possession of first place in the NFC South, and both teams came in at 3-5 — which tells you plenty about the division, and plenty about why this one mattered. Carolina is now 4-5 and snapped a three-game losing streak; New Orleans fell to 3-6.

It was not a clean win. Carolina was flagged for a franchise-record 17 accepted penalties for 152 yards, and the Saints' only lead of the day came on Alvin Kamara's three-yard touchdown run in the third quarter. But the Panthers kept answering — Fitzgerald's 55-yarder after Kamara's score, Hubbard's go-ahead touchdown, and then the walk-off. In a division this tight, that is enough.

By the numbers

Panthers record: 4-5 (sole possession of first place, NFC South)

Saints record: 3-6

Panthers penalties: 17 accepted, 152 yards (franchise record)

Charlie Smyth: 55-yard FG to tie it at 0:41 (longest of his young career)

Alvin Kamara: 3-yard TD run (Saints' only lead)
