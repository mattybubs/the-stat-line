Showa American Story delayed to October 28, 2027 — a year of polish after five years in the making

By Matt

NEKCOM Games has delayed Showa American Story from its 2026 window to October 28, 2027 — the alternate-history RPG where Japan bought America, with the core game complete and the extra year going to polish.

The wait for one of gaming's strangest upcoming RPGs just got a year longer. NEKCOM Games has delayed Showa American Story from its planned 2026 window to October 28, 2027, publisher 4Divinity confirmed today. The post-apocalyptic romance RPG — the alternate-history oddity set in a 1980s where Japan bought America — will launch on PlayStation 5 and PC via Steam.

It is the second schedule move for the game, and this time it comes with a firm date and a reason to believe it. In a message from the studio's executive producer, NEKCOM said the core game is now complete after five years in development, and the extra time will go entirely to polishing and optimizing. "We're dedicating our full focus to polishing and optimizing the experience so that when you step into Showa American Story, it feels cohesive, responsive, and complete," the message reads.

A long road to a real date

Showa American Story has been slipping quietly for a while. First announced in January 2022, it originally targeted a 2025 release before NEKCOM pushed it into 2026 last November, saying development was entering its final stages. The Steam page tipped the latest move earlier today when its release window flipped to 2027 — the official date followed within hours.

The game re-entered the spotlight at Gamescom 2026, where a new trailer and the first hands-on demo were shown during Opening Night Live in Cologne. It was one of Game Informer's favorites of the show — a zany, raunchy action game that stood out precisely because nothing else on the floor looked like it.

What the game actually is

The pitch is as wild as the delay is mundane. You play Choko — Chigusa Choko — a young woman who suddenly revives from the dead and sets off on a cross-country journey through a post-apocalyptic California, in a world where Japan's 1980s economic power was strong enough to buy America outright. The city she travels through is Neo Yokohama, formerly Hollywood. The road is filled with zombies, monsters, and an eccentric cast of survivors — some friendly, others downright bad — including Michael Hero, Bubba, and the mysterious Mutsuki Akitora.

The Gamescom trailer, narrated by the United States' anime-obsessed President, showed off the mix that defines the game: brutal hack-and-slash combat against wasteland gangs and the undead, motorcycle travel across the desert, and a pile of minigames — arcade cabinets like SpaceBunny and ShowaRun, a hopscotch activity, and a Space Harrier-inspired sequence — all wrapped in Showa-era Japanese pop culture colliding with American roadside iconography.

The delay stings for a game that felt close — the Gamescom showing had it penciled in for sometime this year, and the demo suggested something playable was nearly there. But a finished core game and a full year of polish is a much better place to be than another vague window. The next stop is a real date on the calendar, and for a game this strange, that is progress worth waiting for.
