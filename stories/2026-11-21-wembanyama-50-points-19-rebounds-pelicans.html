Wembanyama drops 50 and 19 — a stat line nobody in NBA history had ever posted

By Matt

Victor Wembanyama went for 50 points, 19 rebounds, five blocks and four steals as the Spurs rolled the Pelicans 114-91 — the first 50-19-5-5-4 game in NBA history.

Victor Wembanyama did something Saturday night that no NBA player had ever done before. The 22-year-old Spurs star poured in 50 points, grabbed 19 rebounds, blocked five shots, hit five three-pointers and added four steals as San Antonio rolled the short-handed New Orleans Pelicans 114-91 at Frost Bank Center — a combination of numbers that had never appeared in a single box score in league history.

He did most of the damage before halftime. Wembanyama had 32 points and 13 rebounds in the first two quarters alone, shooting 12-of-15 from the field in 18 minutes — and single-handedly outscored New Orleans' entire starting five. The Spurs led 61-45 at the break and never looked back, stretching the lead to 29 in the fourth before Wembanyama reached the half-century mark on consecutive three-pointers, the second putting San Antonio up 108-82 with 6:29 left. He checked out shortly after.

The final line: 20-of-29 from the field, 5-of-11 from deep, 5-of-6 at the line, one assist, three turnovers, and a plus-35 in 31 minutes and 51 seconds of work.

A first half for the record books

The first half alone would have been a career night for most players. Wembanyama's 32 points before the break were the second-most in a half by any Spur since the league started using play-by-play data in the 1996-97 season, and he became just the second player to compile at least 30 points, 10 rebounds, two steals and two blocks in a single half since the NBA introduced half-by-half tracking in 1997-98.

He is also the first player this season to record 30 points, 10 rebounds, three blocks and three steals in a game — and he had it wrapped up by halftime.

The Pelicans tried to get physical with him, and it backfired twice. Midway through the second quarter, Jaron Pierre Jr. shoved Wembanyama from behind after a foul by Karlo Matković, drawing technicals for Pierre and for Keldon Johnson, who stepped in. Wembanyama answered by scoring the next seven points, including a spin around Yves Missi for a one-handed slam. A few minutes later, De'Aaron Fox was whistled for a technical after a two-handed shove on Jeremiah Fears — and Wembanyama responded again, scoring the next five.

"Tonight he got it going," Pelicans coach Jamahl Mosley said afterward. "A lot of that was tough to guard and obviously he made some big-time shots."

The history around the night

The full line puts Wembanyama in company of three. He is only the fourth player ever to record at least 50 points, 15 rebounds and five blocks in a game, joining Wilt Chamberlain — who did it five times — Anthony Davis and Bob McAdoo. Remarkably, it is the second time he has done it this month: on November 4 he went for a career-high 52 with 24 rebounds and eight blocks in a 112-111 win over Dallas, the first 50-20-5 game in 50 years.

He also joined David Robinson as the only players to record multiple 50-point, five-block games since blocks became an official stat in 1973-74.

It was the third 50-point game of Wembanyama's career, and his second of the season. The Nov. 4 outburst against the Mavericks remains his career high by two points.

Asked afterward how special the night was, Wembanyama couldn't resist a joke at the rest of the league's expense: "It is very special. But unlike many of my peers, I'm not very good at stat-padding. So, I haven't reached it yet," he said, via the Spurs' account on X.

The Spurs keep rolling

The win moved San Antonio to 14-3, tied with the Los Angeles Lakers for the best record in the Western Conference, and the Spurs have now won 14 of their last 15. They did it without Dylan Harper, who missed his second straight game with a strained left hip, and still limited themselves to 13 turnovers after committing a season-high 21 in their first game without him.

Stephon Castle added 19 points, six assists and three rebounds while tying his career high with five three-pointers. De'Aaron Fox handed out 10 assists, and Devin Vassell and Keldon Johnson each scored 10.

The numbers behind the dominance are stark. Entering Saturday, Wembanyama was averaging 28.6 points, 11.9 rebounds and a league-leading 3.9 blocks per game. San Antonio owns a plus-26.7 net rating with him on the floor this season — and a minus-14.4 when he sits.

For New Orleans, Jeremiah Fears led the way with 29 points, Jordan Poole scored a season-high 25 off the bench, and Derik Queen posted 15 points and 15 rebounds. But the Pelicans were gutted before tip: Zion Williamson was a late scratch with a left thigh contusion, Trey Murphy III sat with a knee issue, Bennedict Mathurin remained out with plantar fasciitis, and Dejounte Murray is still away from the team after having his agent request a trade on November 13. The loss was their third straight and ninth in their last 10.

The Pelicans host Phoenix on Monday. The Spurs visit Sacramento the same night — and after a night like this one, the rest of the league is running out of ways to describe what Wembanyama is doing.
