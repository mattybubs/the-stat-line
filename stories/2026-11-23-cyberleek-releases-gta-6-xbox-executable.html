Cyberleek released GTA 6's missing Xbox executable — the full build is out, and an unofficial PC port is now in play

By Matt

Cyberleek delivered on its promise Monday, releasing the missing executable for its leaked 209GB Xbox build of Grand Theft Auto VI — the final piece unofficial PC port projects have been waiting for. Rockstar has not commented, and the files have not been independently verified.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The last piece of the leaked GTA 6 build is out. On Monday, the leaker known as Cyberleek released the missing Xbox executable for Grand Theft Auto VI — the one file it had held back from the near-complete build it put on torrent trackers on launch day. With it, the full November 2026 Xbox build is now circulating, and the unofficial PC port projects that have been waiting on exactly this file can start testing in earnest.

What was released

Cyberleek had distributed the bulk of the build — roughly 209 gigabytes of Xbox game files, tagged version 1.0.2.1 — on November 19, the day GTA 6 launched on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. The main executable was the only piece withheld, held on a countdown that was set to expire today, November 23.

The file arrived about three hours ahead of that deadline, as a 57MB archive posted to Cyberleek's site alongside SHA-256 checksums for the executable and its ZIP, with a note to verify the files before trusting them. "The exe is out. Always free," Cyberleek wrote. "Take the Xbox build and the main executable as an early Christmas gift, from CYBERLEEK to every gamer."

The crypto countdown

The early release came with a price tag attached — just not one anyone paid. Cyberleek had said it would drop the executable early if its CYBERLEEK memecoin reached a $15 million market cap, a target already lowered from $30 million. The token was trading around $1.3 million, nowhere near the goal, and Cyberleek released the file on the promised date regardless. The coin pitch has been the throughline of this saga from the start: back in October, Cyberleek made the same threat — release the build unless the token hit its target — and the memecoin framing has drawn open skepticism that the whole operation is a pump scheme as much as a leak.

What this does — and doesn't — mean for PC

The executable does not make GTA 6 playable on PC. It is an Xbox build, not a Windows one, and there is still no official PC version announced — Rockstar has said nothing about a PC release date at all. What the file does is unblock the recompilation projects that have been preparing for it.

The most prominent is Leonida, from NotRexed — the modder behind the native PC recompilation of the Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. The project aims to bring the Xbox version to Windows through static recompilation, translating the console's precompiled GPU programs and reimplementing its system software so the game's code runs natively on PC. Its own status page is blunt about where things stand: early, and the game does not boot yet. Everything that could be done without the executable was already in place, so the real work starts now. Cyberleek has predicted the community will have the build emulated within a week, running past 30 frames per second — a bold claim for a project that has not booted once.

The catch: nothing is verified, and copycats are already circulating

Every report on this release carries the same caveat: the files have not been independently authenticated, and neither Rockstar nor Take-Two has commented on them. Checksums prove only that a download matches what Cyberleek posted — not that Rockstar compiled the binary, and not that it is free of tampering. One porting project inspecting the earlier 209GB package already found signature hash mismatches across 12 of its DLL files that nobody has explained.

That uncertainty is now a security problem, not just a provenance one. Trojan-laced copycats of the leak are already circulating, and several outlets are warning readers outright not to go hunting for the files. This post will not link to Cyberleek's site or the executable.

Why Cyberleek says it's doing this

The readme accompanying the release frames the leak as a fight against anti-consumer practices — subscriptions bolted onto single-player games, paid "Ultimate Editions" — and insists the target is Take-Two and Rockstar, not players. Cyberleek says its earlier leaks deliberately avoided story spoilers and never leaked the ending, because ruining the game would hurt the people it claims to stand with.

That framing sits uneasily next to the record. Cyberleek spent the months before launch leaking gameplay footage and the in-game map, and Rockstar described those leaks as "heartbreaking" after years of work on the game. Take-Two has filed subpoenas seeking to identify the leaker through Microsoft and Discord, and the leaker remains unidentified. Whatever the stated motive, the memecoin sat at the center of every deadline — and the executable only arrived once the countdown, not the market cap, ran out.

Four days after launch, GTA 6 is the biggest entertainment release ever — and now its complete console build is in the wild, with a community race to get it running on PC before Rockstar ever announces one. Whether that race produces a playable game in a week, as Cyberleek claims, or a pile of malware and dead ends, is the next thing to watch.
