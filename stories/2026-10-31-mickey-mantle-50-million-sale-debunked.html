The $50 Million Mickey Mantle Sale That Wasn't — Goldin Calls the Claim 'False News'

By Matt

A podcast claim that a PSA 10 1952 Topps Mickey Mantle sold privately for $50 million sent the hobby into a frenzy — until Goldin founder Ken Goldin shot it down 'with 100% fact' and Darren Rovell reported the deal was contemplated but never consummated.

The hobby got its biggest headline of the year this week — and then the headline got debunked within hours.

Entrepreneur and collector Patrick Bet-David said on his podcast that one of the three PSA 10 examples of the iconic 1952 Topps Mickey Mantle #311 had traded privately for $50 million. Had it been real, it would have been the most expensive trading card ever sold by an enormous margin. The claim spread fast. Then the pushback arrived even faster.

"That is false news"

Ken Goldin, founder of Goldin auctions, responded directly on X: "That is false news. An aggressive rumor that is not true; I can state that with 100% fact. A 1952 mantle has NOT sold for $50 million."

Sports business reporter Darren Rovell added the nuance that explains how the rumor likely started: "While this transaction has been more than contemplated, I have been told that it has not been consummated." In other words, a deal involving one of the three PSA 10 Mantles may have been discussed or negotiated — but no money changed hands, no ownership transferred, no buyer or seller has been publicly identified, and no auction house has confirmed any sale.

Why $50 million never passed the smell test

The card's rarity is real: PSA's population report lists only three copies of the 1952 Topps Mantle graded Gem Mint 10, with none graded higher. But the math never worked. The highest recorded public sale for a 1952 Mantle is the CGC-graded 9.5 version that sold for $12.6 million through Heritage Auctions on August 28, 2022. A jump from $12.6 million to $50 million in four years — with no public confirmation from any party to the deal — was always going to need more than a podcast mention.

The episode is a useful reminder for a hobby running hot: the difference between a contemplated deal and a completed one is everything, and the biggest number in the room is not always a sale. The record for a Mantle remains $12.6 million. The $50 million card, for now, exists only as a rumor.
