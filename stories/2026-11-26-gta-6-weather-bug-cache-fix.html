Rockstar's patch didn't fix GTA 6's stuck weather for everyone — players found a cache-clear workaround that brings the storms back

By Matt

Leonida was supposed to have fog, thunderstorms, and hurricanes — but some players have had nothing but sunshine since launch, even after Rockstar's first patch. A cache-clear workaround players are sharing today is finally letting the weather change.

One of the things Rockstar talked up most before GTA 6 launched was the weather. Thick rolling fog, violent thunderstorms, full-blown hurricanes — a system that could rain on one side of Leonida while the sun shone on the other, with wet roads, puddles, and clothes that stayed damp after a storm. A week into the game, a lot of players have seen none of it. Their Leonida has been stuck on sunny, day after day, and Rockstar's first patch didn't fix it for everyone. Now players have found their own fix, and it's a quick trip through the console's Safe Mode.

The patch that was supposed to fix it

The stuck weather was a known bug from launch week. Rockstar's first post-launch update, 1.002.002, rolled out on November 23 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its patch notes included a line aimed squarely at the problem: "Fixed an issue that resulted in the weather not changing for extended periods." The same patch fixed the broken autosaves, crashes, and story-blocking phone calls.

For plenty of players, that was the end of it. But numerous players kept reporting the same problem after updating — endless sunshine, no rain, no storms, no change at all. One player at the center of today's workaround had gone 56 hours without experiencing dramatic weather before trying the fix.

The fix players are passing around

The workaround surfaced on November 26, when GameRoll shared it on X, crediting user @mauricioce85796 with originally finding it. The idea is simple: clear the console's system cache, which appears to let the game's weather system start cycling properly again. Several players have since reported that storms appeared after loading their save following the clear.

On PlayStation 5, the steps are:

1. Turn off the PS5 console completely.

2. Once it is fully powered down, press and hold the power button until you hear a second beep — about seven seconds — to enter Safe Mode.

3. Connect your controller with a USB cable and press the PS button.

4. Scroll down to Clear Cache and Rebuild Database.

5. Select Clear System Software Cache and let the console restart.

Then launch GTA 6 and resume playing. On Xbox Series X|S, the equivalent is a full shutdown, unplugging the power cable for two minutes, then plugging it back in and turning the console on.

What to expect — and what Rockstar hasn't said

A few things to know before trying it. First, don't expect a thunderstorm the second you load back in. The weather system is still dynamic, so it may take some time exploring Leonida before the next storm rolls through — the fix appears to unstick the cycle, not summon rain on demand. Second, clearing the system cache does not delete game saves, so there's no progress at risk. And third, Rockstar has not officially confirmed this workaround as a solution. It is player-reported, not studio-endorsed — but the number of players reporting success, including one outlet that tried it and noticed a change the same night, makes it worth trying if your Leonida has been stuck in permanent sunshine.

If neither method works and the patch hasn't helped, the honest answer is to wait for another update. But for players who have spent a week wondering where the weather went, a quick Safe Mode clear is a cheap thing to try.

One more thing not to confuse with the bug: hurricanes. They don't just show up in the normal weather rotation — they unlock through a secret bunker side mission up in North Leonard County, by Lake Leonida, and only start appearing randomly after that quest is done. If your weather is cycling but you've never seen a hurricane, that's the game working as designed, not the bug.
