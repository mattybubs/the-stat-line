Red Sox predicted to sign Freddy Peralta for $92M

By Matt

Sports Illustrated's Nicholas Selbe predicts Boston lands Freddy Peralta on a four-year, $92 million deal — a bet on the ace's Tampa turnaround after Sonny Gray elected free agency.

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. I earn a commission on qualifying purchases.

The prediction

The Red Sox need a starter, and one of the offseason's biggest names is being linked to Boston. Sports Illustrated's Nicholas Selbe predicted the Red Sox will sign Freddy Peralta to a four-year, $92 million contract this offseason.

The fit is obvious. Sonny Gray has elected free agency, leaving a hole at the top of Boston's rotation. Peralta is a two-time All-Star and one of the top arms on the open market — and after a wild 2026, his value is trending back up at exactly the right time.

The Jekyll-and-Hyde season

Peralta's 2026 was a tale of two teams. He opened the year with the New York Mets, where it went sideways: a 5-9 record and a 4.99 ERA in 22 appearances. A deadline trade to Tampa Bay changed everything.

With the Rays, Peralta went 5-2 with a 3.25 ERA in 10 appearances, allowing just three earned runs over his final six regular-season starts — a 0.79 ERA down the stretch. Overall for the season, he finished 10-11 with a 4.42 ERA and 156 strikeouts in 32 games.

Then came October. Peralta was named ALCS MVP after posting a 0.75 ERA over three appearances (12 innings) against Cleveland, helping the Rays reach the World Series against his old club, Milwaukee.

Why Boston believes in the Tampa version

The Rays version of Peralta is the one Boston would be paying for — and it's not hard to see why. A year earlier, in his final season with the Brewers, Peralta went 17-6 with a 2.70 ERA and 204 strikeouts, finishing fifth in NL Cy Young voting. That is the track record underneath the messy Mets half-season: a durable, strikeout-heavy starter with 30 starts in four consecutive years.

The question for Boston is which version shows up in 2027. The case for the Red Sox: the Tampa turnaround wasn't a small sample blip. It was a full second-half reset — a 3.25 ERA, a dominant closing stretch, and an ALCS MVP — from a 30-year-old entering his age-31 season who has done this before.

The competition

Boston won't be alone in the bidding. MLB Trade Rumors' Steve Adams predicted Peralta lands with the Chicago Cubs on a five-year, $110 million deal, and the Blue Jays, Giants, Padres, White Sox, and Orioles have all been named as fits. Spotrac has floated a seven-year, $201 million projection, while The Athletic's Jim Bowden predicted five years and $140 million.

Selbe's $92 million over four years would be the shorter, cheaper end of that range — a bet that Boston can land the ace without going to the nine-figure lengths other suitors might. Whether Peralta takes a shorter deal or chases the longer one is the open question of his winter.

What it would mean for Boston

If the prediction holds, the Red Sox would be buying a proven top-of-the-rotation arm to replace Gray — and keeping him away from the rest of the AL East in the process. The Rays aren't expected to re-sign him at this price, and every team in the division knows what the Tampa version of Peralta looks like. Boston landing him would be a statement that the rotation hole gets filled with a real ace, not a stopgap.
