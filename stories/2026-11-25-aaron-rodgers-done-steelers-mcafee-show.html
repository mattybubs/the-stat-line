Aaron Rodgers says he's done in Pittsburgh — 'that ship has sailed' — and torches Mike McCarthy on Pat McAfee

By Matt

Aaron Rodgers went on the Pat McAfee Show on Wednesday and said he will not return to the Steelers — then accused Mike McCarthy of sleeping through meetings, skipping walk-throughs, and calling '1-2-3 Packers' at halftime in Philadelphia.

The quote

Aaron Rodgers is done with the Pittsburgh Steelers, and he said so himself on Wednesday.

Appearing on ESPN's "Pat McAfee Show" in his first public comments since the Steelers benched him late Tuesday night, Rodgers dismissed any possibility of a return after Mason Rudolph was named the starter for Friday's Black Friday game against the Denver Broncos.

"At this point, I'm not going back to Pittsburgh," Rodgers said. "That ship has definitely sailed."

USA Today, Sports Illustrated, the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel and the New York Post all carried the quote within the hour. Rodgers added that he did not see this ending coming, and did not want it to end this way — but that the team has chosen to back McCarthy, and the players' position is clear.

The ultimatum

Rodgers said the break came down to a conversation on Monday, when he told McCarthy that the offense needed a change for the betterment of the team. In Rodgers' telling, he gave the coach a choice: stop calling plays, or release him.

"I think there's got to be a change," Rodgers said he told McCarthy. "You don't want me to call it because you don't think I'm going to call enough runs? Fine, let somebody else call it, that's what's in the best interest of the team."

McCarthy did neither. The Steelers instead announced Tuesday night that Rudolph would start and Rodgers would be inactive — fourth string, behind 2025 draft pick Will Howard and 2026 third-rounder Drew Allar. Rodgers said he never heard the word "benching" from McCarthy at all, and did not learn of the decision from the team. He found out in a phone call with tight end Pat Freiermuth after a contentious meeting with McCarthy on Tuesday night, and said he has not heard from McCarthy since their last conversation in the 6 p.m. window that evening.

He also said the issue was not a secret inside the building. Rodgers claimed he told general manager Omar Khan about McCarthy's alleged sleeping issues, and he criticized Khan and owner Art Rooney II for permitting what he described as unprofessional behavior while knowing how McCarthy conducted himself. He called it "comedy" that Khan would defend McCarthy while knowing about it.

The accusations

The heart of the interview was a series of specific claims about McCarthy's preparation and presence — claims Rodgers said go back to training camp, not just the last week.

He alleged that McCarthy frequently fell asleep in meetings or did not show up to them at all. He described one quarterbacks meeting last Wednesday where McCarthy came in late and began snoring within 10 minutes: "Woke himself up, was rattling his papers, we all looked around, five minutes later, you hear the snoring again," Rodgers said. "Less than five minutes after that, he walked out of the room like nothing happened."

Rodgers further claimed that McCarthy missed an offensive meeting and a protective assignments walk-through on Thursday, skipped Friday's team walk-through, and missed Saturday's morning meeting before the Eagles game — a meeting that mattered more because the team did not meet Saturday night in Philadelphia.

"I don't think he knows the plan," Rodgers said. "I think he learns the plan during the week. ... That part is disrespectful to the game itself."

He also alleged that McCarthy has made little effort to spend time with the defensive players, and quoted the coach's response when Rodgers encouraged him to do so: "I don't think I need to." Rodgers said McCarthy had "kind of lost a lot of those guys," and contrasted him with former Steelers coach Mike Tomlin: "You don't have to be Mike T, but you have to connect with the guys."

Rodgers said McCarthy denied the allegations and would get "super angry" if others mentioned them, and said multiple other players in the locker room would vouch for having witnessed the behavior. He framed his own willingness to speak as a function of having nothing to lose: the coaching staff worries about job security, the players worry about their money and job security, and he does not.

McAfee, listening to it all, replied that he was worried about McCarthy after hearing what Rodgers said.

"1-2-3 Packers"

The moment Rodgers called the breaking point for the locker room came at halftime of Sunday's 19-16 loss to the Eagles — a defeat to a team that had lost seven straight, and Pittsburgh's second loss in a row.

According to Rodgers, McCarthy went to break down the team and instead of saying "1-2-3 Steelers," said "1-2-3 Packers."

"That was one of those things where nobody was even like saying Steelers because it was so in the moment and it was like this weird rambling something," Rodgers said, "that it was odd enough everyone across the entire locker room heard it and I think we all looked around going, 'What the f–k? What is going on? Where's his head at at this point?'"

McCarthy coached Green Bay from 2006 until a midseason firing in 2018, and Rodgers alleged the coach still holds that firing against him. "In Mike's mind, I got him fired in 2018. I'm the reason. He told a staff member that," Rodgers said. "That he still doesn't forgive me for getting him fired in 2018."

Ten years in the making

Rodgers said he has been sitting on this for a decade — that the same behavior was happening in Green Bay, and that he covered for McCarthy there for years.

"I sat on this for a long time. I sat on it for 10 years. 10 years in Green Bay with this stuff going on," he said. "But we worked around it, we won division championships, went to the playoff for eight straight years. ... I covered for him for a lot of years. The people in Green Bay knew what was going on. The people in Dallas knew what was going on."

He said he looked into McCarthy's tenure with the Cowboys from 2020-24 and found the same things happening there. He also contrasted McCarthy's process with that of his former Packers coach Matt LaFleur and Rams coach Sean McVay, saying LaFleur was not missing those types of meetings.

The irony, which Rodgers owned on the show, is that he helped bring McCarthy to Pittsburgh. After Mike Tomlin stepped down last January, Rodgers said he advised Khan to give McCarthy an interview — and vouched for him in his exit interview after the 2025 season, hoping it would be different in McCarthy's hometown, in his dream job, with Rodgers in his final year.

"It's on me. Because I vouched for him and I had the hope that it was going to be different," Rodgers said. "It's insanity. It's on me. I bought into the insanity. I knew this was a possibility."

He signed a one-year deal to return to Pittsburgh in May, citing the relationship with McCarthy — his coach for the Packers' Super Bowl win at the end of the 2010 season and two of his four MVP awards — as an enticing reason to come back for a 22nd season. He also said on his return that this season would be his last in the NFL.

What happens now

The Steelers excused Rodgers from Wednesday's practice, and he took them up on the offer. The team has not released him — he remains on the roster, inactive as the fourth-stringer — and there were no immediate plans to release him, per NFL Network's reporting earlier Wednesday.

Rudolph starts Friday against the Broncos. Rodgers, who turns 43 next week, has started 10 games for Pittsburgh, completing 60.9% of his passes for 2,241 yards, 16 touchdowns and six interceptions, and has been sacked 31 times. The Steelers sit at 6-4, holding an AFC wild-card spot, with their season now in Rudolph's hands.

Rodgers said he regrets that he will not get to move forward with teammates he respected, who he insisted "felt the exact same way." Whether he has played his final NFL game is now an open question — he has said this is his last season, and he has now said he will not play another snap for Pittsburgh. A release would let another team sign him. Until then, the most decorated quarterback of his generation is a healthy scratch who just told the world exactly why.
