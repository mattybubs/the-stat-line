Julian Erosa's Fairytale Ending: A First-Round KO in His Retirement Fight

By Matt

Julian Erosa was badly hurt in the opening seconds of his retirement fight — then turned the tables and knocked out JeongYeong Lee at 1:31 of round one, walking away with a $100,000 bonus and a 32-13 career record.

Julian Erosa could not have scripted it better, and for a few terrifying seconds it looked like the script was going the other way. The featherweight veteran, fighting the final bout of his career on Saturday's UFC Vegas 123 prelims, was badly hurt in the opening seconds against South Korea's JeongYeong Lee. Then, in one of the wildest momentum swings of the year, Erosa turned the tables and knocked Lee out cold with a right hand at 1:31 of round one.

The sequence was pure Erosa: hurt, unfazed, dangerous. Lee touched gloves and came out swinging, landing the kind of shots that end retirement fights early. Erosa weathered them, found an opening, and uncorked the right hand that put Lee to sleep — ground-and-pound finishing the job before the referee could even wave it off.

A career that defied the odds

Erosa retires with a 32-13 record and a reputation built the hard way: as the underdog who kept winning fights he was supposed to lose. Saturday was the perfect distillation of that career — nearly finished in the first ten seconds, victorious ninety seconds later, $100,000 Performance of the Night bonus in hand.

Fighters almost never get the ending they want. The sport is cruel that way; most careers end on a loss, in a quiet arena, with the crowd already heading for the exits. Erosa got the rare one: a knockout win, on a Halloween card, with the whole building on its feet, walking away on his own terms.

The bonus picture

Erosa was one of four Performance of the Night winners at UFC Vegas 123, joining main-event winner Tom Nolan, submission artist Joe Kropschot and debuting finisher Lucia Szabova at $100,000 apiece. It is hard to imagine a more fitting final paycheck for a fighter who made a career out of exceeding expectations.

Enjoy retirement, Julian. You earned the storybook version.
