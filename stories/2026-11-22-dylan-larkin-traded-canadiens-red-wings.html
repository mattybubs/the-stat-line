Canadiens acquire Dylan Larkin from Red Wings in blockbuster — Dach, Montembeault, Hage and four picks go to Detroit

By Matt

The Montreal Canadiens acquired captain Dylan Larkin from the Detroit Red Wings on Sunday, sending Kirby Dach, Sam Montembeault, the rights to Michael Hage and four draft picks — including first-rounders in 2027 and 2029 — the other way.

The captain is gone

The Montreal Canadiens acquired Dylan Larkin from the Detroit Red Wings on Sunday, ending a month-long standoff that had the Detroit captain sitting at home, suspended without pay, waiting for a trade.

Canadiens general manager Kent Hughes announced the deal, and the price was a haul. Detroit gets forward Kirby Dach, goaltender Sam Montembeault, the rights to forward prospect Michael Hage, a first-round pick in 2027, a second-round pick in 2027, a fourth-round pick in 2028 and a first-round pick in 2029. Montreal also gets Detroit's second-round pick in 2028 and its third-round pick in 2027.

Larkin is 30, and he has been one of the most consistent goal scorers in the league — at least 30 goals in each of the last five seasons, six times in his career. He had six points (two goals, four assists) in 10 games with Detroit this season, and he led Red Wings forwards in average ice time at 21:41 a night.

What Montreal is getting

Since making his NHL debut in 2015, Larkin has 278 goals and 649 points in 818 games with Detroit, ranking tenth in franchise history in both categories. He also helped the United States win the gold medal at the 2026 Milano-Cortina Olympics and represented his country at the 4 Nations Face-off in 2025.

The fit is obvious. Nick Suzuki already commands Montreal's top line with 20 points in 17 games, and Lane Hutson leads the club with 24 points from the blue line — but the Canadiens have been hunting a second-line center all season. Larkin is that, with a track record of 30-goal seasons and a captain's resume.

His contract runs through 2030-31 at an $8.7 million cap hit.

How it got here

Larkin told Detroit in October that he would not play another game for the team. The Red Wings announced it, suspended him without pay, and he has been sitting out since October 29 — forfeiting more than $1 million in earnings along the way, according to The Athletic.

The holdup was never the package. It was Larkin's no-movement clause. In October, the Detroit Free Press reported he would facilitate a move to one of 10 teams — Minnesota, Florida, Vegas, Dallas, Washington, Carolina, Utah, Tampa Bay, New Jersey and Colorado. Montreal was not on that list, and The Athletic reported the Canadiens were frozen out despite strong interest.

So this deal needed Larkin's sign-off, and he gave it. Detroit, sitting at 8-11-3 under head coach Todd McLellan, finally got the reset it has been circling for weeks — two first-round picks, a young goalie, a former top prospect in Dach, and Hage's rights.

For Montreal, the message is just as clear. The Canadiens paid a premium — two firsts, two more picks, a roster forward, a goalie and a prospect — because they believe Larkin is the piece that turns a good young core into something more. Now he has to prove them right, in a new sweater, in the same division as the team he just left.
