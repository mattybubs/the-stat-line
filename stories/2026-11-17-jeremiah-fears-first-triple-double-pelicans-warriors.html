Jeremiah Fears, 20, becomes the youngest Pelican ever with a triple-double — New Orleans snaps its skid, 115-94 over Golden State

By Matt

Jeremiah Fears had 19 points, 10 rebounds and 10 assists for his first career triple-double, and at 20 years and 33 days old he is the youngest player in Pelicans history to do it — passing Chris Paul's mark from April 2006 — as New Orleans snapped a six-game losing streak with a 115-94 win over Golden State.

The night

The Smoothie King Center crowd knew what was happening before the box score did. Late in the third quarter, Jeremiah Fears grabbed his 10th rebound, the building erupted, and a 20-year-old rookie had the first triple-double of his career — 19 points, 10 rebounds and 10 assists in a 115-94 win over the Golden State Warriors that snapped New Orleans' six-game losing streak.

Fears did it at 20 years and 33 days old. That makes him the youngest player in Pelicans franchise history to record a triple-double, passing a mark Chris Paul set at 20 years and 331 days on April 2, 2006 — a record that had stood for more than two decades. He is also the ninth-youngest player in NBA history to post one.

The game itself was never close. New Orleans led 35-11 after one quarter and pushed the lead as large as 31 in the third. Fears shot 6-of-13 from the field and 1-of-4 from three in 29 minutes, and by the time he sealed the milestone the only question left was the final margin.

The help around him

Saddiq Bey led the Pelicans with 23 points, hitting five threes, and Zion Williamson added 16. Yves Missi grabbed 14 rebounds in his second game back from a knee injury that sidelined him 11 games, and Derik Queen — drafted six spots after Fears — came off the bench for 17 points and 10 rebounds, tying a career high with four threes. Jeron Pierre Jr. added 10 points.

New Orleans shot 42.3 percent from the field and 15-of-40 from three, outscored Golden State 44-32 in the paint, and outrebounded the Warriors 57-50. It was the kind of complete night the Pelicans, now 5-10, have been waiting for all season.

The other side

Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg scored a game-high 28 points for Golden State, with Brandin Podziemski adding 15 and Gui Santos 12. But the Warriors shot 33.3 percent from the field and 11-of-46 from three, and this was their third straight loss — the second in a row by 20 or more, coming on the heels of a 123-85 defeat at San Antonio on Friday night.

Golden State is playing without Stephen Curry, sidelined at least two weeks with a right oblique tear suffered in a 115-108 loss at Houston on Wednesday. The gap between the Warriors with Curry and without him has been stark, and Monday night was the latest evidence.

What it means

A triple-double at 20 years and 33 days puts Fears in rare company — and passing Chris Paul to do it, in a Pelicans uniform, is the kind of line that follows a player. Paul's mark had survived every Pelican since, including Anthony Davis and Zion Williamson. Fears cleared it by nearly ten months.

The win matters too. Six straight losses had the Pelicans buried at the bottom of the West, and a 21-point win over a shorthanded Warriors team doesn't fix the season — but a franchise-record night from the seventh overall pick is exactly the kind of thing a rebuild is supposed to produce.

Up next: the Warriors visit Dallas on Wednesday, and the Pelicans host Detroit the same night.
