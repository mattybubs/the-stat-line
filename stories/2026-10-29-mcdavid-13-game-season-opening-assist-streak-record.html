McDavid Sets NHL Record With an Assist in a 13th Straight Game to Open the Season as Oilers Blank Flames 4-0

By Matt

Connor McDavid set up two goals in Edmonton's 4-0 shutout of Calgary, passing Gretzky, Linseman and Aho for the longest season-opening assist streak in NHL history — 13 games and counting, with 36 points already.

Connor McDavid is rewriting the record book before November. The Edmonton captain had two assists in the Oilers' 4-0 shutout of the Calgary Flames on Wednesday night, giving him at least one assist in each of Edmonton's first 13 games of the season — the longest season-opening assist streak in NHL history.

The old mark was 12, shared by three names you do not pass lightly: Wayne Gretzky (1982-83), Ken Linseman (1985-86) and Sebastian Aho (2018-19). McDavid broke it in the first period, feeding Mathieu Joseph for the game's opening goal on Edmonton's first shot, then added a second helper on Kasperi Kapanen's power-play goal early in the third.

The numbers are silly

McDavid now has 36 points (8 goals, 28 assists) in 13 games — a pace that leads the NHL by a wide margin. For context on how hot October has been: his 33 points in the month tied Gretzky's 1986 mark for the third-most points in a single October in league history, per Sportsnet's game coverage.

The rest of the night belonged to the supporting cast. Mattias Ekholm scored in his return to the lineup, Alex Formenton added a goal 21 seconds after Kapanen's, and Tristan Jarry stopped all 24 shots he faced for his first shutout of the season and the 24th of his NHL career.

The other side

Calgary's four-game winning streak is over, and the Flames lost more than a game: starting goalie Devin Cooley left after the first period with a lower-body injury after a collision in the crease with Edmonton's Colton Dach, and forward Brennan Othmann also left after the first following a fight. Dustin Wolf stopped 20 of 23 in relief.

Edmonton (9-2-2) is back on top of the Western Conference. McDavid's streak, meanwhile, rolls on — every game now is a new record.
