Thanksgiving Grandma story becomes a Lifetime movie

By Matt

A wrong-number Thanksgiving text in 2016 turned into a decade-long friendship — and now Wanda Dench and Jamal Hinton's story is becoming a Lifetime movie, aiming for a November 2027 release.

The announcement

Ten years after a wrong-number text turned two strangers into family, the story is becoming a movie. Wanda Dench and Jamal Hinton have signed the rights to their story to Lifetime, which is developing a dramatization under the working title The Thanksgiving Grandma. Producer Jack Leslie is aiming to start filming in early 2027, with a November 2027 release.

Hinton confirmed the deal on Instagram on November 19, writing that he was "very excited to officially be in business with Lifetime." Dench told The Arizona Republic on November 16 that the two had recently signed over the rights, and that Lifetime told them up front the film would be based on their story.

The text that started it

In November 2016, Dench meant to text her grandson an invitation to Thanksgiving dinner. He had changed his number, so the message landed on the phone of Hinton, then a 17-year-old high school student in Arizona who had never met her. When he asked who was texting, she replied, "It's your grandma."

Hinton sent back a selfie and a question that made the whole thing famous: "You're not my grandma. Can I still get a plate though?" Dench's reply — "Of course you can. That's what grandmas do… feed everyone" — turned a mix-up into an invitation. He showed up at her home in Mesa that Thanksgiving, and he has been at the table every year since.

A friendship that outlasted the viral moment

What makes the story worth a movie is everything that happened after the screenshot faded. The two stayed in each other's lives through the hard parts: Dench's husband Lonnie died in 2020, and Dench faced a breast cancer diagnosis in 2024. She is now cancer-free, and the pair still meet every year around the holiday, their families growing into each other's.

Both have put the attention to work. Hinton is developing a nonprofit called Bounce Back, focused on youth sports and skills, while Dench runs neighborhood support efforts. The film is expected to cover more than the original mix-up — how they supported each other through the losses and the diagnosis, and what a decade of showing up actually looks like.

The movie

Screenwriter Glen Dolman is writing the script and recently spent a weekend at Dench's home getting to know the pair. Production is expected to begin in early 2027, with a possible shoot in the Phoenix metro area or New Mexico. No cast has been announced.

This isn't the first screen adaptation of the story — Netflix announced its own version, The Thanksgiving Text, back in December 2021. The Lifetime film is the one moving now, with Dench and Hinton involved directly and a release targeted for next Thanksgiving.
