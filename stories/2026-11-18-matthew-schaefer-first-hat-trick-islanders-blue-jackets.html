Matthew Schaefer, 19, just became the third teenage defenseman in NHL history with a hat trick — and the first Islander to do it since Denis Potvin in 1982

By Matt

Matthew Schaefer scored his first career hat trick — at 19 years and 73 days old, the second-youngest defenseman ever to do it — as the Islanders beat the Blue Jackets 5-1 at UBS Arena for their third straight win.

The night

The hats rained down at UBS Arena, and they were raining for a teenager who plays defense.

Matthew Schaefer scored his first career hat trick on Tuesday night, leading the New York Islanders to a 5-1 win over the Columbus Blue Jackets. At 19 years and 73 days old, he became the second-youngest defenseman in NHL history to record a hat trick — behind only Phil Housley, who was 18 years and 320 days old when he did it — and just the third teenage defenseman ever to do it, after Housley in January 1983 and Hannu Virta in March 1983.

He is also the first Islanders defenseman with a hat trick since Denis Potvin on March 4, 1982, and only the third in franchise history, joining Denis and his brother Jean Potvin.

Schaefer opened his scoring 1:34 into the second period, ripping a shot from the slot past Cam Talbot. He added a second at 7:01 of the third, backhanding home a rebound off Ryan Pulock's shot after Zach Werenski had cut the lead to 2-1. Then he completed it with an empty-netter at 16:08 of the third, slamming into the boards in elation as the hats flew.

He finished with three goals and nine shots in 23:23 of ice time.

The season

The hat trick is not a bolt from the blue — it is the loudest night yet of a breakout. All nine of Schaefer's goals this season have come in the past 11 games, and he has at least one point in six straight. Through 20 games he has 24 points while averaging 26:58 of ice time a night.

The career numbers are already historic. He now has 32 goals before turning 20, the second-most ever by a teenage defenseman behind Housley's 46. The reigning Calder Trophy winner and No. 1 overall pick of the 2025 draft is playing like a franchise defenseman who happens to still be a teenager.

The game around him

Anthony Duclair opened the scoring with a power-play goal in the final minute of the first period and finished with a goal and an assist. Tony DeAngelo added a goal and an assist of his own, Mathew Barzal had two assists, and Ilya Sorokin stopped 28 shots. Werenski scored the only Columbus goal, and Talbot made 36 saves in the loss.

The win was the Islanders' third straight and moved them to 10-6-4, with points in five straight games.

What it means

A defenseman scoring three goals in a game is rare. A 19-year-old defenseman doing it puts him in a group of three in the entire history of the league — and the other two did it more than 40 years ago. The Islanders have not had a defenseman do this since Potvin, and Potvin is the standard every Islanders defenseman gets measured against. Schaefer is 19, and he is already in that conversation.
