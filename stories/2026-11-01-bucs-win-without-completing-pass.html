No Completions, No Problem: Bucs Win 16-0 Without Completing a Pass, First Time Since 1974

By Matt

Baker Mayfield went 0-for-3 and the Bucs won anyway, rushing for a franchise-record 324 yards in a 16-0 shutout of Atlanta — the first win without a completion since the 1974 Bills.

The Tampa Bay Buccaneers won a football game on Sunday without completing a single pass. Not one. Baker Mayfield went 0-for-3, and the Bucs still beat the Atlanta Falcons 16-0 in the rain at Raymond James Stadium.

It was the first time any NFL team had won a game without a completion since the Buffalo Bills did it on September 29, 1974, when Joe Ferguson went 0-for-2 against the Jets. That Bills team also scored exactly 16 points. Fifty-two years later, Tampa Bay matched the feat and the score.

How you win throwing nothing

You run it 56 times for 324 yards, a franchise record, against a top-five rushing defense, in a downpour.

Bucky Irving carried 27 times for a career-high 170 yards and the game's only touchdown, a 4-yard run with 3:47 left in the third quarter that pushed the lead to 16-0. Kenny Gainwell added a career-high 140 yards on 25 carries of his own. It was just the second time in Bucs history that two backs topped 100 yards in the same game, after Mike Alstott and Warrick Dunn did it on November 1, 1998.

Chase McLaughlin did the rest of the scoring, hitting field goals from 46, 34, and 42 yards for a 9-0 halftime lead. Irving also lost a fumble through the end zone for a touchback in the first quarter, but on this day, nothing else went wrong for Tampa Bay.

The defense did its part too

Atlanta's offense never had a chance. Michael Penix Jr. finished 8-of-24 for 125 yards with four interceptions — two by Alex Anzalone in the first half, plus one each from Jacob Parrish and Zyon McCollum. The Falcons managed 224 total yards, went 0-for-12 on third down, and were stopped three times on fourth down. Bijan Robinson, the back Tampa Bay was worried about all week, managed 76 yards on 19 carries. Nick Folk missed a 53-yard field goal on Atlanta's opening trip, and the Falcons never got closer.

It was only the second shutout of Atlanta in series history for Tampa Bay, the other coming in 2004.

What it means

The win is Tampa Bay's second straight, after last week's 43-13 rout at Carolina, and it snapped an 0-4 home skid. The Bucs are 3-5, 2-0 against NFC South opponents, and within a half-game of Carolina for first place in a division where nobody is above .500. A team that looked like it was playing out the string two weeks ago is suddenly right in the middle of a wide-open race — and it got there by not completing a pass.
