Tyler1 lasted one week on League's new voice chat before Riot banned him

By Matt

League's team voice chat launched November 18. One week later, Tyler1 — one of the game's biggest streamers — got hit with three punishments at once for what he said on it, live on his own stream.

League of Legends finally gave every player a microphone — and it took Tyler1 one week to get banned for what he said into it.

Tyler Steinkamp, better known as Tyler1, picked up three punishments at once: a three-day ban from League itself, a seven-day suspension from voice chat, and a 30-day suspension from the League Partner Program. He found out about it live on his own Twitch stream, mid-game, the same way his audience did.

What happened on stream

The ban traces back to a clip from his stream that went viral. Playing with a randomly matched teammate on Nami, Tyler1 repeatedly insulted the player on the new team voice chat — at one point telling his teammate to kill themselves, then covering it up by verbally drumming over the words.

His defense, delivered on stream after the ban landed: he was joking around with the Nami player, they laughed about it afterwards, and the game was a stomp anyway. "Context doesn't matter apparently," he said, repeatedly, while railing against the severity of the punishment.

Riot's side of it is straightforward. When the company announced team voice chat, it said from the start that voice conversations would be recorded and could lead to in-game punishments. A report option for voice abuse was built into every reporting menu from day one. Tyler1 tested the system in its first week, on camera, in front of millions of followers — and the system did exactly what Riot said it would do.

The feature was seven days old

Team Voice arrived November 18 with Patch 26.23, and it is the biggest change to how League players talk to each other since the game launched. Before this patch, voice chat only worked inside a premade party. Now any player can talk to their whole team — randomly matched strangers included — during champion select and in game, in every queue except Classic.

The rollout is deliberately narrow for now: North America and Oceania only, with other regions to follow once Riot trusts the moderation tooling. It is push-to-talk only, players below Honor level 3 can listen but not speak, and five optional Voicemod voice skins let players change how they sound to their team. Riot framed the whole thing as a test, and said it would expand only as it gained confidence in moderation.

Tyler1 is the test's first famous failure. That is the part that matters for everyone else: the moderation is not theoretical, it is not slow, and it does not care how big your stream is. One week, one viral clip, three punishments.

The same story as 2016 — and the opposite

There is history here, and it is the reason this ban landed the way it did. Tyler1 first became famous for getting banned. In April 2016, Riot handed him an indefinite, identity-level ban — no League account, ever, under any name — after a documented history of verbal abuse and harassment, with a Riot employee calling him a "genuine jerk" in the announcement. He spent 613 days in exile, streaming variety content and running his own Tyler1 Championship Series, before Riot lifted the ban in January 2018. His return stream peaked at more than 382,000 concurrent viewers, a non-tournament record on Twitch at the time.

Since then he has been the redemption story Riot points to: a partner, a regular on official broadcasts — he even appeared on the Worlds 2026 final broadcast this month — and a streamer who built his brand on having cleaned up his act. That is what makes the partner-program suspension the sharpest of the three punishments. A three-day game ban is a weekend off. A 30-day suspension from the League Partner Program is Riot saying, publicly, that the reformed-Tyler1 story has a crack in it.

He is not the only one testing the new lines, either. Fellow streamer TheBausffs was also suspended recently for feeding, which suggests Riot is enforcing the new voice-chat era quickly and visibly rather than letting the first month slide.

The question now is whether the ban changes anything. Tyler1 has been here before — banned, furious, insisting it was a joke — and the last time, the ban made him bigger. This time the stakes are smaller and the message is narrower: the microphone is on, it is being recorded, and the first week proved it.
