Red Sox open to trading Duran and Yoshida

By Matt

With Roman Anthony, Wilyer Abreu, and Ceddanne Rafaela locked in as the starting outfield, the Red Sox are open to moving Jarren Duran and Masataka Yoshida this offseason.

The Boston Red Sox are open to trading Jarren Duran and Masataka Yoshida this offseason. That's the word from Alex Speier of The Boston Globe, reported by Bleacher Report, as Boston looks to improve a roster that struggled down the stretch and was swept in the AL Wild Card round.

The reason is simple: the outfield is crowded, and the future is already set. Roman Anthony, Wilyer Abreu, and Ceddanne Rafaela are the starting outfield, all under team control through at least 2029. That leaves Duran and Yoshida, as Speier put it, in "a familiar state of limbo."

"With that trio in place for years, Duran and Yoshida reside in a familiar state of limbo," Speier wrote. "Either can contribute but the Sox are open to moving one or both."

The logjam

Boston has five outfielders for three spots, and the three who are staying are the three youngest and cheapest. Anthony, Abreu, and Rafaela are locked in for years. Duran and Yoshida are the odd men out — both useful, neither with a clear everyday role anymore.

Duran played 151 games in 2026 in large part because Anthony was unavailable for so much of the season. If Anthony had been healthy, Duran's role would have been a talking point for months. Yoshida, meanwhile, has been squeezed into a left field / designated hitter platoon that doesn't really have room for him either.

Duran: the fascinating, frustrating trade chip

Duran is the harder call. His ceiling is high and his floor is what makes teams hesitate.

He was an All-Star in 2024 and won the All-Star Game MVP award, hitting .285/.342/.492 with 21 home runs and 34 steals that season. Then came the slide. He hit just .207/.266/.361 in 151 games in 2026 — but still went 20/20, with 20 home runs and 21 steals.

That's the paradox of Duran's trade value right now: the power-speed combination is still there, but his chase and whiff rates soared in 2026 and his batting average cratered. His value is at an all-time low even after a 20/20 campaign, and the Sox have flirted with dealing him for two years now.

The money complicates it further. Duran turned 30 on September 5 and has two years of arbitration remaining before free agency. He's projected to earn about $11 million in 2027 through arbitration — an eight-figure salary for a player without an everyday role. The Sox may decide they'd rather sell low than carry that number.

Still, teams looking for outfield help would almost certainly have interest. A 30-year-old with 20/20 ability, two years of control, and a track record of an All-Star season is a real trade chip, even coming off a down year. Back in 2025, the San Diego Padres reportedly offered a package including ace Dylan Cease and elite catching prospect Ethan Salas for Duran, per Sean McAdam of MassLive — a reminder of what his value looked like before the slide.

Yoshida: the contract is the problem

Yoshida's situation is more straightforward, and harder to solve. He's entering the final season of the five-year, $90 million contract he signed to come over from Japan after seven seasons with the Orix Buffaloes. He has $18.6 million remaining on the deal.

He hit .274/.347/.399 in 87 games in 2026 — respectable numbers, but he's played just 142 games over the last two seasons, and at $18.6 million for a part-time DH, the contract is the obstacle. As MassLive's Chris Cotillo put it, Yoshida "looks like a goner" — Boston could try to trade him again, something it hasn't had luck with before, or simply cut bait and eat the money.

The Sox need to add a power bat this winter, and they'll need somewhere to play him. Moving Yoshida — or Duran — is how that spot opens up.

What it means for the offseason

This isn't a fire sale. It's a roster squeeze with a clear logic: three young outfielders are set, two veterans are in the way, and Boston needs to upgrade elsewhere — power, pitching, or both.

The open question is what the return looks like. Duran's value is depressed after 2026, and Yoshida's contract limits his market. The Sox will explore both markets, and how aggressive they get may depend on how much they need the money and the roster spot for the bigger moves they want to make.

One thing is clear: it would be a surprise if Boston entered the 2027 season with the same outfield it had in 2026.
