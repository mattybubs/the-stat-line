Guardians bet $17.5 million on Kris Bubic — a one-year splash for a franchise that rarely makes them

By Matt

Cleveland agreed to a one-year, $17.5 million deal with 2025 All-Star Kris Bubic on Tuesday, betting the left-hander's shoulder is behind him and his 3.02 ERA is real.

The move

Cleveland does not do this often — which is exactly why this one stands out.

The Guardians agreed to a one-year, $17.5 million contract with left-handed pitcher Kris Bubic on Tuesday, Ben Nicholson-Smith of Sportsnet first reported, with ESPN's Jeff Passan confirming the agreement and MLB's Mark Feinsand among the reporters corroborating it. Passan's summary said it plainly: the 2025 All-Star heads to one of the best pitching organizations in baseball on a big one-year number.

For a franchise that almost never splashes in free agency, $17.5 million for one season of a pitcher is a statement. The short term softens the risk. The number says they believe in the arm.

The player

Bubic's case is a bet on the pitcher he was before his shoulder gave out.

He pitched just 55 innings in 2026 after missing a big chunk of the season with a shoulder injury, and he was traded from Kansas City to the Dodgers at the deadline. When he finally got back on a mound in September, he fired 4 2/3 scoreless innings for Los Angeles — a small sample, but a clean one.

Go back a little further and the upside is obvious. Since the beginning of last year, Bubic has a 3.02 ERA with 167 strikeouts against 65 walks in 166 2/3 innings over 29 starts. That is a mid-rotation starter's line, and the All-Star nod in 2025 says the league noticed.

The fit

Cleveland still needs to solidify its rotation for 2027, and Bubic projects to slot in as a No. 3 or No. 4 starter — a real arm in the middle of the staff, not a lottery ticket at the back of it.

That is also the Guardians' whole model: take a pitcher with good stuff and a good track record, put him in one of baseball's best pitching-development organizations, and let the infrastructure do the rest. If the shoulder holds, $17.5 million for one year of a 3.02 ERA starter is a bargain. If it doesn't, the deal is over in twelve months.

What it means

The Guardians are not usually the team writing checks like this in November. That they did — a day after their first signing of the offseason — says they see a window and they are not waiting for the lockout to close it.

Bubic gets a one-year stage in the best possible place to rebuild his value. Cleveland gets a proven starter at a price that only stings if he can't stay on the mound.
