Eklund ties it with 3 seconds left, Islanders edge Blues in shootout

By Matt

Rookie Victor Eklund buried a one-timer with 2.3 seconds left to force overtime, and Bo Horvat and Mathew Barzal converted in the shootout as the Islanders beat the Blues 3-2 at UBS Arena.

Victor Eklund had 2.3 seconds to save the game, and he used every one of them. The 20-year-old rookie buried a one-timer from just above the right circle to tie the St. Louis Blues 2-2 on Wednesday night at UBS Arena, and the New York Islanders finished the job in the shootout for a 3-2 win.

It was the Islanders' seventh game-tying goal within the final three seconds of a game in franchise history — and the first since Anders Lee did it on March 3, 2015.

The final seconds

The Islanders pulled Ilya Sorokin for an extra attacker with a little over two minutes left, trailing 2-1 after Mason McTavish had put St. Louis ahead with six minutes to go. The pressure built, but the breakthrough didn't come until the very last shift.

Brayden Schenn collected a loose puck below the goal line and fed it up top to Eklund, who beat Joel Hofer blocker side with the one-timer. It was the Islanders' first 6-on-5 goal of the season — and it kept both points on the table.

After the game, Eklund earned the team's postgame oar for the moment. Asked by MSG's Shannon Hogan what he was thankful for with American Thanksgiving coming up, the rookie answered: "I'm thankful to be an Islander!"

How the game got there

Jean-Gabriel Pageau opened the scoring 5:37 into the second period, deflecting a Calum Ritchie feed past Hofer with his skate. St. Louis answered at 15:24 of the same period, when Dylan Holloway took a pass off a Robert Thomas faceoff win and beat Sorokin from the high slot.

McTavish's go-ahead goal came at 14:00 of the third, collecting a loose puck near the right post after a blocked shot and lifting it past Sorokin. The Blues, riding a four-game winning streak, looked poised to close it out on the road — until Eklund's shot changed everything.

Sorokin stopped 21 shots in regulation, then made two more saves in the shootout, including a glove stop on McTavish — the same player who had given St. Louis its late lead.

The shootout

Mathew Barzal scored in the first round, faking a shot and lifting the puck past Hofer's right arm. Bo Horvat followed in the second round, motoring through the middle of the slot before lifting a shot over Hofer's stick and right arm. Jake Neighbours scored the only shootout goal for St. Louis, and Horvat's tally stood as the winner.

What it means

The win moved the Islanders to 5-1-3 in their last nine games, and it snapped the Blues' four-game winning streak. For Eklund, a rookie still earning his place in the lineup, it was the kind of night that sticks — a game-tying goal in the final seconds of a game his team had no business winning, on a night the franchise hadn't seen anything like it in more than a decade.
