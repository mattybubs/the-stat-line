Cooley is the fastest active player to five hat tricks — and Caufield just set the NHL's overtime record

By Matt

Logan Cooley scored his second hat trick in four games and passed Alex Ovechkin as the fastest active player to five career hat tricks — but Cole Caufield's overtime winner gave Montreal a 5-4 win and made Caufield the fastest player ever to 15 career OT goals.

Logan Cooley scored his second hat trick in four games on Thursday night in Montreal, and it still wasn't enough. Cole Caufield scored 30 seconds into overtime to give the Canadiens a 5-4 win over the Utah Mammoth at Bell Centre — a night that produced two separate NHL speed records, one for each team.

Cooley became the fastest active player to record five career hat tricks, reaching the mark in 228 games and passing Alex Ovechkin, who needed 230. Caufield became the fastest player in NHL history to score 15 career overtime goals, doing it in 384 games — blowing past Connor McDavid's old pace of 559.

How the game played out

Cooley gave Utah a 1-0 lead at 1:42 of the first period, taking a stretch pass from Mikhail Sergachev at the blue line, driving in, and cutting across the goalmouth to tuck a backhand around Jakub Dobes' left pad. He made it 2-0 on the power play at 9:24, slipping behind the Montreal defense for a breakaway and scoring five-hole off a feed from Nick Schmaltz.

Montreal answered back. Nick Suzuki, playing his first game after missing six with an upper-body injury, cut it to 2-1 at 15:25, left unmarked at the left post to bury a cross-ice feed from Juraj Slafkovsky. Arber Xhekaj tied it 2-2 at 17:10 with a slap shot from the left point — his first goal of the season.

Cooley completed his fifth career hat trick with a power-play goal at 11:05 of the second period, putting Utah back ahead 3-2. Kirby Dach tied it 3-3 at 1:09 of the third in his return from illness, jamming in a loose puck at the left post. Schmaltz then gave Utah a 4-3 lead on the power play at 7:14, off a feed from Cooley — his fourth point of the night.

Adam Engstrom tied it 4-4 at 14:54 of the third, moving to the top of the slot and scoring through traffic for his first NHL goal. Thirty seconds into overtime, Caufield ended it: a 2-on-1, a fake slap shot, and a wrist shot over Sebastian Cossa's right shoulder. Cossa made 30 saves in the loss; Dobes stopped 16 for the win.

Cooley's heater is real

The hat trick was Cooley's second in four games, and the numbers behind the stretch are loud: nine points (seven goals, two assists) over his past four games. He has 11 goals in his last 10 games after a slow start to the season in which he went without a goal through his first half-dozen games.

The record he broke is a fun one to hold. Ovechkin — the greatest goal scorer in NHL history — needed 230 games to reach five hat tricks. Cooley did it in 228. It doesn't make Cooley the next Ovechkin, and nobody in Utah would say that. But being ahead of Ovechkin's pace at anything involving putting the puck in the net is a line that belongs in a young player's biography forever.

Cooley is doing it as the engine of a Mammoth team that keeps finding ways to stay in games. Utah fell to 10-6-1 but took a point on the road, and Sergachev's nine-game point streak (three goals, eight assists) is now the second-longest in Utah history, behind Clayton Keller's 10-gamer in 2025-26.

Caufield's overtime record is the quieter stunner

Fifteen career overtime goals in 384 games. McDavid needed 559 games to get there. Caufield has built his reputation as a shooter, not a volume guy, and overtime is where that skill pays off fastest — one clean look, one release, game over.

Thursday's winner was textbook Caufield: patience on the 2-on-1, a fake that froze the defense, and a wrist shot picked over the goalie's shoulder. It was also a response goal for a team that needed one. Two nights earlier, Montreal blew a 4-0 lead with 2:39 left in the third period and lost 5-4 in overtime to Winnipeg — the latest four-goal comeback in NHL history, and a loss that could have lingered.

It didn't. The Canadiens became just the fourth team in NHL history to win with a multigoal comeback in the game immediately after suffering a loss in which they held a four-goal lead, joining the Calgary Flames (October 18, 2007), the Toronto Maple Leafs (November 30, 2000), and the Edmonton Oilers (October 26, 1991). Suzuki put it simply after the game: "Big goals from different guys. Arber, 'Engy,' Kirby comes back, gets a big one there. It's a collective game."

Montreal improved to 10-3-3 and has won six of seven. Suzuki finished with a goal and two assists in his return, and coach Martin St. Louis was blunt about the bounce-back: "I loved the players' response to that game. I loved the players' response in this game."

What it means

Two records, one game, and both of them say something about where their teams are headed. Utah has a young center scoring goals faster than anyone in the league — and losing anyway, because the Mammoth are still learning how to close. Montreal has a team deep enough to survive its best players' absences, a captain who came back and produced immediately, and a closer in Caufield who now owns a piece of NHL history outright.

They meet again in the standings race soon enough. For one night, both fan bases got to watch history — just not the same kind.
