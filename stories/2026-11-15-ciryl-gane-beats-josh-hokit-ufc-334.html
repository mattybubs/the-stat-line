Ciryl Gane survives Josh Hokit in a record-breaking war to keep the heavyweight title at UFC 334

By Matt

Ciryl Gane kept his undisputed heavyweight title with a 48-47 unanimous decision over Josh Hokit at Madison Square Garden — a five-round war that set the UFC heavyweight record for combined significant strikes, and left half the building arguing about the scorecards.

The night

Twenty-five minutes, 796 total strikes thrown, and three scorecards that all read the same — and the building still wasn't sure.

Ciryl Gane retained the undisputed UFC heavyweight title on Saturday night at Madison Square Garden, beating previously unbeaten challenger Josh Hokit by unanimous decision (48-47, 48-47, 48-47) in the main event of UFC 334. It was Gane's first defense of the undisputed belt, and it took everything he had.

Hokit, the No. 4 contender, charged forward and tripped Gane seconds into the fight, then spent the early rounds in the champion's face — bloodied Gane's nose with right hands, kept a break-neck pace, and banked the early rounds on pressure and volume. The plan was obvious: make the French technician tired, and take the belt off him late. For three rounds, it looked like it might work.

Then the championship rounds arrived, and Gane did what champions do. He rallied in the fourth with knees to the body as Hokit's pace slowed, and in the fifth he landed the more impactful, significant blows as Hokit emptied out. By the final bell, the two heavyweights had combined to land 541 of those 796 strikes — Gane connecting on 298 of 412, Hokit on 243 of 384 — and set a new UFC heavyweight record for combined significant strikes in a fight.

The argument

The decision itself is the story now. All three judges scored it 48-47 for Gane, but plenty of people watching — including Alex Pereira, cageside, who called it a robbery for Hokit — had the challenger winning. MMA Junkie's own live scoring gave the fight to Hokit, 48-47.

Hokit suffers his first professional loss (now 10-1, 4-1 in the UFC). Gane improves to 15-2 (12-2 in the UFC, with one no-contest). Both men took home $100,000 Fight of the Night bonuses, and both left with something real: Gane keeps the belt, and Hokit — who was supposed to be the underdog in over his head — proved he belongs at the very top of the division.

"All the people around the world underestimated him. Not me. He did so well tonight. It was a tough fight," Gane said afterward.

Also on the card

The undercard delivered its own history. Stephen Thompson closed out his career with a spinning wheel kick TKO of Charles Radtke in the third round of the featured prelim — a retirement fight that ended the only way Wonderboy could end one. Bia Mesquita submitted Macy Chiasson with a rear-naked choke in the first round, breaking Ronda Rousey's record for the most submissions in UFC women's bantamweight history (four, passing Rousey's three) and becoming the first woman in UFC history to win four straight fights by submission. And Luis Gurule, a +700 underdog, took a split decision over previously unbeaten Bilal Hasan.
