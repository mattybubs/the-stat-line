Nolan's TKO of Moicano Stands — But Monday's Firestorm Is About the Referee, Not the Finish

By Matt

Tom Nolan's third-round TKO of Renato Moicano at UFC Vegas 123 was the biggest win of his career. Two days later, nobody is arguing about the finish — they're arguing about referee Dan Miragliotta standing Moicano up while he had Nolan's back in round two, a call 'Big' John McCarthy says gave Nolan an unfair advantage.

Tom Nolan won the biggest fight of his career on Saturday night. By Monday, his win was the second-biggest story about the fight.

Nolan stopped Renato Moicano by TKO at 1:28 of round three in the UFC Vegas 123 main event at the Meta APEX in Las Vegas, improving to 12-1 and, in his first UFC main event, punching his ticket toward the lightweight top 10. But the result has been swallowed by one moment in round two — and by what two of the sport's loudest voices said about it on Monday.

The stand-up that changed the fight

Moicano controlled the early going. He took Nolan down in the first round, took his back, and threatened submissions. In the second, he did it again — takedown, back control, body triangle, working for a rear-naked choke, a finish he has used nearly a dozen times in his career.

Then, after a brief stall of roughly eight seconds, referee Dan Miragliotta stood the fighters up.

Moments later, Nolan hurt Moicano with punches and a massive knee and closed the round with all the momentum. In the third, after Moicano shot for another takedown, Nolan reversed position and finished him with punches and elbows from top position. The finish itself was not controversial. The road to it was.

Monday's verdicts: McCarthy, Rogan, Florian

On Monday's "Weighing In" podcast, longtime referee John McCarthy said Miragliotta's stand-up was "completely unfair to one fighter — Moicano. Moicano earned that position. He worked for that position." McCarthy, who has officiated at the sport's highest level for decades, said he wasn't sure the stand-up happened for a good reason at all.

Joe Rogan was none too pleased either, and former title challenger Kenny Florian criticized the intervention in posts on November 1 and 2 — while pushing back on speculation that the UFC ordered it. Florian's point was narrower and sharper: the issue is positional progress, and Moicano had it — the back take and a secured body triangle.

Nolan, for his part, insists he didn't ask for the stand-up. At the post-fight press conference he said he was talking to Miragliotta because Moicano was punching him in the back of the head — "I wasn't trying to ask for a stand-up... I had nothing to do with the ref standing it up. It's not my fault." Moicano, notably, has no plan to appeal the result, per reports Monday.

What stands, and what doesn't

The record book is settled: Nolan by TKO, round three, 1:28; Nolan on a six-fight UFC winning streak with a callout of Mateusz Gamrot waiting; Moicano at 21-8-1, now 1-3 in his last four. Nobody is taking the win away.

But the debate is about something bigger than one fight: how much time a dominant grappling position gets before a referee restarts it on the feet. Moicano earned the position twice. He got to keep it neither time the second round came around — and the second time, it cost him the fight's momentum, then the fight. That's why Monday sounded less like a post-fight show and more like a referendum.
